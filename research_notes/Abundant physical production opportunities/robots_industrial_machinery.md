## 1. Market size today ($ and units/year, global and US)

### Takeaway
The established machine markets are large and measured (603k industrial robots worth $18.6B in 2025; about $135B of chip equipment in 2025 heading to $166B in 2026; about $120B of machine tools), while humanoids are still tiny (13k-20k units in 2025, 50k-60k forecast for 2026, more than 85% Chinese, mostly sold to labs and for shows). On-demand custom parts is a proven, fast-growing but still small digital slice (Xometry about $0.94B and Protolabs about $0.6B revenue in 2026) of a fragmented supply base for which I could not retrieve an authoritative total.

## 2. Lead times and price gaps; where lead time itself is the product

### Takeaway
The longest, best-documented waits are in castings/forgings (6-18+ months, some alloys 70-80 weeks, lengthening in 2026) and data-center power/cooling gear (12-18 months), with injection tooling at 4-20 weeks; these are the places where customers already pay for speed. The US-China gap on tooling is roughly 2-3x on price and 2x on quoted time, though quoted Chinese lead times are disputed.

### Cited Findings
- [consulting analysis, 2024] Aerospace castings/forgings take 6 to 18+ months per cycle; quality or demand surprises add 12+ months; titanium raw material about nine months; one sole-sourced steel alloy 70-80 weeks; many US foundries/forges cut half or more of staff during COVID; rebuilding lost engineering experience takes 5-10+ years; BCG recommends paying rush fees and premiums for performance — [BCG](https://www.bcg.com/publications/2024/fixing-aerospaces-supply-chain-for-casting-and-forging)
- [company statement, 2026] ATI said in Q1 2026 that lead times are extending for superalloy nickel, premium titanium, isothermal forgings and exotic alloys; on its Q4 2025 call it said some lead times had doubled in a quarter — [ATI Q1 2026 call](https://www.fool.com/earnings/call-transcripts/2026/04/30/ati-ati-q1-2026-earnings-call-transcript/); [ATI Q4 2025 call](https://www.aol.com/articles/ati-ati-q4-2025-earnings-150307374.html)
- [secondary, 2026] Aviation items that once took 4-6 weeks "now routinely quote 20 to 40 weeks"; an estimated 3,500+ commercial engines are stalled awaiting castings and forgings — [Fastener trade press citing Safe Fly Aviation](https://fastenershows.com/news/17000-aircraft-on-backlog-aviations-supply-chain-crisis/145609/)
- [consulting study] Across 15 defense programs, companies cited supply-chain constraints more consistently than labor, engineering or capital equipment as the limit on scaling — [Bain](https://www.bain.com/insights/aerospace-and-defense-winning-the-race-to-scale/)
- [vendor/secondary] Injection molds: China about 30-45 days plus 10-15 days shipping vs US 6-10 weeks (one vendor); 4-6 weeks China vs 10-14 weeks US (another); cost about $5k-25k China vs $15k-80k US for a mid-complexity production mold. One worked example: a $45k US mold vs a Chinese mold landing at $23.7k after Section 301 tariff and freight — [Zetar Mold](https://zetarmold.com/?p=53884); [Dewin](https://dewin-us.pages.dev/blog/injection-mold-tooling-cost-guide)
- [practitioner testimony, 2019] A US molder said US tool shops typically take 18-20 weeks per tool versus Chinese tools shipping in 4 weeks and in production in 10; a commenter countered that US tools are 10-12 weeks and Chinese sampling can add ~20 weeks. USTR received 200+ exemption requests from molders when tariffs hit molds in 2018 — [Core77](http://www.core77.com/posts/82152/Explaining-What-the-Hell-is-Going-On-With-US-Tariffs-and-Chinese-Injection-Molds)
- [practitioner] "About 8 to 12 weeks" is the standard quoted tooling lead time, and it often runs longer in practice — [QualityInspection.org](https://qualityinspection.org/why-injection-mold-tooling-often-takes-longer-than-you-were-quoted/)
- [measured, Q4 2025] Vertiv's large orders deliver 12-18 months out, some into 2027 — [Data Center Frontier](https://www.datacenterfrontier.com/machine-learning/article/55357689/vertivs-ai-infrastructure-surge-record-orders-liquid-cooling-expansion-and-grid-scale-power-reshape-data-center-growth)
- [company claims, 2026] Speed is the pitch of the new entrants: Hadrian says it cuts lead times "from months to days"; Machina Labs says digital design to production compresses "from months to days"; Rangeview says tooling costs "become nonexistent" — [TNW on Hadrian](https://thenextweb.com/news/hadrian-1-37bn-series-d-7-87bn-defence-factories); [Robotics & Automation News on Machina](https://roboticsandautomationnews.com/2026/02/08/machina-labs-raises-124-million-to-scale-manufacturing-infrastructure/98760/); [Rangeview](https://www.rangeview.com/)
- [company claim, 2026] Formic quotes a 12-week average lead time to stand up an automation cell with $0 capex — [Formic](https://formic.co/)
- [measured] Robotaxi vehicle supply: Jaguar I-Pace order totals 3,500 with the last entering service in 2026; Zeekr-built Ojai fleet expected to reach "thousands" by end-2026; Hyundai plans 50,000 Ioniq 5s for Waymo starting 2028 — [Wikipedia: Waymo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waymo)

### Inferences
- Lead time is the product wherever a late part idles a much more valuable asset: jet engines (3,500 stalled), missiles and submarines (Hadrian's customers), and AI data halls (Vertiv's $15bn backlog). In these markets a 10x speed gain can be sold at a premium rather than a discount, which is the opposite of the Model T logic and a better wedge for a capital-light entrant.
- Castings/forgings look like the single most supply-constrained category in this sector: long cycles, lost workforce, 5-10 year know-how rebuild, and customers (primes, engine OEMs) already advised to pay rush premiums.
- Injection tooling is a 2-3x price and ~2x time gap versus China rather than a 10x gap, so the unlock there is more about tariffs/IP/iteration speed than raw cost.

### Gaps
- No sourced data for custom CNC-part lead times or price premiums for expedite (Xometry/Protolabs do not publish them in the releases retrieved).
- No sourced lead times for PCBs/PCBA, pressure vessels, heat exchangers, large pumps/valves, harmonic reducers, or machine tools themselves.
- No authoritative US-vs-China cost comparison for machined parts or castings.

## 3. Cost breakdown (humanoids; custom parts) and what AI/automation removes

### Takeaway
Actuation is 40-60% of a humanoid's bill of materials and hands another ~15%, and the cost leader (Unitree) sells at about $24k average with a ~60% gross margin by making motors and reducers in-house, which implies a build cost near $10k. For custom parts I found only vendor evidence that AI removes up to ~80% of CAM programming time; no authoritative split of programming/setup/quoting versus machine time and material was retrieved.

### Cited Findings
- [consulting estimate] Actuators are 40-60% of humanoid BOM; typical unit BOM is roughly $30k-$150k — [McKinsey, via search digest](https://www.mckinsey.com/industries/industrials-and-electronics/our-insights/turning-humanoid-supply-chain-constraints-into-billion-dollar-wins); echoed by [The Robot Report](https://www.therobotreport.com/unitree-ipo-shows-a-real-hardware-business-the-humanoid-case-is-still-early/)
- [secondary compilation] At roughly a $50k unit: actuators 50%, hands/end effectors 15%, sensors 10%, compute 8%, structure 7%, wiring 5%, battery 5%. China holds 63-70% of the humanoid supply chain (attributed to Morgan Stanley and Bain); NVIDIA has 80%+ of humanoid compute — [Humanoid.guide](https://humanoid.guide/the-humanoid-robot-supply-chain/)
- [bank estimate, secondary] Morgan Stanley's teardown of a Tesla Optimus Gen 2-class robot: about $55k BOM, actuators ~56%. Its longer view: average BOM with a non-China supply chain about $131k today, falling to about $23k by 2045 [forecast] — [Humanoid.guide](https://humanoid.guide/?p=17836); [iTiger summary](https://www.itiger.com/news/1174085574)
- [measured, prospectus] Unitree humanoid ASP fell from RMB 593,400 (~$85k) in 2023 to RMB 167,600 (~$24-25k) in the first nine months of 2025 (-35.7% YoY); quadruped ASP RMB 27,200 (~$3.9k, -15.8%) — [The Robot Report](https://www.therobotreport.com/unitree-ipo-shows-a-real-hardware-business-the-humanoid-case-is-still-early/); [KrASIA](https://kr-asia.com/what-is-the-secret-behind-unitrees-high-gross-margins)
- [measured, prospectus] Unitree gross margin 9M 2025: 59.5% overall, humanoids 62.9%, quadrupeds 55.5%, components 60.4%; peers UBTech ~30%, Dobot ~43%. It makes motors, gear reducers, dexterous hands and LiDAR in-house and outsources PCBA and injection molding. R&D was only ~$12.5M in 9M 2025 (7.73% of revenue) — [KrASIA](https://kr-asia.com/what-is-the-secret-behind-unitrees-high-gross-margins); [The Robot Report](https://www.therobotreport.com/unitree-ipo-shows-a-real-hardware-business-the-humanoid-case-is-still-early/)

- [company claims] 1X NEO: $20,000 purchase or $499/month. Figure: CEO said home lease about $600/month (May 2026). Tesla: Musk estimated eventual cost $20,000-$30,000 (Oct 2024) — [TNW](https://thenextweb.com/news/1x-neo-humanoid-factory-hayward-10000-home-robots); [Wikipedia: Figure AI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Figure_AI); [Wikipedia: Optimus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Optimus_(robot))
- [illustrative estimate] Agility Digit 5 about $200,000 price and about $400,000 five-year ownership cost (TechRepublic estimate, flagged by the author as not universal) — [Robotics 24/7](https://www.robotics247.com/article/humanoid-orders-vs-actual-deployments-how-much-commercial-progress-is-really-happening)
- [historical] Dexterous hands have been extreme cost items: Robonaut 2 hand ~$250k each, Shadow hand ~$100k; a human hand has ~17,000 touch receptors vs 17-94 tactile sensors on cited robot hands — [Construction Physics, Apr 2025](https://www.construction-physics.com/p/robot-dexterity-still-seems-hard)
- [robotaxi hardware, company claims] Pony.ai's new robotaxi has total manufacturing cost (vehicle + battery + driving suite) below RMB 230,000 (~$33.7k); WeRide claims a 50% kit cost cut; Baidu says RT6 unit cost is below $30,000 vs $66,000 for 2021's RT5. Waymo's vehicle is estimated above $100k-$150k (2024-2025 estimates) — [Caixin](https://www.caixinglobal.com/2026-05-04/chinese-robotaxi-firms-race-to-cut-costs-as-profitability-remains-out-of-reach-102440604.html); [Sacra](https://sacra.com/chat/h/639de3b8-6804-458c-b0f2-8d237fab8580/); [Wikipedia: Waymo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waymo)
- [vendor claims, custom parts] CloudNC CAM Assist: "complete up to 80% of your CAM program in minutes"; used by 1,000+ factories; one shop saves 2 days/month of programming worth $50,000/yr; another cut a 1-hour task to 12 minutes; one reports 7 minutes to 80% then 15 minutes of human fine-tuning. Limits: 3-axis and 3+2 milling only, no lathes or mill-turn — [CloudNC](https://www.cloudnc.com/)
- [measured] Digital-manufacturer gross margins: Protolabs 44.5% (Q3 2026, down 140 bps on materials, machine upgrades and labor); Xometry marketplace 36.0% (take between buyer price and supplier cost), total 39.1% — [Protolabs](https://finviz.com/news/411151/protolabs-reports-financial-results-for-the-third-quarter-of-2026); [Xometry](https://finviz.com/news/414090/xometry-reports-record-third-quarter-2026-results)

### Inferences
- Unitree's ~63% humanoid gross margin at a ~$24k ASP implies cost of goods near $9k per average unit (2025). This is the real benchmark a Western entrant faces, not the $55k-$131k Western BOM estimates. The China-vs-non-China BOM gap is roughly 3-6x today.
- Because actuators plus hands are ~65% of BOM, a new entrant that makes actuators, reducers, motors or hands 5-10x cheaper outside China addresses most of the cost and sells to every humanoid maker. The component layer is where "radically cheaper production" maps most directly onto a product.
- Xometry's 36% marketplace margin is the price buyers pay for instant quoting and sourcing on top of shop cost; this is a rough measure of how much of a custom part's price is coordination (quoting, sourcing, risk) rather than cutting metal.
- AI demonstrably compresses CAM programming on simple 3-axis work; setup, fixturing, inspection and 5-axis/mill-turn programming remain manual in the evidence retrieved.

### Gaps
- No authoritative breakdown of a custom machined part's cost into quoting/programming/setup/inspection labor vs machine time vs material.
- No sourced unit prices for harmonic reducers, planetary roller screws, frameless motors, tactile sensors or magnet content per robot (one site estimates ~30 magnet-driven motors per robot; weight per robot varies by source).
- Unitree R1 (~$5.9k) price, Tesla Optimus actual BOM, Figure and 1X BOM and assembly-labor share were not retrievable in this session.
- Goldman Sachs' cost-decline figures were not retrievable (page blocked).

## 4. Latent demand

### Takeaway
The strongest measured latent demand in this sector is for capacity and speed, not for cheaper robots: aerospace/defense metal parts, AI data-center gear, chip tools, robotaxi rides (Waymo grew rides 10x in under two years on a fleet that grew far less) and on-demand parts (Xometry +40%). Humanoid demand at $20k is so far demonstrated only for labs, shows and pre-orders; productive deployments total hundreds to low thousands of units working at 30-50% of human speed.

Note Uber's figure includes delivery, so this is a rough upper-bound comparison. Against that, known supply is 3,500 Jaguars (complete), "thousands" of Ojai vans, and 50,000 Hyundais starting 2028. Purpose-built, low-cost autonomous vehicles are supply-gated, and China's sub-$34k robotaxi is 3-4x cheaper than Waymo's estimated vehicle.
- The humanoid price-elasticity evidence is real but shallow: a 3.5x price cut produced a ~1,000x unit increase from a base of five units, into buyers (labs, universities, showrooms) whose demand is finite. The Model T analogy only holds once a robot does a job; at 30-50% of human productivity and "pilot" reliability the binding constraint is capability, not price.
- The Model-T-style latent demand that is measurable today sits in the picks-and-shovels: metal parts for defense/aero/space, tooling, chip tools, data-center power/cooling, robotaxi vehicles, and robot components.

## 5. Regulatory and timeline gates

### Takeaway
For humanoids and custom robots the gate is technology readiness first (dexterity, reliability), then standards (no published safety standard for balancing robots until ISO 25785-1 lands in late 2026-2027), with trade policy now a wild card in both directions: the FCC's July 2026 Covered List action blocks new foreign (mostly Chinese) robots from US approval while China's rare-earth magnet licensing can choke US robot makers. For robotaxis the gate is regulatory and vehicle supply (2,500-vehicle exemption cap, state/local politics); for aerospace/defense parts it is qualification and workforce.

### Cited Findings

**Robot safety standards**
- ISO 25785-1 (safety for industrial mobile robots with actively controlled stability: bipeds, quadrupeds, wheeled balancers) is at Committee Draft stage; the working group kept "humanoid" out of the title; a European prEN ISO 25785-1 project began 9 Jan 2026 — [ISO](https://www.iso.org/standard/91469.html); [DIN](https://www.din.de/en/getting-involved/standards-committees/nam/projects/wdc-proj:din21:399126717)
- BSI: there is no ISO safety standard published for these robots; ISO 10218 and ISO 3691-4 do not cover the mobility/fall risks — [BSI](https://standardsdevelopment.bsigroup.com/projects/9025-11561)
- [secondary] Expected ratification 18-36 months after the May 2025 draft, i.e. late 2026 or 2027; "volume deployment of humanoids working alongside people is unlikely before 2027". Draft requires calculated and floor-marked fall zones, controlled descent on power loss, and force limits from ISO/TS 15066 (now in ISO 10218-2:2025). Scope is industrial only; homes fall under ISO 13482 (personal care robots), which does not address these stability hazards. OSHA's General Duty Clause already applies to falling robots; insurers have begun auditing deployments against consensus standards — [i-SCOOP](https://www.i-scoop.eu/iso-25785-1-explained-and-what-it-means-for-humanoid-robot-safety/)
- A former Figure head of product safety sued (Nov 2025) alleging she was fired for warning the robots could fracture a human skull — [Wikipedia: Figure AI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Figure_AI)
- Home privacy: as of Oct 2025 most 1X NEO tasks were done by a remote human operator in VR, prompting privacy concerns — [Wikipedia: 1X Technologies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1X_Technologies)

**Trade, tariffs, security**
- Section 232 (robotics and industrial machinery): opened 2 Sept 2025; on 28 Oct 2026 the President accepted Commerce's finding that imports threaten national security but imposed no tariffs, ordering negotiations with a 180-day report-back; tariffs remain available afterwards — [Mayer Brown](https://www.mayerbrown.com/en/insights/publications/2026/10/president-trump-issues-proclamation-on-imports-of-robotics-industrial-machinery-and-their-parts-and-components); [Manufacturing Dive](https://www.manufacturingdive.com/news/trump-opts-trade-talks-over-tariffs-robotics-industrial-machinery/873201/); [The Robot Report](https://www.therobotreport.com/white-house-makes-section-232-determination-regarding-foreign-made-robots/)
- FCC (late July 2026) added foreign-made "advanced robotic devices" to its Covered List: new humanoids and robot dogs, and almost any new wireless, software-controlled, sensing robot over 4.4 lb (incl. robot mowers, delivery bots, robot vacuums), cannot get the equipment approval needed for US sale; already-approved models stay. China's commerce ministry threatened countermeasures — [TNW](https://thenextweb.com/news/china-retaliation-us-fcc-robot-import-ban-rare-earths)
- The Defense Department added Unitree to its list of Chinese military-linked companies in June 2026; researchers reported backdoor/remote-access and data-collection vulnerabilities in Unitree robots in 2025 — [Wikipedia: Unitree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unitree_Robotics)
- Rare earths: China's April 2025 licensing controls on heavy rare earths (incl. dysprosium, terbium) remain in force; on 22 June 2026 MOFCOM added ten US entities, including MP Materials and USA Rare Earth, to its export-control list — [S&P Global](https://www.spglobal.com/market-intelligence/en/news-insights/articles/2026/6/china-tightens-rare-earth-export-curbs-on-10-us-companies-103014947); [SMM](https://news.metal.com/newscontent/103965490-China-Hits-Back-MOFCOM-Restricts-Rare-Earth-Exports-to-10-US-FirmsSMM-Analysis)
- [commentary sources; dates conflict] China makes over 90% of rare-earth magnets (about 70% of mining, ~90% of refining and magnets). The broader October 2025 controls are suspended, with one source saying until 10 Jan 2027 and another citing a 10 Nov 2026 expiry; magnet exports to the US still fell 21% in August despite the pause — [OilPrice](https://oilprice.com/Energy/Energy-General/Robot-Wars-How-China-Has-the-Robotics-Industry-in-Its-Grip.html); [Rare Earth Exchanges](https://rareearthexchanges.com/news/chinas-robot-warning-exposes-americas-real-vulnerability-its-not-the-robot-its-the-supply-chain/)
- "It has become nearly impossible to build a competitive humanoid without Chinese-made parts such as sensors and joints" — [TNW](https://thenextweb.com/news/china-retaliation-us-fcc-robot-import-ban-rare-earths)

**Autonomous vehicles**
- NHTSA granted Zoox a Part 555 exemption in July 2026, the first for a passenger-carrying ADS vehicle, covering parts of eight FMVSS; Zoox may add 2,500 vehicles a year for two years — [Federal Register notice](https://public-inspection.federalregister.gov/2026-15485.pdf); [Axios](https://axios.com/2026/08/05/tesla-zoox-regulatory-win-trump)

- Waymo in 2026: NHTSA recall query on 2,895 vehicles (6 Nov 2026, school-bus passing); California DMV suspended its freeway operations in Nov 2026; California passed a law (Oct 2026) allowing fines for impeding emergency services; New York dropped a proposal to allow robotaxis outside NYC and Waymo's NYC test permits expired 31 Mar 2026 — [Wikipedia: Waymo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waymo)
- Safety evidence in Waymo's favor: IIHS (July 2026) found injury/fatal crash rates 81% lower than human drivers — [Wikipedia: Waymo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waymo)
- China: ministries ordered industry-wide "rectification" in mid-April 2026 after ~100 Apollo Go robotaxis stalled in Wuhan on 31 Mar 2026 — [Caixin](https://www.caixinglobal.com/2026-05-28/china-robotaxi-firms-expand-fleets-despite-regulatory-pause-102448697.html)

**Technology readiness**
- Manipulation, not locomotion, is the unsolved problem; many demos are teleoperated, edited or avoid hard tasks; Rodney Brooks predicts humanoid dexterity will remain "pathetic" versus humans beyond 2036 — [Construction Physics, Apr 2025](https://www.construction-physics.com/p/robot-dexterity-still-seems-hard)
- IFR (2026): "General-purpose Physical AI systems and learning from simulation and experience are not yet industry-grade"; widespread humanoid deployment "will depend on substantial improvements in reliability, safety, operating time, and economic viability" — [IFR Executive Summary](https://ifr.org/img/worldrobotics/Executive_Summary_WR_2026_-_Industrial_Robots.pdf)
- Aerospace/defense parts: engineering shortages slow new-part qualification; rebuilding experience takes 5-10+ years; training production staff 3-6 months — [BCG](https://www.bcg.com/publications/2024/fixing-aerospaces-supply-chain-for-casting-and-forging)

### Inferences
- Gate by category: humanoids = technology readiness > standards > magnets/components; industrial arms/cobots = integration cost and labor, standards mature (ISO 10218:2025); robotaxis = regulation and vehicle supply, technology largely proven (Waymo); aero/defense castings, forgings and machined parts = qualification, workforce and capacity, not technology; semicap and data-center gear = production capacity.
- The FCC action plus the DoD listing create a protected US market for domestically built robots (legged, mobile, lawn, vacuum) starting from new models, while the magnet controls mean "domestic" builders still depend on Chinese inputs. A non-China actuator/magnet/reducer supply chain is therefore both a compliance need and a product.
- The Section 232 outcome (finding of threat, no tariff, 180-day talks ending around late April 2027) leaves machinery import costs uncertain for anyone planning a factory now.

- Not researched to source: export controls on machine tools and semicap (BIS rules), AS9100/NADCAP/first-article timelines in months, product-liability regimes for robots, home-robot privacy law, existing tariff rates on imported robots and CNC machines, state AV permit counts.
- ISO 25785-1's exact current stage is unclear (ISO page snapshots differ); no confirmed publication date.
- The current status (Nov 2026) of China's suspended rare-earth controls is unresolved between sources.
- Outcome of the NHTSA Cybercab audit not found.

## 6. Objections and actor responses

### Takeaway
Buyers object on ROI and uptime, and the data back them: humanoids cost several times a task-specific robot for lower throughput; order books are conditional and far exceed delivered, accepted units. Incumbent and Chinese cost positions are strong (China holds 63-70% of the humanoid supply chain, 90%+ of magnets, and 59% of robot demand), labor and local politics are actively blocking robotaxis, and investors are pricing the model layer (Skild $14B, Physical Intelligence $5.6-20B, zero revenue) on the bet that hardware commoditises.

### Inferences
- The commoditisation risk cuts two ways. A new US entrant in whole humanoids fights Tesla, Figure ($39bn) and Chinese cost at once; a component or deployment entrant sells to all of them.
- The measurable buyer objection is not price but accepted, productive hours. Any robot business should plan to be paid per unit of work (RaaS) and to publish uptime, as Formic does (99.30%).
- Weak job-shop capex means the fragmented incumbent base is not automating itself; that leaves room for well-capitalised new factories (Hadrian model) and for software sold into shops (CloudNC model).

### Inferences
- Pattern in the failures: a new production process or machine sold as a product (metal binder-jet printers, an electric autonomous tractor) with outsourced manufacturing and optimistic SPAC-era projections. Pattern in the survivors: sell the output (parts, uptime, rides), not the machine; anchor on customers with urgent, well-funded demand (defense primes, Navy, hyperscalers); keep critical components in-house.
- Revenue per dollar raised is extremely low for Western humanoid makers (Agility: $640M+ raised, $1.8M sales). Unitree's R&D of ~$12.5M in nine months against $167M revenue shows a very different capital efficiency.
- Xometry needed about $1bn of revenue to reach GAAP breakeven at a 36-39% gross margin; marketplaces in parts are viable but thin.

## 8. Buildability: capital to first revenue, best wedge, gross margins seen

### Takeaway
On the evidence, the buildable wedges are (a) speed-critical, qualification-heavy metal parts for defense/aero/space (castings, forgings, formed sheet, machined assemblies), (b) non-China robot components (actuators, reducers, motors, magnets, hands), and (c) deployment/service businesses that sell robot work by the month; whole humanoids and robotaxis are multi-billion-dollar games already crowded by Tesla, Figure, Waymo and Chinese cost leaders. Gross margins seen range from ~36% (parts marketplace) to ~45% (digital factory) to ~60% (vertically integrated Chinese robot maker).

### Inferences
Suggested ranking for this sector, by evidence of unmet demand, gate type and capital needed (my judgement from the findings above, not a sourced ranking):
1. **Fast castings/forgings and other long-lead metal parts for defense, aero engines, space and energy.** Longest documented waits (6-18+ months, lengthening in 2026), customers already told to prepay and pay premiums, government contracting for capacity (Navy/Rangeview), qualification as a moat once passed. Risk: qualification time and metallurgy talent (5-10 years to rebuild).
2. **Non-China robot components: actuators, reducers, motors/magnets, hands, tactile skins.** 50-65% of every humanoid's BOM, 3-6x China-vs-West cost gap, export-control and FCC pressure forcing dual sourcing, and demand from every humanoid, cobot and drone maker regardless of who wins. Risk: volumes are still small (50-60k humanoids in 2026) and Unitree-class makers build in-house.
3. **AI-run "factory as a service" for precision parts and assemblies (Hadrian/Isembard model), or software for existing shops (CloudNC model).** Demand proven (Xometry +40%, Hadrian valuation 5x in seven months), but the factory version now needs $100M-$1bn+ and competes with a $7.9bn leader; the software version is cheap to start but covers only part of the workflow.
4. **Data-center power/cooling modules and other AI-infrastructure hardware.** Measured backlog ($15bn at one vendor, 12-18 month waits), high margins; incumbents are scaling fast and customers are few and demanding.
5. **Robotaxi-grade purpose-built vehicles and retrofit kits.** Demand is clearly supply-gated, but entry costs tens of billions for operators; a vehicle or component supplier role is more realistic. Gates: 2,500/yr exemption cap unless Congress acts; tariffs on Chinese-built platforms.
6. **Robots-as-a-service using mature arms/cobots/AMRs for mid-market factories and farms (Formic, Carbon Robotics pattern).** Works today with proven uptime and 1-3 year paybacks; growth limited by integration effort per site.
7. **Whole humanoids (factory or home).** Largest claimed prize, but capability-gated (30-50% of human productivity, teleoperation in homes), standards not yet published, and capital-saturated ($11.8bn across 26 firms). A new entrant here is betting against Tesla, Figure and Chinese makers with ~$9k build costs.
8. **New-process machine vendors (metal 3D printers, novel tractors) sold as products.** Worst track record in the evidence (Desktop Metal, Fast Radius, Monarch).

Other inferences:
- Capital to first revenue is lowest for software-into-shops and RaaS with third-party robots (single-digit $M), moderate for a single specialised foundry or forming cell backed by a defense contract (tens of $M, as Machina/Rangeview/Isembard suggest), and highest for humanoids, robotaxis and multi-site automated factories ($1bn+).
- "Sell the output, not the machine" is the consistent survivor rule in this sector.
