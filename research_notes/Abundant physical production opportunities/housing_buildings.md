## 1. Market size today (units/year and $)

### Takeaway
US housing is about 1.36M starts plus about 103k manufactured homes a year (2025), and the factory-built share has been flat near 7-10% for a decade. The existing cheap factory product (HUD-code homes at roughly half the cost per sq ft) sells one-sixth of its 1973 volume, which is the single most important fact for this sector.

### Cited Findings
**US site-built starts**
- 2025 total starts: 1,355,700; single-family 939,700; multifamily 416,000 (Census figures as tabulated by MHI) — [MHI Economic Report, March 2026](https://www.manufacturedhousing.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/MHI-Economic-Report-March-2026.pdf)
- 2025 total starts 1.36M, down 0.6% from 1.37M in 2024; single-family 943k (-6.9%); multifamily about 417k (+17.4%) — [NAHB Eye on Housing, Feb 2026](https://eyeonhousing.org/2026/02/overall-housing-starts-inch-lower-in-2025/) (via search summary; small difference from the MHI table is revision timing)
- US housing stock is about 148M units (2025); net additions averaged about 2.0M/yr in the 1980s, 0.8M/yr in the 2010s, 1.4M/yr in 2020-2025 — [Boston Fed conference paper, Patel, 2026](https://www.bostonfed.org/-/media/Documents/events/2026/econ-conference/measuringhousingunderproductionwhyestimatesdivergeandwhatanationalnumbercansayelenapatel111326.pdf)

**Manufactured (HUD-code) homes**

## 2. Price, build time, cost breakdown, and the hard floor

### Takeaway
The structure that a factory can make is about half of a new home's sale price, and labor is about half of that, so automation alone removes perhaps 10-25% of delivered price; land, site work, soft costs and margin form a floor near 45-50%. A century of prefab evidence shows typical savings of 0-20%, with HUD-code homes the only product that is durably 35-50% cheaper.

### Cited Findings
**Site-built single-family cost stack (NAHB 2024 survey, national averages, sample skews to larger homes)**
- Average sales price $665,298; construction cost $428,215; 2,647 sq ft finished area — [NAHB](https://www.nahb.org/blog/2025/01/cost-to-construct-a-home-rose-significantly-over-last-two-years) (via search summary)
- Share of sales price: construction 64.4% (series high since 1998), finished lot 13.7% (series low), builder profit 11.0%, overhead 5.7%, sales commission 2.8%, financing 1.5%, marketing 0.8% — [NAHB Eye on Housing](https://eyeonhousing.org/2025/01/cost-of-constructing-a-home-in-2024/)
- Share of construction cost by stage: interior finishes 24.1%, major system rough-ins 19.2%, framing 16.6%, exterior finishes 13.4%, foundations 10.5%, site work 7.6%, final steps 6.5%, other 2.1% — [NAHB Eye on Housing](https://eyeonhousing.org/2025/01/cost-of-constructing-a-home-in-2024/)
- Direct labor is roughly half of single-family construction cost, versus about 10-12% of manufacturing cost for cars — [Construction Physics, "Elusive Cost Savings"](https://www.construction-physics.com/p/the-elusive-cost-savings-of-the-prefabricated)
- Regulation accounts for 23.8% of the final price of a new single-family home ($93,870 on a $394,300 home in 2021): 10.5% during lot development, 13.3% during construction. Fees paid after lot purchase are 3.1% of price; impact and hookup fees at site work are 3.0% — [NAHB special study, 2021](https://www.nahb.org/-/media/NAHB/news-and-economics/docs/housing-economics-plus/special-studies/2021/special-study-government-regulation-in-the-price-of-a-new-home-may-2021.pdf) (industry survey, builder self-reports)
- NAHB's survey overweights high-end builders: NAHB 2022 cost was about $207/sq ft versus Census averages of $168 (mean) and $158 (median) — [Construction Physics on ICON](https://www.construction-physics.com/p/what-progress-has-icon-made-on-3d)

**Build time (Census Survey of Construction, 2025)**
- Start to completion: single-family built-for-sale 6.1 months; contractor-built 10.5; owner-built 12.8; all single-family 7.4; buildings with 2+ units 16.5; 20+ units 19.1 months — [Census, start-to-completion table](https://www.census.gov/construction/nrc/data/time.html)
- Permit to start: 1.4 months single-family, 2.4 months multifamily (2025). This excludes all pre-permit entitlement time — [Census, authorization-to-start table](https://www.census.gov/construction/nrc/data/time.html)

**Manufactured homes**
- About $87/sq ft versus about $166/sq ft for site-built, excluding land (MHI figures) — [Sun Communities citing MHI](https://www.suncommunities.com/blog/manufactured-homes-vs-traditional-homes-comparing-costs) (via search summary)
- On like-for-like specs, a single-section home costs 35-47% of a comparable site-built home, a double-section 60-64%, a CrossMod 73-80% (Harvard JCHS 2023). After quality adjustment a single-wide is "probably 40-50% cheaper" — [Construction Physics, "Elusive Cost Savings"](https://www.construction-physics.com/p/the-elusive-cost-savings-of-the-prefabricated)
- Manufactured home prices have risen roughly with inflation, unlike most manufactured goods — [Construction Physics, "Elusive Cost Savings"](https://www.construction-physics.com/p/the-elusive-cost-savings-of-the-prefabricated)
- The steel chassis adds about 12% to cost (historical estimate); removing it is estimated to save $5,000-$10,000 per home, though a Lincoln Institute researcher says estimates vary widely — [Construction Physics](https://www.construction-physics.com/p/the-rise-and-fall-of-the-manufactured); [Niskanen Center](https://www.niskanencenter.org/two-big-developments-in-manufactured-housing-reform-new-rules-new-bill/); [HousingWire](https://www.housingwire.com/articles/new-policy-impact-may-ignite-a-manufactured-housing-blue-sky-era/)
- One Florida plant produces about 15 homes a day — [Bay News 9, Sep 2026](https://baynews9.com/fl/tampa/news/2026/09/12/new-federal-housing-law-could-reshape-manufactured-homes-in-florida)
- Backlogs are short: Cavco $195M, Champion $316M at March 2026, about 5-6 weeks of annual sales by my arithmetic — [Cavco 8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0000278166/000162828026037575/cvco-2026328xex991.htm); [Champion 8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0000090896/000119312526237809/sky-ex99_1.htm)

**Prefab and modular savings actually observed**
- Historical record: 1950s prefab, only 6 of 22 systems were cheaper per sq ft; Douglas Commission (1969) estimated 16.5% savings for a fully factory-built house; 1998 HUD/NAHB study about 15%; Volumetric Building Companies estimated 5-10%; Bensonwood says it is not price-competitive with code-minimum stick-built. Operation Breakthrough: 5 of 22 systems claimed 5-20% savings and 20 of 22 were discontinued — [Construction Physics, "Elusive Cost Savings"](https://www.construction-physics.com/p/the-elusive-cost-savings-of-the-prefabricated)
- McKinsey (2019): modular cuts schedules 20-50%; cost savings "up to 20%" are the exception, with risk of up to 10% cost increase if logistics or materials outweigh labor savings — [McKinsey, "Modular construction: From projects to products"](https://www.mckinsey.com/capabilities/operations/our-insights/modular-construction-from-projects-to-products) (via search summary; page not opened)
- Terner Center (2026): documented savings of up to 20% on three-to-four-story wood-frame multifamily and timelines 40-50% shorter in specific applications. Hard costs are typically more than 60% of multifamily total development cost; California multifamily runs $400,000-$500,000+ per unit; RAND finds California hard costs 2.3 times Texas — [Terner Center, March 2026](https://committees.assembly.ca.gov/media/81)
- 833 Bryant (San Francisco, Factory OS modules, 145 units): $383,000 per unit versus about $700,000 average for San Francisco affordable housing; three years versus six or more. Terner measured 30% time and 25% cost savings — [CalMatters, 2021](https://calmatters.org/commentary/2021/03/modular-housing-california-san-francisco-cost-unions/); [Terner Center](https://ternercenter.berkeley.edu/research-and-policy/building-affordability-by-building-affordably-the-case-for-off-site-multifamily-construction/) (Terner figure via search summary)
- Savings often come from labor arbitrage (build in Utah or China, ship to California) rather than factory productivity — [Construction Physics, "Elusive Cost Savings"](https://www.construction-physics.com/p/the-elusive-cost-savings-of-the-prefabricated)
- Sweden: new single-family homes cost about $286/sq ft in 2023 versus $166 in the US, more than 70% higher, despite 85% prefab; a Lindbäcks executive said factory-built homes will not be cheap. Japan: prefab cost about 8% more per sq ft than site-built in 2000 — [Construction Physics, Sweden](https://www.construction-physics.com/p/should-us-homebuilders-emulate-sweden); [Construction Physics, "Elusive Cost Savings"](https://www.construction-physics.com/p/the-elusive-cost-savings-of-the-prefabricated)
- 3D printing: ICON's printed walls cost $34/sq ft on its Lennar project (down from $160 at House Zero) against an RSMeans estimate of about $16-17.50 for a conventional exterior wall, so 2-4 times conventional; learning rate about 27% per doubling. ICON's Titan targets $20/sq ft — [Construction Physics on ICON](https://www.construction-physics.com/p/what-progress-has-icon-made-on-3d); [The Real Deal, Mar 2026](https://therealdeal.com/texas/2026/03/12/icon-opens-3d-home-printing-tech-to-outside-builders/) (TRD via search summary)
- Scale economies in buildings are weak: a building seven times larger costs just under 20% less per sq ft; Pulte (20,000+ homes/yr) sells at about $230/sq ft versus $220 for a small regional builder nearby — [Construction Physics, economies of scale](https://www.construction-physics.com/p/why-are-there-so-few-economies-of)

**ADUs**
- California median ADU cost $150,000, $250/sq ft; 37% under $100,000 and 71% under $200,000; median rent $2,000/month (2021 survey) — [Terner Center CCI ADU survey](https://ternercenter.berkeley.edu/blog/cci-adu-survey)
- Samara Backyard: "starting at $152k plus installation," as few as eight weeks on site, California only — [Samara](https://www.samara.com/backyard)
- Boxabl Casita: $60,000 before shipping, installation and site work (first advertised at $50,000); average order value about $74,000 in 2026 — [The Real Deal, Jul 2026](https://therealdeal.com/national/2026/07/21/boxabl-ignites-spac-market-goes-public-at-3-5b-valuation/); [Crunchbase News](https://news.crunchbase.com/business/tiny-home-builder-boxabl-spac-merger/) (via search summary)
- Anecdotes: a detached ADU at $165,000 all-in with about 8 months of construction; one East Palo Alto owner faced a $50,000+ fee condition — [California Insider](https://californiainsider.com/california-news/business/real-estate/inside-the-backyard-housing-boom-in-california-5900675)

**Components**
- Elevators would be 13-44% cheaper if North America allowed European cabin sizes; the union contract bans much preassembly and prefabrication — [Center for Building, "Elevators"](https://admin.centerforbuilding.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/12/Elevatorsexecutivesummary.pdf)
- Bathroom pods are usually cost-neutral in average-cost markets and cheaper only in high-cost ones; the gain is schedule and defects — [Hotel Management](https://www.hotelmanagement.net/design/when-hotels-should-consider-modular-bathrooms) (via search summary). Marriott's project lead said modular should be "at least cost neutral," with savings from opening sooner — [Smart Meetings, 2015](https://www.smartmeetings.com/news/69858/hotels-embrace-modular-construction-for-new-projects)

### Inferences
- Arithmetic on NAHB 2024: site work plus foundations are 18.1% of construction cost (11.7% of price). The factory-addressable scope (framing, exterior, rough-ins, interior, final steps) is about 82% of construction cost, or about 53% of sale price. With labor at about half, factory-addressable labor is about 26% of sale price.
- So if automation removes all on-site labor in that scope, delivered price falls about 26%; a realistic 50% labor cut gives about 13%. Reaching 50% lower delivered price requires materials and components to get cheaper too (plausible under an "abundant production" premise, since they are manufactured goods), plus lower overhead, sales cost and margin.
- Approximate floor at today's structure: lot 13.7% + site work/foundation 11.7% + overhead 5.7% + profit 11.0% + sales and marketing 3.6% + financing 1.5% = about 47% of price untouched by a cheaper factory box. In coastal metros land is a far bigger share, so the floor is higher.
- Speed matters little for tract single-family (6.1 months, financing is 1.5% of price) and far more for multifamily and hotels (16-19 months), where earlier revenue is the main documented benefit.
- NAHB construction cost implies about $162/sq ft ($428,215 / 2,647), consistent with the $166 figure.

### Gaps
- No sourced split of materials versus labor within factory-built homes, or bill-of-materials for a HUD-code home.
- No sourced land share of home value by metro.
- No sourced all-in installed price for Samara, Cover or other prefab ADUs; Cover's site lists no prices.
- Kitchen/cabinet, window and heat pump installed-cost breakdowns (product versus installation labor) were not researched.

## 3. Evidence of latent demand

### Takeaway
Shortage estimates cluster at 3.7-4.9M homes (range 2.0-5.5M), with 1-3M suppressed households, which is 2-4 years of current production rather than a Model T style 10x expansion. The clearest demand-unlock on record, California ADUs, came from legalization, not from cheaper production.

### Cited Findings
- Published national shortage estimates: Moody's 2.0M; Freddie Mac 3.7M (Q3 2024); Up for Growth 3.78M (2025); Realtor.com 4.03M; Zillow 4.7M; Brookings 4.9M; NAR 5.5M. Outliers on different definitions: NLIHC 7.2M (affordable rentals for extremely low-income renters), Harvard JCHS 0.6M — [Boston Fed, Patel, 2026](https://www.bostonfed.org/-/media/Documents/events/2026/econ-conference/measuringhousingunderproductionwhyestimatesdivergeandwhatanationalnumbercansayelenapatel111326.pdf); [CRS IN12628, Dec 2025](https://www.everycrsreport.com/reports/IN12628.html)
- A White House estimate puts the shortage at "at least 10 million" — [National Mortgage News](https://www.nationalmortgagenews.com/articles/housing-shortage-is-at-least-10-million-homes-white-house-says) (headline only, article not read)
- 2.5-3.0M additional households would have formed by 2024 had they faced 2006 housing prices. Realtor.com counts 1.82M missing young-adult households in 2025; Moody's about 1.2M; Freddie Mac about 1M — [Boston Fed, Patel, 2026](https://www.bostonfed.org/-/media/Documents/events/2026/econ-conference/measuringhousingunderproductionwhyestimatesdivergeandwhatanationalnumbercansayelenapatel111326.pdf); [Freddie Mac, Nov 2024](https://www.freddiemac.com/research/insight/housing-supply-still-undersupplied)
- Zillow (2023 data): deficit 4.7M; 8.1M families doubled up; 1.8M new families formed versus 1.4M homes added. Largest metro deficits: New York 402k, Los Angeles 339k, Boston 151k, San Francisco 140k, Washington 132k — [Zillow, Jul 2025](https://zillow.mediaroom.com/2025-07-09-US-housing-deficit-grew-to-4-7-million-despite-construction-surge)
- Counter-evidence: there is no universal agreement that an overall national shortage exists, as opposed to shortages in particular places, types and price points — [CRS IN12628](https://www.everycrsreport.com/reports/IN12628.html)
- Housing demand is "income and price inelastic" — [Albouy, Ehrlich and Liu, NBER w22816](https://www.nber.org/papers/w22816) (abstract only; numeric elasticity not retrieved)
- California must build 2.5M homes by 2030 per HCD; the state has 358 housing units per 1,000 people versus 419 nationally — [Terner Center, March 2026](https://committees.assembly.ca.gov/media/81)
- ADU demand after legalization: California permits went up about 154-fold from 2016 to 2022 and reached 30,231 in 2024. 51% of new ADUs are income rentals, 15% house seniors, 16% are rented to acquaintances at low or no cost — [California YIMBY](https://cayimby.org/reports/california-adu-reform-a-retrospective/); [California Insider](https://californiainsider.com/california-news/business/real-estate/inside-the-backyard-housing-boom-in-california-5900675)
- Counter-evidence on cheap product: manufactured homes already cost 35-50% less yet shipments are 102,737 versus 580,000 in 1973. Potter attributes the collapses mainly to lending cycles (repossessions of 80,000-100,000 a year in the late 1990s, recoveries as low as 25%), not to the HUD code — [Construction Physics](https://www.construction-physics.com/p/the-rise-and-fall-of-the-manufactured)
- Counter-evidence on pass-through: a New Hampshire modular project manager notes savings may not reach buyers because local market price sets the sale price — [Construction Physics, Sweden (reader comment)](https://www.construction-physics.com/p/should-us-homebuilders-emulate-sweden) (anecdote)
- Elevators show latent demand suppressed by cost: new US walk-ups reach four to six stories, and the US has 40% fewer elevators per capita than the Netherlands — [Center for Building, "Elevators"](https://admin.centerforbuilding.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/12/Elevatorsexecutivesummary.pdf)

### Inferences
- The one-time backlog (about 4-5M homes plus up to 3M suppressed households) equals about 3-5 years of today's output. If cleared over a decade it adds roughly 0.4-0.8M units a year, a 30-60% lift, not a 10x market.
- With inelastic demand, a 30% lower delivered price likely raises housing consumed by well under 30%; 50% and 70% cuts are outside the range any study covers. No sourced estimate exists for those scenarios, so any report figure should be labeled as an assumption.
- Where demand is constrained by permission (ADUs, small infill, elevators in mid-rise), legal change produced 10x-100x growth. Those are the segments that look most like the Model T pattern, and cheaper product compounds a legal unlock that has already happened.
- Larger upside probably sits in replacement and upgrade of the 148M-unit stock (ADUs on existing lots, added floors, retrofits, heat pumps) rather than in net new households.

### Gaps
- No sourced numeric price elasticity of housing demand or of household formation.
- No study found that models market expansion at 30%/50%/70% lower delivered cost.
- No sourced data on global latent demand (India, China, Africa).
- No survey data on how many homeowners would buy an ADU at a given price point.

## 4. Regulatory and timeline gates

### Takeaway
The biggest federal unlock in 50 years has passed (chassis rule removed in July 2026), but it will not bite until HUD writes standards and states update statutes by July 2027, and local zoning still controls placement. For modular and ADUs, fragmented local code enforcement, duplicate inspections, fees and financing rules, not factory speed, set the timeline.

### Cited Findings
- The 21st Century ROAD to Housing Act was signed 11 July 2026. It changes the federal definition of a manufactured home to "with or without a permanent chassis"; HUD must write standards and labels for chassis-less homes; states must certify by 11 July 2027 that they treat both types the same for titling, financing, insurance, taxation, transport and installation. Pew's October 2026 review found nearly all states still need to amend statutes — [Pew, Oct 2026](https://www.pew.org/en/research-and-analysis/articles/2026/10/29/a-new-road-for-factory-built-homes-states-must-act-in-coming-year) (via search summary; page blocked); [APA](https://planning.org/blog/9335459/what-the-road-to-housing-means-for-tiny-homes/); [HousingWire](https://www.housingwire.com/articles/new-policy-impact-may-ignite-a-manufactured-housing-blue-sky-era/)
- The Act also raised FHA loan limits for manufactured housing, effective on enactment. Zoning and siting rules remain local — [Bay News 9](https://baynews9.com/fl/tampa/news/2026/09/12/new-federal-housing-law-could-reshape-manufactured-homes-in-florida)
- HUD's September 2024 final rule allows up to four units per manufactured structure and multi-story designs; estimated net benefit up to $334M a year — [Niskanen Center](https://www.niskanencenter.org/two-big-developments-in-manufactured-housing-reform-new-rules-new-bill/)
- Manufactured home zoning eased historically (by-right placement rose from under 1% of communities in 1970 to 52% in 1985), yet many zoning rules persist "because of biases against the old mobile homes" — [Construction Physics](https://www.construction-physics.com/p/the-rise-and-fall-of-the-manufactured); [HousingWire](https://www.housingwire.com/articles/new-policy-impact-may-ignite-a-manufactured-housing-blue-sky-era/)
- Financing (CFPB, 2019 HMDA data): 42% of manufactured home purchase loans are chattel loans. Median rate 8.6% chattel versus 4.9% MH mortgage versus 4.1% site-built; chattel median term 23 years. Only 27% of manufactured home loan applications ended in a loan versus 74% for site-built; 50% of chattel applications were denied versus 7% of site-built. Chattel loans are generally not purchasable by Fannie Mae or Freddie Mac — [CFPB, May 2021](https://files.consumerfinance.gov/f/documents/cfpb_manufactured-housing-finance-new-insights-hmda_report_2021-05.pdf)
- A Florida manufacturer says manufactured home rates run one to three points higher and that permit and inspection costs weigh more on a lower-priced product — [Bay News 9](https://baynews9.com/fl/tampa/news/2026/09/12/new-federal-housing-law-could-reshape-manufactured-homes-in-florida)
- California modular: 540 local jurisdictions enforce codes; factory-built units pass state in-factory inspection and then local plan review plus site, foundation and utility inspections, and local inspectors sometimes re-inspect factory-approved work. Manufacturers will not build on spec without cross-jurisdiction approval certainty. California convened an Assembly Select Committee on Housing Construction Innovation in December 2025 — [Terner Center, March 2026](https://committees.assembly.ca.gov/media/81)
- Sweden runs its whole industry on a performance-based code, which interviewees cite as an enabler — [Terner Center, March 2026](https://committees.assembly.ca.gov/media/81)
- California ADU law: 60-day approval clock with deemed approval; no impact fees on ADUs of 750 sq ft or less; no owner-occupancy requirement; 800 sq ft units must be allowed; local agencies must run pre-approved plan programs (AB 1332) — [HCD ADU Handbook](https://www.hcd.ca.gov/sites/default/files/docs/policy-and-research/ADU-Handbook-Update.pdf)
- California also passed SB 9 (2021, up to four dwellings on most single-family lots) and SB 1211 (2024, up to eight detached ADUs on multifamily lots). Counter-evidence: upzoning alone yields little (Minneapolis added 23 small multifamily units in two years; Portland's stock rose about 0.6% from 2021-2024), and San Diego voted to cap units on single-family lots — [California Insider](https://californiainsider.com/california-news/business/real-estate/inside-the-backyard-housing-boom-in-california-5900675)
- Developers report regulatory delay of roughly 6 months during lot development (95.9% report delays) — [NAHB special study, 2021](https://www.nahb.org/-/media/NAHB/news-and-economics/docs/housing-economics-plus/special-studies/2021/special-study-government-regulation-in-the-price-of-a-new-home-may-2021.pdf)
- Elevators: larger mandated cabins, a separate North American code, and labor rules explain the 3x price gap; code harmonization is the proposed fix — [Center for Building, "Elevators"](https://admin.centerforbuilding.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/12/Elevatorsexecutivesummary.pdf)
- 3D printing, doors, windows, wiring and HVAC still need conventional trades and inspections; ICON has shown little progress there — [Construction Physics on ICON](https://www.construction-physics.com/p/what-progress-has-icon-made-on-3d)

### Inferences
- Regulation dominates the timeline in three places: pre-permit entitlement (months to years, not in Census data), local re-inspection and code variance for modular, and titling/financing for HUD-code homes. Factory speed matters only after those gates.
- The July 2027 state certification deadline plus pending HUD standards make 2027-2028 the earliest realistic window for chassis-less, multi-story HUD-code product in infill settings. That is a timing window a new entrant could design for.
- HUD code is the only US regime with national preemption of local building codes. A product that qualifies under it avoids the 540-jurisdiction problem; a modular product does not.

### Gaps
- Status of HUD's chassis-less construction standards (proposed or final) could not be confirmed.
- Single-stair reform status by state and counts of pre-approved plan programs were not retrieved (the tracker page returned 404).
- No sourced entitlement timelines by city (San Francisco, Los Angeles, Texas metros) or impact fee per unit by jurisdiction.
- Other ROAD Act provisions (NEPA, pattern books, zoning incentives) and vote counts were not verified.
- No data on appraisal treatment or insurance pricing for factory-built homes.

## 5. Objections and actor responses

### Takeaway
Every actor around the product has a documented reason to slow a cheaper factory home: lenders price it as personal property, trades unions lose jurisdiction, builders will not bet on unproven suppliers, local governments zone it out, and incumbents profit from the financing bundle. Incumbents treat the 2026 reform as a product-innovation opening, not a price war.

### Cited Findings
- Building trades: the San Francisco Building and Construction Trades Council opposed modular unless built in the city with union workers. Its president said "The quality is crap" and "We're going to fight vigorously with the city not to do any more of these." Factory OS used carpenters' union workers doing multi-trade work, which other trades objected to — [CalMatters, 2021](https://calmatters.org/commentary/2021/03/modular-housing-california-san-francisco-cost-unions/); [NorCal APA summary of SF Chronicle, 2020](https://norcalapa.org/2020/12/san-francisco-trade-unions-at-odds-over-modular-construction/)
- Counter-example: the carpenters' union embraced factory housing, and the trades later negotiated a San Francisco factory deal with FullStack Modular — [Modular Building Institute, 2019](https://www.modular.org/2019/05/29/carpenters-union-embraces-factory-built-housing-to-address-labor-needs-in-northern-california) (via search summary); [NorCal APA](https://norcalapa.org/2020/12/san-francisco-trade-unions-at-odds-over-modular-construction/)
- Elevator union (IUEC) contract bans preassembly and prefabrication of many parts, sometimes requiring on-site disassembly and reassembly — [Center for Building, "Elevators"](https://admin.centerforbuilding.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/12/Elevatorsexecutivesummary.pdf)
- Lenders, investors and insurers see a different risk profile: who pays if the factory fails mid-project, limited bonding capacity, and deposits required before site work. Stakeholders ask for state bonding and deposit financing — [Terner Center, March 2026](https://committees.assembly.ca.gov/media/81)
- Chattel lending is concentrated: the top five lenders make nearly 75% of chattel loans; 21st Mortgage and Vanderbilt, both Clayton subsidiaries, make 56% of chattel lending — [CFPB, May 2021](https://files.consumerfinance.gov/f/documents/cfpb_manufactured-housing-finance-new-insights-hmda_report_2021-05.pdf)
- Consumer reputation: a 2015 Seattle Times/Center for Public Integrity investigation alleged predatory sales and rates above 15% at Clayton; the company denied it — [Wikipedia: Clayton Homes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clayton_Homes) (secondary source)
- Builders: "Builders can't afford to take a major chance on unproven and unprofitable suppliers"; a KB Home executive said Veev's fatal flaw was acting as a supplier, not a competitor that buys land and builds communities. Large builders subcontract nearly everything and compete on land, capital and purchasing — [HousingWire on Veev](https://www.housingwire.com/articles/veev-falters-lessons-learned-for-homebuilding-innovation/); [Construction Physics, economies of scale](https://www.construction-physics.com/p/why-are-there-so-few-economies-of)
- Incumbent manufacturers: Cavco's CEO frames chassis removal as "an innovation-driven thing" for urban and suburban product, not cost-cutting, and says Cavco's modular plants could build chassis-free HUD homes. MHI: "the industry is ready." Clayton already sells CrossMod — [HousingWire, July 2026](https://www.housingwire.com/articles/new-policy-impact-may-ignite-a-manufactured-housing-blue-sky-era/)
- Site-builder and prefab incumbents are converging: Clayton has bought site builders since 2015; Sekisui House bought MDC for about $4.95B; Lennar backed Veev and ICON and bought Veev's assets — [Wikipedia: Clayton Homes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clayton_Homes); [Wikipedia: Sekisui House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sekisui_House); [HousingWire on Veev](https://www.housingwire.com/articles/veev-falters-lessons-learned-for-homebuilding-innovation/)
- Local governments and neighbors: wealthier areas push back on ADUs, critics cite infrastructure strain, and a community group is suing over a 136-unit ADU project in San Diego — [California Insider](https://californiainsider.com/california-news/business/real-estate/inside-the-backyard-housing-boom-in-california-5900675)
- Prefab buyers abroad pay for certainty, not price: a UK prefab CEO cited "certainty of cost, of programme and of quality"; Swedish builders sell quality and energy performance — [Construction Physics, "Elusive Cost Savings"](https://www.construction-physics.com/p/the-elusive-cost-savings-of-the-prefabricated)

### Inferences
- Incumbent HUD-code makers earn 22-26% gross margins with short backlogs and declining shipments. They have capacity and distribution to respond to a cheaper entrant, and their captive lenders control the credit that most buyers need. An entrant needs its own financing answer.
- The least-contested channels are ones with no incumbent trade or GC in the loop: backyard ADUs sold direct to homeowners, disaster and FEMA housing, and build-to-rent communities where the entrant is also the developer.
- Union opposition is strongest in big-city multifamily, the same segment where modular savings are best documented.

### Gaps
- No sourced data on resale value or appreciation of factory-built versus site-built homes, or on consumer stigma surveys.
- No direct statements found from Lennar, D.R. Horton or Clayton on response to much cheaper factory product.
- No sourced insurer pricing data for modular or manufactured homes.

## 6. Startup and incumbent track record

### Takeaway
Roughly $2.8B of venture capital in Katerra (over $2B), Veev (about $600M) and Blu Homes ($217M) alone was lost, mostly to scaling factories before product-market fit and to selling into builders who would not commit volume. What has worked is boring: HUD-code incumbents with captive retail and finance, vertically integrated developer-manufacturers (Lindbäcks, Sekisui), and small regional factories with their own pipeline.

### Cited Findings
**Failures**
- Katerra (2015-2021): raised over $2B including $865M from SoftBank; valuation cut from $4B to $400M in early 2021; 8,000+ employees; about $100M CLT plant sized at roughly 10% of global CLT supply. An insider's diagnosis: it "scaled up massively prior to having a product people wanted to buy," with repeated pivots, unintegrated acquisitions and three-plus years to a first garden-apartment prototype — [Construction Physics, "Another Day in Katerradise"](https://www.construction-physics.com/p/another-day-in-katerradise)
- Veev: raised about $600M ($400M in March 2022); shut down in November 2023 after a financing round was cancelled; Lennar bought the assets in 2024 for "several million dollars" — [HousingWire](https://www.housingwire.com/articles/veev-falters-lessons-learned-for-homebuilding-innovation/); [OneBuild Substack](https://onebuild.substack.com/p/the-prefab-and-modular-graveyard) (the latter is a less rigorous source)
- Entekra: LP invested $45M and took majority control in 2021; about $100M revenue in 2021; Modesto plant cost about $25M and was built for 3,000 house frames a year it never reached; closed in 2023 with 200+ layoffs. Its envelope-only scope covered a fraction of the roughly 25 trades in a build — [HousingWire on Entekra](https://www.housingwire.com/articles/why-entekra-tanked-is-relevant-but-why-it-matters-is-crucial/); [OneBuild Substack](https://onebuild.substack.com/p/the-prefab-and-modular-graveyard)
- Blu Homes raised $217M; Legal & General's UK modular unit lost £236M — [Construction Physics, "Elusive Cost Savings"](https://www.construction-physics.com/p/the-elusive-cost-savings-of-the-prefabricated)
- Mighty Buildings (3D-printed prefab, Oakland): put up for sale in early 2025 after restructuring and layoffs; a claims notice for an assignment for the benefit of creditors exists. Diamond Age liquidated assets in the same period — [3DPrint.com](https://3dprint.com/315768/house-3d-printing-company-mighty-buildings-up-for-sale/amp/); [MB (ABC) LLC notice](https://media.hometeamsonline.com/photos/htosports/ROCKCREEKFA/MB_(ABC)_LLC_Notice.pdf) (notice via search summary, undated)
- Common patterns cited: plants need roughly 70-80% utilization to make money while demand is cyclical; automation added before designs stabilized; each new region needs another factory; venture timelines clash with 5-10 year adoption — [OneBuild Substack](https://onebuild.substack.com/p/the-prefab-and-modular-graveyard); [HousingWire on Veev](https://www.housingwire.com/articles/veev-falters-lessons-learned-for-homebuilding-innovation/)

**Current players**
- Boxabl: went public by SPAC on 17 July 2026 at a $3.5B deemed valuation with about $14M left in trust after redemptions. First nine months of 2026: revenue about $2.6M, net loss $35.9M, 36 Casitas delivered to 13 customers; Q3 orders 209 Casitas worth $16.6M. A 2025 filing carried a going-concern warning — [The Real Deal](https://therealdeal.com/national/2026/07/21/boxabl-ignites-spac-market-goes-public-at-3-5b-valuation/); [Barchart](https://www.barchart.com/story/news/3341835/boxabl-inc-nasdaq-bxbl-completes-business-combination-with-fg-merger-ii-corp-at-3-5-billion-valuation); [Webull](https://www.webull.com/news/15939367184429056) (all via search summary)
- ICON: raised more than $400M; 100+ homes with printed walls; cut 114 staff (about 25%) in January 2025; opened its Titan printer to outside builders in March 2026 on lease, with sale planned for early 2027 at about $899,000 a unit. Wolf Ranch homes listed at about $476,000-$560,000 — [Construction Physics on ICON](https://www.construction-physics.com/p/what-progress-has-icon-made-on-3d); [KVUE](https://www.kvue.com/article/money/business/icon-technology-3d-printing-layoffs/269-fb1f3186-f693-4c42-9a40-8e85b9920f54); [The Real Deal](https://therealdeal.com/texas/2026/03/12/icon-opens-3d-home-printing-tech-to-outside-builders/); [SORA](https://soracasas.com/3d-printed-homes/) (last three via search summary; SORA is low reliability). A reported $200M Series D could not be confirmed from a reliable source — [KSPost](https://www.kspost.biz/en-us/articles/3067)
- Reframe Systems: robotic microfactories, acts as architect of record and general contractor, active in New England and Southern California wildfire rebuilds; claims a three-story home built 50% faster; reported $40M raise in September 2026 — [Reframe Systems](https://www.reframe.systems); [Bricks & Bytes funding roundup](https://bricks-bytes.com/funding-ma/latest-construction-technology-funding-rounds-7th-sep-2026/) (funding via search summary)
- Cover (Los Angeles): steel-structure prefab homes, designed and made in LA; no prices, volumes or funding disclosed on its site — [Cover](https://buildcover.com)
- Samara: six Backyard ADU models from $152k plus installation, California only — [Samara](https://www.samara.com/backyard)
- Fading West: 1,000+ homes since 2016 from one 110,000 sq ft plant, serving its own communities plus municipalities, Habitat and FEMA — [Fading West](https://fadingwest.com)
- Harbinger (factoryos.com redirects here): 4,000 homes delivered; claims 50% time and 25%+ production cost savings — [Harbinger](https://harbinger.homes/)

**What worked**
- Lindbäcks (Sweden): about 800 modules a year, about a quarter of Sweden's wood-frame multifamily market; acts as its own GC; 30% of output is its own development; keeps a 9-12 month confirmed pipeline; targets 80% standardized, 20% custom; turns down over-customized jobs; spends 3% of revenue on R&D — [Terner Center on Lindbäcks](https://ternercenter.berkeley.edu/research-and-policy/scaling-off-site-production-in-the-united-states-lessons-learned-from-swedish-leader-lindbacks/) (2017-era figures)
- HUD-code incumbents: Champion 26.4% gross margin and $206.9M net income; Cavco 22.1% factory-built gross margin and $228.6M operating income, both at record unit volumes in fiscal 2026 while industry shipments were flat — [Champion 8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0000090896/000119312526237809/sky-ex99_1.htm); [Cavco 8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0000278166/000162828026037575/cvco-2026328xex991.htm)
- Japan and Sweden prove factory housing can hold large share, but as a quality and certainty product at equal or higher cost: Toyota Home charged $200-300/sq ft in 2008 versus about $90 US average; Swedish productivity has stagnated since the mid-1990s while prefab share kept rising — [Construction Physics, "Elusive Cost Savings"](https://www.construction-physics.com/p/the-elusive-cost-savings-of-the-prefabricated); [Construction Physics, Sweden](https://www.construction-physics.com/p/should-us-homebuilders-emulate-sweden)

### Inferences
- Japan and Sweden are proof of adoption, not of cost reduction. Using them as evidence for a 3-100x cost story would be wrong; they support a certainty and quality story.
- Boxabl's $3.5B valuation on 36 deliveries in nine months shows public appetite for the narrative and how far delivery lags orders.
- Survivors share three traits: they own demand (their own land, retail lots or development pipeline), they control installation and general contracting, and they sized factories to committed volume.

### Gaps
- BoKlok volumes, prices and UK status were not retrieved.
- Blokable's failure details were not researched.
- Cover and Samara funding, units delivered and factory locations were not found; Factory OS's corporate history versus Harbinger was not confirmed beyond the domain redirect.
- No audited evidence for any startup's claimed cost per sq ft.

## 7. Buildability: capital to first revenue, best wedge, gross margins

### Takeaway
A regional factory can reach revenue on tens of millions of dollars (Entekra's plant cost about $25M) and mature HUD-code makers earn 22-26% gross margins, but margin only appears above roughly 70-80% utilization. The best-supported wedges are direct-to-homeowner ADUs in California, chassis-less HUD-code infill product timed to the July 2027 state deadline, and repeatable components (bath/kitchen pods, mid-rise elevators) where US prices are far above world prices.

### Cited Findings
- Factory capex reference points: Entekra Modesto about $25M; Katerra CLT plant about $100M; Fading West runs on one 110,000 sq ft plant — [HousingWire on Entekra](https://www.housingwire.com/articles/why-entekra-tanked-is-relevant-but-why-it-matters-is-crucial/); [Construction Physics on Katerra](https://www.construction-physics.com/p/another-day-in-katerradise); [Fading West](https://fadingwest.com)
- Burn reference: Boxabl lost $35.9M in nine months of 2026 on $2.6M revenue — [Webull/company release](https://www.webull.com/news/15939367184429056) (via search summary)
- Gross margins observed: Champion 26.4% (FY2026), Cavco factory-built 22.1%; Cavco financial services gross margin 69.4% in Q4. Site builders average 11.0% net profit and 5.7% overhead (NAHB 2024) — [Champion 8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0000090896/000119312526237809/sky-ex99_1.htm); [Cavco 8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0000278166/000162828026037575/cvco-2026328xex991.htm); [Nasdaq Cavco call highlights](https://www.nasdaq.com/articles/cavco-industries-q4-earnings-call-highlights) (via search summary); [NAHB](https://eyeonhousing.org/2025/01/cost-of-constructing-a-home-in-2024/)
- Utilization threshold of roughly 70-80% for profitability — [OneBuild Substack](https://onebuild.substack.com/p/the-prefab-and-modular-graveyard) (less rigorous source)
- Advice from builders: prove the concept by building and selling homes yourself, which needs less seed capital than supplying builders — [HousingWire on Veev](https://www.housingwire.com/articles/veev-falters-lessons-learned-for-homebuilding-innovation/)
- California stakeholders rejected state seed money for new factories until code, bonding and pipeline reforms come first, and asked for demand aggregation — [Terner Center, March 2026](https://committees.assembly.ca.gov/media/81)
- ADU wedge economics: median cost $150k against median rent $2,000/month; 60-day approval; no impact fees at 750 sq ft or less; 30,231 permits a year in California — [Terner Center CCI ADU survey](https://ternercenter.berkeley.edu/blog/cci-adu-survey); [HCD ADU Handbook](https://www.hcd.ca.gov/sites/default/files/docs/policy-and-research/ADU-Handbook-Update.pdf); [California Insider](https://californiainsider.com/california-news/business/real-estate/inside-the-backyard-housing-boom-in-california-5900675)
- Tiny-home price point: ultra-low-cost tiny homes estimated at $25,000-$50,000 excluding land (ULI researcher) — [APA](https://planning.org/blog/9335459/what-the-road-to-housing-means-for-tiny-homes/)
- Elevator wedge: a 3x price gap that the report attributes to code, cabin size and labor rules rather than hardware — [Center for Building, "Elevators"](https://admin.centerforbuilding.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/12/Elevatorsexecutivesummary.pdf)

### Inferences
- Ranked by evidence, within this sector:
  1. Backyard ADUs sold direct in California and copycat states. Legal gate already open, 60-day clock, small site scope, buyer is a homeowner with equity, $150k price against $24k a year of rent. A $60-80k installed unit would roughly halve payback. The risk is that site work and utility hookups, not the box, dominate the installed price (Boxabl's $60k Casita excludes them).
  2. Chassis-less, two-story HUD-code homes and duplex-to-fourplex product for infill and build-to-rent, timed to HUD standards and the July 2027 state deadline. National code preemption, 35-50% cost advantage already proven, incumbents slow and innovation-shy. Requires a financing partner and land or community pipeline.
  3. Volumetric pods and repeatable interiors (bathroom, kitchen, mechanical) for multifamily, hotel and student housing. Buyers accept cost-neutral for schedule gain; a pod at a real discount would be new. Sales cycle is project-based and lumpy.
  4. Mid-rise modular multifamily as developer-manufacturer. Best documented savings (20-25% cost, 30-50% time) but the worst graveyard, strongest union opposition and highest capital need.
  5. Elevators and facades. Large price gaps versus Europe but the gap is regulatory and labor, so a cheaper machine alone does not capture it.
- Whole-house single-family for tract builders looks weakest: 6-month builds, 1.5% financing cost, builders who compete on land, and a 47% non-factory floor.
- A realistic claim for the report is 20-35% lower delivered cost and 50%+ faster for the best wedges; 3x cheaper delivered housing is not supported by any evidence found unless land, site work, regulation and financing change too. A 10-100x claim applies at most to factory labor hours or cycle time for the box itself.

### Gaps
- No sourced capex for a standard HUD-code plant or payback period.
- No sourced unit economics for ADU startups (gross margin, customer acquisition cost, installation cost share).
- No sourced market size for kitchens/cabinets and windows to rank them against pods and elevators.
- Capital raised to first revenue for Cover, Samara and Reframe was not available.
