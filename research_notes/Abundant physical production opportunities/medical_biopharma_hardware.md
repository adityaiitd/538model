## 1. Market size today ($ and units, global and US)

### Takeaway
The verifiable anchor numbers are company-level, not sector-level: Intuitive ($10.06B revenue, 11,106 systems, 3.15M procedures, 2025), Align ($3.2B aligner revenue, 2.6M cases, 2025), Dexcom ($4.66B, 2025), United Imaging (~RMB 13.8B, 2025), CAR-T (~$5.2B, 2025) and Hims ($2.35B, 2025, largely compounded GLP-1 driven). These are large but concentrated markets where a handful of incumbents hold 80-90% share.

### Cited Findings

### Inferences
- Intuitive collects ~$3,190 of total revenue per procedure and ~$906k per installed system per year (my arithmetic: $10.06B / 3.153M and / 11,106). The business is a recurring per-procedure toll, not a capital-equipment sale; a cheaper robot attacks only a minority of that revenue.

## 2. Price vs manufacturing cost gap, and who captures it

### Takeaway
The largest verified price/cost multiples are GLP-1s (estimated cost-based price $0.89-$4.73/month vs $1,000-$1,350 US list, 200-1,000x), basic prosthetic feet (~$45 Jaipur Foot vs $8,000-$12,000 US, ~200x), intraocular lenses (~$2 Aurolab vs $50-100+ imported, 25-50x), and hearing aids (~$150 to make vs ~$2,400 retail per aid, ~15x). CAR-T is different: credible COGS estimates are $35k-$220k against $373k-$594k list, only 2-10x, so manufacturing is a real cost, not just rent. In most categories the gap is captured by clinician channel, IP/exclusivity or reimbursement-anchored pricing, not by the factory.

### Cited Findings
**GLP-1s and insulin (price is patent/exclusivity rent, not manufacturing)**
- Estimated sustainable cost-based price for injectable semaglutide: $0.89-$4.73 per month (incl. device, formulation, margin, tax) vs lowest market prices of $38-$354/month across 12 countries; oral semaglutide $38.62-$72.49/month cost-based; human insulin vials $2.37-$5.98/month; insulin analogue vials $4.86-$10.59/month. Academic estimate from trade-shipment API prices, not company data. — [Barber et al., JAMA Network Open, 27 Mar 2024](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamanetworkopen/fullarticle/2816824)
- A separate study put Wegovy production cost at ~$40/month; Novo declined to disclose production costs. — [Fortune, Mar 2024](https://fortune.com/europe/2024/03/28/ozempic-maker-novo-nordisk-facing-pressure-as-study-finds-1000-appetite-suppressant-can-be-made-for-just-5/)
- US list prices before the Nov 2025 deal: Ozempic ~$1,000, Wegovy ~$1,350, Zepbound ~$1,086 per month. — [AJMC, 5 Feb 2026](https://www.ajmc.com/view/trumprx-launch-brings-savings-and-uncertainty)

**Hearing aids (price is channel + bundled service)**
- Prescription hearing aids averaged $4,744 per pair before the 2022 OTC rule; OTC devices sell for "a few hundred dollars or less". — [American Economic Liberties Project, "Within Earshot", 24 Nov 2025](https://www.economicliberties.us/our-work/within-earshot/)
- Average prescription patient still pays >$2,600 per pair (Hearing Tracker survey); Big Five brand averages range from just over $1,000 to >$4,000. — [Capitol Forum, 5 Jun 2026](https://thecapitolforum.com/four-years-after-otc-hearing-aid-rule-competition-in-hearing-aid-market-remains-limited/)
- A CBC investigation found average manufacturing cost of ~$150 per aid (older figure; page not re-fetched, from search summary). — [CBC](https://amp.cbc.ca/lite/story/1.1367189)
- Average wholesale invoice vs retail per aid: $457 vs $1,785 in 2004; $774 vs $2,284 in 2019 (industry-affiliated blog series; from search summary). — [Hearing Health Matters, Hearing Economics series](https://hearinghealthmatters.org/hearingeconomics/2016/us-hearing-aid-prices-2016-series/)
- Consumer Reports found average retail markup of 117% where wholesale could be verified; many professionals bundle all service costs into the device price. — [AARP](https://www.aarp.org/health/conditions-treatments/info-2016/hearing-aid-costs-prices-cs.html)
- A manufacturing CEO was quoted in 2013: "The hearing aid industry uses every new thing … to raise prices." Low-end wholesale has stayed below $400 per aid. — [Hearing Health Matters, 2013](https://hearinghealthmatters.org/hearing-economics/2013/hearing-aid-pricing-wholesale-level/)
- Vertical integration: four of the Big Five run hearing benefit managers, all five own US retail clinic networks, ~97% of Medicare Advantage plans offer hearing benefits routed through those channels; traditional Medicare covers no hearing aids. — [Capitol Forum](https://thecapitolforum.com/four-years-after-otc-hearing-aid-rule-competition-in-hearing-aid-market-remains-limited/)

**Cell therapy (manufacturing is a genuine cost driver)**
- US list prices of approved autologous CAR-Ts: $462,000-$594,000 (2025). Launch WACs: Yescarta/Tecartus $373,000, Breyanzi $410,300, Abecma $419,500, Carvykti $465,000, Kymriah $475,000, Aucatzyl $525,000. Milliman: mean allowed product cost $616,500. — [BioProcess Online guest column, Dropulić (Caring Cross), 30 Oct 2026](https://www.bioprocessonline.com/doc/closing-the-car-t-access-gap-with-hospital-based-manufacturing-0001); [IntuitionLabs review](https://intuitionlabs.ai/articles/car-t-manufacturing-economics-cogs)
- COGS estimates span an order of magnitude by scope: ~$170,000-$220,000 per batch (GEN/KPMG); $95,780 (2019 Sartorius-cited estimate); €57,000-€63,000 per treatment in a facility-level model; academic decentralized <$68,000 at 18 products/yr; India point-of-care ~$35,000 excl. vector (VELCART $35,107; $47,831 with care costs). COGS is 55-70% of total product cost in one model. — [IntuitionLabs review](https://intuitionlabs.ai/articles/car-t-manufacturing-economics-cogs); [BioProcess Online](https://www.bioprocessonline.com/doc/closing-the-car-t-access-gap-with-hospital-based-manufacturing-0001); [Sartorius](https://www.sartorius.com/en/applications/cell-and-gene-therapy/cell-therapy/cell-therapy-guide/advanced-therapy-perspectives/what-will-it-take-to-realize-the-promise-and-potential-of-immune-cell-therapies-blog)
- Cost structure: >200 labor hours per batch, labor ~71% of batch production cost in one analysis; up to 18 people work one Yescarta batch; viral vector >$16,000 per patient batch, though non-branded anti-CD19 lentiviral vector is now as low as ~$10,000/dose. — [IntuitionLabs review](https://intuitionlabs.ai/articles/car-t-manufacturing-economics-cogs); [BioProcess Online](https://www.bioprocessonline.com/doc/closing-the-car-t-access-gap-with-hospital-based-manufacturing-0001)
- Low-price existence proofs: Spain's hospital-made ARI-0001 reimbursed at ~$101,000 (about one-third of commercial); India's ImmunoACT NexCAR19 at $30,000-$50,000 per dose (2024). — [BioProcess Online](https://www.bioprocessonline.com/doc/closing-the-car-t-access-gap-with-hospital-based-manufacturing-0001); [KPMG CAR-T report (via search summary; PDF unreadable)](https://assets.kpmg.com/content/dam/kpmgsites/ch/pdf/car-t-therapy.pdf.coredownload.inline.pdf)
- Counter-evidence on automation: a facility-level model found full automation cut per-treatment cost only ~9% (€63,000 manual to €57,000 automated); Cellares' "up to 75% lower" is an unverified company claim. — [IntuitionLabs review](https://intuitionlabs.ai/articles/car-t-manufacturing-economics-cogs)

**Prosthetics and eye care (frugal-manufacturing existence proofs)**
- Jaipur Foot: reported at ~$45 (2012), $28 (Time, 2008), ~$30 (2015), $50, and $80 (BMVSS site) vs $8,000-$12,000 for a comparable US prosthesis; BMVSS fits it free with government aid. Comparison is basic vs high-performance devices and sources are dated. — [Japan Times, 2012](https://japantimes.co.jp/opinion/2012/09/26/commentary/world-commentary/transforming-how-india-addresses-its-problems); [IEEE EMBS Pulse](https://www.embs.org/pulse/?p=472); [Wikipedia: Jaipur foot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jaipur_foot)
- Aurolab (Aravind) rigid PMMA intraocular lens ~US$2 (other sources $3-$10) vs $50-$100+ for imported lenses in the early 1990s; Aurolab sells in ~120 countries and makes ~8% of global IOL output. — [Community Eye Health Journal](https://cehjournal.org/?p=1715); [Business Today](https://www.businesstoday.in/magazine/special/innovation-special-aurolab-eye-care/story/195886.html); [Quartz](https://qz.com/india/1086177/with-an-assembly-line-approach-an-indian-hospital-chain-performs-250000-eye-surgeries-a-year)

**Surgical robots, aligners, scans**
- da Vinci system cost cited at ~$2M (2010 source); a 2013 JAMA study found robotic hysterectomy no better on complications than laparoscopy at significantly greater cost. — [Wikipedia: Da Vinci Surgical System](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Da_Vinci_Surgical_System)
- Align's gross margin was 67-70% (2024-25), i.e. manufacturing + treatment planning is ~30% of the ~$1,250 it collects per case. — [Align FY2025 results](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20260203509429/en/Align-Technology-Announces-Fourth-Quarter-and-Fiscal-2025-Financial-Results)
- Whole-body scan retail prices: Prenuvo $2,499 (memberships from $1,199/yr); Neko $499 in the US (was ~$300-$400 in UK/Sweden), non-MRI sensor scan. — [AuntMinnie](https://www.auntminnie.com/industry-news/market-analysis/news/15820463/prenuvo-prices-wholebody-mri-scans-with-other-services); [eWeek](https://www.eweek.com/news/neko-health-us-body-scan/)

### Inferences
- Decomposition by category (my synthesis of the above):
  - GLP-1 / insulin: manufacturing <1-5% of US list price. Gap = patent exclusivity + PBM/rebate system. Cheaper production does nothing for price until exclusivity ends; the binding constraint 2022-24 was physical fill-finish/API capacity, which incumbents have now spent >$50B fixing.
  - Hearing aids: manufacturing ~5-7% of retail, wholesale ~30-35%, clinic channel ~65%. Cheaper production barely moves price; OTC already removed the channel legally and price fell ~10x, yet uptake stayed low.
  - CAR-T: COGS 15-50% of list. This is the one category where a 5-10x manufacturing cost/throughput gain directly relieves a binding supply constraint and could move price (Spain, India prove $30k-$100k is feasible).
  - Aligners: Align keeps ~$1,250 of a patient price several times that; the dentist/orthodontist captures the majority. Manufacturing is ~$375 of COGS per case at 70% GM (my arithmetic). Removing the clinician was tried (SmileDirect) and failed.
  - Surgical robots: price sits in instruments/service per procedure and in hospital procedure reimbursement; capital cost is the smaller part.
  - Basic prostheses, IOLs, glasses, wheelchairs: 25-200x gaps exist but have already been closed by Indian non-profits/social enterprises; the residual barrier is fitting, distribution and financing, not factory cost.
- Rule of thumb: cheaper production lowers price paid where (a) the buyer pays cash, (b) no clinician must fit/prescribe, and (c) no patent blocks entry. Few health categories meet all three.

Must be checked.

## 3. Latent demand and what actually happened when price fell

### Takeaway
Unmet need is enormous on paper (2.5B people need assistive products; hearing-aid output meets <10% of global need; 1B have unaddressed vision impairment; West Africa has ~0.23 MRI units per million vs ~39 in the US). But the record of price cuts unlocking demand is mixed: GLP-1s and cash-pay preventive scans show strong elastic demand, while OTC hearing aids (10x cheaper, 5.7% adoption after three years), portable MRI (10-12 units a quarter) and handheld ultrasound (product revenue +7%) show that non-price barriers (fitting, stigma, clinician workflow, reimbursement, trust) often dominate.

### Cited Findings
**Unmet need (WHO and peer-reviewed)**
- >2.5B people need one or more assistive products, rising to 3.5B by 2050; access is as low as 3% in some low-income countries vs 90% in some high-income countries; 80M people need a wheelchair and only 5-35% have access depending on country; hearing-aid production meets <10% of global demand. — [WHO fact sheet: Assistive technology, 2 Jan 2024](https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/assistive-technology)
- Nearly 1B of the 2.5B are denied access; about two-thirds of users paid out of pocket. — [WHO/UNICEF Global Report on Assistive Technology, 2022](https://www.who.int/news-room/releases/16-05-2022-almost-one-billion-children-and-adults-with-disabilities-and-older-persons-in-need-of-assistive-technology-denied-access--according-to-new-report)
- Hearing: >430M people need rehabilitation for disabling hearing loss (>700M by 2050); ~80% live in low- and middle-income countries; unaddressed hearing loss costs ~US$1 trillion/yr. — [WHO fact sheet: Deafness and hearing loss, updated 3 Mar 2026](https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/deafness-and-hearing-loss)
- Vision: ≥2.2B have vision impairment; ≥1B cases preventable or unaddressed, incl. 826M with unaddressed presbyopia (a reading-glasses problem), 88.4M refractive error, 94M cataract; 2 in 3 people in low-income countries who need glasses lack them; 1 in 2 globally who need cataract surgery lack access; productivity loss $411B/yr; estimated cost to close the gap $25B. — [WHO fact sheet: Blindness and vision impairment, updated 10 Feb 2026](https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/blindness-and-visual-impairment)
- Imaging density: West Africa had 84 MRI units for ~372.6M people in Sept 2016 (~0.23 per million; Nigeria 0.30, Ghana 0.48); 77.6% of Nigeria's units were low-field (<0.3T); ~63% private; all urban. Comparison in the same paper: Japan 51.67, USA 38.96, South Korea 26.47, France 12.59 per million. — [Ogbole et al., Pan African Medical Journal 2018](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6295297/)
- OECD: Japan ~57.4 MRI units per million (2020), lowest OECD member Mexico 2.64; Cambodia, Myanmar, Pakistan, Philippines, Sri Lanka and Bangladesh report <1 per million. OECD notes there is no benchmark for the "ideal" number. — [TheGlobalEconomy (OECD data)](https://www.theglobaleconomy.com/rankings/magnetic_resonance_imaging_units/); [OECD Health at a Glance Asia-Pacific 2022](https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/health-at-a-glance-asia-pacific-2022_c7467f62-en/full-report/medical-technologies_53df0cac.html)
- Orthodontics: ~600M people with treatable malocclusion vs ~22M case starts/yr. — [Align FY2025 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1097149/000109714926000014/algn-20251231.htm)
- CAR-T: only ~2 in 10 eligible US patients receive it (Kite); 5.4% of 3rd-line+ DLBCL Medicare patients got CAR-T (SEER-Medicare); in Europe ~2 in 10 eligible LBCL patients; 60% of wait-listed myeloma patients got a manufacturing slot; a 17-centre survey found a median 25% of patients died waiting; each extra 10 miles from a centre cuts odds of treatment 6.2%. Cellares (citing McKinsey) claims <1% of those who could benefit get access (company-interested figure). — [BioProcess Online, 30 Oct 2026](https://www.bioprocessonline.com/doc/closing-the-car-t-access-gap-with-hospital-based-manufacturing-0001); [IntuitionLabs review](https://intuitionlabs.ai/articles/car-t-manufacturing-economics-cogs)
- Drug shortages: 225 active US shortages in Q3 2026 (227 in Q2, 223 in Q1; peak 323 in Q1 2024); 89 new shortages in 2025, the fewest since 2006; 48% of new 2026 shortages are sole-source products; 10% of new 2026 shortages are CT/MRI contrast agents; ifosfamide in severe shortage after a supplier quality failure. Manufacturer-reported causes (2025): unknown 59%, supply/demand mismatch 16%, manufacturing issues 15%, business decisions 6%, regulatory 2%. — [AJMC, 20 Jul 2026](https://www.ajmc.com/view/active-us-drug-shortages-rise-for-third-straight-quarter); [Becker's: ASHP Q3 report](https://www.beckershospitalreview.com/pharmacy/cancer-drug-shortages-worsen-7-notes-from-ashps-q3-report/); [Becker's: new shortages at 20-year low](https://www.beckershospitalreview.com/pharmacy/new-drug-shortages-hit-20-year-low-ashp-report)

**Natural experiments: price fell, did volume follow?**
- OTC hearing aids (rule final 2022; price from ~$4,744/pair to a few hundred dollars): MarkeTrak 2025 shows 5.7% of people reporting hearing difficulty adopted OTC devices vs 33.5% with traditional devices; 70% of first-time buyers now choose OTC and overall adoption is rising. Uptake "much slower than anticipated"; deregulation called "necessary, but insufficient". Barriers named: no insurance coverage (esp. Medicare), consumer reluctance to buy without a professional, retailer hesitancy, vertical integration. — [Capitol Forum, 5 Jun 2026](https://thecapitolforum.com/four-years-after-otc-hearing-aid-rule-competition-in-hearing-aid-market-remains-limited/); [Thieme, MarkeTrak 2025 analysis](https://www.thieme-connect.com/products/ejournals/html/10.1055/s-0045-1812040); [AELP "Within Earshot"](https://www.economicliberties.us/our-work/within-earshot/)
- OTC exits: GN discontinued Jabra Enhance Plus (2024); Sony discontinued its OTC line (April 2026); Eargo was taken private (Jan 2024) and merged with hearX into LXE Hearing (April 2025). — [Capitol Forum](https://thecapitolforum.com/four-years-after-otc-hearing-aid-rule-competition-in-hearing-aid-market-remains-limited/); [Wikipedia: Eargo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eargo)
- GLP-1 (strong elasticity signal): Nov 2025 White House deal cut cash prices to ~$350/month average for injectables (Zepbound ~$346, from $1,086; Wegovy/Ozempic $350, from $1,350/$1,000), falling to ~$250 within two years; pills from $149/month; Medicare/Medicaid price $245 with $50 Medicare copay; ~10% of Medicare enrollees eligible. Costco already sold Wegovy/Ozempic at $499 cash. TrumpRx launched 5 Feb 2026, cash-pay only, linking to manufacturer sites. — [NBC News, Nov 2025](https://www.nbcnews.com/health/health-news/trump-weight-loss-drugs-cost-wegovy-zepbound-novo-nordisk-eli-lilly-rcna242309); [AJMC, 5 Feb 2026](https://www.ajmc.com/view/trumprx-launch-brings-savings-and-uncertainty)
- GLP-1 compounding as revealed demand at lower price: Hims began compounded semaglutide in May 2024 (reported ~85% below Wegovy) and reached $2.348B total revenue in 2025; Novo ended its Hims partnership in June 2025 over "knockoff" sales; Hims launched then withdrew a compounded oral semaglutide pill within two days in Feb 2026 after Novo threatened and then filed a patent suit. — [Wikipedia: Hims & Hers Health](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hims_%26_Hers_Health)
- IQVIA: direct-to-consumer programmes are contributing to growing GLP-1 utilisation "even if not covered by an insurer"; Zepbound overtook Wegovy in 2025 in most specialties, helped by LillyDirect. — [IQVIA blog, 22 Jan 2026](https://www.iqvia.com/locations/united-states/blogs/2026/01/hcp-adoption-providers-as-the-cornerstone-of-uptake)
- Portable MRI (Hyperfine Swoop, roughly an order of magnitude cheaper than fixed MRI): sold 10 systems in Q1 2026 (revenue $3.9M), 12 in Q2 2026, record Q3 2026 revenue of $6M with 56.7% gross margin; FY2026 guidance $20-22M. ### Inferences
- Pattern across the experiments: demand expands sharply when the product is self-administered or passively received, the benefit is visible and wanted (weight loss, reassurance scan, restored sight via a surgery system that includes the lens), and the buyer can pay cash. It expands weakly when the product needs fitting/behaviour change/stigma tolerance (hearing aids) or must be inserted into a clinician's workflow and billing (portable MRI, handheld ultrasound).
- Aurolab worked because the cheap lens was embedded in a vertically integrated delivery system (Aravind). The transferable lesson is "build the delivery system, not only the factory".
- Hyperfine and Butterfly indicate that a 10x cheaper imaging device does not by itself reach the 0.2-per-million markets: Butterfly's global-health probe sales are shrinking, and the binding constraints there are radiologists, power, service, and payment.
- The WHO presbyopia figure (826M) is the largest single "cheap physical product, no clinician needed" gap in the sector; reading glasses already cost about a dollar to make, so the gap is distribution, not production.

### Gaps

## 4. Regulatory and timeline gates (US focus), incl. 2025-2026 changes

### Takeaway
Device clearance itself is not the slow step for most hardware (510(k) fee $26k, ~128-day average total time under the MDUFA VI draft); the slow steps are reimbursement, clinical adoption, and for biologics the process-is-the-product lock-in plus multi-year plant builds. 2025-26 policy tilts toward new US capacity: QMSR/ISO 13485 harmonisation (Feb 2026), FDA PreCheck (announced Aug 2025, pilot Feb 2026), Advanced Manufacturing Technology designation (Cellares first in cell therapy), and Section 232 tariffs of up to 100% on patented drugs (April 2026) with generics, biosimilars and potentially cell/gene therapies carved out.

### Cited Findings
- FY2026 device user fees: standard 510(k) $26,067 ($6,517 small business); annual establishment registration $11,423. — [FDA MDUFA fees](https://www.fda.gov/medical-device-user-fee-amendments-mdufa)
- Review-time goals: MDUFA V targets a 510(k) decision within 90 FDA days for 95% of submissions. The July 2026 draft MDUFA VI commitment letter (FY2028-2032) sets average total time to decision for 510(k)s at 128 calendar days, falling to 112 by FY2032, and 285 calendar days for PMAs. Draft terms. — [Holland & Knight, 15 Jul 2026](https://www.hklaw.com/en/insights/publications/2026/07/fda-releases-draft-commitments-letter-for-medical-devices-review); [NAMSA](https://namsa.com/resources/blog/mdufa-vi-draft-commitment-letter/)
- QMSR took effect 2 Feb 2026, replacing the Quality System Regulation and incorporating ISO 13485:2016 by reference; FDA retired the QSIT inspection technique the same day. One quality system now serves US and most foreign regulators. — [FDA: QMSR](https://www.fda.gov/medical-devices/postmarket-requirements-devices/quality-management-system-regulation-qmsr)
- OTC hearing aids: OTC Hearing Aid Act 2017, FDA final rule 2022, for mild-to-moderate loss; senators alleged manufacturers lobbied to weaken the rule and later raised "locked" hearing aids with FDA (May 2024). — [Capitol Forum](https://thecapitolforum.com/four-years-after-otc-hearing-aid-rule-competition-in-hearing-aid-market-remains-limited/)
- State professional boards as a gate: orthodontists' association filed complaints against SmileDirectClub with 36 state dental boards. — [Wikipedia: SmileDirectClub](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SmileDirectClub)
- FDA PreCheck: announced 7 Aug 2025 under Executive Order 14293; two phases (Facility Readiness with early FDA engagement on design/construction via a Type V Drug Master File; Application Submission with pre-application CMC feedback); pilot launched early Feb 2026. Context cited: only 11% of API manufacturers for FDA-approved products are in the US; more than half of pharmaceuticals distributed in the US are made overseas. — [Foley Hoag, 8 Aug 2025](https://foleyhoag.com/news-and-insights/publications/alerts-and-updates/2025/august/fda-launches-precheck-program-to-accelerate-onshoring-of-drug-manufacturing-facilities/); [PharmTech, 2 Feb 2026](https://www.pharmtech.com/view/fda-launches-precheck-pilot-program-to-bolster-domestic-drug-manufacturing)
- Section 232 pharma tariffs (proclamation 2 Apr 2026): 100% on patented drugs and associated APIs; default effective 29 Sept 2026 (earlier for listed large companies, ~31 Jul 2026); 20% for firms with Commerce-approved onshoring plans, stepping up to 100% four years after the proclamation; 0% through 20 Jan 2029 for firms with both MFN pricing and onshoring agreements; 15% for EU/Japan/Korea/Switzerland, 10% UK; generics, biosimilars and their ingredients exempt (to be reassessed within a year); cell and gene therapies, plasma products, ADCs, orphan-only drugs eligible for 0% on a Secretary determination; US-origin product exempt. ~$400B of US investment commitments claimed. Medical devices not addressed. — [EY Tax News, 3 Apr 2026](https://taxnews.ey.com/news/2026-0787-us-section-232-proclamation-imposes-up-to-100-percent-tariffs-on-patented-pharmaceuticals-and-active-pharmaceutical-ingredients); [White House fact sheet, Apr 2026](https://www.whitehouse.gov/fact-sheets/2026/04/fact-sheet-president-donald-j-trump-bolsters-national-security-and-strengthens-u-s-supply-chains-by-imposing-tariffs-on-patented-pharmaceutical-products/)
- Lilly and Novo each received three years of tariff relief as part of the Nov 2025 GLP-1 pricing deal. — [NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/health/health-news/trump-weight-loss-drugs-cost-wegovy-zepbound-novo-nordisk-eli-lilly-rcna242309)
- Advanced Manufacturing Technology (AMT) designation: Cellares' Cell Shuttle was the first cell-therapy manufacturing platform to receive it; customers can reference the designated platform in their own filings to speed review. — [PharmTech, 29 Jan 2026](https://www.pharmtech.com/view/cellares-secures-257m-to-scale-global-cell-therapy-manufacturing)
- Plant timelines (observed): Lilly's Lebanon, Indiana API site (to be "the largest API production site in US history") is due to open in 2027; Lilly's US capital commitments since 2020 exceed $50B across ten announced sites; Houston API site ($6.5B) broke ground Sept 2026. — [Lilly press release, 6 May 2026](https://investor.lilly.com/news-releases/news-release-details/lilly-commits-additional-45-billion-across-indiana-manufacturing); [Lilly Houston release](https://lilly.gcs-web.com/news-releases/news-release-details/lilly-breaks-ground-houston-one-ten-us-manufacturing-sites)
- Cell therapy release testing is a gate in itself: compendial sterility culture takes 14 days and mycoplasma culture 28 days vs ~3 hours and <5 hours for rapid methods; every decentralised site must release each product under a unified quality system. — [BioProcess Online, 30 Oct 2026](https://www.bioprocessonline.com/doc/closing-the-car-t-access-gap-with-hospital-based-manufacturing-0001)
- Medicare statute bars coverage of drugs for weight loss alone; the 2025 deal routes obesity coverage through a pilot with unclear legal footing. Traditional Medicare does not cover hearing aids. — [NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/health/health-news/trump-weight-loss-drugs-cost-wegovy-zepbound-novo-nordisk-eli-lilly-rcna242309); [Capitol Forum](https://thecapitolforum.com/four-years-after-otc-hearing-aid-rule-competition-in-hearing-aid-market-remains-limited/)

### Inferences
- For a hardware entrant the FDA fee/timeline is trivial next to commercial adoption time: Hyperfine was cleared in 2020 and still sells ~40-50 units a year; Butterfly was cleared in 2017.
- The tariff design exempts exactly the categories with chronic shortages (generic sterile injectables), so tariffs do not create a price umbrella for a new US generic-injectable plant; shortages there stem from low prices and sole sourcing (48% of new shortages), not from lack of technology.
- AMT designation plus PreCheck plus the possible 0% tariff category for cell/gene therapy make automated cell-therapy manufacturing the area where 2025-26 policy most directly lowers a new manufacturer's regulatory risk.
- Lilly's Lebanon site was announced in 2022 by my recollection, implying ~5 years from announcement to opening for a large API plant; the 2027 opening date is sourced, the 2022 start is not.

### Gaps
- Not sourced this session: cost and duration of 510(k) vs De Novo vs PMA development programmes (the often-quoted ~$31M / ~$94M Stanford/Makower 2010 figures could not be confirmed), EU MDR cost/time, CMS coding/coverage lag and the TCET pathway, BLA comparability rules, FDA platform technology designation, national priority vouchers, typical 3-5 year biologics plant validation timelines, state licensure for dispensing/fitting. Recollection, unverified: new CPT/HCPCS codes and coverage typically lag clearance by 2-5+ years; De Novo commonly takes ~1 year.

## 5. Objections and actor responses

### Takeaway
The recurring failure mode is that the clinician channel, vertically integrated incumbents and reimbursement rules absorb or block the price cut. Incumbents do cut price when forced (Lilly/Novo dropped cash prices ~65-75% under political and compounding pressure; Align's ASP is falling), and they also sue (Novo v. Hims), lobby (hearing aids) or out-build (Lilly's $50B+).

### Cited Findings
- Incumbents cut price and out-invest: Lilly and Novo cut injectable cash prices from $1,000-$1,350 to ~$350 (heading to ~$250) and launched pills at $149; Lilly has committed >$50B to US plants since 2020, plus $3B in China for oral solid dose, and held ~$550M of pre-launch orforglipron inventory; Novo spent ~$6B capex in a year plus $11B for Catalent fill-finish sites. — [AJMC](https://www.ajmc.com/view/trumprx-launch-brings-savings-and-uncertainty); [Lilly release](https://investor.lilly.com/news-releases/news-release-details/lilly-commits-additional-45-billion-across-indiana-manufacturing); [Pharmaceutical Commerce, 12 Mar 2026](https://www.pharmaceuticalcommerce.com/view/lilly-bolsters-glp1-supply-chain-pledging-3-billion-manufacturing-expansion-in-china); [Becker's](https://www.beckershospitalreview.com/glp-1s/5-ozempic-new-study-sparks-calls-for-novo-nordisk-to-cut-prices.html)
- Incumbents litigate: Novo terminated its Hims partnership (June 2025), then sued for patent infringement (9 Feb 2026) within days of Hims' compounded pill; FDA sent Hims a warning letter (9 Sept 2025) over "same active ingredient" claims. — [Wikipedia: Hims & Hers Health](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hims_%26_Hers_Health)
- Incumbents co-opt the cheap segment: four of the Big Five hearing-aid makers launched their own OTC brands; all five own retail clinics; four run hearing benefit managers; "it's really the same hearing aid company with different hearing aid names"; analyst Amlani: the OTC rule "did not create a hearing healthcare access system". — [Capitol Forum](https://thecapitolforum.com/four-years-after-otc-hearing-aid-rule-competition-in-hearing-aid-market-remains-limited/)
- Patients: reluctance to buy a medical device without a licensed professional; retailers wary of that reluctance. — [AELP "Within Earshot"](https://www.economicliberties.us/our-work/within-earshot/)
- Clinicians as gatekeepers: orthodontists' complaints to 36 state boards against DTC aligners; the most-cited SmileDirect lesson is that bypassing provider oversight invites regulatory and legal collapse. — [Wikipedia: SmileDirectClub](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SmileDirectClub); [Orthodontic Products](https://orthodonticproductsonline.com/clinical-tips/aligner-treatment/chris-bentson-on-the-lessons-of-smiledirectclub/)
- Clinical-value objection to cheaper/more devices: robotic hysterectomy showed no outcome advantage over laparoscopy at higher cost (JAMA 2013); OECD says there is no benchmark for ideal scanner density. — [Wikipedia: Da Vinci Surgical System](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Da_Vinci_Surgical_System); [OECD Health at a Glance 2019](https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/health-at-a-glance-2019_4dd50c09-en/full-report/medical-technologies_ded11d43)
- Payers/policy experts on cash-price platforms: most TrumpRx-listed drugs are already insured and cheaper generics often exist, so some patients could pay more; deal terms are not public and may not outlast the administration. — [AJMC](https://www.ajmc.com/view/trumprx-launch-brings-savings-and-uncertainty)
- Payer fraud/liability risk in reimbursed consumer devices: Eargo paid $34.37M to settle DOJ claims over unsupported diagnosis codes billed to the federal employee plan. — [Wikipedia: Eargo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eargo)
- Incumbent defence in robots: Intuitive leases a large share of placements (47% of Q4 2025 placements reported under lease) and sells refurbished Xi systems for cost-sensitive buyers and surgery centres, lowering the capital barrier itself. — [Motley Fool: Intuitive Q4 2025 call transcript](https://www.fool.com/earnings/call-transcripts/2026/01/22/intuitive-surgical-isrg-earnings-transcript)
- China's response is state procurement pressure plus domestic champions: United Imaging gained 6.4 points of domestic MR share in 2025 while the domestic imaging market shrank 21.1% in Q1 2026 under VBP pressure (broker note via aggregator). — [PR Newswire Asia](https://en.prnasia.com/releases/global/united-imaging-healthcare-releases-2025-annual-report-and-q1-2026-results-delivering-strong-growth-with-accelerated-global-expansion-531290.shtml)

### Inferences
- "Will incumbents simply cut price?" Yes where marginal cost is tiny relative to price (GLP-1, hearing aids, aligners, robot instruments): they have 65-95% gross margin headroom and proved willing to use it. A new entrant's cost advantage is not a moat there. In CAR-T the incumbents cannot simply cut price because COGS and capacity are real constraints, which is why BMS pays Cellares rather than competing with it.
- The clinician channel behaves like a cartel-with-licensure: it cannot be removed by a cheaper product (SmileDirect, OTC hearing aids), only supplied (Align) or vertically integrated around (Aravind, Neko's own clinics).
- Hospitals/GPOs: no direct evidence gathered this session; Hyperfine's slow unit sales are indirect evidence that hospital capital committees do not buy on price alone.

### Gaps
- No sourced statements from GPOs, hospital CFOs, CMS or liability insurers; no sourced data on Chinese surgical robots (Toumai, Edge) pricing or FDA status; no data on Siemens/GE price responses to United Imaging. Search budget ran out.
- Radiologist-society objections to whole-body screening (incidental findings, false positives) are well known but were not retrieved with a citation this session.

### Takeaway

## 7. Buildability: capital to first revenue, time to clearance, best wedges, cash-pay vs reimbursed

### Takeaway
On the evidence gathered, the best-supported opportunities for a "radically cheaper/faster production" founder are, in order: (1) automated autologous cell-therapy manufacturing, the only category where production capacity and COGS demonstrably ration access; (2) cash-pay, vertically integrated screening/diagnostic clinics built on cheap sensors; (3) vertically integrated low-cost delivery systems for vision, hearing and prosthetics in India/Africa on the Aravind model; (4) possibly sterile-injectable manufacturing for shortage drugs, which is production-constrained but price-starved. GLP-1 manufacturing, hearing-aid hardware, aligners, portable imaging hardware and surgical robots are poor fits for a production-cost thesis.

### Inferences
Ranked assessment for the report writer (my synthesis; scores are judgement, not data):

| Rank | Opportunity | Is production the binding constraint? | Price/cost gap | Latent demand evidence | Main blocker | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Automated/decentralised autologous cell therapy manufacturing (CAR-T now; autoimmune next) | Yes: slots ration access, labour ~70% of batch cost, 25% die waiting | 2-10x; $35k-$100k shown feasible vs ~$500k list | ~20% (or less) of eligible treated; autoimmune indications would multiply volume | Capital ($0.5B+), BLA process lock-in, incumbent in-house plants improving (99% success), Cellares lead | Strongest fit; wedge is picks-and-shovels for sponsors or hospital point-of-care kits; a late entrant needs a differentiated angle (rapid QC release, in-hospital units, non-viral) |
| 2 | Cash-pay preventive scan / diagnostics clinics on cheap sensors | Partly: cheaper sensing enables the $499 price | n/a (service) | Neko waitlist 350k, 75% rebook; Prenuvo $100M | Clinical-value scepticism, capital for clinics, undisclosed unit economics | Proven demand; avoids payers and clinician channel by owning both |
| 3 | Aravind-style integrated delivery (glasses, cataract, hearing, prosthetics) in India/Africa | No: products already cheap ($2 IOL, $45 foot) | 25-200x already achieved | WHO: 1B vision, 430M hearing, 80M wheelchairs | Distribution, fitting labour, ability to pay; mostly non-profit economics | Huge need, low margin; automation of fitting (AI audiometry/refraction, scan-to-print sockets) is the lever, not factory cost |
| 4 | US sterile injectable / shortage-drug manufacturing | Yes: fragile sole-source plants | Low prices; no gap to harvest | 225 active shortages | Buyers pay commodity prices; tariff exemption removes umbrella | Real problem, weak economics unless guaranteed-offtake contracts exist |
| 5 | GLP-1 peptide/fill-finish capacity | Was (2022-24); no longer | 200-1,000x but patent-protected | Strong elasticity | Lilly/Novo spent $50B+/$17B+, cut prices, sue compounders | Too late for capacity; generic semaglutide outside US after patent expiry is the remaining angle (not researched) |
| 6 | Hearing aids / aligners / OTC devices | No | 10-15x | Large but adoption stuck at 5.7% OTC | Fitting, stigma, channel, incumbents own cheap brands | Production-cost thesis already tested and largely failed |
| 7 | Cheap imaging hardware (portable MRI, handheld ultrasound) and surgical robots | No | Unknown/sub-10x | 0.2 vs 39 MRI per million | Radiologists, workflow, service, reimbursement; Intuitive's leasing | Hardware-only plays have sold slowly for 5-8 years |

- Common thread: in health, "abundant production" pays off only when paired with ownership of the delivery/clinical step or when sold as capacity to the licence holder. A founder optimising factory cost alone will usually find the saving captured upstream (patent holder) or downstream (clinician, benefit manager).
- Cash-pay beats reimbursed for speed: every fast-scaling example in this file (Neko, Prenuvo, Hims, LillyDirect/TrumpRx, Aravind's paying tier) is cash-pay; every stalled one (Hyperfine, Eargo, OTC hearing aids under Medicare) waits on coverage.

### Gaps
- Could not research capital-to-first-revenue or time-to-clearance for typical 510(k) startups, nor for dialysis, CGM, insulin pumps, wheelchairs/elder-care equipment, hospital beds, lab instruments and consumables, home diagnostics, vaccines, biologics/biosimilars, gene therapy vectors. These categories are unranked for lack of evidence, not because they were judged weak. Dialysis (home haemodialysis machines), elder-care/mobility equipment and lab consumables in particular deserve a follow-up pass.
- No sourced evidence on generic semaglutide launches (India/China/Canada/Brazil 2026) and their prices, which is the most direct test of GLP-1 demand at near-production-cost pricing.
- No sourced evidence on autoimmune CAR-T volumes, which determines whether cell-therapy demand is 10x or 100x current capacity.
