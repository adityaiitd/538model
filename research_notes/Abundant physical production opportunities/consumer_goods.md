# High-value consumer physical goods as a target for 10-100x faster/cheaper production (as of 24 Nov 2026)

Research note on method and limits: figures are labeled with their year. "My calc" marks arithmetic I did on cited numbers. Consumer cost-guide sites (Angi, HomeAdvisor, Fixr) and paid market-research blurbs are flagged as low-grade estimates. The shared web-search budget for this session ran out partway through, so several categories in the brief (pianos, home gyms, luxury/Bain, appliances beyond AC, DJI, IKEA, repair/parts, pool unit counts, stairlift unit counts, Tesla $25k car, Rivian "Also") have no fetched source and are listed under Gaps rather than filled from memory.

## 1. Market size today ($ and units, US and global)

### Takeaway
The big-dollar consumer categories are cars (US average new-vehicle price $51,109, Oct 2026) and home remodeling (~$608B/yr US, of which kitchen and bath ~$228B); RVs (~342k units), boats (~157k-215k new units) and eyewear are an order of magnitude smaller, and saunas/cold plunges are tiny. Every discretionary big-ticket category (RVs, boats, kitchens) is flat or shrinking in 2025-2026 on affordability, which is evidence of price-gated demand but also of high cyclicality.

### Cited Findings

**Cars**
- US average new-vehicle transaction price was a record $51,109 in Oct 2026 (up 1.3% from $50,460 in Sept); average new EV price $56,220; incentives 6.8% of ATP; midsize SUV average $50,570. Measured (KBB/Cox). — [Cox Automotive Oct 2026 ATP report](https://www.coxautoinc.com/insights/oct-2026-atp-report/); [The Truth About Cars](https://www.thetruthaboutcars.com/cars/news-blog/new-car-prices-hit-a-record-51-109-in-october-45137059)

- BYD Seagull passed 1 million cumulative sales in just over two years (June 2025); sold abroad as Dolphin Mini (Brazil, Mexico, Colombia, Chile, Philippines) and Dolphin Surf (Europe). — [Electrek, June 2025](https://electrek.co/2025/06/18/byd-seagull-sales-top-1-million-as-10000-ev-goes-global/)

### Inferences
- By dollars, the prize ranks: cars (tens of thousands per household, bought repeatedly) > home renovation ($608B) > kitchens/baths ($228B subset) >> RVs (~$10-20B at wholesale, inferred from Thor being ~$6B of North American sales with a large share) > boats > eyewear > wellness hardware. A founder choosing on market size alone should look at vehicles and installed home products first.
- RVs, boats and kitchens all shrank or stalled in 2025-2026 while prices rose. That fits "demand is price-gated" but it equally fits "these are deferrable, rate-sensitive luxuries"; a cheaper producer enters a cyclical market, not a steadily growing one.
- The used market caps new-unit expansion in durable categories: 80% of boat unit sales are pre-owned.

### Gaps
- No fetched source for US total new light-vehicle units (2025/2026), global car sales, US e-bike unit sales, new pool construction units, stairlift/home-elevator units, piano units, major-appliance units, furniture market size, home-gym market, Bain luxury totals, or US made-to-measure apparel size. Search budget ran out before these could be queried.
- NMMA does not reconcile its 215k and 157k figures publicly; full reports are paywalled ($2,525). NKBA's full report is paywalled ($1,995).
- RV retail dollar market and RV dealer margin not found.

## 2. Price vs production cost and lead time: where does the consumer pay a multiple of factory cost?

### Takeaway
The "consumer pays 3-10x factory cost" pattern is real for diamonds, eyewear, rooftop solar and (by claim) cabinetry, but it is not true for cars or RVs at the manufacturer or dealer level: Thor's gross margin is 12.6% and a US dealer's front-end gross on a new car is about 5%. For cars the gap to China (Seagull at $7.8-10.3k vs US average $51k) comes from product mix, size, regulation, labor and trade barriers, not from channel markup that a new factory could strip out.

### Cited Findings

**Cars**
- BYD Seagull China price: cut to 56,800 yuan (~$7,800) in April 2025; 2026 model launched at 69,900 yuan (~$10,300) with a LiDAR option at ~$13,000. Same car sold in Mexico at ~358,800 pesos (~$19-21k) and Brazil ~99,800 BRL (~$20k), i.e. roughly 2x the China price after tariffs, shipping and local margin. — [Electrek Apr 2025](https://electrek.co/2025/04/08/byds-low-cost-seagull-ev-now-starts-under-8000-china/); [CleanTechnica](https://cleantechnica.com/2025/04/09/byd-announces-price-cut-on-seagull-now-7800/); [Electrek May 2026](https://electrek.co/2026/05/12/byd-launches-seagull-ev-with-lidar-for-13000-first-in-its-class/); [The Nightly/CNBC](https://thenightly.com.au/business/why-a-small-china-made-ev-has-global-auto-execs-and-politicians-on-edge-c-14072786)
- Caresoft teardown (2024): Seagull is "efficiently and simply designed," with unexpected quality; advantage comes from BYD's LFP battery, vertical integration and in-house parts; Caresoft's CEO said BYD makes "some money" or at least breaks even on it. No public bill-of-materials figure. Specs: ~190-250 mile range (China cycle), 74 hp, ~80 mph. — [The Nightly/CNBC](https://thenightly.com.au/business/why-a-small-china-made-ev-has-global-auto-execs-and-politicians-on-edge-c-14072786)
- US dealer margin is thin: AutoNation's 2025 gross profit per new vehicle was $2,564 on average revenue of $52,075 (4.9%, my calc), down 15.8%; parts/service made 48% of dealer gross profit and F&I 30%. Q2 2026 figure cited at $2,764 per unit. — [Dealership Guy / dealer reports](https://news.dealershipguy.com/p/new-car-boom-used-car-squeeze-and-the-future-of-fixed-ops-2025-01-30); [Digital Dealer](https://digitaldealer.com/news/dealerships-still-thriving-even-amid-a-turbulent-year-nada/168193/)
- Kei car reference price: Honda N-One ~$11,390 in Japan before tariffs; the author warns US-built kei cars could be expensive because labor is a large share of a cheap car's cost. — [The Autopian](https://www.theautopian.com/president-trump-said-he-just-legalized-cheaper-smaller-cute-kei-cars-in-america/)
- Slate's cost-out design for a $24,950 truck: unpainted injection-molded composite panels with vinyl wraps, crank windows, no modem, optional speakers, one configuration, direct sales. — [The Next Web, June 2026](https://thenextweb.com/news/slate-auto-ev-pickup-24950-preorders-bezos-production)

**RVs**
- Manufacturer gross margin is low: Thor 12.6% in FY2026, squeezed by "heightened affordability concerns and increasing material costs." Average wholesale value of a North American towable ≈ $33,400 (my calc). — [Thor FY2026 results](https://finviz.com/news/394066/thor-industries-announces-fiscal-2026-fourth-quarter-and-full-year-results)

**Rooftop solar (best-documented decomposition)**
- US $2.53/W vs Australia $0.82/W for a 10 kW system, pre-subsidy (IEA 2023 data). US: hardware $1.28/W + soft $1.25/W. Australia: hardware $0.46/W + soft $0.36/W. EnergySage 2025 US: $2.77/W = $0.94 hardware + $1.83 soft (66% soft). Germany: $1.52-1.69/W. — [Boston University, Local Solar: International Cost Comparisons, July 2025](https://www.bu.edu/eci/files/2025/07/Intnatl-Cost-Comparisons-final.pdf)
- US hardware cost alone ($1.28/W) exceeds Australia's whole installed price. 2023 IEA US panel cost $0.38/W vs US wholesale panel cost $0.12-0.18/W. Marketing/advertising is 20-29% of the US per-watt price; installer profit ~$0.43/W (17%), similar percentage to Australia (18%) but 3x the dollars. LBNL's measured US price is $4.20/W because it includes dealer fees and financing charges, which add 5-50%. — [Boston University](https://www.bu.edu/eci/files/2025/07/Intnatl-Cost-Comparisons-final.pdf)
- Lead time: in Australia the installer quotes remotely, the homeowner can order within a week, work starts nearly immediately and a simple install takes one day; interconnection is blanket pre-approved under ~10 kW. — [Boston University](https://www.bu.edu/eci/files/2025/07/Intnatl-Cost-Comparisons-final.pdf)
- German plug-in "balcony solar" (600-800 W, no contractor) costs as little as €200 (~$230). — [Boston University](https://www.bu.edu/eci/files/2025/07/Intnatl-Cost-Comparisons-final.pdf)

**Diamonds**
- Golan's lab-grown wholesale index is down 96% since 2018; -26% in 2025, -14% YoY in Q1 2026, -13% in Q2 2026; a 3-carat VVS D round was $126 per carat wholesale in Q1 2026. Retailers still mark up more than 80% over wholesale and the markup moved only 1-2 points between 2025 and 2026 (Zimnisky). — [JCK, 10 Aug 2026](https://www.jckonline.com/editorial-article/lab-grown-wholesale-freefall/)
- At retail, a 1-carat lab-grown stone is ~$1,000 or less vs ~$4,200 natural (2025); lab-grown was 26.6% cheaper per carat in 2019 and 72.8% cheaper in 2025. — [Stacker/BriteCo](https://stacker.com/stories/shopping/lab-grown-diamonds-now-cost-73-less-natural-diamonds-what-means-buyers-2026)

**Eyewear**

- Zenni sells complete prescription glasses from single-digit dollars out of its own Chinese factory. — [Wikipedia: Zenni Optical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zenni_Optical)

**Kitchens / cabinets (low-grade sources)**
- Custom cabinets for a full kitchen: $25,000-$60,000+; custom adds 50-100% over stock; cabinet installation alone averages ~$6,400 (range ~$2,000-$11,000), 1-3 days at $40-80/hour. — [Angi](https://www.angi.com/articles/custom-cabinets-cost.htm); [HomeAdvisor](https://www.homeadvisor.com/cost/cabinets-and-countertops/install-cabinets/); [Inch Calculator](https://www.inchcalculator.com/new-cabinets-cost-guide/)

**Pools (low-grade sources)**

**Stairlifts / home elevators (low-grade sources, conflicting)**

**Apparel**
- Shein launches styles in batches of ~100 units (Zara ~500), can produce ~3 days after spotting a trend, and added 2,000-10,000 styles per day in H2 2021. — [Wikipedia: Shein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shein)
- Unspun's Vega 3D-weaving machine makes a garment tube in ~10-20 minutes with ~3% material waste vs ~14% conventional, but per-unit cost is still above mass manufacturing. — [Sacra](https://sacra.com/c/unspun/)

### Inferences
- Rank of "price far above product cost," by evidence quality: (1) rooftop solar: 3x Australia, with hardware only half the US price and panels ~5-7% of it (my calc: $0.12-0.18 of $2.53); (2) lab-grown diamonds: retail still >1.8x wholesale after a 96% wholesale collapse; (3) eyewear: 60-65% gross margins while a $10-40 alternative exists; (4) custom cabinetry: plausible 2-3x through stacked channel layers plus 8-16 week lead time, but only seller-sourced evidence; (5) pools, elevators, stairlifts: high installed prices where on-site work dominates.
- Cars and RVs fail the test. Dealer front-end ≈5% and RV-maker gross ≈13% mean there is no fat channel margin to remove. A 2-5x cheaper US car needs a different product (smaller, simpler, LFP, fewer variants) and a different cost base, which is what BYD already has and what Slate is trying to copy.
- The 2x gap between Seagull's China price and its Mexico/Brazil price shows that even without a US-style ban, logistics, tariffs and local distribution roughly double the price of the cheapest car.
- In solar the consumer overpay is mostly customer acquisition, financing fees, permitting and labor. A cheaper factory does nothing there; a cheaper sales and permitting process does.

### Gaps
- No bill-of-materials for any Chinese EV; no decomposition (materials/labor/logistics/channel/brand) found for furniture, boats, pools, stairlifts, pianos, watches or luxury goods.
- No independent data on cabinet factory cost vs delivered price, or on made-to-order furniture lead times (the brief's 8-16 weeks is supported only for cabinets).
- Boat manufacturer and dealer margins (Brunswick, Malibu), Winnebago results and RV dealer margins were not retrieved.
- Home battery and heat-pump US-vs-abroad cost comparisons not retrieved.

## 3. Latent demand and price elasticity

### Takeaway
Measured elasticity for new cars is low (about -0.3 to -0.5 market-wide, -0.84 for the lowest income group), so a cheaper car mostly redistributes share unless it reaches people who now buy used or nothing. The clean natural experiments say price collapses do unlock volume (rooftop solar in Australia, lab-grown diamonds, e-bikes, TVs, Chinese cars in Mexico/Brazil), but in status categories buyers trade up rather than spend more (diamond carat size rose 87% while ring spend fell), and the Tata Nano shows "cheapest" can itself kill demand.

### Cited Findings

**Measured elasticities (cars)**
- US new-vehicle own-price elasticity: average -0.5; from -0.2 (highest income group) to -0.84 (lowest), household-survey based. — [Leard & Wu, RFF WP 23-33 (2023)](https://www.rff.org/documents/4120/WP_23-33.pdf)
- Long-run market-level elasticity of US new-vehicle demand: -0.34 (accounts for substitution to used). — [Leard, RFF](https://www.rff.org/publications/working-papers/estimating-consumer-substitution-between-new-and-used-passenger-vehicles/)
- Norway, 1.8M transactions 2002-2016: own-price elasticity of gasoline cars -1.08. — [European Transport Research Review](https://link.springer.com/10.1186/s12544-020-00454-2)

**Natural experiments: price fell, what happened to volume**
- Rooftop solar, Australia: installed price fell from ~A$2.4/W (2013) to ~A$0.90/W (2021-2025) and quarterly installations roughly tripled from ~30-40k (2016) to ~90-110k (2020-21), then held at somewhat lower levels. Penetration: 33% of Australian homes vs 7% of US homes (BU), or "1 in 3 vs 1 in 10" (Latitude). — [Boston University](https://www.bu.edu/eci/files/2025/07/Intnatl-Cost-Comparisons-final.pdf); [Latitude Media, Oct 2025](https://www.latitudemedia.com/news/can-the-us-bring-solar-installation-to-below-2-per-watt/)
- Permit Power projection (advocacy estimate): getting US rooftop solar to ~$1/W would add 18.2M solar households and ~200 GW by 2040. — [Latitude Media](https://www.latitudemedia.com/news/can-the-us-bring-solar-installation-to-below-2-per-watt/)
- Lab-grown diamonds: wholesale -96% since 2018; engagement-ring share 6.3% → 47.7% (2019-2025); average lab-grown center stone 1.31 ct → 2.45 ct (+87%); colorless share 37.7% → 85.9%; but average engagement-ring spend fell from ~$6,000 (2021) to ~$5,200 (2024). Tennis bracelets rose from 3.5% to 11.9% of insured purchases (a new use case). — [JCK](https://www.jckonline.com/editorial-article/lab-grown-wholesale-freefall/); [Stacker/BriteCo](https://stacker.com/stories/shopping/lab-grown-diamonds-now-cost-73-less-natural-diamonds-what-means-buyers-2026)
- Counter-signal in diamonds: US jewelry unit sales were down ~10% in March 2026 while dollars rose 5% on higher prices (gold); lab-grown inventory-to-sales ratio at US jewelers rose from high single digits (2020) to nearly 50% (Tenoris), i.e. oversupply. De Beers' realized rough price fell 32% YoY to $105/ct in H1 2026 (secondary report). — [JCK, Apr 2026](https://www.jckonline.com/editorial-article/rising-demand-for-lab-grown-diamonds-collides-with-falling-value/); [Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/commodities/articles/price-diamonds-crashes-lowest-level-181700046.html)
- Televisions: US CPI for TVs fell 94% from Dec 1997 to Aug 2015 (quality-adjusted); PCs -96%; audio equipment -60%. — [BLS](https://www.bls.gov/opub/ted/2015/long-term-price-trends-for-computers-tvs-and-related-items.htm)
- E-bikes: China sales 56,000 (1998) → 21M+ (2008); fleet 380M (2025). EU 200k (2007) → 5M+ (2021). Netherlands: e-bikes outsold regular bikes by 2020 and were 57% of sales in 2022 even at ~3x the price of a regular bike (2010 figure). — [Wikipedia: Electric bicycle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electric_bicycle)
- Cheap Chinese cars in emerging markets: Chinese brands' share of Mexico's new light-vehicle market went from ~7% four years ago to 14% (H1 2025) to 17% (H1 2026; 137,525 units, +28%) despite a 50% tariff from 1 Jan 2026; counting all China-built vehicles the share is ~22-28%. BYD Brazil Q1 2026: 37,637 units (+73.7%), fifth-largest brand; Chinese brands hold >80% of Brazil's EV segment. AlixPartners expects Chinese share in Central/South America to reach 28% by 2030 (forecast). — [Rio Times](https://www.riotimesonline.com/chinese-cars-in-mexico-hit-17-of-sales-despite-a-50-tariff); [Rio Times Brazil](https://www.riotimesonline.com/brazil-auto-sales-march-2026-byd-chinese-brands/); [IBTimes](https://www.ibtimes.com/how-chinese-carmakers-are-taking-latin-america-storm-3801549)
- Kei cars: 40% of Japan's new-car sales at the 2013 peak, over one-third in FY2016 after a 50% kei tax rise in 2014; 7 of the top 10 models in 2018. Demand rests on tax, insurance and parking-certificate exemptions as much as price. "Generally too small and specialized to be profitable outside Japan"; the Smart K was the worst-selling kei car. — [Wikipedia: Kei car](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kei_car)
- US demand signal for a cheap vehicle: Slate took 100,000 $50 refundable reservations in about two weeks and 160,000-180,000 before the final price was known. — [InsideEVs](https://insideevs.com/news/759373/slate-100000-reservations-deposit-weeks/); [The Drive](https://www.thedrive.com/news/180000-people-reserved-slates-electric-truck-before-they-knew-it-would-cost-24950)

**Where cheap did not sell**
- Tata Nano: launched at ₹100,000 (~$2,000-2,500) with ~200,000 pre-orders and 250,000/yr capacity; sales peaked at 74,527 (FY2011-12), fell to 7,591 (FY2016-17), ended May 2018. Causes cited: "cheapest car"/"poor man's car" stigma, fires, zero-star Global NCAP result (no airbags/ABS), factory relocation delays, weak rural dealer reach, narrowing price gap to the Maruti Alto, still expensive relative to a motorcycle. — [Wikipedia: Tata Nano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tata_Nano); [The Wire](https://thewire.in/business/tata-nano-why-did-the-people-not-want-the-peoples-car); [Quartz](https://qz.com/india/1326635/tata-nano-the-slow-death-of-the-worlds-cheapest-car)
- Prior-generation Chevrolet Bolt, the cheapest US EV, sold only 8,400-23,900 units a year in the US (2017-2024). — [Wikipedia: Chevrolet Bolt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chevrolet_Bolt)
- Ford Bronco data: only ~10% of buyers chose two doors, a caution for minimalist formats like Slate's. — [The Next Web](https://thenextweb.com/news/slate-auto-ev-pickup-24950-preorders-bezos-production)
- An auto writer doubts broad US demand for kei cars: much smaller than a Nissan Versa, poor on highways, better for city and commercial use; small cars have been dropped by most US automakers. — [The Autopian](https://www.theautopian.com/president-trump-said-he-just-legalized-cheaper-smaller-cute-kei-cars-in-america/)

**Saturation**
- Air conditioning: 88% of US households (2020 RECS), 93% in homes built 2010-2020. — [EIA](https://www.eia.gov/todayinenergy/detail.php?id=52558)
- Vehicles: US ~800 per 1,000 people vs India 38, Indonesia 100, China 333 (see section 1). — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_and_territories_by_motor_vehicles_per_capita)

### Inferences
- With a constant elasticity of -0.34 to -0.5, halving US new-car prices raises units only ~27-41% (my calc); at -0.84 (lowest income group) ~79%. A Model T-style multiplication needs the product to pull in used-car buyers and non-owners, which the market-level estimates say is limited in a country already at ~800 vehicles per 1,000. The large latent car demand is in India, Indonesia, Africa and similar markets, and Chinese makers are already serving it.
- The strongest US "unlock" evidence is in categories with low penetration and a foreign price benchmark: rooftop solar (7-10% vs 33%) and e-bikes (Netherlands majority share). Both are available cheaply today from China; the US constraint is soft costs and tariffs.
- Lab-grown diamonds are the cautionary case for founders: a ~96% cost collapse converted into bigger stones and lower spend per ring, producers got commoditized, and the surviving margin sat with retailers (80%+ markup). In positional goods, abundance destroys the scarcity that carried the price. Expect the same for watches, luxury goods and probably pianos.
- Three reasons cheap fails, from Nano: (a) the product signals low status, (b) it compromises safety or quality visibly, (c) the next-cheapest alternative is only slightly dearer. Slate's "customizable, not cheap" positioning and Shein's "trendy, not cheap" positioning both avoid (a).
- Saturated categories (AC, and by extension core appliances) offer replacement demand only; cheaper production there lowers revenue per household rather than expanding units.

### Gaps
- No measured price elasticities found for RVs, boats, furniture, kitchens, pools, appliances, eyewear or apparel.
- No fetched data for DJI drones, IKEA, air-conditioner adoption curves abroad, or appliance penetration other than AC.
- The Knot's 2026 claim that more than half of engagement rings bought in 2025 had lab-grown stones appeared in search summaries but I could not tie it to a verified page.
- US e-bike unit trend (and the post-tariff drop) is not quantified beyond all-bicycle import data.

## 4. Installation and service as the real bottleneck

### Takeaway
For home-installed products the factory is a minority of the delivered price. In US rooftop solar, soft costs are 49-66% of price, marketing alone is 20-29%, and panels are roughly 5-7%; Australia delivers the same hardware installed in a day for a third of the price. Kitchens, pools, elevators and stairlifts show the same shape in weaker data, and JCHS names labor shortage as the remodeling industry's main constraint.

### Cited Findings
- US residential solar: soft costs $1.25/W of $2.53/W (49%, IEA 2023) or $1.83/W of $2.77/W (66%, EnergySage 2025). The three largest soft costs (marketing, profit, installation labor) are 43% of installed cost. Marketing/advertising 20-29% of price. Dealer fees and financing add 5-50% (LBNL). — [Boston University](https://www.bu.edu/eci/files/2025/07/Intnatl-Cost-Comparisons-final.pdf)
- Australia: soft costs $0.36/W; installers have "minimal customer acquisition costs" because demand is high and steady; permitting and commissioning handled by installer software; automatic interconnection approval under ~10 kW; install labor about one-third less per kW; one-day installs at a fixed fee. — [Boston University](https://www.bu.edu/eci/files/2025/07/Intnatl-Cost-Comparisons-final.pdf)
- Headline: US ~$3/W vs Australia under $1/W (Oct 2025). Tesla's stated path to $3.02/W for solar-plus-storage without subsidies: $0.54/W from regulatory reform, $0.57/W from installer operations, $0.67/W from hardware changes; it says solar alone would then be under $2/W. Tesla's backup switch is approved by only 40% of utilities. Instant permitting saves only ~$0.04/W in labor by itself. — [Latitude Media](https://www.latitudemedia.com/news/can-the-us-bring-solar-installation-to-below-2-per-watt/)
- Older cross-country comparison (2018): soft costs ~65% of system cost in the US vs ~25% in Australia and ~15% in Germany (do not mix with 2025 price levels). — [EnergySage](https://www.energysage.com/solar/why-is-solar-more-expensive-in-the-us)
- Remodeling: JCHS headline is that the industry "struggles to address labor shortages"; the $600B+ market is constrained by trades capacity. — [JCHS press release](https://www.jchs.harvard.edu/press-releases/remodeling-soars-new-heights-industry-struggles-address-labor-shortages-and-urgent)
- Stairlift: labor ~$2,000 on a $1,000-$3,000 unit (Fixr estimate), so installation is 40-65% of delivered price (my calc). — [Fixr](https://fixr.com/costs/stairlift-installation)
- Cabinets: installation averages ~$6,400 on top of cabinets (cost-guide estimate). — [HomeAdvisor](https://www.homeadvisor.com/cost/cabinets-and-countertops/install-cabinets/)
- Dealer economics show where service money sits in vehicles: parts and service are 48% of dealer gross profit, F&I 30%, new-vehicle sales the small remainder (AutoNation 2025). — [Digital Dealer](https://digitaldealer.com/news/dealerships-still-thriving-even-amid-a-turbulent-year-nada/168193/)

### Inferences
- On a 10 kW US system at $2.53/W ($25,300), marketing at 20-29% is about $5,000-$7,300 of customer acquisition per household (my calc). That number, not the module price, is what a new entrant must beat.
- A robot or factory that makes the product 10x cheaper moves delivered price by single-digit percent in solar and probably 10-30% in kitchens, baths and pools. The 3x lever is a vertically integrated "productized install": standard SKUs, remote quoting, instant permitting, one-day crews, no dealer fee. Australia proves it is achievable with ordinary labor.
- Products that remove the installer entirely are the cleanest wedge: plug-in balcony solar at ~$230, freestanding or plug-in appliances, flat-pack or snap-in cabinetry, through-floor elevators that avoid a shaft (Stiltz is priced at less than half a conventional home elevator).
- If on-site robotics is part of the "abundant production" thesis, home installation labor is where it would pay; that belongs to the housing/construction researcher's scope but it determines this sector's ceiling.

### Gaps
- No sourced on-site-labor share for kitchens, bathrooms, pools or heat pumps. NREL benchmark tables and heat-pump and battery comparisons were not retrieved before the search budget ran out.
- No sourced customer acquisition cost for remodelers, pool builders or stairlift dealers.

## 5. Regulatory and timeline gates

### Takeaway
For vehicles the gate is regulatory and trade policy, not production: FMVSS crash rules exclude kei-class cars, the 25% chicken tax and a connected-vehicle rule (software from model year 2027) exclude Chinese-made or Chinese-linked cars even if built in the US, and Trump's December 2025 "tiny cars" approval had produced no final rule as of the sources found. For home products the gate is local permitting and utility interconnection. For diamonds, eyewear, apparel and furniture, regulation is not the binding constraint.

### Cited Findings

**Microcars and kei cars**
- 3-5 Dec 2025: Trump said he had "approved TINY CARS to be built in America" and authorized DOT to approve production; Secretary Duffy said NHTSA was "clearing the deck." In March 2026 Duffy was still "working with NHTSA"; which FMVSS change is unclear. No final rule found in results through mid-2026. — [Axios](https://www.axios.com/2025/12/05/trump-japan-micro-cars); [The Autopian](https://www.theautopian.com/president-trump-said-he-just-legalized-cheaper-smaller-cute-kei-cars-in-america/); [The Drive](https://www.thedrive.com/news/why-trumps-call-for-legalizing-kei-cars-in-the-us-is-fake-news.md)
- Legal position: new vehicles must certify to all FMVSS (49 USC 30112, 49 CFR 567), so new highway-use kei vehicles are "de facto banned." Part 555 temporary exemptions are typically capped at 2,500 vehicles per year, time-limited and slow. NHTSA could write a kei-specific rule modeled on the low-speed-vehicle carve-out; rulemaking "can take years." Imports over 25 years old are exempt. Several states (Georgia, Maine, New York, Rhode Island, Pennsylvania) restrict registration. — [Covington, Dec 2025](https://cov.com/en/news-and-insights/insights/2025/12/kei-trucks-and-us-regulation-opportunities-and-obstacles-ahead); [Axios](https://www.axios.com/2025/12/05/trump-japan-micro-cars)
- Specific obstacles: unbelted-occupant crash testing, US-specific lighting, and CAFE footprint rules that set tougher targets for small cars. Stellantis's CEO publicly welcomed the announcement. — [The Autopian](https://www.theautopian.com/president-trump-said-he-just-legalized-cheaper-smaller-cute-kei-cars-in-america/)
- Low-speed vehicles: four wheels, top speed 20-25 mph, GVWR under 3,000 lb, limited equipment list (belts, lights, mirrors), allowed in nearly all states on roads posted 35 mph or less. — [Wikipedia: Low-speed vehicle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Low-speed_vehicle)

**Trade barriers**
- Chicken tax: 25% tariff on imported light trucks since 1964, still in force; exempt if built in Mexico/Canada under USMCA; it removed small Japanese and VW pickups from the US market. — [Wikipedia: Chicken tax](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chicken_tax)
- Connected-vehicle rule (Commerce/BIS, final 14 Jan 2025): bans Chinese/Russian-linked vehicle connectivity software from model year 2027 and hardware from model year 2030; also bars sale of connected vehicles by manufacturers with a sufficient PRC nexus even if built in the US, from MY2027. — [BIS](https://www.bis.gov/press-release/commerce-finalizes-rule-secure-connected-vehicle-supply-chains-foreign-adversary-threats); [Arnold & Porter](https://www.arnoldporter.com/en/perspectives/advisories/2025/02/commerce-finalizes-connected-vehicle-prohibitions)
- As of March 2024 Chinese-built EVs faced a 27.5% US tariff; USMCA assembly in Mexico was the feared route. — [The Nightly/CNBC](https://thenightly.com.au/business/why-a-small-china-made-ev-has-global-auto-execs-and-politicians-on-edge-c-14072786)
- Other countries are also gating: Mexico's 50% tariff on cars from non-FTA countries took effect 1 Jan 2026; Brazil's EV import tariff rises to 35% by July 2026 (BYD localized at the former Ford plant in Camaçari); Canada allows a quota of 49,000 Chinese-made EVs a year, with BYD planning 20 dealerships from 2027. — [Rio Times](https://www.riotimesonline.com/chinese-cars-in-mexico-hit-17-of-sales-despite-a-50-tariff); [IBTimes](https://www.ibtimes.com/how-chinese-carmakers-are-taking-latin-america-storm-3801549); [Wikipedia: BYD Auto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BYD_Auto)
- E-bikes and bikes: added China tariffs from Feb 2025; in Aug 2025 e-bikes were added to the 50% steel tariff list (Riese & Müller paused US shipments), later reported dropped for bikes; PeopleForBikes warned recovery may take until 2030; Aventon moved production to Thailand. Over 90% of US e-bikes are made in China or contain Chinese parts. — [Electrek](https://electrek.co/2025/08/26/major-e-bike-maker-hits-pause-on-us-imports-after-new-tariffs/); [Washington Examiner](https://www.washingtonexaminer.com/?p=4518818); [road.cc](https://road.cc/content/news/trump-tariffs-led-bike-sales-free-fall-315789); [Electrek, Feb 2025](https://electrek.co/2025/02/01/will-the-new-trump-tariffs-on-china-increase-electric-bike-prices/)
- Apparel: the US ended de minimis duty-free entry for China/Hong Kong parcels in April 2025; Shein raised US prices and shipping slowed. — [Wikipedia: Shein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shein)

**Certification and timeline for a new US vehicle**
- Slate (as of June 2026) still needed federal validation/certification before first deliveries; status undisclosed. Telo's first deliveries are "pending completion of crash testing and regulatory approval." — [The Next Web](https://thenextweb.com/news/slate-auto-ev-pickup-24950-preorders-bezos-production); [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/09/23/telo-raises-20-million-to-build-tiny-electric-trucks-for-cities)

**Home installs**
- Solar: permitting, inspection and interconnection reform is worth ~$0.54/W (Permit Power/Tesla estimate); equipment approval varies utility by utility. Australia uses national product and installer accreditation plus automatic interconnection. — [Latitude Media](https://www.latitudemedia.com/news/can-the-us-bring-solar-installation-to-below-2-per-watt/); [Boston University](https://www.bu.edu/eci/files/2025/07/Intnatl-Cost-Comparisons-final.pdf)

### Inferences
- Vehicles: the gate is regulatory and political. China already produces at the "abundant" cost. A US startup cannot import that cost base (tariffs, chicken tax), cannot use Chinese connected software or, from MY2030, hardware, and cannot sell a kei-class car without an FMVSS change that has been promised but not delivered. The 2,500-unit exemption cap makes the exemption route useless for a volume business.
- The LSV class is the one legal microcar lane today, but 25 mph and 35-mph roads confine it to campuses, resorts, retirement communities and dense neighborhoods.
- A kei/microcar rule, if finalized, would be the single biggest regulatory opening in this sector; a founder should treat it as an option with unknown expiry, not a base case.
- Tariffs cut both ways for a US builder: they protect a domestic low-cost vehicle or e-bike from Chinese finished goods but raise the cost of the Chinese components that make low cost possible.
- Diamonds, eyewear, apparel, furniture: no meaningful regulatory gate; outcomes there are set by brand, channel and working capital.

### Gaps
- Current (late 2026) status of the NHTSA microcar/kei rulemaking, the Section 301 tariff level on Chinese EVs (widely reported as raised to 100% in 2024; not verified this session), Section 232 auto tariffs, and any change to the connected-vehicle rule were not retrievable after the search budget ran out.
- Dealer franchise law, CPSC/UL certification timelines, furniture flammability rules, boat (ABYC/USCG) and RV (RVIA/NFPA 1192) standards, and pool/elevator permitting were not researched with sources.

## 6. Objections and actor responses

### Takeaway
The strongest objection is that China is already at abundant-production cost in cars, e-bikes, solar hardware, eyewear, apparel and synthetic diamonds, so in the US the constraint is trade policy and distribution rather than production capability. The second is that consumers often want status and trust over a low price (Nano, eyewear, diamonds). The third is that incumbents and channels have absorbed price collapses without passing them through (jewelers kept an 80%+ markup).

### Cited Findings

**Chinese manufacturers are already there**
- BYD sells a highway-capable EV at $7,800-$10,300 at home and makes at least break-even on it per Caresoft; BYD said in Feb 2024 it was "not planning to come to the US," citing trade barriers; any Mexico plant would target the local market. — [CleanTechnica](https://cleantechnica.com/2025/04/09/byd-announces-price-cut-on-seagull-now-7800/); [The Nightly/CNBC](https://thenightly.com.au/business/why-a-small-china-made-ev-has-global-auto-execs-and-politicians-on-edge-c-14072786); [Wikipedia: BYD Auto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BYD_Auto)
- Caresoft's Terry Woychowski (ex-GM) called the Seagull a "clarion call for the rest of the auto industry." — [The Nightly/CNBC](https://thenightly.com.au/business/why-a-small-china-made-ev-has-global-auto-execs-and-politicians-on-edge-c-14072786)
- China makes over 60% of the world's synthetic diamonds; Shanghai Diamond Exchange lab-grown exports rose 65.3% YoY in H1 2026 (secondary report). — [Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/commodities/articles/price-diamonds-crashes-lowest-level-181700046.html)
- Australia's low solar hardware cost comes from bulk-buying directly from China with no tariffs. — [Boston University](https://www.bu.edu/eci/files/2025/07/Intnatl-Cost-Comparisons-final.pdf)
- Shein already runs ~100-unit batches with ~3-day response and $38B revenue; Zenni already runs a ~1,000-person Chinese factory selling direct to US consumers. — [Wikipedia: Shein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shein); [Wikipedia: Zenni Optical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zenni_Optical)
- Even with a 50% tariff, Chinese brands grew to 17% of Mexico's market; but early entrants Motornation, MG and Chirey saw 2025 sales fall 19-28%, so not every Chinese brand wins. — [Rio Times](https://www.riotimesonline.com/chinese-cars-in-mexico-hit-17-of-sales-despite-a-50-tariff); [Mexico News Daily](https://mexiconewsdaily.com/business/chinese-cars-sold-in-mexico-2025/)

**Consumers: cheap vs status and trust**
- Nano: buyers avoided being seen in the "cheapest car." — [The Wire](https://thewire.in/business/tata-nano-why-did-the-people-not-want-the-peoples-car)
- Eyewear: despite Zenni's price, EssilorLuxottica's revenue is €28.5B vs Zenni's $390M (2021) and Warby Parker's $872M (2025). — [Euronext/EssilorLuxottica](https://live.euronext.com/en/products/equities/company-news/2026-02-11-essilorluxottica-q4fy-2025-results-revenue-growing-184-q4); [Wikipedia: Zenni Optical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zenni_Optical); [Wikipedia: Warby Parker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warby_Parker)
- Diamonds: about two-thirds of Gen Z engagement-ring buyers chose lab-grown; natural's share of engagement rings ticked back up from 52.6% (end 2024) to 57.3% (early 2025) in BriteCo's data, suggesting some status rebound as lab-grown became visibly cheap. — [Stacker/BriteCo](https://stacker.com/stories/shopping/lab-grown-diamonds-now-cost-73-less-natural-diamonds-what-means-buyers-2026)
- Reservations are weak evidence: Slate's were $50 refundable until converted to $300 non-refundable preorders in June 2026; "not every reservation holder ultimately becomes a customer." — [CarPro](https://www.carpro.com/blog/preorders-open-for-the-slate-pickup); [The Next Web](https://thenextweb.com/news/slate-auto-ev-pickup-24950-preorders-bezos-production)

**Incumbents and channels**
- Jewelers: retail markup over lab-grown wholesale stayed above 80% and moved 1-2 points as wholesale fell 26% in 2025; JCK's headline warns retail margins "may be next." A dealer cites a client who paid $25,000 for a 5-carat lab-grown ring in the early 2020s, now worth a small fraction. — [JCK](https://www.jckonline.com/editorial-article/lab-grown-wholesale-freefall/)
- Automakers: GM relaunched the Bolt at under $30,000 with a CATL LFP battery (production from Jan 2026 at Fairfax, Kansas), but per Wikipedia production is announced to end in mid-2027 after ~18 months. — [Wikipedia: Chevrolet Bolt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chevrolet_Bolt)
- RV makers: Thor restructured its North American operating model "to protect attainable price points" as margins fell to 12.6%. — [Thor FY2026 results](https://finviz.com/news/394066/thor-industries-announces-fiscal-2026-fourth-quarter-and-full-year-results)
- Dealers: with only ~5% front-end margin, dealers live on service and F&I, so a direct-sales entrant threatens their service base more than their sales margin. — [Digital Dealer](https://digitaldealer.com/news/dealerships-still-thriving-even-amid-a-turbulent-year-nada/168193/)

**Regulators and trade policy**
- US policy has moved toward exclusion (connected-vehicle rule, tariffs) while Mexico and Brazil raised tariffs and Canada opened a 49,000-unit quota. — [BIS](https://www.bis.gov/press-release/commerce-finalizes-rule-secure-connected-vehicle-supply-chains-foreign-adversary-threats); [Wikipedia: BYD Auto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BYD_Auto)

**Installers and trades**
- US and Australian installer profit percentages are about the same (17-18%); the difference is volume and process, so installers are not obviously over-earning. — [Boston University](https://www.bu.edu/eci/files/2025/07/Intnatl-Cost-Comparisons-final.pdf)

### Inferences
- "If China already makes it at one-fifth the price, the constraint is trade policy and distribution." This holds for: small EVs, e-bikes, solar modules/inverters/batteries, eyeglasses, fast fashion, synthetic diamonds, and very likely appliances and furniture components. A US "abundant factory" in these categories is competing with an existing abundant factory behind a tariff wall, and its margin is the tariff. That is a policy bet with reversal risk in both directions.
- Where the constraint is production or process inside the US and cannot be imported: installed home products (labor, permitting, selling), large bulky made-to-order goods with high freight (cabinets, boats, RVs, pools), and anything requiring US certification plus local service.
- Incumbent response differs by category. Jewelers kept margin. Automakers have responded with one-off cheap models that they then discontinue (Bolt), which suggests low commitment and leaves room for an entrant, but also suggests the segment's profit pool is thin.
- Slate's two-seat, no-screen format and kei cars both face the "Americans won't buy small/basic" objection; only non-refundable deposits and deliveries will settle it.

### Gaps
- No sourced responses from Xiaomi, Midea/Haier, DJI or appliance incumbents; no data on installer/trade-union or dealer-association lobbying against direct sales in 2025-2026.
- No survey data found on US consumer willingness to buy Chinese-brand cars.

## 7. Startups and incumbents attempting this, and results

### Takeaway
Vehicle startups need $1B+ to reach first revenue (Slate) and the under-capitalized ones stall or die (Aptera, Arcimoto); the made-to-order furniture cohort mostly ended in distress sales (Interior Define, Burrow, Floyd); custom-fit apparel start-ups stayed small or pivoted to B2B (Unspun). The winners at "radically cheaper" in this sector are vertically integrated operators with Chinese production and direct distribution (BYD, Zenni, Shein), plus Warby Parker as a break-even mid-price brand.

### Cited Findings

**Vehicles**
- Slate Auto: two-seat EV pickup at $24,950 (SUV kit +$5,000; expected ~60% of sales); the original "under $20,000" assumed the $7,500 federal credit that Congress eliminated. ~$1.5B raised over three rounds including a $650M Series C (April 2026, TWG Global); Bezos family office invested. Warsaw, Indiana plant (~$400M), target 150,000/yr, hand-building ~3 trucks/day as of June 2026; deliveries targeted Q4 2026; 160-180k reservations converted to $300 non-refundable preorders from 24 June 2026. Company claims gross-margin-positive from the first vehicle, break-even at ~80,000/yr, FCF-positive in 2027 (unproven). — [The Next Web](https://thenextweb.com/news/slate-auto-ev-pickup-24950-preorders-bezos-production); [CarGurus](https://www.cargurus.com/Cars/articles/2027-slate-truck-pricing-specs-release-date)
- Telo Trucks: MT1 mini pickup from ~$41,000 (not cheap), 350-mile range, ~12,000 preorders; $20M Series A (Sept 2025; ~$26.8M total); ~25 employees; contract manufacturing of ~5,000/yr via Schwab Industries (Michigan); first delivery targeted end-2026 pending crash tests. — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/09/23/telo-raises-20-million-to-build-tiny-electric-trucks-for-cities); [WardsAuto](https://www.wardsauto.com/news/michigans-schwab-industries-signs-on-to-help-build-tiny-telo-electric-truc/824301/)
- Aptera: ~$140M raised by April 2025, ~$130M of it crowdfunded; Nasdaq listing (SEV) Oct 2025 with a $75M equity line; ended 2025 with ~$10M cash and a ~$44M net loss; price $40,000; 57 employees (2026); low-scale production no earlier than March 2027 pending another $50M; deliveries originally promised for 2021. — [Wikipedia: Aptera Motors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aptera_Motors)
- Arcimoto: three-wheel EV; market cap peaked ~$1.2B (Feb 2021); factory idled Jan 2023; $232k cash vs $9.5M past-due bills in Q3 2023; delisted. Cause given: "failure in scaling production to reduce unit price." — [Wikipedia: Arcimoto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arcimoto)

### Inferences
- Pattern across failures: the product was not much cheaper (Telo $41k, Aptera $40k, Arcimoto ~$20k+ for a three-wheeler), scale never arrived, and capital ran out. Slate is the only US attempt that pairs a real price cut (about half the average transaction price) with enough capital; its first deliveries are the key data point for this whole thesis and were still pending in the latest sources.
- Made-to-order furniture died of working capital and freight, not lack of demand: customers pay up front, production is offshore, lead times are long, and one cash squeeze strands paid orders. Local automated production with days-not-weeks lead time would attack exactly that failure mode, but no sourced example of it succeeding was found.
- Custom-fit apparel has not found consumer pull at a premium price; the companies that survive sell tooling to brands. Shein shows the mass market rewards variety and speed at low price, not individual fit.
- The durable winners own production and the customer relationship and skip the channel: BYD, Zenni, Shein, and (if it works) Slate.

## 8. Buildability: capital to first revenue, wedge, gross margins, customer acquisition

### Takeaway
Capital needs run from ~$1.5B (a new car company) down to tens of millions (contract-built niche vehicles, DTC eyewear, productized home installs). The most buildable wedges in this sector are the ones where the product is already cheap and the price gap is process: installed home energy and renovation products sold with near-zero acquisition cost and one-day installs, and locally made-to-order bulky goods with short lead times. Highway vehicles are the biggest prize but the hardest and most policy-exposed.

### Cited Findings
- Car company: Slate has raised ~$1.5B before its first customer delivery, spent ~$400M on a 150,000/yr plant, and says break-even is ~80,000 vehicles/yr. — [The Next Web](https://thenextweb.com/news/slate-auto-ev-pickup-24950-preorders-bezos-production)
- Asset-light vehicle: Telo plans ~5,000/yr by contract manufacturer with ~25 staff on ~$27M raised, but at a $41,000 price. — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/09/23/telo-raises-20-million-to-build-tiny-electric-trucks-for-cities)
- Crowdfunded vehicle: Aptera's ~$140M+ was not enough to start production. — [Wikipedia: Aptera Motors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aptera_Motors)
- Regulatory small-volume lane: Part 555 exemptions cap at 2,500 vehicles/yr; LSVs avoid most FMVSS. — [Covington](https://cov.com/en/news-and-insights/insights/2025/12/kei-trucks-and-us-regulation-opportunities-and-obstacles-ahead); [Wikipedia: Low-speed vehicle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Low-speed_vehicle)
- Gross margins by layer: RV manufacturer 12.6% (Thor FY2026); car dealer new-vehicle front-end ~5% (AutoNation 2025); solar installer ~17-18%; eyewear ~61-65%; lab-grown diamond retail markup >80% over wholesale. — [Thor](https://finviz.com/news/394066/thor-industries-announces-fiscal-2026-fourth-quarter-and-full-year-results); [Digital Dealer](https://digitaldealer.com/news/dealerships-still-thriving-even-amid-a-turbulent-year-nada/168193/); [Boston University](https://www.bu.edu/eci/files/2025/07/Intnatl-Cost-Comparisons-final.pdf); [Fortune](https://fortune.com/europe/2025/06/04/essilorluxottica-ray-ban-oakley-med-tech-vision); [JCK](https://www.jckonline.com/editorial-article/lab-grown-wholesale-freefall/)
- Customer acquisition: 20-29% of US residential solar price is marketing; Australian installers have "minimal" acquisition cost at high volume. — [Boston University](https://www.bu.edu/eci/files/2025/07/Intnatl-Cost-Comparisons-final.pdf)
- Scale of a mid-price DTC brand after 15 years: Warby Parker at $872M revenue, 323 stores, about break-even. — [Wikipedia: Warby Parker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warby_Parker)
- Machine-seller model: Unspun estimates 30-50% gross margin on hardware (Sacra's estimate, not reported results). — [Sacra](https://sacra.com/c/unspun/)
- Working-capital risk: Interior Define collapsed with customer-paid orders it could not ship. — [Business of Home](https://businessofhome.com/articles/interior-define-bankruptcy-questions-answered)

### Inferences (a sector ranking for the report writer; judgment, not measured)
1. **Productized home energy install (solar + battery, later heat pumps).** Evidence: 3x price gap to Australia with identical hardware, 7-10% vs 33% penetration, acquisition cost of roughly $5-7k per home to attack, a one-day install benchmark, Tesla and Permit Power publishing the cost-down path. Capital: tens of millions. Gate: local permitting and utility approval, tariffs on hardware, loss of federal credit. The innovation is process and software, with automation in install as upside. Plug-in solar is the no-installer variant.
2. **Affordable simple vehicle for the US (Slate-style) or a microcar if the NHTSA rule lands.** Largest dollar prize; demand signal of 160-180k reservations; price half the US average. But capital ~$1.5B, elasticity -0.3 to -0.5 limits market expansion, margins in the segment are thin, and the moat is a tariff wall against BYD. Best treated as "watch Slate's first year" before copying.
3. **Short-lead-time made-to-order cabinets/kitchens and fitted furniture from local automated plants.** Evidence: $228B K&B market, 8-16 week lead times, claimed stacked 15-30% channel markups, cohort failures caused by offshore lead time and working capital. Weakness: evidence on true factory cost is thin and installation still needs trades.
4. **Installed accessibility and wellness products (stairlifts, shaftless elevators, saunas, plunge tubs, pools).** High ticket and install-heavy; aging-in-place need noted by JCHS; but unit volumes and margins are unsourced here and wellness markets look small (hundreds of millions).
5. **LSVs / neighborhood EVs and e-bikes/light EVs.** Legal today, capital-light, Europe/China prove demand at the right price and infrastructure; but the US supply chain is 90%+ Chinese, tariffs have already cut imports sharply, and LSVs are limited to 25 mph.
6. **RVs, campers, boats.** Low manufacturer margins, deep cyclicality (RV shipments -14% in H1 2026, boats -9% in 2025), and an 80% used share in boats. A 3x cheaper product might expand the market but no evidence of elasticity was found; weak wedge.
7. **Avoid as "abundance" plays: diamonds, watches, luxury, branded eyewear frames, custom-fit apparel.** Price collapse already happened (diamonds) and destroyed producer value; status and channel hold the margin; China already supplies at cost.

- Common wedge logic: go where delivered price is a multiple of hardware cost for reasons a vertically integrated operator can remove (selling, permitting, scheduling, channel layers, lead time), not where Chinese factories already set the cost floor.
- Realistic gross margins if the model works: installer-type businesses ~17-20% today, with upside only if acquisition cost falls; vehicle makers in the cheap segment low-to-mid teens at scale at best; DTC product brands with own production 40-65% but with high marketing spend.

### Gaps
- No sourced CAC for any category except solar (as a share of price); no sourced capital-to-first-revenue for kitchen, furniture, pool or stairlift start-ups; no gross-margin data for boat builders, cabinet makers or pool builders retrieved.
- The ranking above is an inference from uneven evidence; categories with thin sourcing (3, 4) could move up or down with better data.
