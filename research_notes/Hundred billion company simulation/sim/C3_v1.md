# C3 v1: AI-run billing office paid on collections (healthcare revenue cycle)

## What is sold
The complete revenue cycle function for US healthcare providers that are not on Epic: specialty physician groups, ambulatory surgery centers, home health, skilled nursing, durable medical equipment suppliers, 20-500 clinicians. The company takes over eligibility checks, prior authorization, coding, claim submission, denial appeals, payment posting and patient billing. AI agents do the work, with licensed coders and billers reviewing exceptions.

## Standard unit
One claim taken from encounter to cash.

## Price and contract
- 4.5% of net collections. Incumbent outsourced billing for physician groups charges about 4-8%.
- Three-year contract. Go-live in 45 days.
- Guarantee: net collection rate up at least 2 points and days in accounts receivable down at least 20% within six months, or fees are refunded to cover the shortfall.
- A client collecting $50M a year pays $2.25M a year.

## Founder's cost assumptions
Cost to serve 40% of revenue in year one, falling toward 20% as automation improves. $100M of revenue needs about $2.2bn of collections under management, for example 45 clients averaging $50M.

## Why a customer buys today
Denial rates and labor costs are rising. Payers use AI to deny. Billing staff are hard to hire. Research notes say Waystar's clients are moving to performance-based contracts and that buyers prefer outcome pricing but few vendors offer it. Payer prior-authorization interfaces become mandatory in January 2027.

## Growth plan
More specialties, then hospitals, then payer-side work, then a clearing network between providers and payers. Target: the AI-native equivalent of Optum Insight plus R1.

## Known weak points the founder wants tested
- Will model providers' deployment ventures, Epic, athenahealth or Waystar do the same and bundle it?
- Does the price per unit collapse the way support tickets fell to about $1 per resolution?
- Is the guarantee affordable? How long until cash arrives after go-live?
- Is this a product company or a labor-heavy outsourcing firm?
- Is there any compounding advantage beyond being efficient?
