# C4 v1 demand panel, round 1 (Western tungsten conversion)

Date: 24 November 2026. Everything below is a simulated hypothesis from archetypal personas. It is not customer validation. No real person or company was interviewed.

Evidence labels: [source: URL], [notes] = physical_production.md, [dk] = domain knowledge, unverified.

Web budget: 8 of 8 calls used. Two fetches failed (USGS tungsten chapter and one Argus article, both HTTP 403), so US consumption figures rest on domain knowledge.

## 0. Facts the panel worked from

### Price and units
- Rotterdam APT was $278.22/kg in October 2026 against $74.43/kg ex-works China, a ratio of 3.74. The ratio averaged 1.24 from September 2021 to June 2025. February 2025 prices were $34.60 and $28.92. [notes]
- SMM's 12 November 2026 weekly review put overseas APT at $2,500-3,000/mtu, mid $2,750, with the top of the range revised down. [source: https://news.metal.com/newscontent/ELZJNMICITBSGK6ODY3SHIIQZ4IPD34TK5NQ4GA-apt-under-pressure-at-high-levels-scrap-sentiment-follows-chinas-rise-european-tungsten-market-weekly-review]
- One mtu is 10 kg of WO3. So $2,750/mtu is $275 per kg of WO3, which matches the $278 figure. The "$/kg" in the notes is therefore per kg of WO3, not per kg of tungsten. WO3 is 79.3% tungsten, so the same price is about $347-351 per kg of tungsten contained. [dk for the conversion]
- The brief sets its unit as "one kilogram of tungsten contained" but applies the $278 quote to it. Those do not match. The panel reads the floor both ways: $120/kg W is $952/mtu, and $120/kg WO3 is $1,200/mtu. Either is above the Chinese price ($744/mtu) and 2.8 to 3.5 times the February 2025 Rotterdam price ($346/mtu).
- A Xiamen producer raised its APT long-term contract price to 445,000 yuan/t for early November 2026. [source: https://news.metal.com/newscontent/104207473-chongyi-tungsten-enterprises-raise-long-term-contract-prices-for-the-first-half-of-november]
- In summer 2026 European APT offers were $2,900-3,250/mtu, and traders called offers near $3,200 inflated. [source: SMM, via search result on news.metal.com]

### Incumbents and committed capacity
- Global Tungsten & Powders (Plansee group) at Towanda has about 10,000 t/yr of tungsten oxide capacity. A joint venture with Manhattan Five Partners raises it to about 12,000 t/yr, with a large share going to a strategic oxide reserve for government and defense users. First deliveries are expected in 2026. [source: https://www.metalnomist.com/2026/08/gtp-tungsten-oxide-stockpile-expansion.html]
- The Department of War put $450M of redeemable preferred equity into The Elmet Group to build "North America's only independent APT facility". More than $165M goes to existing plants in Maine, Michigan and Ohio, and about $150M to the Springer complex in Nevada. Mining restarts Q4 2027 and APT conversion starts H2 2028. Partners are Blue Moon Metals and EQ Resources. No price floor or offtake is mentioned. [source: https://www.metaltechnews.com/story/2026/09/23/tech-metals/dow-backs-elmet-with-450m-tungsten-deal/2958.html]; [source: https://war.gov/News/Releases/Release/Article/4599882/department-of-war-announces-a-450-million-investment-in-the-elmet-group-to-secu/]
- Almonty's Sangdong mine in South Korea started processing in June 2026. In July 2026 it extended its offtake with Global Tungsten & Powders to 21 years and 4.41M mtu, with a minimum of 210,000 mtu/yr, covering about 90% of Phase I output. Almonty values this at $490M a year at current APT prices. Almonty also plans its own downstream oxide plant. [source: https://www.miningweekly.com/article/almonty-secures-longer-term-higher-volume-and-improved-pricing-with-offtake-amendment-2026-07-15]
- Vietnam is considering allowing tungsten exports only as APT or oxide. [source: SMM, October 2026, via search result]
- Other Western and allied converters: Wolfram Bergbau (Sandvik, Austria, with its own mine), H.C. Starck Tungsten (Mitsubishi Materials, Germany, largely scrap-fed), Masan High-Tech (Vietnam), Japan New Metals, A.L.M.T., Kennametal, Buffalo Tungsten. [dk]

### The sourcing rule
- Through 31 December 2026 the clause covers tungsten metal powder and tungsten heavy alloy melted or produced in China, Russia, North Korea or Iran. From 1 January 2027 it reaches back through mining and the rest of the chain, with a test of 50% or more covered material by weight. A July 2026 executive order ends non-availability waivers from 1 January 2027 unless a mitigation plan exists. [source: https://acquisition.gov/dfars/252.225-7052-restriction-acquisition-certain-magnets-tantalum-and-tungsten.]; [source: https://www.orrick.com/en/Insights/2026/09/Defense-Supply-Chain-Compliance-Critical-Minerals-Waiver-Cessation-Takes-Effect-January-1-2027]
- The rule binds tungsten metal powder and heavy alloy in defense end items. It does not bind the cemented-carbide tools a commercial toolmaker sells to car and aerospace machining shops. [dk, reading of the clause summary]

### Derived: the founder's margin
- Almonty's $490M a year on 210,000 mtu is $2,333/mtu (panel arithmetic). That is 85% of the $2,750 mid in November, or 76% of the $3,075 mid of the summer range when the deal was announced.
- So a non-Chinese miner is paid 76-85% of the Rotterdam APT price for concentrate. The founder proposes to sell APT at 85% of Rotterdam. Gross margin before any conversion cost is 0-9% of Rotterdam, or $0-25 per kg WO3.
- Western conversion cost from concentrate to APT is about $6-12 per kg WO3 before capital charges. [dk]
- The plant only earns a real margin when the floor binds. That is exactly when the buyer loses money on the contract.

## 2. Personas

### P1. Raw materials director, large Western cemented-carbide toolmaker
- **Who:** buys 3,000-6,000 t W/yr for a group with its own APT or oxide plant, its own zinc-process and chemical recycling, and in one case its own mine. [dk]
- **Incentives and constraints:** security of feed, cost per kg against competitors that still use Chinese-origin carbide in commercial tooling, recycled-content targets, keeping the in-house converter full.
- **Alternatives:** own recycling (50% or more of feed), own converter, long-term concentrate contracts, Masan in Vietnam, Japanese converters, buy-back loops with tool customers.
- **Decision:** refuses the five-year offtake and the prepayment. Signs a non-binding memorandum and a sample-testing agreement only.
- **Terms that would be accepted:** 150-300 t W/yr for two to three years starting after qualification, monthly average of the low Rotterdam quote minus 15%, no floor or a floor at or below $60/kg WO3 with a hard cap at $200, volume flex of plus or minus 30%, exit if delivery slips more than six months.
- **Reasons:**
  - "My shortage is concentrate, not APT. I have kilns and digesters with spare hours. You are bidding against me for Bolivian and Rwandan material."
  - The products are wrong for most carbide makers. Non-integrated carbide plants buy tungsten carbide powder, not APT, and the brief has no carburising step.
  - A 15% discount is real money (usual long-term discounts are 2-8% [dk]), but it is worth nothing on product that does not exist until 2029.
- **What would change it:** a signed concentrate contract of 2,000 t or more that the director cannot get; a tolling offer on the director's own scrap at a fee below $10/kg WO3; three consecutive commercial lots passing specification.

### P2. Supply chain lead, defense prime or munitions maker
- **Who:** either a prime that buys finished heavy-alloy parts, or a munitions maker that presses 200-600 t/yr of tungsten powder itself. [dk]
- **Incentives and constraints:** compliance is binary from 1 January 2027 and waivers end [source above]. Material cost is passed through on cost-type contracts and repriced on fixed-price lots. Career risk is a line stop or a false certification, not price.
- **Alternatives:** Towanda (with its government-directed oxide reserve), Elmet (now government-funded), Kennametal and other heavy-alloy houses, Buffalo Tungsten. All deliver today.
- **Decision (prime):** refuses. It does not buy APT or powder. It will write a support letter for a grant application and tell its tier-two suppliers the founder is an acceptable second source once qualified.
- **Decision (munitions maker):** signs a conditional second-source offtake, 100-150 t W/yr of metal powder for three years from first qualified lot. Accepts the $120 floor. Refuses the prepayment. Demands a hard ceiling of $250/kg in place of the 50/50 share above $300, and mine-to-powder chain-of-custody certificates on every lot.
- **Reasons:**
  - "My deadline is five weeks away. Yours is 2029. I have already locked compliant supply."
  - Advance payments to an unsecured supplier need contracting-officer consent and are not normal practice.
  - This is the only persona that tolerates the floor, and the volume is 3-5% of the plant.
- **What would change it:** compliant powder available in 2027 through tolling at an existing plant; a government-furnished-material arrangement; an incumbent failing to deliver.

### P3. Materials buyer, semiconductor gas or sputtering-target maker, or superalloy producer
- **Who:** buys 20-100 t/yr (gas or target maker) or 200-500 t/yr (superalloy melter) of high-purity tungsten. [dk]
- **Incentives and constraints:** purity of 99.999% or better with uranium and thorium at parts-per-billion levels for semiconductor use; 99.95% with tight oxygen, sulfur and phosphorus for superalloys; any source change triggers customer change notification. [dk]
- **Alternatives:** H.C. Starck, Plansee group, Japanese and Korean purifiers, existing US powder makers.
- **Decision:** refuses the offtake and the prepayment. Will take free samples. Will place paid evaluation orders of 1-5 t at market price once the commercial line runs.
- **Reasons:** standard hydrometallurgical APT is not high-purity feed without extra purification steps the brief does not include. Qualification takes 18-36 months after first commercial lots. A five-year volume commitment before qualification breaks the buyer's own customer contracts. Volumes are too small to matter to a 3,000 t plant.
- **What would change it:** a dedicated high-purity line with 12 months of lot data; a one-year contract follows, at a purity premium of 20-50% over APT. [dk]

### P4. Program officer, Defense Logistics Agency stockpile or Pentagon industrial base office
- **Who:** manages stockpile acquisitions and industrial base grants, loans and equity.
- **Incentives and constraints:** closing a named capability gap, cost share, avoiding duplicate awards, audit exposure. The stockpile buys delivered material from producers that exist.
- **Alternatives already funded:** Elmet at $450M for the same capability with APT in H2 2028 [source above]; the Towanda oxide reserve [source above]; mine-level grants in Nevada and Canada [dk].
- **Decision:** refuses a price floor and refuses an offtake. Invites a white paper. A realistic award is a $15-40M matched grant, 12-18 months from white paper to obligation, tied to a distinct capability.
- **Reasons:**
  - "The independent APT seat is taken. I would be funding a competitor to my own $450M investment."
  - The neodymium-praseodymium floor at $110/kg is one bespoke deal. Project Vault has no producer floor. [notes]
  - The Helicon case shows promised money can stall. [notes]
  - A loan from the Office of Strategic Capital needs bankable offtake, which the founder does not have.
- **What would change it:** a scrap-only plant in a different region that adds redundancy; or leading with germanium, gallium or heavy rare earth separation, where the officer still has gaps (heavy rare earths at 16 times the Chinese price [notes]).

### P5. Chief financial officer of the toolmaker
- **Who:** signs off on prepayments, take-or-pay commitments and provisions.
- **Exposure arithmetic (panel's):**
  - Prepayment on 300 t/yr: 300,000 kg at $236 (85% of $278) is $70.9M of first-year purchases. 20% is $14.2M of unsecured credit to a company with no plant, for 24 months or more.
  - Floor: if Rotterdam returns to 1.24 times the Chinese price of $74, the market is $92. The floor costs $28/kg, $8.4M a year on 300 t, $42M over five years, and an onerous-contract provision the day prices fall.
- **Decision:** refuses both.
- **Terms that would be accepted:** prepayment only against a bank standby letter of credit, or in escrow released on construction milestones, or as a secured convertible note. Floor at or below $55-60/kg WO3, or a floor that steps down each year. Take-or-pay capped at 10% of annual need.
- **Reasons:** "A floor above today's Chinese price makes my commercial tools uncompetitive against every rival that buys Chinese carbide." A Ganzhou producer reports overseas buyers will not pay an explicit premium without binding law. [notes] The rule does not bind commercial tooling. [dk]
- **What would change it:** a government backstop on the floor; an investment-grade guarantor; or the prepayment converted into equity with a board observer.

### P6. Procurement executive
- **Who:** owns the approved vendor list, dual-sourcing policy and supplier credit checks.
- **Process:** quality-system audit, responsible-minerals smelter audit (tungsten is a conflict mineral, and Rwandan and Bolivian feed makes this the first question), financial health score, business-continuity plan. A smelter audit needs an operating plant. [dk]
- **Decision:** refuses volume commitment and prepayment. Signs a confidentiality agreement and a zero-volume framework agreement. Lists the founder as a "development supplier", capped at 10-15% of category spend for the first two years after qualification.
- **Reasons:** no financial statements, no plant, single site, single process line, feed from the two highest-diligence origins on the list. A third or fourth source is welcome, but Masan and the Japanese converters are non-Chinese and deliver now.
- **What would change it:** audited smelter status, 12 months of on-time delivery, parent or bank guarantee.

### P7. Metallurgist who qualifies powder
- **Who:** owns the specification and the release decision.
- **Requirements:** APT with molybdenum under 20 ppm and sodium, potassium, iron, silicon, arsenic, phosphorus and sulfur each under about 10 ppm; controlled grain size distribution after reduction; three to five consecutive commercial lots; sintered-grade tests for hardness, strength, porosity and unwanted phases; tool-life trials at customers. [dk]
- **Timeline:** 9-15 months from the first commercial lot for standard grades. 18-24 months for submicron grades and defense heavy alloy. Pilot-plant product does not count. [dk]
- **Decision:** refuses to support any commitment. Signs a sample-testing protocol.
- **Reasons:** mixed feed is the hardest case. Scrap brings cobalt, tantalum, titanium, chromium and vanadium. Bolivian and Rwandan concentrates bring arsenic, tin and molybdenum. Lot-to-lot drift is the usual failure of new plants. "First qualified product at month 24" is not credible: first product at month 30-36 and qualification at month 40-54.
- **What would change it:** a named process team that has run a commercial APT plant; a single stable feed for the first two years; statistical data on 10 or more lots.

### P8. Minor-metals commodity trader
- **Who:** intermediates concentrate, APT and scrap; finances producers against inventory and offtake.
- **Incentives:** margin of 2-5% and marketing rights, no flat-price exposure beyond 60-90 days. There is no tungsten futures market to hedge a floor. [dk]
- **Decision:** refuses the floor and refuses to fund equipment. Offers, once the plant runs: concentrate supply at 80-88% of the APT quote; offtake of APT at Rotterdam minus 3-5% with exclusive marketing; inventory-secured working capital at roughly 6-9 points over the base rate. [dk]
- **Reasons:**
  - "The $278 quote is a range of $250-300 on thin volume, and the top is being marked down." [source above]
  - "A 3.7 ratio against a 1.24 history is an arbitrage. Material leaks through third countries and the ratio closes." [notes for the ratios; dk for the leakage]
  - "You are selling at 85% and buying at 76-85%. You have given your margin to the customer."
- **What would change it:** a running plant, title to inventory, a government-backed offtake the trader can lend against.

## 5. Five most damaging objections

1. **No margin at the offered price.** Miners are paid 76-85% of Rotterdam for concentrate and the founder sells at 85%. The plant earns money only when the floor binds, which is when buyers will not honour it. [source for Almonty figures; panel arithmetic]
2. **The government seat and the feed are already taken.** Elmet has $450M and an APT start in H2 2028. Towanda is expanding to 12,000 t and holds Sangdong for 21 years. [source]
3. **The rule-bound market is small and already compliant.** The rule covers powder and heavy alloy for defense, 1,000-2,500 t W/yr, not commercial carbide tools. Buyers needed supply by 1 January 2027, not 2029. [source for scope; dk for tonnage]
4. **No commercial buyer signs a floor above the Chinese price, and none prepays a start-up.** [notes for the Ganzhou observation; persona reasoning]
5. **Timeline.** Product at month 30-36 and qualification at month 40-54 puts first real revenue in 2030, after 4,000-7,000 t of competing additions and several chances for the premium to collapse. [dk; source for competitor dates]

## 6. Conditions under which each "no" becomes a "yes"

- **P1 toolmaker raw materials:** founder holds 2,000 t or more of contracted concentrate the toolmaker cannot reach, or offers tolling below $10/kg WO3, and delivers three passing commercial lots. Then 150-300 t/yr, index minus 15%, no floor.
- **P2 defense:** compliant powder in 2027 through tolling at an existing plant. Then 100-150 t/yr at the floor, with a $250 hard ceiling, no prepayment.
- **P3 high purity:** dedicated purification line plus 12 months of lot data. Then a one-year contract at a premium.
- **P4 government:** a capability nobody else is funded for (scrap-only redundancy, or germanium, gallium, heavy rare earths first) and 50% private cost share. Then a $15-40M grant, no floor.
- **P5 finance chief:** prepayment secured by bank guarantee or escrow, floor at or below $60 or government-backstopped, take-or-pay at 10% of need.
- **P6 procurement:** passed smelter audit, 12 months of deliveries, guarantor.
- **P7 metallurgist:** proven process team, stable single feed, 10 lots of data.
- **P8 trader:** operating plant and title to inventory. Then marketing at Rotterdam minus 3-5% and secured working capital.

## 7. Panel's probabilities

- First paid commercial delivery within 18 months, for this version: **3%**. The only route is to trade or toll through someone else's plant, which is not the business in the brief.
- $1 billion of annual recognized revenue within 7 years: **2%**. It needs 3,000 t W sold near today's spike price with the plant full, or a second and third metal at scale, while the price ratio has averaged 1.24 and incumbents are adding capacity with government money.
