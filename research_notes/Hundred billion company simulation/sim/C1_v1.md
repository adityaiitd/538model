# C1 v1: Firm power sooner (flexible bridge capacity for AI campuses)

## What is sold
Firmed power for a grid-connected AI data center that cannot get full firm grid service until 2029-2031. The company installs, owns and operates a grid-forming battery block at the campus, plus controls tied to the utility's dispatch signal. The campus takes interruptible or non-firm grid service early (Texas SB 6 large-load rules, PJM connect-and-manage, utility flexible-connection programs). When the utility calls a curtailment, the battery carries the load so compute does not stop. The company signs the curtailment obligation with the utility and carries the performance risk. When idle, the battery earns grid revenue (ancillary services, capacity), shared with the customer.

## Standard unit
One firm megawatt for one month. Standard block: 50 MW / 200 MWh (four-hour).

## Price and contract
- $22,000 per MW-month capacity fee, 10-year take-or-pay. Reference: gas behind-the-meter capacity is about $38,000 per MW-month before fuel (derived from Solaris backlog).
- Prepayment: 10% of the first three years' fees at signing.
- Termination for convenience: 50% of remaining undiscounted fees.
- Guarantee: load is carried through any utility curtailment of up to four hours per event and 200 hours per year. Liquidated damages if not, capped at 12 months of fees.
- Customer keeps 30% of net grid revenue from the battery.

## Delivery
9-12 months from signed contract to commercial operation, subject to battery delivery and utility approval.

## Founder's cost assumptions
- Installed cost $309/kWh, so $1.24M per MW for four hours. A 50 MW block costs about $62M, about $43M after a 30% investment tax credit.
- Block revenue $13.2M a year in fees, plus grid revenue.
- Financing: 70% non-recourse project debt against the contract, 30% equity from an infrastructure fund. The company's own $5-10M pays for origination, engineering, deposits and utility studies.

## Why a customer buys today
One month of earlier energization is said to be worth about $500,000 per MW to an AI operator. Turbines are sold out to 2030-2032, engines ordered now arrive in 2028, large transformers take 128+ weeks. Batteries are the one power asset that can still be bought in under a year.

## Growth plan
Blocks at many campuses, then onsite generation, then full retail power supply and trading for large loads. Target: the largest owner of flexible capacity serving compute.

## Known weak points the founder wants tested
- Will utilities actually energize earlier in return for a battery-backed curtailment commitment, at 50 MW+ scale, and who controls the breaker?
- Can a hyperscaler or developer just buy Megapacks and do this itself?
- Battery supply (Tesla 2027 nearly sold out), FEOC rules, tariffs.
- Curtailment events longer than four hours.
- What happens to the asset when firm service arrives.
