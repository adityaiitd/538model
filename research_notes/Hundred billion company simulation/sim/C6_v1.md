# C6 v1: Owned libraries of expert work environments for AI labs (new candidate, entered after round one)

## Why this candidate enters now
All five first candidates failed round one. The fastest verified revenue ramps on small capital in the research were suppliers of training inputs to AI labs (Mercor about $2bn gross run-rate; Fleet $1M to about $60M in a year). The screen rejected that category for buyer concentration and lack of a compounding advantage. This version tries to fix those two faults by owning the intellectual property and by adding an independent evaluation product.

## What is sold
1. Licensed libraries of expert-built training and evaluation environments for professional work in finance and adjacent regulated fields: investment banking models, credit underwriting files, fund accounting, audit workpapers, insurance claims, tax returns. Each environment is a self-contained task with realistic data, tools, a grader, and an expert-verified solution path.
2. From year two: an independent benchmark and certification of AI agents for specific regulated tasks, paid for by AI vendors who need it to sell into banks, insurers and asset managers.

## Standard unit
One verified environment. Libraries are sold by subscription per domain with quarterly refresh.

## Price and contract
- Non-exclusive licence: $500-2,000 per environment, or $2-10M a year per lab per domain library. Research notes: tasks sell for $200-2,000 and exclusivity costs 4-5 times more.
- The company keeps ownership of every environment. Exclusivity is sold as a time-limited window at 4 times the price.
- Certification: $150,000-500,000 per agent per task family per year (founder's assumption, to be tested).

## Founder's cost assumptions
Experts paid $100-200 an hour. AI-assisted authoring cuts expert hours per environment by half within a year. Gross margin 55-65%. $100M of revenue needs about five large buyers at $10-20M each plus enterprise customers.

## Why a customer buys today
Lab revenue is $70-80bn run-rate each for the two leaders and growing. Coding is well covered. Professional work in finance, law and insurance is the next area the labs must train and prove. Enterprises convert fewer than half of pilots and say they lack evidence that agents are reliable.

## Growth plan
More professions, then the certification standard that regulated buyers require, then performance warranties on certified agents.

## Known weak points the founder wants tested
- Two to five buyers on quarter-length contracts. Will they license, or insist on owning the work?
- Do labs build this in-house, or buy from the 35+ existing suppliers?
- When a model passes an environment it is worthless. Is this a treadmill with no accumulation?
- Does a held-out benchmark stay uncontaminated when the same company sells training data?
- Is certification a real budget line at banks and insurers, or a hope?
- Can this ever be more than a $1-3bn data vendor?
