# C4 v1: Western tungsten conversion (first plant of a critical-metals refining platform)

## What is sold
Ammonium paratungstate (APT), tungsten oxide and tungsten metal powder made in the United States from non-Chinese concentrate and recycled scrap, compliant with the US defense sourcing rule that starts 1 January 2027.

## Standard unit
One kilogram of tungsten contained in APT or powder, to published specifications.

## Price and contract
- Five-year offtake at a 15% discount to the Rotterdam APT price, with a floor of $120/kg and a ceiling shared 50/50 above $300/kg. Research notes give Rotterdam APT at about $278/kg against about $74/kg inside China (October 2026).
- 20% prepayment of first-year volume at signing, to fund long-lead equipment.
- Delivery of first qualified product 24 months from financing.

## Founder's cost assumptions
- Plant capacity 3,000 tonnes a year. Capital cost $80-120M (founder's assumption, to be tested). At $200/kg realized price, revenue is $600M a year at full output.
- Feed: concentrate from mines in South Korea, Portugal, Spain, Australia, Bolivia, Rwanda, plus carbide scrap.
- Financing: customer prepayments, US government loans or price-floor contracts (as given to MP Materials), strategic equity.

## Why a customer buys today
China controls most tungsten conversion and has restricted exports. Prices outside China are 3.7 times the Chinese price. The US defense sourcing rule bars Chinese tungsten from 2027. Research notes say the limit is conversion capacity, not ore.

## Growth plan
Add germanium, gallium, antimony and heavy rare earth separation on the same site and customer base. Target: the Western refiner of critical minor metals.

## Known weak points the founder wants tested
- If China reopens exports, the premium collapses before the plant qualifies.
- Is concentrate actually available, or already contracted?
- Permitting time, hydrometallurgy know-how, qualification time with carbide and defense customers.
- Is the total market big enough for a $100bn company?
- Why would existing Western converters not simply expand?
