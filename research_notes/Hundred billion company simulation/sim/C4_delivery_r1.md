# C4 delivery panel, round 1: can the tungsten conversion plant actually be built and deliver?

Date: 24 November 2026. Brief tested: `C4_v1.md`. Everything below is a simulated hypothesis from archetype personas, not customer or supplier validation. No real person was interviewed.

Evidence labels: [source: URL] = checked this session (8 searches/fetches used, the full budget). [notes] = `physical_production.md`. [dk] = domain knowledge, unverified. [calc] = my arithmetic from labelled inputs.

## 0. Facts that frame every persona

**Units.** Rotterdam APT is quoted per mtu (10 kg WO3). The notes' "$278/kg" is per kg of WO3 in APT, about $2,780/mtu, not per kg of tungsten metal [calc from notes]. SMM put overseas APT at $2,500-3,000/mtu, average $2,750, on 12 Nov 2026 with the top of the range revised down [source: https://news.metal.com/newscontent/ELZJNMICITBSGK6ODY3SHIIQZ4IPD34TK5NQ4GA-apt-under-pressure-at-high-levels-scrap-sentiment-follows-chinas-rise-european-tungsten-market-weekly-review]. All-time high was $3,140/mtu on 1 May 2026 [source: https://www.fastmarkets.com/insights/bn-strategic-metals-has-potential-to-cover-up-to-60-of-us-apt-needs/]. The brief's "one kg tungsten contained" priced off the Rotterdam APT quote is a 26% unit mismatch (WO3 is 79.3% W) [calc].

**Plant size in feed terms.** 3,000 t/yr APT at 88.5% WO3 = 2,655 t WO3 = 265,500 mtu of product. At 96% recovery it needs about 275,000 mtu of feed a year, or about 4,250 t of 65% concentrate [calc].

**The government already picked a horse.** In September 2026 the Department of War committed $450M of redeemable preferred equity to Elmet, about $150M of it for the Springer complex in Nevada (mine, mill and APT plant restart, a joint venture with Blue Moon Metals and EQ Resources). Mining restarts Q4 2027, APT conversion starts H2 2028. Elmet supports more than 100 defense programs [source: https://www.metaltechnews.com/story/2026/09/23/tech-metals/dow-backs-elmet-with-450m-tungsten-deal/2958.html]. Search snippets give Springer's first-phase APT design capacity as roughly 4,000 t/yr, EQ Resources' stake as 10%, and a supply deal of over 1,000 t/yr of concentrate from Tungsten West's Hemerdon mine to Elmet [source: search snippets, https://www.mining-technology.com/?p=714364 and https://oilprice.com/Company-News/EQ-Resources-Takes-Stake-in-US-Tungsten-Processing-JV.html, not full-text verified].

**Two more US APT projects are in motion.** BN Strategic Metals (Atolia, California) targets first tungsten Q4 2027 and full production Q1 2028, capacity undisclosed, feasibility due about now [source: Fastmarkets URL above]. American Tungsten & Antimony has hired Metso for a Class 5 study of a 2,500 t/yr APT plant, expandable to 5,000, at its existing permitted Del Sol refinery in Nevada [source: https://www.miningweekly.com/article/american-tungsten-and-antimony-plans-imminent-phased-restart-of-del-sol-refinery-2026-08-06]. S&P estimates about $1.2B of capital to unlock all announced tungsten supply by 2028 [source: search snippet of https://www.spglobal.com/content/dam/spglobal/global-assets/en/special-reports/critical-mineral-market-reports/Market%20Report%20-%20Tungsten%20-%20Oct26.pdf].

**The sourcing rule does not require US production.** From 1 January 2027 DFARS 225.7018 reaches back to mining and feedstock, including recycled material, but it bars material from China, Russia, North Korea and Iran. Austrian, German, Vietnamese, Korean and Japanese APT complies. There is a nonavailability determination, and the FY2026 NDAA reportedly adds an exception for recycled material (conditions not verified) [source: https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/48/225.7018-2; https://www.pillsburylaw.com/a/web/s2UdJ57REqw1QWGxcCzMhH/chart_fy2026_ndaa_critical_minerals.pdf].

**Scrap is trapped onshore and cheap.** A BIS order makes US sellers allocate 100% of monthly tungsten scrap sales to US buyers for one year from 27 August 2026, with exemptions possible. US scrap exports were 2,183 t in H1 2026. Carbide scrap fell to $28-34/lb by 31 July, down 12-13% in a month. Sources say the US lacks capacity to convert it [source: https://www.argusmedia.com/en/news-and-insights/latest-market-news/2863444-us-tungsten-scrap-export-curbs-raise-concern; https://www.argusmedia.com/en/news-and-insights/latest-market-news/2861081-us-restricts-black-mass-tungsten-scrap-exports].

## 1. Commercial director, non-Chinese tungsten mine

**Who.** Sells 1,000-2,500 t/yr of 60-68% WO3 concentrate. Answers to project lenders whose covenants require bankable offtake.

**Incentives.** Maximise payable percentage, sell to counterparties lenders accept, keep the conversion margin for the mine if possible.

**What is already contracted.**
- South Korea: about 90% of Sangdong Phase I is sold to Global Tungsten & Powders (Plansee) for 21 years, minimum 210,000 mtu a year after ramp-up. Mill throughput only began 1 July 2026 [source: https://www.miningweekly.com/article/almonty-secures-longer-term-higher-volume-and-improved-pricing-with-offtake-amendment-2026-07-15; https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001670061/000149315226033123/ex99-1.htm]. One mine's whole Phase I is smaller than the founder's feed need of 275,000 mtu.
- Spain: Los Santos tailings material is on take-or-pay to Sandvik's Wolfram Bergbau [source: https://www.mining-technology.com/news/almonty-sandviks-wbh-tungsten-supply-deal/]. EQ Resources (Barruecopardo, and Mt Carbine in Australia) now owns part of the Springer APT venture [source: snippet above].
- UK: Hemerdon concentrate goes to Elmet [source: snippet above].
- Vietnam and Austria: captive to in-house converters [dk].
- Portugal (Panasqueira), Rwanda, Bolivia: roughly 500, 1,200 and 1,600 t W a year. Much of Panasqueira and the audited Rwandan output already goes to established converters or traders. Bolivia is cooperative material sold lot by lot through traders, high in tin and arsenic [dk].

**My estimate of uncommitted supply from the brief's six countries:** 125,000-190,000 mtu a year, which is 45-70% of what the plant needs, and only if the founder wins every free tonne against Plansee, Sandvik, Mitsubishi and traders [dk, calc].

**Price.** Almonty's amended contract implies about $2,333/mtu ($490M a year over 210,000 mtu), roughly 78-85% of Rotterdam APT at the time [calc from sources above]. The mine takes most of the scarcity premium. The converter does not.

**Would I integrate downstream?** Yes, or tie up with a government-backed converter. EQ Resources did, BN and American Tungsten are doing so.

**Decision: refuses a five-year volume contract.** Will sign a non-binding memorandum. Will sell spot lots of 25-100 t from 2029 at 82-85% of Rotterdam APT, 90% provisional payment by confirmed letter of credit at bill of lading, penalties for Mo, As, Sn, P.

**What changes it.** A cash prepayment or equity cheque into my mine of $20-50M, or a take-or-pay backed by an investment-grade or US government guarantee, plus a built plant.

## 2. Carbide scrap dealer and recycler

**Who.** Collects hard scrap (inserts, drills, dies) and soft scrap (grinding sludge) from machine shops. Turns inventory in 30-45 days, pays cash.

**Availability.** Better than at any time in a decade. About 4,400 t a year of scrap that used to go to Japan, Germany and Korea must now be offered in the US [source: Argus above].

**Price as a share of APT.** $28-34/lb is about $62-75/kg of scrap, about $75-92/kg of contained W at 82% W. APT at $2,750/mtu is about $347/kg W. Scrap is trading at roughly 22-27% of APT value [calc from sources]. The long-run norm for hard scrap is 55-75% [dk]. This is the one place the founder's arbitrage is real today.

**Who already buys.** Global Tungsten & Powders, Kennametal, Sandvik take-back programs, Buffalo Tungsten, zinc-process reclaimers and dealer-processors such as Tungco [dk]. They pay in days and have 20-year relationships.

**Decision: will sell, spot only.** Truckload lots at index-linked prices (a percentage of APT, reset monthly), payment in 10 days. No five-year fixed price, no fixed volume.

**Reasons.** The order lapses 27 August 2027 unless renewed, exemptions are being granted, and three new US APT plants plus incumbents will bid the discount away by 2028-29. I expect scrap to return to 50-65% of APT once conversion capacity exists.

**Warning to the founder.** Scrap needs a different front end (oxidation roast or fusion, cobalt recovery, or the zinc process which skips APT entirely). A plant designed for concentrate cannot simply swallow it.

## 3. Process engineering firm (hydrometallurgical plants)

**Who.** Mid-size EPCM contractor. Will take a study now. Will not give a lump-sum price or process guarantee on a first plant with undefined feed.

**Flowsheet.** Three front ends are needed for the feed list: soda pressure digestion for scheelite, caustic digestion for wolframite, oxidation or fusion for scrap. Then silica, phosphorus and arsenic precipitation, molybdenum removal as sulfide, solvent extraction, ammonia strip, evaporative crystallisation, calcination to oxide, hydrogen reduction to powder [dk].

**Capital cost, Class 5, minus 30 to plus 50% [dk, my estimate].**

| Scope | Founder | Panel estimate |
|---|---|---|
| APT only, brownfield chemical site with utilities and outfall | n/a | $140-200M |
| APT + oxide + hydrogen-reduced powder, greenfield, 3,000 t/yr | $80-120M | $250-350M |
| Zero-liquid-discharge salt crystalliser if no outfall | not counted | add $20-40M |
| What $80-120M buys | 3,000 t/yr | about 1,000 t/yr APT-only brownfield, or a scrap-only plant |

Cross-check: the government put about $150M into a brownfield restart of Springer, which already has a mill and a designed APT plant [source: Metal Tech News above]. A greenfield plant with a powder line cannot cost less than that.

**Schedule from financing [dk].**
- Studies and front-end design: 9-12 months (overlaps permitting).
- Long-lead items: titanium or alloy-lined autoclaves 12-14 months; hydrogen pusher furnaces (15-20 needed) 12-18 months; evaporative crystalliser 10-14 months; medium-voltage switchgear and transformers 12-36 months. Transformer queues reach four years for large units [notes].
- Construction 14-18 months, commissioning 3-6 months.
- Mechanical completion: 28-36 months. First on-spec APT: 32-42 months.

**Reagents and waste per year at 3,000 t APT [dk].** Soda ash and caustic 6,000-9,000 t, sulfuric acid 4,000-6,000 t, ammonia 600-900 t, sodium hydrosulfide, kerosene and amine extractant, hydrogen about 300 t. Out: 6,000-10,000 t of sodium sulfate brine or salt, 2,000-3,500 t of digestion residue (iron, manganese, calcium, tin, sometimes uranium and thorium from wolframite), arsenic-bearing cake, molybdenum sulfide cake, hydrogen sulfide and ammonia off-gas to scrubbers.

**Decision: sells a $1.5-3M study and a $15-25M front-end design. Refuses a fixed-price EPC with performance guarantees.** Would offer EPCM reimbursable, damages capped at 10% of fees.

**What changes it.** A fixed feed specification, a 6-12 month pilot on the actual blend, and a licensed flowsheet from an operating converter.

## 4. State and federal environmental permitting authority

**Who.** State air and water permit writers, state hazardous waste program, EPA region, and the NEPA lead if federal money is used.

**Approvals required [dk].**
- Air: state minor-source construction permit if ammonia, particulate, kerosene vapour and hydrogen sulfide are scrubbed below major thresholds. Major-source review adds 12 months or more.
- Water: individual NPDES permit, or an industrial pretreatment permit to a municipal plant, under the federal nonferrous metals effluent rules (40 CFR 421, tungsten subcategories). The high-salt, ammonia-bearing stream is the hard part. Many municipal plants will refuse it.
- Hazardous waste: tungsten processing residues are not covered by the mining-waste exclusion. Arsenic cake that fails the leach test is hazardous waste. The plant will be a large-quantity generator. Any storage beyond 90 days or on-site treatment needs a full permit taking 2-4 years, so everything must ship off site.
- Chemical safety: ammonia and hydrogen inventories trigger EPA risk management and OSHA process safety programs.
- Radioactive material: some wolframite concentrates carry enough uranium and thorium to need a state licence for residues.
- NEPA: a federal loan or grant triggers at least an environmental assessment, 6-12 months, longer if contested.
- Not environmental but mandatory: tungsten is a conflict mineral. Customers require an audited Responsible Minerals Assurance Process status, 6-12 months, before they can list the plant.

**Realistic time to construction-ready permits [dk].**

| Location type | Time |
|---|---|
| Existing chemical park in Texas, Tennessee, Alabama, Nevada with sewer capacity | 10-16 months |
| Pennsylvania, New York, Ohio | 18-30 months |
| California or any greenfield with a new river outfall | 30 months or more |

**Community opposition.** "Arsenic", "ammonia", "hydrogen" and "radioactive residue" will all appear in the first public notice. Tungsten has legacy sites (a New York Superfund site, a Nevada town with a contested health cluster) that opponents will cite [dk].

**Decision: will permit it, not in the founder's timeline.** No permit application can be deemed complete without a final feed list and water balance, which the founder does not have.

**What changes it.** A brownfield site with an existing outfall and an owner who already holds the permits, and a decision to exclude high-arsenic and radioactive feeds.

## 5. Veteran plant manager, existing Western tungsten converter

**Who.** Thirty years running digestion, extraction and reduction. Has commissioned one expansion.

**Yields [dk].** Concentrate to APT 95-97% at steady state, 88-92% in year one. APT to powder 98-99%. Scrap by the chemical route 93-96%.

**Operating cost [dk, my estimate].**
- Cash conversion, concentrate to APT, at nameplate: $12-18/kg APT. About $40-50M a year: labour $15-18M, reagents and utilities $6-9M, maintenance $8-12M, waste $3-6M, overhead and insurance $5-8M.
- Oxide and powder step: add $6-10/kg W.
- Years one and two at 30-50% utilisation: $30-45/kg APT.
- With capital recovery on $300M at 12%: add about $14/kg. All-in $28-35/kg APT.
- Chinese conversion cost: $2-4/kg APT.

**Staffing.** 130-170 people for a 24/7 plant with powder: about 72 operators on four crews, 20-25 maintenance, 12-15 laboratory, 10-12 engineers, 6-8 environment and safety, the rest commercial and administration [dk]. The scarce ones are 6-10 people who have actually run APT crystallisers and reduction furnaces. Perhaps a few dozen exist in the West, almost all employed by the companies the founder would compete with [dk].

**Ramp-up.** 12-24 months from first feed to 80% of nameplate. Each customer then qualifies each product grade [dk].

**Why Western capacity closed.** From the 1980s China sold APT below Western conversion cost, and Western converters switched to buying Chinese APT and kept only the powder step. The Rotterdam to China ratio averaged 1.24 for four years to mid-2025 [notes]. At that ratio the conversion spread is $5-9/kg WO3, a third of this plant's all-in cost [calc].

**The margin problem in the brief.** Selling at 85% of Rotterdam and buying concentrate at 80-85% of Rotterdam leaves 0-5% of the price, $0-14/kg WO3 at today's price [calc]. That does not cover cash conversion cost, let alone capital. The plant only earns money on scrap bought at a quarter of APT value, or on tolling for a fee.

**Why incumbents will expand first.** They add an autoclave and furnaces to a permitted site with a trained crew for $20-60M in 12-18 months, and Elmet now has $165M of government money for exactly that at three existing plants [source: Metal Tech News above].

**What kills new plants.** Feed that differs from the design basis, molybdenum and potassium out of specification, salt disposal, cash burn during a slow ramp, and a price collapse with four months of high-priced inventory in the pipeline.

**Decision: would not bet his pension on it. Would consult at a day rate.**

## 6. Insurer or surety

**Who.** Surety underwriter and a specialty construction and trade-credit insurer.

**Completion bond.** Sureties underwrite the contractor's balance sheet, not the project. Available only if an investment-grade contractor signs a lump-sum contract, which persona 3 refuses. Typical rate 0.7-1.5% of contract value for a qualified contractor [dk].

**Offtake delivery bond.** Declined. No surety guarantees a start-up's five-year supply obligation without 50-100% collateral or a parent indemnity [dk].

**Advance payment guarantee for the 20% prepayment.** A bank standby letter of credit, 100% cash-collateralised for a company with no assets. The prepayment therefore cannot fund equipment unless the customer goes unsecured. At roughly 1,500 t in year one and $235/kg that is about $70M of unsecured credit to a company without a plant [calc].

**What is insurable.** Builder's risk and delay in start-up at 0.3-0.6% of insured value, excluding defective design. Marine cargo. Trade credit on receivables. Political risk on Rwandan or Bolivian prepayments at 1.5-3% a year [dk].

**What is not.** Process performance, price, feed availability, qualification delay. Tungsten has no exchange futures, so inventory price risk cannot be hedged [dk].

**Decision: refuses completion and offtake cover as structured.**

**What changes it.** A US government guarantee (loan office or defense backing), or a strategic parent's indemnity, or a pilot plus independent engineer's report and a wrapped contract.

## 7. Qualification authority at a defense customer

**Who.** Materials and processes lead responsible for tungsten heavy alloy parts (penetrators, fragments, counterweights). Career risk is a ballistic test failure traced to a new powder source.

**How it works [dk].** The program qualifies the alloy maker. The alloy maker qualifies the powder source. Powder must come from the frozen production process, not a pilot. Requirements: chemistry to parts per million (molybdenum, potassium, sodium, iron, oxygen), particle size and morphology, sintering response, three consecutive conforming lots, first-article and ballistic acceptance tests, then change control.

**Timeline.** 12-24 months after first production-representative lots for defense. 6-12 months for a commercial carbide maker per grade. Analogues: a new rocket-motor binder source takes 18-24 months [notes]. MP Materials finished GM's magnet qualification with deliveries only starting Q4 2026 [notes].

**Alternatives.** Elmet, which the Department has just funded and which is already on more than 100 programs [source above]. Global Tungsten & Powders and Kennametal. Austrian and German powder, which is compliant [source: DFARS above]. If none can supply, a nonavailability determination.

**Decision: refuses a purchase order, refuses prepayment.** Will issue a non-binding qualification plan and accept sample lots at the founder's cost.

**Volume reality.** Defense use is perhaps 5-10% of US tungsten demand, roughly 500-1,200 t W a year [dk, low confidence]. The rule-forced market is far smaller than the plant.

**What changes it.** Powder from the commissioned line, a program office sponsor, and qualification funded by the industrial base office.

## 8. Senior hydrometallurgist asked to be technical co-founder

**Who.** Twenty years in tungsten or adjacent extraction circuits. Currently paid $250-350K with a pension, bound by trade-secret obligations.

**Would I leave?** Not for this version. The brief has no site, no feed contract, no pilot, a capital number that is a third of reality, and a competitor with $450M of government equity and a two-year head start.

**What breaks first, in order.**
1. Feed contracts. The plant cannot be designed until the blend is known, and the blend cannot be contracted until the plant is financed.
2. The margin. Miners capture the premium (persona 1).
3. Wastewater permit for the salt stream.
4. Three front ends for scheelite, wolframite and scrap in one plant.
5. Crystalliser control. APT crystal size and trace potassium decide downstream powder behaviour, and customers qualify on it.
6. Molybdenum removal to tens of parts per million on high-molybdenum Korean-type feed.
7. Money during a two-year ramp with $250M or more locked in inventory.

**Terms on which I would join.** 8-12% equity, $5-10M for a pilot on real feed, a brownfield site already identified, a scrap-first Phase 1 of 500-1,000 t/yr, and a signed government or strategic anchor.

**Decision: declines now. Would advise for cash.**

## Germanium, gallium, antimony, heavy rare earths on the same site

Mostly separate businesses.

| Metal | Feed and who controls it | Process | Customers | Shared with tungsten |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Germanium | Zinc smelter residues, fly ash; controlled by zinc smelters | Chlorination, distillation, hydrolysis, zone refining | Infrared optics, fibre, chips | Laboratory, utilities |
| Gallium | Alumina refinery liquor; must sit at the refinery | Extraction from caustic, electrowinning | Compound semiconductor wafer makers | Almost nothing |
| Antimony | Stibnite concentrate; scarce outside China | Smelting or sulfide leach; heavy arsenic load | Flame retardants, batteries, primers | Arsenic handling, which worsens permitting |
| Heavy rare earths | Ionic clay or xenotime concentrates; contested by MP, Lynas, others with federal balance sheets [notes] | Hundreds of extraction stages, radioactive residue licence | Magnet makers | Extraction know-how, partly |

Real sharing is overhead, laboratory, permitting staff and the government relationship: 10-15% of cost at most [dk]. Feed, chemistry, equipment, customers and qualification are all different. Adding antimony and rare earths to the site adds arsenic and radioactive licensing to one permit file, so one contested application can hold the tungsten plant hostage. The genuine chemical neighbours of tungsten are molybdenum, cobalt (from carbide scrap), rhenium, tantalum and niobium [dk].

## Panel conclusions

- The product can be made. The chemistry is a century old. The company in the brief cannot deliver it in 24 months for $80-120M.
- The binding constraints, in order: concentrate is contracted or priced at 80-85% of APT; the Department has already funded a rival with a head start; capital and working capital are five to six times the brief; qualification adds one to two years after start-up; the premium is policy-made and Rotterdam is already softening.
- The only feed with real margin today is US scrap, and that margin has a visible expiry date.

## Probabilities (panel's honest estimate)

- This version reaches a first paid commercial delivery within 18 months: 3%.
- This business reaches $1B of annual recognised revenue within 7 years: 2%.
