## Panel arithmetic used by several personas

Reachable audience
- 283k advisers. About 88k (31%) sit in independent or hybrid RIAs and can choose a tool. About 195k sit in broker-dealers where the home office approves tools [source for headcount; split derived].
- The brief's 50,000 weekly users equals roughly 100% of independent RIA advisers, or 57% of independent plus hybrid.
- OpenEvidence's 65% is a monthly figure [notes]. Weekly use of a research tool is lower. Panel assumption: weekly actives are 20-25% of verified registrants and half of monthly actives [DK].

Query inventory
- OpenEvidence: about 20M consultations a month on roughly 650k monthly physicians, so about 30 per user per month [notes, derived].
- Adviser fund research is episodic: quarterly model reviews, one to three new-client proposals a month, a few alternative-fund reviews a year. Panel assumption: 5-10 research queries per monthly user per month unless the tool also does meeting prep and notes [DK].

Revenue per active user
- OpenEvidence: $300M / 650k = about $460 per monthly physician per year [notes, derived]. The notes' "$1,000 CPM with 5% of inventory sold" cannot produce $300M on 240-300M consultations a year; it implies either a sold-inventory CPM above $10,000 or far higher sell-through. The reliable derived figure is $0.6-1.0 of revenue per consultation.
- Engagement model for advisers: 10,000 weekly users x 10 queries a week = 5.2M queries a year. If 25% are sponsor-relevant, 30% of those are sold and 3-15% produce a qualified engagement, that is 11,700-58,500 engagements. At $100 each: $1.2-5.9M, or $120-590 per weekly user per year.
- The brief needs $2,000 per weekly user ($100M / 50,000).

Sponsor budget
- Asset managers' cost to reach advisers, excluding 12b-1 trails: wholesaling about $6-7bn (roughly 8,000 external wholesalers at $0.7M loaded plus 8,000 internals at $0.15M), marketing departments about $2bn, platform and revenue-sharing payments to distributors several billion more [DK]. That is $30k or more per adviser per year, higher than pharma's roughly $20k per physician ($20.3bn to professionals in 2016, JAMA 2019) [DK].
- The line a third-party digital platform can actually take is small: adviser trade media and digital advertising about $0.4-0.6bn, plus sponsored events and education about $0.3bn [DK]. Panel estimate of the budget addressable by a sponsored-engagement platform: $250-400M a year across all vendors. A strong single platform takes 10-15%: $25-60M.
- Sponsors able to pay $1M a year to one adviser digital channel: fewer than 30 [DK]. The long tail (new interval funds, boutique ETF issuers) pays $50-250k.

## Per-decision value: adviser against physician

| Measure | Physician | Adviser |
|---|---|---|
| Population | about 1.05M physicians; 1.6M prescribers with NPs and PAs [DK] | 283k [source] |
| Supplier revenue directed per head per year | $490bn net drug revenue / 1.6M prescribers = about $305k; about $465k if divided by physicians only [DK] | about $33tn adviser-managed assets / 283k = $117M a head; x 70% in funds x 0.34% asset-weighted fee = about $280k [DK] |
| Decisions per year | about 4,300 prescriptions, about 400 branded, 60-100 new branded starts [DK] | 10-25 product selections, of which 1-3 are alternative funds [DK] |
| Supplier revenue per decision | typical branded new start about $5k a year, $10-15k over persistence; specialty $30-150k a year [DK] | traditional fund swap in a model: about $1.3M moved x 0.20% = $2.7k a year, about $19k over 7 years. Alternative fund adopted for 20 clients: $3M x 2.0% = $60k a year, about $360k over 6 years [DK] |
| Who decides | the physician, inside a payer formulary | the adviser at about 30% of headcount; home-office approved list, model provider or firm CIO for the rest |
| Employer gate on the information tool | none in practice | compliance approval, books and records |
| Outcome measurement for the sponsor | prescriber-level script data bought from data vendors | adviser-level flows hidden in custodial omnibus accounts; firm-level with a 30-60 day lag, sold by custodians and platforms |
| Supplier's existing spend per head | about $20k | about $30k+ |

Reading: an adviser directs about as much supplier revenue per year as a prescriber ($0.28M against $0.3-0.46M), and one alternative-fund decision is worth more than almost any single prescription. But advisers make 20-40 times fewer decisions, are 3.5 times fewer, and about 70% of them do not control the valuable decision. Sponsored inventory is therefore roughly 2-4% of OpenEvidence's. Even at two to three times the price per impression, that is 5-10% of OpenEvidence's revenue: $15-30M.

# Delivery personas

## Persona 1. Licensing head, fund-data and research publisher

- Who: runs data redistribution licences at the dominant US fund-data and ratings publisher. The publisher sells adviser seats at about $4-7k a year and analyst desktops at $17k+ a seat, licenses ratings to asset managers for use in marketing, and sells advertising to asset managers on its own site [DK]. It already has its own AI assistant and a connector inside Claude that serves customers who hold a subscription [source: claude.com blog; search summary].
- Incentives: protect seat revenue and the asset-manager wallet. Asset managers are my largest customer group. A free, sponsor-funded tool showing my data attacks both.
- Alternatives for me: do nothing; the start-up's users who already pay me can connect their own licence.
- Decision: **refuses a redistribution licence for free, ad-supported display. Offers bring-your-own-licence access at no charge.**
- If pressed for a price: $2.5M minimum a year, three-year term, prepaid annually, plus $300 per monthly active user above 5,000. At 24,000 monthly users that is $8.2M a year, or $340 a user against panel revenue of $300-900 a user.
- Restrictions in any version: no star or medalist ratings or analyst text on any screen carrying a sponsored unit; no model training or fine-tuning; no caching beyond 24 hours; no use of my categories or identifiers in analytics sold to asset managers; audit rights; termination on change of control or on 90 days' notice.
- What the start-up can do without me [DK]: SEC filings are free (N-PORT holdings, N-CEN, prospectus fee and risk data, N-2 for interval funds, 10-K/10-Q for non-traded BDCs and REITs, Form ADV, Form D). A second-tier fund-data vendor will license performance, classifications and share-class data for $0.4-0.75M a year. Index benchmarks cost $50-250k per index family. Private-fund offering documents come only from the managers, who are also the paying sponsors.
- Panel estimate of total data cost: year one $0.6-1.5M; at 24,000 monthly users $4-10M a year.
- Changes my answer: the start-up becomes a paid product whose users each hold my licence, or it signs a revenue share of 25% of sponsor revenue with a $2.5M floor.

## Persona 2. Partnerships lead, custodian or adviser-technology platform

- Who: owns third-party integrations at a top-two RIA custodian. The same answer applies, with smaller numbers, at a portfolio-management or TAMP platform.
- Incentives: asset managers already pay us for platform placement, conference sponsorship, adviser-level sales data and no-transaction-fee shelf space [DK]. Access to our advisers is a product we sell. Adviser data is an asset we do not hand to someone who resells "intent analytics" to the same asset managers.
- Own plans: we have just put a frontier lab's adviser product inside our adviser portal with a credit toward the paid plan, connected to live custodial data [source: search summaries]. Asset managers supply content to it through connectors.
- Decision: **refuses marketplace listing and co-marketing. Read-only data integration only after 50 mutual RIA firms request it in writing, which in practice is 9-15 months, plus a security review and SOC 2 Type II.**
- Contract terms if it ever happens: custodial data may not be used to target, score or report advisers to product sponsors; no storage beyond the session; we may terminate without cause on 30 days' notice; no fee paid in either direction.
- Consequence: without positions, cost basis and tax lots the start-up's "portfolio proposals" are built on what the adviser types or uploads. The bundled assistant has them natively.
- Changes my answer: the start-up drops sponsor analytics on our advisers and becomes a paid tool that our RIAs ask for by name; or it pays us a share of sponsor revenue that exceeds what we lose in our own asset-manager programmes. No number I would accept is below 40%.

## Persona 3. Regulator and enforcement view

Not a no-action position. Staff-level reading of the model as described [DK throughout unless marked].

Stage 1: free assistant plus labelled sponsored education. Lawful if all of the following hold.

1. Status of the platform. Advice about securities for compensation makes an investment adviser, and compensation from a third party counts. The publisher exclusion covers impersonal, general, regularly circulated material. Fund data and generic comparisons can sit inside it. "Portfolio proposals" and "client-ready explanations" for a named client cannot. Expect to register as an investment adviser (the internet-adviser route is plausible), with Form ADV conflict disclosure, a code of ethics, examinations, and a fiduciary duty to the adviser-users. The incumbent publisher runs its research through a registered adviser subsidiary for this reason.
2. Anti-touting, Securities Act section 17(b). Anyone who describes a security for consideration from an issuer, underwriter or dealer must disclose that it was paid and how much. Every sponsored unit must say who paid and the amount or rate. "Sponsored" alone is not enough.
3. Organic answers must be payment-blind and provably so. If ranking, inclusion or wording in the unsponsored answer varies with sponsor payment and that is not disclosed, it is fraud under Advisers Act section 206 and Exchange Act rule 10b-5. Enforcement history on revenue sharing, share-class selection and payment for order flow is about undisclosed conflicts and misleading descriptions of revenue sources, not about the payments themselves. Keep logs that show the answer generated before the sponsor auction.
4. Sponsor content rules. Registered funds: the distributor's communication under the self-regulator's communications rule. Shown only to verified registered persons and advisers it is an institutional communication: no filing, no item-by-item principal pre-approval, but written procedures and a reasonable basis to believe it is not forwarded to retail. If the assistant copies sponsor content into a client-ready document it becomes a retail communication, needs principal approval and, for registered funds, filing. Private fund managers: the SEC Marketing Rule applies to their advertisements (net performance, limits on hypothetical performance). Result: sponsor units must be static creative approved by the sponsor's compliance team, typically a 2-6 week cycle. The model may not paraphrase or generate them.
5. Private placements. A fund offered under rule 506(b) cannot be generally solicited. A password-protected service limited to verified registered advisers is defensible under old staff letters on pre-qualified accredited audiences; 506(c) funds are cleaner. Fees must stay flat per engagement. A fee that varies with subscriptions is transaction-based compensation and needs broker-dealer registration.
6. Adviser-side. An RIA using a tool funded by product sponsors has a conflict to disclose in Form ADV Part 2A and must be able to show that sponsored material did not drive a recommendation. Broker-dealer representatives are subject to non-cash compensation limits on what product offerors may provide and to firm supervision; they may use the tool only if their firm contracts for it. Advisers must retain prompts and outputs that form the basis of advice. Client information typed into the tool makes the platform a service provider under the amended privacy rule, with incident notice duties, and that information may not feed sponsor targeting.
7. Policy climate. The 2023 predictive-analytics conflicts proposal was withdrawn in 2025 [DK], so there is no rule aimed at this model. Examination priorities still cover AI use, the Marketing Rule and retail recommendations of illiquid alternatives [DK], and 2026 redemption queues in non-traded credit funds [notes; source above] make sponsored promotion of private credit to advisers a visible target.

Stage 2: placement and servicing fees. Requires a registered broker-dealer.

- Time: statutory 180 days from a substantially complete membership application; 9-14 months in practice from first drafting. Buying a shell does not avoid a change-of-control application of 6-9 months.
- Cost: $250-500k to set up (counsel, consultants, application fee of $7.5-55k). Ongoing $1-2M a year at small scale: chief compliance officer and two to four staff, outsourced financial principal, licensed principals and representatives, annual audit $40-80k, errors-and-omissions cover $100-400k with high retentions and frequent exclusions for non-traded products. Minimum net capital is only $5,000 for a firm that holds no customer funds, but staff will expect 6-12 months of operating capital.
- Liability: a reasonable-investigation duty on every private placement sold; private placement filings; arbitration claims when a fund gates or marks down. Once the platform's affiliate earns a fee on subscriptions, every assistant answer about a fund is a communication of a member firm with a financial interest, subject to content standards and supervision whether a person or a model wrote it. The "independent research" claim does not survive that.
- Fee level. The brief's 1-3.5% is the selling commission paid to the representative's own broker-dealer on brokerage share classes. Fee-only RIAs buy no-load institutional classes. A platform that does not own the client receives 0.10-0.50% from the sponsor or a feeder fund [DK].

Decision: **stage 1 is not unlawful as described, subject to conditions 1-6. Stage 2 is lawful only with registration and changes the legal character of the whole product.**

Changes the view: nothing makes stage 1 condition-free. Dropping personalised proposals keeps the platform inside the publisher exclusion and removes condition 1.

## Persona 4. Partner manager, frontier model provider

- Who: manages start-up and financial-services accounts at a leading lab. The lab is both supplier and competitor: it shipped an adviser product ten weeks ago with about 25 connectors, including five asset managers, both alternative platforms and the largest RIA custodian [source: claude.com blog].
- Incentives: sell tokens to everyone; grow our own enterprise seats; never accept a term that limits our roadmap. No application company is large enough to have pricing power over us [notes].
- Decision: **sells API access on standard terms. Refuses co-marketing, marketplace promotion as a preferred adviser tool, exclusivity or price protection.**
- Terms: list price; 10-20% off against a $1M+ annual commitment; start-up credits of $100-350k; zero data retention available; no commitment on model deprecation beyond standard notice [DK].
- Cost to the start-up [DK]: a research query over fund documents runs 30-60k input tokens and 1.5k output, about $0.10-0.25 on a top model, a fifth of that on a small model. At 40 queries per weekly user per month that is $50-120 per weekly user per year. At 10,000 weekly users, $0.5-1.2M a year. Inference is not the constraint.
- Competitive facts the founder should price in:
  - Asset managers are distributing content into our product through connectors and paying the start-up nothing for it [source: claude.com blog]. That is the sponsor's alternative to a paid placement.
  - Our product is paid, with no advertising. The other leading lab has an advertising business near a $1bn run-rate [notes] and could sell asset-manager placements against finance queries without building anything adviser-specific.
  - Labs entered legal, coding and deployment directly; application companies kept growing but lost margin and several sold [notes].
- Changes my answer on partnership: the start-up owns content we cannot get elsewhere, for example a rights-cleared library of private-fund offering documents. Then we would list it as a connector, which makes it a feature of our product, not a destination.

## Persona 5. Senior product and engineering leader at an adviser-technology company, asked to join as co-founder

- Who: ten years building portfolio and proposal software at a private-equity-owned adviser platform. Total pay about $400k plus illiquid equity [DK].
- What breaks first, in order:
  1. Weekly use. Research is a monthly job for most advisers. The products with daily use are meeting notetakers and CRM assistants, and those now sit inside the lab's product as connectors [source]. To reach weekly use this tool must do meeting prep and client work, which means competing with them and with the custodian bundle.
  2. Numbers. Share classes, fee waivers, expense caps, incentive-fee mechanics, stale NAVs on interval funds, performance as-of dates. One wrong figure in a client-ready page is the adviser's Marketing Rule problem. Every number must come from a deterministic data layer with the model writing only the prose. That is 9-12 months of data engineering before the product is safe [DK].
  3. Firm approval. Any RIA above about $1bn sends a vendor questionnaire and asks for SOC 2 Type II, which takes 6-12 months to earn [DK]. Those firms hold 58% or more of RIA assets [source] and are the advisers sponsors want.
  4. Adverse selection. A free tool attracts solo and sub-$100M advisers. Sponsors pay for $500M+ firms, where a CIO or investment committee picks funds. The people worth reaching number perhaps 3,000-5,000 gatekeepers, not 283,000 advisers [DK].
  5. Sponsor creative. Every unit waits on the sponsor's compliance team. Dynamic matching of sponsor content to the question is not possible.
- Decision: **refuses to leave for this version.**
- Would join on these terms: $4M or more of seed money closed; three sponsors signed with at least $100k each paid up front; one RIA aggregator with 300+ advisers as a design partner; 15-20% equity and $220k salary; and a paid tier for firms from day one.
- What I would build instead: due-diligence automation for home offices, outsourced CIOs and large RIAs, sold at $50-150k per firm per year. Fewer than 2,000 buyers, but they control the decision and they pay.

# Capital and competition personas

## Persona 7. Skeptical venture investor (seed and Series A)

Audience ceiling
- 283k advisers; 88k free to choose tools; panel base case 24,000 monthly and 12,000 weekly users in year five.
- Time to 50,000 weekly users: not reached in seven years in the base case. It needs about 200-250k registrants at a 20-25% weekly ratio, which means enterprise deals with several broker-dealers that sell sponsor access themselves.
- Acquisition cost [DK]: $150-400 per verified registrant through conferences, trade media and paid social; $700-1,800 per weekly user. Buying 50,000 weekly users would cost $35-90M if the gatekeepers allowed it. There is no adviser equivalent of residency programmes to seed word of mouth; advisers sit in about 15,000 small firms.

Revenue per active adviser
- Sponsored: $300-900 per monthly user per year.
- For comparison, advisers pay $1,200-1,800 a year for a meeting notetaker and $4-7k for a research seat [DK]. In this market subscription revenue per user is higher than advertising revenue per user. Free-plus-sponsors is the lower-yield model.

- Year-five range: bear $3M (custodian bundle wins, company sold for its team at $10-30M); base $22M; bull $70M (60,000 monthly users at $900 plus $15M of placement fees, worth about $0.8bn at 12 times revenue).
- Probability-weighted year-five value: 55% x $15M + 35% x $175M + 10% x $800M = about $150M. Cumulative net burn to get there: $45-50M. Present value at a 30% discount rate: about $40M before dilution.
- Gross margin: the brief's 70% needs revenue above about $50M. At $22M, data licences of $5-8M, inference of $1-2M and broker-dealer and compliance cost of $2M leave 50-60%.

Comparables for exit value
- Leading alternative-investment platform: $7.5bn after twelve years and about $300bn of platform assets [source].
- Largest adviser-technology take-private: about $4.5bn on about $1.3bn of revenue; a large TAMP sold for about $2.7bn; a portfolio-reporting leader was valued near $3bn [DK].
- The physician network that sells the same kind of audience trades near $10bn on about $0.6bn of revenue [DK].
- Ceiling for this company: sponsored revenue $85-170M plus placement at 10% of $200bn of flows x 0.3% = $60M. Total $145-230M, worth $1.5-3bn at 10-13 times. The brief's fear is right, with one correction: $1-3bn is the ceiling, not the expected outcome. The expected outcome is $100-200M.

Decision: **refuses the seed as described. Refuses the Series A.**

Terms I would accept
- Seed: $3M at $12M post-money, after (a) three sponsors have paid deposits of $50k or more, (b) a closed beta shows 1,000 verified weekly users averaging eight or more queries a week for 90 days, and (c) a written answer to the custodian bundle.
- Series A: $15M at $60M post-money at $2M of contracted annual sponsor revenue, 5,000 weekly users, and one firm of 300+ advisers deployed with compliance sign-off.

## Persona 8. Incumbents responding rationally

(a) Dominant fund-research publisher
- Response: licence refusal or bring-your-own-licence only (persona 1). Ships its assistant inside existing seats. Keeps selling asset-manager advertising on its own properties. Incremental cost: near zero.
- Would it buy the start-up? Only for the team, $10-30M.

(b) Two largest custodians
- One has already bundled a lab's adviser assistant into its portal with a credit [source]. The other has announced nothing found in this session [source: search, no result], and should be assumed to follow within 12 months [DK].
- Response: integration terms that bar sponsor targeting (persona 2); own asset-manager programmes repackaged as "insights inside the assistant".
- Effect on the start-up (panel estimate): acquisition cost per weekly user doubles or triples; achievable share of RIA advisers halves; the valuable $1bn+ firms standardise on the bundled tool. "Free" still matters for small RIAs that will not buy an enterprise plan, and those are the advisers sponsors value least.

(c) Largest independent broker-dealer
- About 30,000 advisers [DK]. Sponsor programmes are a revenue line worth several hundred million dollars a year [DK]. Off-channel communication fines make any unapproved tool a firing matter.
- Response: **refuses.** Blocks the tool by policy. Will not let a third party sell access to its advisers. Would consider a white-label version with no third-party sponsors, at $10-20 per adviser per month, with sponsor content limited to its own approved list and sold by its own team.

(d) Leading alternative-investment distribution platform
- Already has the sponsor contracts, subscription processing, education programme, due-diligence reports and a connector in the lab's product [source].
- Response: adds a research assistant to its marketplace; declines to give the start-up subscription-processing integration; tells sponsors that engagement on its own platform ends in an order ticket. Serial acquirer [source: search summary]; would pay $20-80M for the start-up if it reached 5,000+ engaged RIA advisers.

(e) Frontier lab's financial-services product
- See persona 4. Paid enterprise seats, compliance logs, custodial data, asset-manager content at no charge to the adviser or the manager. It does not take the sponsor budget directly; it removes the reason to spend it.

# Tests the founder asked for

1. Adviser acquisition cost and time to 50,000 weekly users: $150-400 per verified registrant, $700-1,800 per weekly user. 50,000 weekly users is not reached in seven years in the base case; 1,200-3,500 at 18 months.
2. Data licence cost: $0.6-1.5M in year one using filings plus a second-tier vendor; $4-10M a year at 24,000 monthly users. The dominant publisher refuses ad-supported redistribution or prices it at $340 per user.
3. Sponsor budget addressable per year: $250-400M across all vendors of sponsored digital engagement with advisers; $25-60M obtainable by one strong platform; panel base case $18M in year five.
4. Cross-side network effect: none. More advisers make the platform more valuable to sponsors, linearly. More sponsors do not make it more valuable to advisers and may make it less trusted. Advisers gain nothing from other advisers. Both sides use several platforms at once. A real two-sided effect appears only when subscriptions are processed, and two platforms already hold that position.
5. Broker-dealer registration: 9-14 months, $250-500k to set up, $1-2M a year, due-diligence and arbitration liability on every fund placed, and every assistant answer becomes a supervised communication of a firm with a financial interest. Realistic fee 0.10-0.50%, not 1-3.5%.
6. Custodians bundling a free assistant: already under way at one of the two [source]. Acquisition cost up two to three times, achievable penetration halved, best firms lost, sponsors given a no-cost route for their content.
7. Scale and defensibility: scale makes it larger, not safer. Switching cost for advisers and sponsors is near zero. Inputs it does not own (trusted fund data, custodial data, the model, subscription rails) belong to parties who compete with it. Growth raises the incentive for each of them to tighten terms.
