## Part A. The panel's own numbers

### A1. Unit basis (dispute)
The notes' $278.22/kg Rotterdam and $74.43/kg China are per kg of WO3 in APT (the trade quotes per metric ton unit, 10 kg WO3). The February 2025 figure of $34.60 matches about $346/mtu. [notes; dk for the convention] The brief's unit is "one kilogram of tungsten contained". WO3 is 79.3% tungsten, so the same price is about $351 per kg of tungsten. At the founder's $200/kg, a "3,000 tonne" plant earns:

| What 3,000 t means | WO3 contained | Revenue at $200/kg WO3 |
|---|---|---|
| 3,000 t of APT (88.5% WO3) | 2,655 t | $531M |
| 3,000 t of WO3 | 3,000 t | $600M |
| 3,000 t of tungsten | 3,783 t | $757M |

The panel uses 3,000 t WO3 a year below.

### A2. Margin per kg under three price scenarios
Assumptions, all [dk] unless marked:
- Contract as in the brief: 15% discount to Rotterdam, $120 floor.
- Concentrate is bought on a formula tied to the Rotterdam APT price. Normal payable is about 78% of APT. In today's dislocated market the panel allows a 60% case. The notes report concentrate only 30-40% dearer outside China in April 2026 [notes], so the spread may be wider still today, but miners reprice within one contract cycle.
- US conversion cost: $12/kg variable (reagents, energy, labour, waste) plus $5/kg fixed at full output. Chinese and Vietnamese plants are assumed at $3-6/kg.

| Scenario (Rotterdam APT, $/kg WO3) | Realized price | Concentrate at 78% | EBITDA per kg | EBITDA per year | Concentrate at 60% | EBITDA per kg | EBITDA per year |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Premium persists ($278) | $236 | $217 | +$2.5 | +$7.5M | $167 | +$52.5 | +$157M |
| Premium halves ($176) | $150 | $137 | -$4.7 | -$14M | $106 | +$27 | +$81M |
| China parity ($75), floor not honoured | $64 | $58.5 | -$11.7 | -$35M | $45 | +$1.8 | +$5M |
| China parity ($75), $120 floor honoured on all volume | $120 | $58.5 | +$44.5 | +$133M | $45 | +$58 | +$174M |

Readings:
- The normal gross conversion spread is about 20% of the APT price. The brief gives 15 points of it to the customer. At normal concentrate terms the plant earns almost nothing even at today's record price.
- $600M of revenue is mostly concentrate cost passed through. Value added is the spread.
- The business is a bet that (a) concentrate stays cheap relative to APT outside China, and (b) buyers honour a floor. Buyers on this panel refuse the floor (persona 7).
- If parity means the 2015-24 level of about $30-35/kg rather than today's Chinese $74, every unfloored case is negative.

### A3. Plant cost, payback, working capital
- Capital cost: panel estimate $180-250M for a greenfield US plant with APT, oxide and hydrogen-reduced powder, against the brief's $80-120M. Basis: Mitsubishi pays about $52M to add 1,200 t at an existing site, about $43,000 per annual tonne brownfield [source: Kitco link above]; Springer needs $150-175M to restart an existing mine, mill and APT plant [source: Manufacturing Dive link above]; greenfield plus prevailing-wage and domestic-content conditions on federal money add 30-60% [dk]. $80-120M is plausible only for APT alone on a permitted brownfield site.
- Pre-revenue operating cost and commissioning: about $40M [dk].
- Working capital: 120-160 days from ship loading to cash (ocean freight, assay, plant stock, in-process, finished goods, receivables), with miners paid 80-90% on shipment [dk]. That is about 1,000-1,300 t WO3 at $167-217/kg = $165-285M at today's prices. This is as large as the plant.
- Total to first steady output: about $400-550M, not $80-120M.
- Payback on $240M of plant and pre-opening cost: 1.5 years in the best cell (premium persists, cheap concentrate), 3 years if the premium halves with cheap concentrate, never in four of the eight cells.
- Probability-weighted, the panel's state weights for 2029-30 (premium persists 15%, halves 40%, parity 45%; cheap concentrate 35%, normal 65%) give expected EBITDA of about $7M a year with no floor and about $33M a year if a floor is honoured on one third of volume. That is a 2-8% unlevered yield on plant cost with a large loss tail.

### A4. Inventory price risk
Tungsten has no exchange contract and cannot be hedged [dk]. With about 1,000 t in the pipeline bought at $217/kg, a halving of the price inside one cycle loses $80-100M unless every purchase is priced on the same quotation period as a matching sale. The price is eight times its February 2025 level [notes], so lenders value collateral well below cost.

### A5. Loss if the plant fails qualification
- Sunk: $180-250M plant, about $40M pre-opening, first-fill inventory of $70-150M.
- Recoverable: inventory at 75-85% (sold as off-grade or re-tolled at a competitor), plant salvage 10-20% [dk].
- Equity loss about $200-250M. Senior lender recovers 30-50%. Customers who prepaid $120M unsecured recover 0-20%.

### A7. Does scale make this more defensible or more cyclical?
More cyclical.
- Added capacity destroys the scarcity that creates the margin.
- Working capital grows in step with price and volume and cannot be hedged: more than $1B at platform scale.
- The five metals do not share feed. Tungsten comes from mines and scrap, germanium from zinc smelter residues, gallium from alumina refinery liquor, antimony from gold-antimony mines, heavy rare earths from clays and xenotime. Each feed already has an owner. The customers differ too: carbide toolmakers, fibre and infrared optics, compound semiconductors, flame retardants and ammunition, magnet makers. The brief's "same site and customer base" holds only for the Pentagon as buyer of last resort.
- The one source of defensibility, being the government's chosen supplier with a stockpile contract or floor, was awarded in tungsten two months ago to someone else.

### A8. What the company controls that others cannot copy
At founding: nothing. The process is textbook hydrometallurgy; most living expertise is in China and cannot be licensed under federal money. No mine, no scrap loop, no permit, no qualified product, no captive customer. After four years it could hold a permitted site with waste disposal, defense qualifications and feed contracts. Incumbents hold all three today.

## Part B. Personas

### 1. Loan officer, US government lender or industrial-base office
- Who: credit officer covering Office of Strategic Capital loans, Defense Production Act Title III and industrial-base grants, with knowledge of Export-Import Bank and Development Finance Corporation rules.
- Incentives and constraints: statutory "reasonable assurance of repayment" for loans; credit subsidy scored against a fixed appropriation; environmental review on any federal money; domestic-content and prevailing-wage conditions; no ownership, feed or technology from a foreign entity of concern; must show incremental need after the Elmet award. Career risk is a default headline. [dk]
- Alternatives: add to the Elmet package (existing plants, 100-plus defense programmes, permitted Nevada APT plant) [sources above]; fund GTP, Kennametal or Niagara-type brownfield expansions; fund mines with attached conversion.
- Decision: refuses a loan or price floor today. Offers three things.
  1. Non-binding letter of interest in 60-90 days. Worth little.
  2. Title III or industrial-base grant of $5-30M for engineering, pilot and qualification lots, with about 50% cost share, 9-18 months from white paper to award. Earlier tungsten awards to juniors were $6-16M. [dk]
  3. Senior loan of 50-60% of eligible plant cost ($100-150M), only at final investment decision.
- Loan conditions: 30-40% cash equity spent first; binding offtakes giving 1.3x debt cover at the Chinese parity price, not at Rotterdam; feed contracts for five years; fixed-price construction contract; permits and environmental review complete; government warrants or equity at a discount (USA Rare Earth gave 8-16% at about a 31% discount [notes]). Money arrives 18-30 months after first contact. The Vulcan loan of $620M went with $50M of Commerce equity and a named 10,000 t target [notes]; Wolfspeed gave 7.5% equity [notes].
- Price floor: MP holds the only one, as the sole operating mine and separator, and it has cost nearly $120M in a year [notes]. Project Vault has no producer floor [notes]. Tungsten's stockpile contract is taken [source above].
- Export-Import Bank: its domestic manufacturing programme needs an export share; its supply-chain programme funds foreign mines that sell to US plants. Development Finance Corporation cannot lend to a US plant; it could fund Rwandan or Bolivian feed. [dk]
- Would change the decision: a signed defense prime or stockpile offtake for at least 1,000 t a year; a brownfield permitted site; feed not already owned by Elmet's partners; a letter from a service acquisition office stating that Springer plus existing plants do not cover demand.

### 2. Commodity-finance banker
- Who: head of metals structured trade finance at a bank that funds traders and smelters.
- Incentives and constraints: credit committee will not take construction, technology and unhedgeable price risk together on a company with no balance sheet. Collateral must be countable, insured, saleable and under an independent collateral manager. Material dissolved in process tanks is not eligible. [dk]
- Alternatives: lend to a trading house that tolls through the plant; lend to incumbents.
- Decision on construction: refuses. Would take a tranche only if 100% guaranteed by a federal agency or an investment-grade sponsor gives a completion guarantee.
- Decision on inventory: conditional yes after qualification. Borrowing base of $50-100M. Advance 50-60% of eligible concentrate and finished APT, valued at the lower of cost and a stress price near the Chinese level. Only material matched to a formula-priced sale to an approved buyer on the same quotation period. Margin 450-600 basis points over the reference rate. Concentration limits per buyer and per origin; Rwandan and Bolivian material needs conflict-mineral chain-of-custody papers. [dk]
- Gap: need is $165-285M; the bank provides at most $100M. The rest is equity or a trader.
- Before qualification: only a trading house will fund inventory. It owns the concentrate and the APT and pays a toll of perhaps $15-30/kg. It takes exclusive marketing rights and costs 10-14% a year equivalent. Under tolling, revenue is $45-90M, not $600M. [dk]
- Would change the decision: twelve months of qualified production; offtakes with investment-grade or government buyers; a government first-loss tranche on the borrowing base.

### 3. Partner, natural-resources private equity fund
- Who: partner at a fund that buys mines, royalties and processing assets, targeting 20-25% returns.
- Incentives and constraints: wants a resource or a cost-curve position. A converter has neither; it earns a spread set by whoever is short. Limited partners remember Molycorp's 2015 bankruptcy. [dk]
- Alternatives: integrated mine-plus-conversion projects that have government money (Springer, Sangdong, Mactung-type); royalties on tungsten mines, which capture the Rotterdam-linked price with no conversion risk.
- Decision: refuses now. Would issue a conditional term sheet at final investment decision: $50-75M of preferred equity, 12% payment-in-kind coupon, 1.5x liquidation preference, board control rights, 10-15% warrants.
- Conditions: 60% of feed contracted for five years at a formula that leaves at least $25/kg; take-or-pay for 50% of output with investment-grade or government buyers; fixed-price construction; permits in hand; government loan committed.
- Reason: at normal concentrate terms and the brief's 15% discount, EBITDA is $7.5M on $240M (A2). The fund would sooner own the mine that sells to the founder.
- Would change the decision: ownership of, or a long call on, a feed source; an existing plant bought out of an incumbent.

### 4. Skeptical venture investor (seed)
- Who: seed and Series A partner at a hard-technology fund, $5M cheques, needs a credible path to 100x.
- Total addressable revenue: tungsten outside China $22.5B at today's price, $6.0B at China's price, $2.6B at 2015-24 prices. Germanium, gallium, antimony and heavy rare earths together $5.8B at peak, $1.2-1.5B otherwise (A6). A converter's share of that is the spread, about 20% in tungsten.
- Cyclicality: Rotterdam APT rose from $34.60 in February 2025 to $278 in October 2026; the ex-China to China ratio averaged 1.24x from 2021 to mid-2025 [notes]. Rotterdam antimony is now $18,300-19,000/t with availability improved [notes], roughly 70% below its 2025 peak [dk]. NdPr already trades below the US floor because ex-China producers flooded the ex-China market [notes]. The graphite duties vanished on one ruling [notes].
- Has any minor-metals refiner been worth $100bn? No. Peaks the investor recalls [dk]: China Northern Rare Earth about $25-30B in 2021-22 with state backing; Lynas and MP about $13-16B in 2025; Umicore about EUR 14B in 2021 on battery materials, a fraction of that now; Chinese tungsten majors $5-10B; Kennametal about $2B. The $30B-plus names in this chain (Sandvik) earn it from tools, not conversion.
- Dilution path: seed $5M at $20M post (25%). Series A $30M for pilot, engineering and permits. First plant needs $400-550M (A3); at 50% debt and government money that is $200-275M of equity before revenue, part of it to the government at a discount. Founders hold 10-15% at first revenue and 3-5% after a five-metal platform needing $2-3B of plant and $1B-plus of working capital. Seed stake ends near 4%. At the $3.6B central ceiling that is $144M on $5M, 29x if it works. At the panel's roughly 5% odds of working, expected value is about 1.4x.
- Decision: refuses the seed. Wrong kind of capital: project-finance risk with venture dilution and a commodity ceiling.
- Would change the decision: a version that needs under $30M to first revenue (tolling through an incumbent's spare capacity, or a scrap-recycling line with a defense buyer); a binding feed contract; a named plant manager who has run an APT plant; or a process step that halves conversion cost and is patentable. Then $2-3M at $10M pre-money.

### 5a. Chinese exporters and the Chinese state
- Incentives: keep conversion and downstream value in China; deny Western plants a financeable price; keep leverage for trade talks.
- Options and what is rational:
  - Reopen fully: unlikely as policy; it surrenders leverage.
  - Tighten further: used selectively. Dysprosium, terbium and scandium exports fell to zero in August 2026; 14 European entities were listed in July 2026 [notes]. Only 15 firms may export tungsten [notes].
  - Most likely: licence selectively to civil buyers in Europe, Japan and Korea at prices between the Chinese and Rotterdam levels, export carbide and tools instead of APT (carbide exports up nearly 150% in three months [source: Fastmarkets playbook link]), keep buying foreign concentrate (imports up about 180% [same source]), and loosen licences each time a Western plant approaches financing or start-up.
- Effect on the founder: the law shields only the defense share, which the panel puts at 1,500-2,500 t of tungsten a year in the US [dk], less than the plant's output and already served. On the other 85-90% of the market, China sets the price at will. Every lender prices the plant at Chinese parity for this reason.
- Unknown: the 27 November 2026 expiry of the suspension on gallium, germanium and antimony [notes].

### 5b. Existing Western converters and integrated toolmakers
- Who: the US, Austrian, German and Japanese converters, several owned by toolmakers, with qualified product, permits, scrap loops and captive demand.
- Response: expand on existing sites at $25,000-45,000 per annual tonne against the entrant's $60,000-80,000; tie up scrap (the US scrap export licence rule keeps it onshore to their benefit [source: Manufacturing Dive link]); extend mine contracts. Announced so far: Akita to 2,400 t by April 2029, Goslar plus 2,000 t, Springer about 4,000 t in H2 2028, Almonty's oxide plant [sources above]. That is at least 7,200 t of new non-Chinese capacity, a rise of 36% or more on a base under 20,000 t, landing before or with the founder's plant.
- Decision on the entrant: will not buy from it, fund it or toll for it at volume. Might toll small lots at a full market fee to learn its feed sources. Would hire its engineers.
- Would change: sustained Rotterdam spread above $50/kg through 2029 with their own expansions sold out; then they would rather buy the entrant than see a trader own it.

### 5c. Non-Chinese mines integrating downstream with government money
- Who: operators at Sangdong, Panasqueira, Barruecopardo, Mt Carbine, Springer, and developers in Canada, Nevada and Kazakhstan.
- Incentives: concentrate is the scarce input once conversion is added; sell on a Rotterdam-linked formula and keep the rent; or build conversion with a federal cheque.
- Facts: EQ Resources (Spain, Australia) is a partner in the Springer APT venture [source above]. Sangdong and Panasqueira output is tied to long-term offtakes with an incumbent converter [dk]. Kazakh output largely goes to China [source: Fastmarkets playbook link].
- Decision: refuses a fixed-price or China-linked concentrate contract with a start-up. Would sell spot lots at 75-85% of Rotterdam APT with 90% provisional payment on shipment and a letter of credit. No five-year volume without a prepayment from the founder.
- Would change: founder prepays or takes a mine stake of $30-100M; or the mine's own conversion plan fails.

### 5d. Vietnamese, Korean and other allied converters
- Who: the Vietnamese integrated mine and chemical plant, Korean and Japanese powder makers.
- Position: the defense rule bars tungsten mined, refined or processed in China, Russia, Iran and North Korea [source: search result on the rule, Army Recognition/Manufacturing Dive coverage]. It does not require US production. Allied APT from non-Chinese ore complies.
- Cost: conversion at $4-6/kg against $12-17 in a new US plant [dk]. They have spare qualified capacity now, not in 2029-30.
- Response: ship to US carbide makers at Rotterdam less 3-5%, on annual contracts, no prepayment. They take the US civil market and part of the defense market before the founder's plant exists.
- Would change: a tariff or domestic-content rule that distinguishes US from allied conversion. None is in the notes.

### 6. Competing junior or start-up with an announced, government-funded US project
- Who: management of a listed junior inside a federally funded US tungsten restart with an existing APT plant.
- Position: permitted site, existing plant, own mine feed plus partner feed from Spain and Australia, about $150M of federal-origin money, an operator with qualified downstream product, and a stockpile contract with $150M guaranteed. Start-up H2 2028 [sources above].
- Moves against the founder: offer carbide makers Rotterdam less 5-8% with no prepayment and no floor; sign remaining Iberian and Australian concentrate; tell the industrial-base office a second plant is duplicative; hire the same dozen hydrometallurgists; announce a second-phase expansion the week the founder seeks financing.
- Weak points the founder could use: restart of a plant idle for decades may slip; capacity figure is unverified; operator role is disputed between sources; stockpile contract covers concentrate and sodium tungstate, not APT sales to industry.
- Decision: no cooperation. Would buy the founder's team or feed contracts cheaply if the founder stalls.

### 7. Purchasing head, European hardmetal producer
- Who: head of raw-material purchasing at a mid-size European cemented-carbide maker buying 300-800 t of tungsten a year as APT and powder.
- Incentives and constraints: security of supply at the market price; bonus tied to cost against the Rotterdam index; no authority to prepay unsecured; competitors include Chinese toolmakers exporting finished tools made from $74 material. Not bound by the US defense rule except for a small US defense line.
- Alternatives: the Austrian and German converters, the US converter owned by a European group, the Vietnamese plant, in-house zinc-process recycling, and four new projects all asking for offtakes.
- Decision: refuses the 20% prepayment. Refuses the $120 floor: it is 1.6 times today's Chinese price and 3.5 times the February 2025 Rotterdam price [notes]; signing it would hand rivals a cost advantage in any downturn. Refuses five years take-or-pay.
- What he signs: a non-binding memorandum now. After three production lots pass his specification (molybdenum, sodium, potassium, phosphorus, arsenic limits; grain size) he signs a one-year, renewable frame for 100-200 t a year at the Rotterdam low quotation less 5-8%, priced monthly, with a right to cancel if delivery slips six months. He plays the four projects against each other and gives each 5-10% of his need.
- Why not more: a US plant gives a European buyer freight cost and no legal benefit.
- Would change: Chinese licences to Europe stop for six months or more and incumbents put him on allocation. Then: two-year fixed volume, a collar of $90-220, and prepayment only against a bank guarantee.
