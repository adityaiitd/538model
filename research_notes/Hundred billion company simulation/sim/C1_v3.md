# C1 v3: Contracted onsite power for compute, entered by buying a fleet (second redesign)

## What changed and why
Seven candidates have now been tested. In five of them the panels' own condition for "yes" was to buy a holder of the scarce input rather than start from nothing: buy a billing firm, buy into a mine, run inside a rated insurer, own a breaker source, be a fleet owner. In C1 v2 the buyers said they would pay $38,000-50,000 per MW-month, but only to a fleet owner with engines, permits experience and crews. The start-up had none.

| Round-two objection to v2 | Change in v3 |
|---|---|
| The company holds no fleet, engine slots or crews; fleet owners take the sites | The company starts by acquiring an operating gas-generator rental business that has all three |
| Non-incumbent engine capacity is already sold in 684 MW and 1,000 MW lots | No dependence on new non-incumbent supply for the first 12-24 months. Growth orders are placed through the acquired company's dealer relationships |
| Real cost $1.75-2.9M per MW, payback 9-12 years on new units | The first megawatts are bought as a going concern at a price per MW to be tested. New-build growth is accepted at the panel's 9-10% unlevered return |
| $38,000 is below market and below cost of capital | Price raised to the panels' clearing range |
| Buyers and their lenders will not approve a first-time operator | The operator has years of field history |
| Venture investors will not fund a project developer | Not a venture business. Sponsor is an infrastructure or private equity fund. The founder is chief executive with a minority stake |

## What is sold
Firm prime power for compute sites of 25-200 MW, islanded behind the meter: gas gensets with spare units, a load-following battery, switchgear, fuel management, and round-the-clock operations.

## Standard unit
One firm megawatt for one month.

## Price and contract
- Investment-grade buyers: $38,000-42,000 per MW-month, 10-15 years.
- Sub-investment-grade buyers: $45,000-50,000 per MW-month, five to seven years, 12 months of fees as security, direct agreement with the end tenant.
- Fuel passed through. 99.9% availability at first; 99.99% offered where the site has a second fuel path.

## Entry step
Acquire control of a private natural-gas generator rental company with 50-150 MW of owned units, field crews, a dealer relationship with an engine maker, and existing oilfield or industrial rental contracts. Founder's reference points, to be verified by the panel: Solaris entered this business in 2024 by buying a roughly 150 MW rental fleet for about $200M, and Atlas Energy bought a generator fleet operator in 2025 for about $220M. Budget: $100-300M enterprise value. Funding: the founder's $5-10M pays for the search, diligence and a deposit. A sponsor provides the equity. Debt 40-45%.

## Plan after closing
1. Move units from oilfield rental to compute sites as contracts roll off, at two to three times the oilfield rate.
2. Use the dealer relationship and the sponsor's balance sheet to order new units for 2028.
3. Sell the standard MW-month product to neoclouds, colocation developers and hyperscalers through direct enterprise sales, which most small fleet owners lack.
4. Refinance at the fleet level once 300 MW is contracted.

## Founder's honest view of the economics
- Nothing here is proprietary. Advantages are scale economies: spares, crews, allocation, borrowing cost.
- New-build returns are about 9-10% unlevered at the clearing price. The gain comes from repricing an acquired fleet and from leverage.
- At $1bn of revenue (about 2 GW) comparable companies have been valued in the high single-digit to low double-digit billions. The founder would own a minority.
- This does not obviously reach $100bn. The panel is asked to say what the ceiling is.

## Known weak points the founder wants tested
- Are there targets left to buy, and at what price per MW in late 2026? Would the owners sell, or contract with data centers themselves?
- Why would a sponsor back this founder instead of an operator, or do it through a platform it already owns?
- Do engine slots and dealer terms survive a change of control?
- Can oilfield rental units meet data-center duty: continuous prime power, emissions limits, N+2, 99.9-99.99%?
- Public oilfield-power companies with cheap paper bid for the same targets.
- Is this a company that compounds, or a leveraged asset pool that is sold in five years?
