# Decision ledger

All panel output is simulated. Personas are archetypes played by independent agents that were told to pursue their own interests. Nothing here is a real interview, order or commitment. Probabilities are the panels' own estimates.

P(delivery) is the chance of a first paid commercial delivery within 18 months. P($1bn) is the chance of $1bn of annual recognized revenue within seven years.

## Round one: five first versions

| Candidate | Buyers who would sign as offered | P(delivery) | P($1bn) | Outcome |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| C1 v1 Battery bridge power, $22,000 per MW-month | 1 of 8, conditional (a B-rated neocloud) | 7-12% | 1-2% | Redesign |
| C2 v1 Reinsurer for AI campuses | 0 of 8 | 12% | 1% | Rejected |
| C3 v1 AI billing office at 4.5% of collections | 1 of 8 (equipment supplier), pilots from 3 | 55-70% | 1-2% | Rejected |
| C4 v1 US tungsten conversion | 0 of 8, 0 prepayments | 2-3% | 2-3% | Rejected |
| C5 v1 Factory-built power blocks | 1 of 8, conditional 10 MW order | 25-30% | 3-4% | Rejected |

### C1 v1: battery bridge power

| Objection | Evidence | Status after round one |
|---|---|---|
| No rule in force grants earlier energization in return for a battery-backed curtailment promise at 50 MW+ | FERC rejected at least part of PJM's plan on 9 Oct 2026. Texas SB 6 imposes curtailment and gives no speed. Only precedent is 31 MW at Aligned in Oregon, customer-funded, built by Macquarie's Calibrant. | Fatal to v1 |
| The utility holds the breaker and contracts only with the customer of record | Utility Dive: one utility calls breaker control "non-negotiable" | Fatal to v1 |
| Four hours does not cover real events | Idaho Power asks 10 hours per event and 225 hours a year. Winter emergencies run 8-70 hours. | Fatal to v1 |
| Self-supply is cheaper | Buyer's own cost $10,000-12,000 per MW-month. Start-up break-even $14,000-18,000. | Unresolved |
| Real cost $68-98M per block, not $62M | 250 MWh needed to deliver 200 MWh in year ten. US-compliant cells $215/kWh. | Return falls from 19% to 6-8% |
| Lenders give 40-56%, not 70% | Grid revenue counted at zero. ERCOT batteries earned $24-29 per kW-year in 2026. | Unresolved |
| Value of a month is overstated | $150,000-240,000 per MW for a colocation landlord. $350,000-830,000 only for an operator with GPUs waiting. | Brief corrected |

What changed for v2: dropped the grid-flexibility thesis and the battery as main asset. Moved to islanded gas engines with a small battery, short terms, weak-credit buyers by design. New risks introduced: engine supply, air permits, gas interconnects, crews, late entry against funded fleets.

### C2 v1: reinsurer for AI campuses. Rejected.

| Objection | Evidence |
|---|---|
| Collateral must equal limit on single-site excess layers | $300M supports $300-450M of limit, not $1.5bn. Premium $2-14M, not $25-45M. |
| Price squeeze | Remote layers pay 0.15-1.0% rate on line. Fully collateralized investors need about 10-13%. |
| Capacity arrived in 2026 | Aon facility $5bn, Marsh $10bn with 30 providers, FM $5bn. Zurich took a $1bn quota share. |
| Lenders waive the requirement before paying more | Standard "commercially reasonable terms" carve-out |
| Credit wrap loses money and is financial guaranty | Lenders give up 100-125bp. Expected loss on B and BB tenants is 100-300bp. |
| No owned asset; scale does not help | At $30bn of capital the panel sees a 6.8% return and a value of 1.0-1.5 times book |

Why no redesign: the only condition that produced a "yes" was running the team inside an existing rated platform. That is a job, not a company.

### C3 v1: AI billing office. Rejected.

| Objection | Evidence |
|---|---|
| 4.5% is not a discount at target size | Groups collecting $50M+ pay or spend 2.3-4.2%. athenahealth advertises from 2.99%. |
| Records vendors gate access and bundle billing | athenahealth, eClinicalWorks, Homecare Homebase and others sell their own service |
| Cost to serve is 60-95% in year one and 45-50% at scale, not 40% falling to 20% | Only 30-40% of denial, authorization and call work is automated in year one. Compliance requires human sign-off. |
| Guarantee is uncapped, gameable and uninsurable | Insurer declines. Expected refunds 10-20% of first-year fees. |
| Nothing proprietary compounds | Clearinghouses see over 100 times the denial data. R1 sold at 3.9 times revenue. |
| Fee falls toward 2.5% by 2031 | Support resolutions already fell from $1.90 to $0.99 |

Panel estimate at 1,000 clients: about $600M of revenue and $1.7-3.3bn of value. The founder's own assumptions give $17-25bn. A workable small business, not the target. The panels' route to "yes" was to buy an existing billing firm and automate it, which is a roll-up that funded competitors already run.

### C4 v1: tungsten conversion. Rejected.

| Objection | Evidence |
|---|---|
| The converter does not capture the premium | Almonty's contract implies miners receive 76-85% of the Rotterdam price for concentrate. Selling at 85% leaves $0-14 per kg against $12-18 of conversion cost. |
| Feed is already contracted | About 90% of Sangdong goes to Plansee's US plant for 21 years. Los Santos to Sandvik. Hemerdon to Elmet. |
| The government seat is taken | The Pentagon put $450M into Elmet in September 2026 for the same capability |
| The sourcing rule covers little demand and does not require US production | Defense powder and heavy alloy, roughly 500-2,500 tonnes a year. Austrian, German, Vietnamese and Korean material complies. |
| Capital and time are several times the brief | $400-750M including working capital. Qualified product in 40-66 months. |
| Ceiling | All five named metals outside China are worth about $28bn a year at spike prices and $7.5bn at Chinese prices. No minor-metals refiner has reached $100bn. |

### C5 v1: factory-built power blocks. Rejected.

| Objection | Evidence |
|---|---|
| Delivery schedule is set by competitors | 15 kV switchgear takes 52-80 weeks. The makers also sell their own skids. |
| Margin is 5-12% early and 15-22% at volume, not 25-30% | About 70% of cost is bought-in components. Factory power scope is only about 5% cheaper than field build (SemiAnalysis). |
| Large buyers free-issue the equipment | Billable revenue falls to $0.15-0.5M per MW |
| Working capital of $65-100M per 100 MW in build; nobody pays 30% unsecured to a start-up | Lender refuses until $50M of delivered revenue |
| The 800 V DC change reopens specifications, not supplier lists | Nvidia's named partners are Vertiv, Eaton, Schneider, Delta and Flex |
| Value does not scale | Fibrebond sold at 12.7 times EBITDA. Panel sees $13-20bn of equity value at 10 GW a year. |

The only route to "yes" was to own a certified DC power component, which needs technology the founder does not have.

## Lessons carried into round two

1. In every candidate the scarce input belonged to someone else, who kept the margin: miners, breaker makers, the utility, the lead insurer, the records vendor.
2. Buyers discount a start-up's long-dated promise to zero. Guarantees, floors, wraps and warranties from a thin balance sheet did not sell anywhere.
3. Creditworthy buyers supply themselves. The buyers who would sign were weak credits, which then blocked financing.
4. Selling AI labor at a discount does not hold. Rivals have the same agents and price falls to cost.
5. A commodity asset under contract is competed down to about an 8% equity return.

### C1 v2: islanded gas engine blocks

| Round-one objection | Status after v2 |
|---|---|
| No rule grants earlier energization | Resolved. Islanding removes the utility. Replaced by air permit and gas tap. |
| Utility holds the breaker | Resolved |
| Four hours too short | Resolved |
| Self-supply is cheaper | Partly. Target buyers cannot self-supply engines, but fleet owners can serve them. |
| Cost understated | Unresolved. Panels put cost at $1.75-2.9M per MW sold against the brief's $1.45M. |
| Lenders give less than planned | Unresolved. 40-45% at about 11%. |

| New objection | Evidence |
|---|---|
| The "open" engine capacity is already sold | HD Hyundai sold 684 MW and 1,000 MW lots to funded US developers in 2026 |
| Fleet owners with idle capacity take these sites | ProPetro has about 2.1 GW secured and uncontracted and signs 60 and 80 MW deals. Panel says they deliver from stock in 2027 at $42,000-50,000. |
| The block is not redeployable | Available engines are 9.6-20 MW on foundations. N+2 needs 48 MW installed for 25 MW sold. |
| Texas paused data-center air permits on 21 September 2026 | Search summary only, not verified |
| $38,000 is below market and below cost of capital | Panels put the clearing price for weak credit at $45,000-58,000. Return at the clearing price is 8.5-9.8% unlevered. |
| Nothing compounds | At $1bn of revenue the panel values the company, as opposed to the assets, at $0.4-0.5bn |

What changed for v3: the company starts by buying an operating generator rental fleet, so it has units, crews, a dealer relationship and an operating record on day one. Price raised to the clearing range. Sponsor is a private equity or infrastructure fund, not venture.

### C6 v1: owned expert environments. Rejected.

| Objection | Evidence |
|---|---|
| Exclusive is the norm and non-exclusive is the discount product, so owning the library earns less | Epoch and SemiAnalysis on environment pricing. Panel: a task costs about $2,500 to build and sells non-exclusive for $300-900. |
| Finance is already supplied | OpenAI has paid 100+ former bankers since October 2025. Mercor's benchmark has covered investment banking since January 2026. |
| Certification has no payer | The Federal Reserve's SR 26-2 keeps validation with the bank. AIUC has raised $55M and holds the reference customers. |
| Labs are taking the work in-house and reallocate quarterly | About 30% in-house now, per panel |
| Selling training data disqualifies the same company as certifier | Panel reasoning |

Panel revenue estimate: $2.5M, $12M, $28M and $55M in years one, two, three and five. Value $200-450M. Losing the largest customer halves revenue within 90 days.

### C7 v1: adviser assistant paid by sponsors. Rejected.

| Objection | Evidence |
|---|---|
| A frontier lab already shipped it | Anthropic launched Claude for Financial Advisors on 14 September 2026. The panel reports a large custodian embedded it in October. |
| Payers want named leads and users refuse to be named | Panel reasoning, unverified |
| Few advisers control the decision | 283,137 US advisers (Cerulli). 54% use manager-built model portfolios (Escalent). About 88,000 are free to choose tools. |
| Inventory is 2-4% of the physician case | 5-10 queries a month against about 30. Revenue per active adviser $300-900. |
| No cross-side network effect | Sponsors gain from advisers. Advisers gain nothing from sponsors or each other. |

Panel revenue estimate: $0.2M, $1.8M, $5.8M and $22M in years one, two, three and five.

## Round three: C1 v3, entry by buying a fleet

Tested by the retained capital panel and one fresh market panel.

| Standing objection | Status after v3 |
|---|---|
| Utility contract, price too low, default exceeds security, crews | Resolved |
| Equipment, permits, weak credits, capital | Partly resolved |
| Nothing proprietary | Unresolved |
| Cost of $1.8-2.2M per firm MW | Unresolved |
| 99.9% availability islanded on one gas line | Unresolved |

| New objection | Evidence |
|---|---|
| There is no repricing gain | Rental fleets already earn about $240,000 of EBITDA per nameplate MW-year. Compute at $47,500 per firm MW is about $35,600 per nameplate MW-month against about $34,000 in rental, before $0.4-0.6M per MW of conversion. |
| Entry prices have risen | Panel: multiples went from 4.3 times EBITDA in January 2025 to 7.4 times in February 2026. Kodiak Gas Services bought Distributed Power Solutions in February 2026. |
| Public buyers pay in stock at 11-12 times and win auctions | Panel estimate |
| Dealer allocation does not transfer by contract | Panel reasoning |
| The sponsor funds the deal but not the founder as chief executive | Offers commercial head with 5-8% |

Capital panel estimates: acquisition closes within 12 months 15%. First paid compute delivery within 18 months 12%. $1bn of revenue within seven years 2%. Realistic ceiling: 300-600 MW, $150-350M of revenue, sold in four to six years for $1-2.5bn, founder's share $20-80M. Upper bound: 2 GW, $1bn of revenue, about $6bn of enterprise value.

## Does any version still satisfy the original ambition?

No. Ten versions of seven businesses were tested. None kept a path to $100bn of equity value after the panels' corrections. The highest ceilings the panels assigned were $13-20bn (power blocks at 10 GW a year, judged unreachable on capital) and about $6bn (onsite power at 2 GW).
