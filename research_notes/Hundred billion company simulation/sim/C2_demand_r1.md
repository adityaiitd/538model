# C2 v1 demand panel, round 1 (simulated, 24 Nov 2026)

Everything below is a simulated hypothesis from archetype personas. It is not customer validation. No real person was interviewed.

Evidence labels: [notes] = the founder's research notes; [source: URL] = page opened in this session; [snippet: URL] = search summary only, page not opened; [DK] = domain knowledge, unverified.

## The arithmetic the panel kept returning to

1. Brief: $300M of collateral writes $1.5bn of limit. That is 20% collateralized. Someone holds $1.2bn of unsecured exposure to an unrated vehicle. Fully collateralized, $300M writes about $300M of limit. [DK: standard collateralized reinsurance practice]
2. At the brief's 1.5-3.0% rate on line, $300M of limit earns $4.5-9M of premium, not $25-45M. The underwriting company's 12% fee is $0.5-1.1M a year. [derived]
3. Buyers pay roughly 0.2-1.0% a year for layers attaching above $2.5-3.5bn. [DK] Basis: whole construction programmes cost about 0.2-0.4% of insured value for the full build, and most of that goes to the first $500M.
4. Fully collateralized investors need about 6-9% gross rate on line on a remote layer. [DK] Basis: 4-6 points over collateral yield, plus expected loss, grossed up for about 30% of frictions (brokerage 10-15%, fronting 5-7%, the founder's 12% fee). Euler's "above 15%" target is consistent.
5. So the brief's price is above what buyers pay and below what fully collateralized capital needs. Only the 5x leverage makes investor returns work, and the leverage is what counterparties reject.

## Persona 1: Global risk manager, top-five hyperscaler

- Incentives and constraints: minimise total cost of risk. Large captive. Approved insurer list with a minimum rating of A- (S&P or AM Best). [DK] Capex above $100bn a year [notes], so a $3bn loss is small against the balance sheet.
- Alternatives: retain in the captive; buy reinsurance for the captive from rated reinsurers; let the JV or SPV buy what its lenders require.
- Decision: REFUSES. No purchase order, no deposit.
- Price: retains everything above the facility limit unless a layer costs under about 0.3-0.5% rate on line. At 1.5-3.0% the captive keeps it. [DK]
- Reasons: the uninsured half to two-thirds is a choice, "calculated risk" [source: ENR]. A campus is many separated buildings, so the fire maximum loss is one or two halls; only wind and hail reach an excess layer, and the firm diversifies that across 50+ sites. Site telemetry will not be shared with a small excess market for security reasons.
- Would change the decision: a JV lender that will not waive a limit the rated market cannot fill; price at or under 0.5%; the founder's capacity sitting behind a carrier already on the approved list.

## Persona 2: Sponsor, project-financed data center SPV ($10-25bn campus)

- Incentives and constraints: reach financial close on date. One month of slip on $15bn at 7% costs about $90M of carry. [derived from notes pricing] Cover must satisfy the lenders' insurance adviser. The sponsor does not choose markets; the broker and the adviser do.
- Alternatives: flex the required limit down under the "commercially available" carve-out; a tenant or sponsor backstop; more lines from rated markets through the broker facility; an owner captive fronted by a rated carrier.
- Decision: REFUSES TODAY (no licence, no fronting carrier, no capital, nothing to bind). CONDITIONAL YES later.
- Price: would authorise the broker to bind a $100-250M layer at up to 2.0% a year, perhaps 3.0% if it is the last layer blocking close. That is $2-7.5M a year, about 1-5 basis points of project cost. Price is not this buyer's constraint. [DK]
- Conditions: policy issued by a carrier rated A- or better that is unconditionally liable for 100%; follow-form on the lead's wording; claims control with the lead; non-cancellable for a build of 3-4 years plus the DSU indemnity period.
- Reasons: this is the one persona for whom a real gap is worth far more than the premium. The buyer set is small: perhaps 10-20 campuses a year without a hyperscaler balance sheet behind the casualty risk. [DK] At $250M and 2% on every one, that is $50-100M of premium needing $2.5-5bn of collateral. [derived]
- Would change the decision: a live fronting agreement, collateral in trust, and the lenders' adviser confirming in writing that the paper counts toward the required limit.

## Persona 3: CFO, neocloud with lender-mandated insurance and expensive debt

- Incentives and constraints: interest expense exceeds adjusted operating income at the sector leader ($846M against $379M a quarter) and bonds have traded at 15% [notes]. Buys the covenant minimum and nothing more.
- Alternatives: covenant waiver (lenders already postponed coverage tests and allowed unlimited equity cures [notes]); the Ryan Specialty Tera hardware programme (over $1bn per policy) [notes]; broker facilities; vendor and customer backstops from Nvidia and Google at no cash premium [notes].
- Decision on excess property: REFUSES unless a credit agreement names a limit the rated market cannot fill. Then pays the least available, up to about 1% rate on line. [DK]
- Decision on the offtake credit wrap: REFUSES as offered. Would sign a term sheet at up to 100-125bp a year on wrapped notional, only if the wrap comes from an entity rated A- or better and is unconditional, irrevocable and pays on time.
- Reasons: the wrap is worth the spread saved, 225-325bp between an investment-grade and a non-investment-grade offtaker [notes]. On a $3bn loan that is $67-98M a year. The wrapper's expected loss on a B or BB tenant is about 100-300bp a year before capital cost [DK], and sector credit default swaps reached about 1,000bp in October [notes]. The wrapper needs more than the saving. An unrated wrapper also produces no rating uplift, so the saving is zero.
- Would change the decision: a wrap at or above A- for 125bp or less; or a lender stating in a term sheet that the founder's policy cuts the spread by at least twice its cost.

## Persona 4: Lenders' insurance adviser

- Incentives and constraints: professional liability for the insurance sign-off. Uses market-standard clauses. Will not be the adviser who approved novel security on a $20bn deal.
- Standard requirements: insurers rated A- or better; replacement within 30-60 days on downgrade; lenders as loss payee; non-vitiation; required limit set from a maximum-loss study; DSU sum insured covering debt service and fixed costs for 12-24 months; and a carve-out for cover "not available on commercially reasonable terms". [DK]
- Decision: ACCEPTS the founder's layer only as a policy issued by a carrier rated A- or better with full unconditional liability and no pay-as-paid wording. REFUSES any direct unrated paper and any structure that relies on a cut-through to a trust. Will NOT raise the required limit because new capacity exists.
- Price view: no opinion on price until it hits the "commercially reasonable" test. A rate of 1.5-3.0% on a remote layer would itself be evidence that the cover is not commercially reasonable, which lets the borrower off the requirement. [DK]
- Reasons: the availability carve-out means a shortage lowers the requirement before it raises the premium. The adviser also caps each insurer's share by its size. Programme fronting carriers with a few hundred million of surplus would be capped well below $250M lines, likely $25-50M. [DK]
- Would change the decision: lenders or rating agencies refusing maximum-loss-based limits (lenders already resist them [source: Insurance Business]); a fronting carrier with multi-billion surplus; rating agencies penalising SPV debt for insurance shortfalls (the Moody's snippet, unverified).

## Persona 5: Head of a large broker's data center facility

- Incentives and constraints: commission and facility fees; an errors-and-omissions exposure on every market recommended; a market security committee that vets the issuing carrier. Facility panels are pre-agreed line slips of rated carriers. [DK]
- Alternatives: add lines from existing panel members, who are themselves bringing third-party capital behind their own paper (Zurich's $1bn quota share, Euler's sidecar plan, a reinsurer-sponsored cat bond expected) [sources above]. The broker's own capital-markets arm arranges those structures and earns fees on them.
- Decision: REFUSES to show business today. Would give a following line on 2-4 excess placements a year once a fronted, funded vehicle exists with a team the market already knows.
- Price: the layer gets the tower's price, about 0.3-1.0% rate on line, less 15-20% brokerage and the facility fee. No premium for being new. If the top of a tower is truly short, a scarce layer can clear at 1.5-2.5% on that one deal. [DK]
- Reasons: capacity grew fast (Aon $3.5bn in April, reportedly $5bn in July; Marsh $2.7bn, reportedly up to $10bn for operating assets). The shortage is specifically construction with DSU above about $3.5-6bn on one site. The pen goes to teams leaving the construction books of the leaders with their relationships intact. Third-party capital is entering as quota shares behind incumbents, which gives investors a diversified book and a rated counterparty, and gives the broker no new security question.
- Would change the decision: two or three senior construction underwriters with named lead experience; a fronting carrier on the broker's approved list willing to issue $100M+ lines; collateral posted; acceptance of the lead's terms.

## Persona 6: Procurement or treasury executive assessing counterparty security

- Incentives and constraints: counterparty policy sets a minimum rating of A- and a concentration cap per insurer. Any exception goes to the CFO and the lenders. [DK]
- Decision: REFUSES direct unrated paper. REFUSES the structure as described. ACCEPTS exposure to a rated fronting carrier, capped at about 10% of the tower and subject to a downgrade replacement clause.
- Reasons:
  - $300M backing $1.5bn is not collateralized. Either the fronting carrier carries $1.2bn of unsecured recoverable from an unrated vehicle, or the limit is $300M. Fronting carriers have demanded full, high-quality collateral since the 2023 Vesttoo letter-of-credit fraud. [DK]
  - Construction policies run 3-5 years with long DSU claim settlement. Investors in insurance-linked funds want annual liquidity. Trapped collateral and commutation terms are unresolved. [DK]
  - A thin fronting carrier is a covenant risk in itself: a downgrade below A- forces replacement of the layer mid-build.
  - Accumulation: one tornado on one campus can exhaust the vehicle [source: ENR for the $10bn single-site figure], and campuses cluster in the same weather regions.
- Would change the decision: a financial strength rating of A- on the reinsurer itself (which needs roughly $1bn+ of equity and a diversified book [DK]); or collateral equal to full limit in a trust with an A-rated bank; or a fronting carrier with multi-billion surplus accepting the full credit risk.

## Persona 7: Construction risk engineer, insured's side

- What exists already: lead insurer engineering surveys; the lenders' technical adviser's monthly schedule report; the contractor's own scheduling and site-capture tools; hot-work permits, water-leak detection, battery spacing to fire codes, and commissioning protocols for liquid cooling. [DK]
- Decision: REFUSES to pay for the telemetry and AI monitoring. Would allow a free pilot on one site if the lead underwriter asks for it.
- Value in practice: zero to 2% premium credit. An excess layer follows the lead's survey programme and has no leverage to impose its own. [DK]
- Reasons: site access and data are restricted by tenant security rules. Sharing schedule-slip data with a DSU insurer can prejudice a later claim, so counsel will limit it. The losses that reach an excess layer are wind, hail and campus-wide events; monitoring construction progress does not reduce them. Useful engineering is hardening of laydown yards and stored equipment, battery separation, and hot-testing controls, and the lead already does this.
- Would change the decision: a lead market making the telemetry a condition of capacity with a credit of 5% or more; proof on comparable sites of fewer water or commissioning losses.

## Persona 8: Head of structured credit, private lender to GPU fleets

- Incentives and constraints: fund targets about 10-12% returns [DK]. The spread is the product. Loans are sized on contracted cash flow at 1.2-1.35x coverage and amortise inside the contract term [notes], so residual value exposure at maturity is small on investment-grade deals. Rated facilities need agency-recognised credit substitution.
- Alternatives: Nvidia guarantees ($118bn outstanding) and Google backstops, at no cost to the lender [notes]; 15-25% customer prepayments as first loss [notes]; mezzanine investors; CME and ICE compute futures once liquid [notes]; simply keeping the spread and the risk.
- Decision on residual value cover: REFUSES. Would pay at most 50-100bp of covered notional up front for a floor at about 30% of cost in year 4, and would cut loan spread by 25-50bp at most. [DK] Would count only the cash-collateralized amount.
- Decision on offtake-default cover: REFUSES. Would give up at most half the 225-325bp differential, and only to a wrapper rated A- or better with guarantee-style wording and no insurance defences.
- Reasons: an insurance policy with conditions and exclusions is not credit substitution. An unrated vehicle earns no rating credit beyond its cash. All GPU residual value cover pays in the same state of the world, so $300M is a one-event vehicle. Documented used-GPU sales total $26.3M over three years [notes], so no floor can be priced. A fully cash-collateralized first-loss piece is mezzanine at 12-15%, and mezzanine bidders already exist. Banks seeking capital relief would value this more than a credit fund does.
- Would change the decision: a rated wrap accepted by the agency rating the facility; Nvidia and Google withdrawing backstops (rent-back commitments already fell from $36bn to $32bn [notes]); a liquid recovery-price index with real volume.

## Answers to the five test questions

1. Unavailable or just expensive? Both, by segment. For hyperscalers it is a choice at current prices. For project-financed construction with DSU above about $3.5-6bn on one site, the rated market runs out "at commercially viable rates" [source: ENR]. The carve-out in credit agreements lets the requirement fall first.
2. Unrated collateralized paper behind a fronting carrier? Accepted by every persona if the fronting carrier is rated A- or better, large enough, and liable for 100%. Nobody looks through to the vehicle. The problem moves to whether such a carrier will issue $100-250M lines against 20% collateral. Persona 6 and persona 4 say no.
3. Rate on line today and for more? Roughly 0.2-1.0% on remote excess layers today; 1.5-3.0% only for the last scarce layer on a deal that cannot otherwise close. No source confirms either figure. [DK]
4. Would lenders relax pricing enough to pay for residual value or wrap cover? No. Their maximum give-up (25-50bp for residual value, 100-125bp for a wrap) is below the wrapper's expected loss, and is zero for an unrated provider.
5. Is a new entrant ever shown the business? Yes, as a following line on the top of short towers, after funding, fronting and a known team are in place. Not before, and not at a premium to the tower.

## Panel estimate

- First paid commercial delivery (a bound layer with premium received) within 18 months: 12%.
- $1bn of annual recognized revenue within 7 years: 1%. At 1-2% rate on line that is $50-100bn of limit, which a collateralized vehicle cannot carry; it needs a rated, diversified balance sheet with several billion of equity, which is a different company.
