# C7 v1: Free AI research assistant for financial advisers, paid for by product sponsors (new candidate, entered after round one)

## Why this candidate enters now
All five first candidates failed round one. Lessons: own the scarce input, avoid long-dated promises from a thin balance sheet, do not sell labor at a discount. The research notes show one model that fits: OpenEvidence gives physicians a free AI assistant and sells pharmaceutical companies access to that verified audience. It went from $7.9M to about $300M annualized revenue in 19 months and claims 65% of US physicians. The scarce input it owns is the attention of a licensed professional whose decisions direct large spending. This candidate applies that model to financial advisers, which fits the founder's financial-markets and enterprise-sales background.

## What is sold
To advisers, free: an AI assistant for investment research and due diligence. It answers with citations from licensed fund data, regulatory filings and manager documents. It produces fund comparisons, portfolio proposals, due-diligence memos on private funds, and client-ready explanations. Every answer is archived to meet books-and-records rules. Advisers are verified by their regulatory registration number.

To asset managers and product sponsors, paid: clearly labelled sponsored education shown beside relevant answers, measured engagement with verified advisers, and aggregated intent analytics. Later: placement and servicing fees on alternative funds subscribed through the platform, through a registered broker-dealer.

## Standard unit
One sponsored engagement with a verified adviser.

## Price and contract
- $40-150 per qualified engagement, or annual sponsor packages of $250,000-2M (founder's assumptions, to be tested).
- Sponsors buy quarterly or annually. No long-dated obligations on either side.

## Founder's assumptions
- About 300,000 US advisers. First target: independent registered investment advisers, who choose their own tools. Goal: 50,000 verified weekly users within 12-18 months.
- Asset managers spend heavily to reach advisers: wholesaler teams, platform fees, and placement fees of 1-3.5% on private funds sold to wealthy individuals.
- $100M of revenue needs about 100 sponsors at $1M, or fewer sponsors plus placement fees.
- Cost: model inference, data licences, a compliance team, and sponsor sales. Gross margin above 70% at scale.

## Why it could exist now and not five years ago
Models can now read fund documents and filings accurately enough for professional use. Alternative asset managers are pushing into the wealth channel and need to educate advisers at scale. Cheaper inference makes a free product affordable.

## What would accumulate
Verified adviser audience, first-party data on what advisers ask, licensed content, compliance integrations. Sponsor value rises with adviser numbers.

## Growth plan
Advisers in the US, then insurance agents and retirement-plan advisers, then other countries, then the main distribution platform for funds sold through advisers.

## Known weak points the founder wants tested
- Will broker-dealer and adviser compliance departments allow the tool? Do sponsored placements beside advice breach fiduciary, best-interest or marketing rules?
- Why would advisers use this instead of a general chatbot, Morningstar, or the tools from Schwab, Fidelity, LPL, Orion and Envestnet?
- Do asset managers have a budget line for this, at these prices, and can results be measured?
- Will data owners license to it?
- Is an adviser's attention worth as much as a physician's?
- Does this top out as a $1-3bn media business?
