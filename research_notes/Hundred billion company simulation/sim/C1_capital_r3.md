### 0.2 Purchase price today
- Data-center-grade fleet (2.5 MW class gensets, some compute contracts): $1.7-2.1M per nameplate MW, 7.5-9x EBITDA. Basis: the February 2026 print plus nine months of rising public multiples. [calc, dk]
- Wellsite fleet of 0.1-0.6 MW units: $1.0-1.3M per MW, 4.5-5.5x. Mostly not usable at a 25-200 MW compute site. [dk]
- The founder's budget of $100-300M buys 50-150 MW, which is 40-110 firm MW after spare units.

### 0.3 The repricing premise is wrong
"Two to three times the oilfield rate" does not survive the deal data. [calc]
- Oilfield and industrial rental already produces about $240,000 of EBITDA per nameplate MW-year. At a 58% margin that is about $34,000 of revenue per nameplate MW-month.
- A compute contract at $47,500 per firm MW, with 25% of units held as spares, is about $35,600 per nameplate MW-month.
- Compute also needs $0.4-0.6M per firm MW of battery, switchgear, emissions controls and site works.
- One provider says its oil and gas contracts pay back nearer four years against four to six overall [notes].
- So moving a unit to compute changes contract length and the valuation multiple. It does not change cash earned per unit.

## 1. Acquisition and project lender

**Decision: yes to the acquisition debt, with a sponsor.** This is the first unconditional "would lend" in three rounds, because a generator rental company is a known credit.
- Asset-based revolver at 75-85% of appraised orderly liquidation value of the fleet, SOFR+225-325.
- Term loan to total leverage of 3.0-3.5x EBITDA, SOFR+550-650. Blended cost about 8.5-9.5%. [dk]
- At a 7.5x purchase that is 40-47% of price, which matches the founder's ask. At 9x it is 33-39%.
- Covenants: borrowing base tested monthly; springing fixed-charge cover; growth capital only against signed contracts; change-of-control consent; key-person clause on the acquired operating managers, not on the founder.
- Growth units: delayed-draw loan against signed compute contracts. SOFR+250-300 for investment-grade tenants. Sub-investment-grade contracts get borrowing-base credit only.

**Reservations.** Appraised value is at a cyclical high. Engine hours on a used fleet reduce it. A conversion from portable oilfield permits to stationary-source permits at a compute site is new work [dk].

**What would change the decision.** No sponsor: no loan.

## 2. Infrastructure fund (core-plus)

**Decision: refuses the acquisition.** Short-contract oilfield rental is not infrastructure. Would buy investment-grade-contracted growth blocks of ten years or more at 10-12%, which at $40,000 the blocks do not earn (7.6% unlevered).

## 3. Private equity / value-add infrastructure sponsor asked to fund the purchase and back the founder as chief executive

**Who.** Partner at a $3-8B fund. Targets 20% gross IRR and 2.5x. Equity cheque $60-165M fits.

**Alternatives.** Its own portfolio platform. Sponsors already own such platforms [source: Firefly and Fortress above] [notes: Blackstone in VoltaGrid]. Or back the target's own management.

**Decision: refuses to back the founder as chief executive. Would fund the transaction on these terms:**
- The founder brings a signed exclusive letter of intent at or below 6.5x EBITDA. In a bank-run auction the fund loses to public buyers and does not bid.
- The target's operating head, or an executive from the fund's network, runs the company. The founder is chief commercial officer or executive chair.
- Founder economics: co-investment of the founder's remaining cash plus incentive equity vesting at 2.0x and 2.5x. Total 5-8% at exit, 3-5% after growth equity.
- The fund controls the board, the exit and the timing. Hold period four to six years.

**Reasons.**
- At today's $1.8M per MW the model shows 10-16% levered, below hurdle.
- Public acquirers trade at 11-12x forward and pay partly in stock, so 7-9x purchases add to their earnings. They outbid.
- A first-time chief executive with no fleet operating record does not pass the lender's key-person test.

**What would change the decision.** A proprietary target under exclusivity; two signed compute contracts conditional on closing; a named operating partner.

## 4. Venture investor

**Decision: refuses, and agrees with the founder that this is not venture.** Would not fund the search. Each failed deal costs $1-3M in diligence [dk]; the founder's $5-10M covers two or three attempts.

## 5. Incumbents

- **Public oilfield-power companies.** Bid for the same targets with stock valued at 11-12x. Three of the four known targets went to them. Response to this entrant: outbid, or hire its target's crews.
- **Large behind-the-meter providers.** Buy suppliers and fleets to secure equipment; one agreed to buy a key equipment supplier [notes]. Fund at 7.4% [round two].
- **Engine makers and dealers.** Allocation is a dealer relationship, not an assignable contract [dk]. Purchase orders in hand transfer with consent; future priority does not. Dealers now take compute work themselves [source: rermag above].
- **Rental majors.** Buy regional fleets routinely at 6-8x with investment-grade debt [dk]. Would price a 50-150 MW fleet above a 20%-return buyer.
- **Would the owners sell?** Owners of data-center-grade fleets can contract with compute sites themselves or sell to a public buyer for stock that may rise. The ones left for a new buyer are wellsite fleets.

## 6. Competing start-up

A sponsor-backed vehicle closed this exact play in August 2026 [source: Firefly above]. It has the fleet, the crews and the capital now. Response: buys the next target first; hires enterprise salespeople, which is the one capability the founder claims and the cheapest to copy.

## New objections
1. No repricing gain. Rental already earns what compute pays per unit (section 0.3).
2. Entry price has moved. 4.3x in January 2025, 7.4x in February 2026, 7.5-9x now.
3. Auctions are won by public buyers paying in stock.
4. Suitable targets are largely gone; four or more have sold since 2024.
5. The sponsor will not make the founder chief executive, and founder economics are 3-8%.
6. The founder's cash is exposed to broken-deal costs.
7. Dealer allocation does not transfer by contract, and dealers compete.
8. The gain is a one-time change in valuation multiple, captured at exit by whoever controls the sale.

## Ceiling and compounding
- Realistic outcome if a deal closes: 300-600 MW, $150-350M of revenue, sold in four to six years to a public consolidator for $1-2.5B of enterprise value. Founder proceeds $20-80M.
- Upper bound: about 2 GW, $1B of revenue, $6B enterprise value by year seven. That is the Solaris path, which began with a listed company's cash flow and stock.
- $100B: no.
- Compounding: none. Scale economies in spares, crews and borrowing cost, shared by larger owners. A leveraged asset pool with one re-rating.

## Probabilities
- An acquisition closes within 12 months: 15%.
- First paid compute delivery within 18 months: 12%. Mostly the case where the acquired fleet already serves a compute site.
- $1B of annual revenue within 7 years: 2%.
