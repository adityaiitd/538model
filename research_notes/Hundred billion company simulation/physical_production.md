Research date: 24 November 2026. ## 1. Critical minerals and materials: export controls, government deals, ex-China vs China prices

### Takeaway
The clearest "cannot buy enough" evidence is in heavy rare earths, tungsten and germanium, where ex-China prices are 3x to 16x Chinese domestic prices in 2026, while light rare earths (NdPr) are oversupplied outside China and trade below the $110/kg US floor. Washington is now an equity holder, lender and price guarantor across the chain (MP, USA Rare Earth, Vulcan, Lithium Americas, Wolfspeed), but China's one-year suspension of its US-specific ban on gallium, germanium and antimony expires on 27 November 2026 with no extension found.

### Inferences
- The exploitable gap is in conversion and separation, not mining: tungsten APT conversion, heavy rare earth (Dy/Tb) separation and magnet-scrap recycling carry premiums of 3.7x to 16x, while NdPr, lithium and graphite carry no durable premium. The implied China Dy price is about $215/kg vs $3,500/kg in Europe (my derivation from Benchmark's ratio).
- Light rare earth and magnet capacity is being crowded by subsidised giants (MP, USAR, Vulcan each target 7,000-10,000 t/yr). A small entrant competes with federal balance sheets there.
- Premiums are policy-made. Buyers pay them only where law forces it (DFARS from 1 Jan 2027) or supply is physically unavailable. The graphite ITC ruling shows trade protection can vanish.
- Revenue at new magnet makers is still tiny against capital committed: USAR $1.6M revenue vs $3.1B raised/committed; MP magnetics $18.6M a quarter.

## 2. Defense production: budgets, munitions, solid rocket motors, drones, shipbuilding, energetics, new entrants

### Takeaway
Money is not the constraint: the FY2027 request is $1.5T with $31.8B to expand munitions production and $65.8B for shipbuilding, and Germany's 2027 defence budget is €139.6B. The constraint is sub-tier capacity (energetics, ammonium perchlorate, nozzles, cases, skilled shipyard labor), and new entrants that reached scale did so on a handful of very large government vehicles after raising billions.

### Cited Findings

**Budgets**
- FY2027 US request: $1.5T, of which about $1.1-1.15T base discretionary and $350B dependent on a reconciliation bill; up about 42-44% on FY2026 — [Axios](https://www.axios.com/2026/04/08/pentagon-budget-request-2027-trump); [GT Law](https://gtlaw.com/en/insights/2026/5/understanding-the-presidents-fy-2027-budget-request-for-the-department-of-war)
- $31.8B to expand munitions production (12 critical munitions, more multiyear contracts); base procurement just under $260B — [Comptroller FY2027 request](https://comptroller.war.gov/Portals/45/Documents/defbudget/FY2027/FY2027_Budget_Request.pdf); [Military Times](https://www.militarytimes.com/news/pentagon-congress/2026/04/21/pentagon-seeks-funds-for-golden-dome-drones-ai-in-largest-ever-budget-request/)
- $65.8B shipbuilding for 18 battle force and 16 other ships, plus $8.7B for shipyard infrastructure — [TWZ](https://www.twz.com/air/here-is-what-trumps-gargantuan-1-5t-defense-budget-has-in-it); [MarineLink](https://www.marinelink.com/news/us-unveils-details-trillion-defense-538293)

### Inferences
- FY2027 implied MACE unit price is about $442K per missile ($156M / 353, my arithmetic), an order of magnitude below legacy hypersonic programmes. That is the pitch that won Castelion production money about four years after founding.
- Time to $100M: Saronic took about three years from founding (Sacra estimate). Anduril took about seven years to $1B. Neros's airframe run-rate is roughly $125M a year at 1,200 a week and $2,000 each (my arithmetic, not reported revenue). All raised $250M to $5B first.
- For a $5-50M entrant, the open seats are sub-tier: nozzles, insulators, cases, igniters, binders, energetic fills, drone payloads. Primes and well-funded entrants all hit the same single-source suppliers. The Helicon example shows the risk: government money promised, then stalled.
- European demand is real but slow: Helsing's HX-2 was funded in February and not yet qualified in late October.

## 3. Robotics: deployed units, revenue, pricing, components, foundation models

### Takeaway
Industrial robots are a large, real market (603,000 installed in 2025; 5 million in operation) and Chinese humanoid makers ship thousands of units with real revenue and margins, but mostly into entertainment, research and data collection. Western humanoid revenue is negligible (Agility: $1.78M in 2025), and robot foundation model companies are valued at $14-20B on little or no revenue.

## 4. Contract manufacturing: machining, PCB assembly, castings and forgings, packaging

### Takeaway
Castings and forgings are the best-documented chokepoint, and the incumbent earns software-like margins (Howmet segment EBITDA margin 31.8%) while adding capacity slowly. Hadrian shows investors will fund AI-native machining at huge scale ($1.37B at $7.87B), but its revenue is undisclosed and the model is capital-heavy.

## 5. Construction and skilled trades

### Takeaway
Electricians and mechanical trades are the binding constraint on data center delivery, and moving that work into factories is already standard: prefab cuts licensed-electrician hours by about 85% and is worth roughly $500K per MW per month of schedule gained. The space is crowded (80+ vendors) and upstream gear (transformers, switchgear) remains the harder limit.

### Cited Findings
- ABC: the industry needs about 349,000 net new workers in 2026, the lowest gap since 2021, with over half replacing retirees; about 20% of the workforce is over 55. Nominal construction spending fell about 1.5% over the year. Electrician and mechanical shortages are a "binding constraint" for data centers and megaprojects. Contractors above $100M revenue report the highest backlog since 2021; those under $30M the lowest — [ENR, 15 Jan 2026](https://www.enr.com/articles/62348-constructions-labor-relief-masks-structural-risk-as-demand-cools)
- Shortage of about 439,000 workers as of Nov 2025; data center jobs pay up to 30% more; over 400 data centers under development; contractor backlogs close to a year — [ITIF citing WSJ](https://itif.org/publications/2026/01/12/construction-industry-facing-worker-shortage-driven-by-growth-of-data-centers/)
- Data center capex: about $13.5M/MW modular vs $14.6M/MW stick-built for a 50 MW liquid-cooled hall. Electricians are 30-40% of man-hours. Factory MEP cuts on-site hours about 63% (to about 4,500 hours/MW) and licensed-electrician hours about 85%. Modular builds take 12-18 months, about 36% faster. One month earlier is worth about $500K/MW to an owner-operator. Vertiv content rises from about $3.5M/MW to about $7M/MW with full-stack modular; its modular lead times exceed 12 months. Over 61 GW of modular capacity tracked across 1,000+ sites; 80+ vendors. Field commissioning (3-8 months) is the largest remaining gap. Crusoe builds about 1 MW units in its own Colorado factory after buying Easter-Owens in 2022 — [SemiAnalysis, 29 July 2026](https://newsletter.semianalysis.com/p/the-wild-wild-west-of-lego-datacenters)
- Vertiv ended fiscal 2025 with a $15B backlog, more than double the prior year — [Motley Fool](https://www.fool.com/investing/2026/03/09/this-ai-stock-has-an-15-billion-revenue-backlog-th/)
- Main transformer lead times of 80-150 weeks vs 12-20 historically; medium-voltage switchgear over 80 weeks (vendor whitepaper citing Bank of America) — [Jabil](https://www.jabil.com/dam/jcr:2e35eb04-6b07-43ce-8a33-dd4c88d856f2/Jabil_Grid%20Constrants%205%20Ways_WP_DIGITAL_05%20(1).pdf)
- Katerra (founded 2015) raised over $2B from SoftBank's Vision Fund, had 7,500 employees in 2018 and filed for bankruptcy in June 2021 — [Wikipedia, citing FT and Forbes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Katerra)

### Inferences
- The prefab premium is paid for speed. At about $500K/MW per month, a 100 MW campus gains about $50M per month saved (my arithmetic from the SemiAnalysis figure), which dwarfs any hardware markup.
- The under-served piece is commissioning and integration (3-8 months), and skids for sites that incumbents with 12-month-plus lead times cannot serve.
- Katerra failed as a vertically integrated general builder for a fragmented housing market. Data center prefab differs: a few very large repeat buyers, standardised reference designs, buyers who value time over cost.
- Labor shortage is real but cyclical outside megaprojects: ABC's gap is at a five-year low and small contractors' backlogs are weak.

## 6. Semiconductors beyond GPUs

### Takeaway
Shortages are in advanced packaging (CoWoS gap about 20%), ABF substrates and above all indium phosphide optical components, where Lumentum is reportedly sold out to 2029. These are capital-heavy, qualification-bound markets where Nvidia is pre-funding incumbents with $2B cheques; silicon carbide is a cautionary tale (Wolfspeed bankruptcy).

### Cited Findings
- CoWoS: TSMC capacity could reach 120,000-140,000 wafers a month in 2026, plus 50,000-60,000 from OSATs; the supply gap of about 20% is expected to narrow to about 10% by end-2026; TSMC plans over 60% more capacity by 2027; panel-level CoPoS pilot targeted mid-2027 — [TrendForce, 15 June 2026](https://www.trendforce.com/news/2026/06/15/news-tsmc-cowos-supply-demand-gap-reportedly-seen-narrowing-from-20-to-10-by-end-2026-as-capacity-expands)
- CoWoS assembly lead times of 52-78 weeks; ABF substrate shortfall forecast about 10% in late 2026 and about 21% in 2027, lead times 16-24 weeks — [Zaikei, 29 July 2026](https://zaikei.co.jp/article/20260729/863348.html)
- HBM reported sold out through 2026 (distributor blog, low reliability) — [Fusion Worldwide](https://info.fusionww.com/blog/inside-the-ai-bottleneck-cowos-hbm-and-2-3nm-capacity-constraints-through-2027)
- InP optics: Lumentum's CEO says the InP gap now exceeds DRAM and NAND and the company ships more than 30% below customer demand; Nvidia committed $2B each to Lumentum and Coherent with long-term supply agreements (Mar 2026); imbalance expected beyond 2027 — [Tom's Hardware](https://www.tomshardware.com/tech-industry/semiconductors/lumentum-ceo-says-the-indium-phosphide-shortage-will-become-worse-than-memory); [TrendForce](https://trendforce.com/news/2026/08/06/news-inp-shortage-emerges-as-ai-optical-interconnect-bottleneck/)

## 8. Physical-production startups founded 2017-2025 that reached $100M+ revenue quickly

### Takeaway
The confirmed fast scalers sold finished systems to one very large buyer (the US military or Chinese research and entertainment buyers), not components, and all raised hundreds of millions to billions first. ### Cited Findings

- Castelion: low-cost hypersonic missiles to the US Navy; about $470M of 2026 awards and ceilings; revenue undisclosed — [Defense News](https://www.defensenews.com/news/your-navy/2026/09/17/us-navy-awards-defense-startup-200-million-for-blackbeard-hypersonic-missile/)
- Hadrian (2020): precision machined parts to aerospace and defense primes; valued at $7.87B; revenue undisclosed — [BNN Bloomberg](https://www.bnnbloomberg.ca/business/company-news/2026/08/06/manufacturing-company-hadrian-valued-at-us787-billion-in-latest-funding-round/)

- Unitree (older than 2017 per general knowledge; included as benchmark): $247M 2025 revenue with about RMB 288M net profit — [KrASIA](https://kr-asia.com/unitree-targets-ipo-after-revenue-quadruples-on-robotics-demand)

- Counter-examples (capital without revenue): Agility $1.78M 2025 sales on a $140M operating loss ([Fortune](https://fortune.com/2026/10/16/when-will-physical-ai-robots-pay-off/)); USA Rare Earth $1.6M ([release](https://usare.gcs-web.com/news-releases/news-release-details/usa-rare-earth-reports-fourth-quarter-and-full-year-2025)); Physical Intelligence zero revenue at about $11.5B ([Sacra](https://sacra.com/c/physical-intelligence/)); Divergent about $72M after a decade ([StockAnalysis](https://stockanalysis.com/private/divergent-technologies/))

### Inferences
- Pattern: (a) one buyer with a multi-billion budget and a new contract vehicle (OTA, IDIQ, Drone Dominance); (b) a finished, cheap, attritable product; (c) production capacity built ahead of orders with venture money. Speed to $100M tracks access to capital and a programme of record, not technology novelty.
- Five most plausible entry points for $5-50M, with main failure reason:
  1. Tungsten and heavy rare earth conversion or recycling (APT from scrap; Dy/Tb from magnet scrap). Premiums are 3.7x and 16x and DFARS rules bite 1 Jan 2027. Fails if China reopens exports and the premium collapses before the plant is qualified.
  2. Sub-tier munitions parts (SRM nozzles, insulation, cases, igniters, binders, energetic fills). Lead times are 7-12 months and primes fund suppliers. Fails on 18-24 month qualification and stalled government money (Helicon).
  3. Prefab electrical and mechanical skids plus commissioning for data centers. Saves about 85% of electrician hours; a month is worth about $500K/MW. Fails because 80+ vendors compete and transformer or switchgear allocation and AI capex cycles are outside its control.
  4. Components and payloads for attritable drones (NDAA-compliant motors, batteries, warheads). 60,000 drones funded and a 1M-a-year Army goal. Fails because $2,000 airframes force commodity margins and winners integrate vertically; no minimum quantities are guaranteed.
  5. Narrow-task robot labor sold by the hour or by output (palletising, machine tending, welding) on off-the-shelf arms and third-party models. Fails if utilisation stays under about 2,700 hours a year or each site needs custom engineering, making it a low-margin integration business.
