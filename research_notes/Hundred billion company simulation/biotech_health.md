## 1. Drug manufacturing capacity: GLP-1 supply, oral launches, compounding, CDMOs, BIOSECURE, tariffs, US investment

### Takeaway
GLP-1 API scarcity has largely ended; the binding constraints in late 2026 are fill-finish and injection devices, while oral small-molecule and oral peptide GLP-1s moved the market to $149-349/month cash prices and gutted the legal basis for mass compounding. CDMO demand is strong (Samsung, Lonza, WuXi all growing double digits) but BIOSECURE has not yet moved volume away from WuXi, and the one well-funded US CDMO entrant (Resilience) shrank by 60% of its sites, so capacity-building is a balance-sheet game, not a $5-50M game.

How a recent US CDMO entrant fared
- National Resilience (founded 2020, ~$2.25B raised) announced in June 2025 it would close 6 of its 10 plants (Alachua, Fremont, San Diego, Allston, Bedford, Marlborough); an affiliate filed bankruptcy to shed leases; CEO said capacity expansion "outpaced industry demand"; $250M bridge financing from existing investors; later $825M to convert Cincinnati and Toronto to biologics and aseptic fill (2025 data) — [Fierce Pharma](https://www.fiercepharma.com/manufacturing/national-resilience-will-close-6-plants-and-continue-streamlined-cdmo); [BioPharma Dive](https://www.biopharmadive.com/news/national-resilience-manufacturing-operations-wind-down/750246/); [Pharma Manufacturing](https://www.pharmamanufacturing.com/industry-news/news/55326975/national-resilience-expands-biomanufacturing-operations-with-825m-investment)

### Inferences
- The GLP-1 "shortage trade" is over. Volume is up 41-53% while net price falls 7-15% a year, list prices halve in 2027, and the fastest-growing product (orforglipron) is a conventional small molecule. Peptide API capacity bought on 2023-24 scarcity assumptions faces overcapacity risk; fill-finish and pen/autoinjector components are the remaining pinch points but are served by scale players (Novo-Catalent, Thermo, Stevanato).
- BIOSECURE's effect so far is option-buying (dual sourcing, mapping, tech-transfer planning), not volume migration: WuXi grew 38% and came off the 1260H list, and prohibitions cannot bite before 2028. The saleable thing today is help with switching (2-8 year transfers), not new steel.
- Tariffs make US-made patented product worth up to 100 points of tariff avoidance, so big pharma is building its own plants. That absorbs engineering, validation and skilled labor, where small asset-light suppliers could sell, but the Resilience outcome shows merchant capacity without anchor contracts is fatal even at $2B+.

## 2. Clinical trials: cost, duration, recruitment, CRO financials, AI-native entrants, FDA policy 2025-2026

Large CROs hold $60B+ of backlog and are booking strongly again (book-to-bill 1.2-1.56x), yet ICON's revenue is shrinking and IQVIA wrote $2.1B of dead trials out of backlog, which shows how slow trial execution is. ### Inferences
- Sponsors are spending: three CROs booked ~$7.2B net in one quarter. But margins differ sharply (ICON 16.6%, Medpace 24%), which implies execution quality, not price, decides share. A new entrant must prove faster enrollment on real trials, which takes 2-3 years of reference work.
- US policy is trying to pull Phase 1 work back from China (17+ months vs 7). Fast US Phase 1 units and IND-enabling packages are a policy-backed gap, but they are site and regulatory operations, not software.
- FDA speed programs depend on personnel; the voucher program stalled within six months of its sponsor leaving. Do not build on a single FDA pilot.

## 3. AI drug discovery and autonomous labs: what is demonstrated, what customers pay for

### Takeaway
By Nov 2026 no AI-designed drug has a positive Phase 3; the most advanced (Insilico's rentosertib) only began Phase 3 in mid-2026 and Isomorphic has not dosed a patient. Pharma pays tens of millions upfront (typically 2-6% of headline deal value) and capital markets pay billions for platforms, while the public companies that sell lab automation or AI discovery as a service (Ginkgo, Recursion) earn $20-30M a quarter.

### Inferences
- The common pattern: (1) the payer is a consumer or a pharma marketing budget, not a hospital or insurer; (2) fulfilment is rented from incumbents (Quest, OpenLoop, compounding pharmacies, foundation models); (3) the founder's edge is distribution; (4) no FDA approval was needed for the product itself.
- Enterprise health AI gets to $100M too, but in five to seven years and usually with $500M+ raised.
- No company in the sample got there by selling to payers, by manufacturing, or by drug discovery.
