## 1. Frontier lab financials and behavior (late 2026)

### Takeaway
OpenAI and Anthropic together run at well over $100B annualized, Anthropic's S-1 shows recognized H1 2026 revenue of $16.3B at a reported ~85% gross margin, and both labs have moved directly into coding agents, deployment services, legal/knowledge-work plugins and advertising. Application companies kept growing after lab entry, but the ones whose cost of goods is a lab's model have thin or barely positive gross margins and several have sold to larger platforms.

### Inferences
- Lab gross margin (~85% reported) versus application-layer gross margin (0–50% in coding) implies the model provider captures most of the gross profit in token-heavy applications.
- Labs have chosen to own deployment labor themselves (two JVs with $5.5B of capital) and to own service firms (Thrive Holdings stake). A services start-up should assume the lab is a direct competitor for mid-market deployment work, not only a supplier.
- Growth of Harvey, Cursor and Cognition after lab entry shows entry does not stop revenue growth; the pressure shows up in margin and in exits to larger balance sheets (SpaceX, Salesforce).
- With Anthropic's top-five customers at 21% of Q3 revenue, no single application company is a large enough buyer to have pricing power over the lab.

### Gaps
- Exact OpenAI and Anthropic revenue split between API, consumer subscription and enterprise agents: not disclosed in any source reached. The S-1 summaries reached give no API vs subscription split.
- November 2026 OpenAI run-rate: no reliable figure found.
- Lab entry into devices, payments, healthcare, security and finance: known launches were not re-verified with URLs in this session (search budget exhausted), so they are omitted.
- Google Gemini API revenue is not broken out from Google Cloud.

### Inferences
- "ARR" at coding companies is usage-heavy monthly revenue times 12, bought by customers who re-evaluate vendors every six months. It should be discounted relative to contracted SaaS ARR.
- Cognition at ~50% gross margin burning ~$800M on ~$900M ARR, and Cursor near zero gross margin at $4B, indicate that scale alone has not fixed unit economics; owning a model (Cursor's Composer) is the lever that moved margin.
- Per-seat pricing (Harvey, Glean) has so far produced steadier growth and higher valuations per dollar than usage pricing, but it is the model the February 2026 selloff priced as most exposed.

## 3. Outcome-priced AI services and AI-native roll-ups

### Takeaway
Per-resolution pricing in customer support has converged near $1–$2 and is now offered by incumbents, so outcome pricing there is a commodity price point, not a moat. Roll-ups have raised large sums (Thrive Holdings $2B at $12B; Long Lake 40 acquisitions plus the Amex GBT take-private) but have published only operating anecdotes, not audited margin gains.

### Cited Findings

**Outcome pricing**
- Fin: $0.99 per resolved conversation, cut from $1.90 at launch [R] — [SaaStr](https://www.saastr.com/hubspot-switching-ai-pricing-from-per-use-to-per-resolution/)
- Sierra: about $1.50 per resolution, negotiated per customer, about 10% of human-agent cost, resolving ~80% of common questions [E] — [Sacra](https://sacra.com/research/sierra-vs-decagon/)
- Zendesk bills only "Verified Resolutions" after a May 2026 restructure, roughly $1.20–$1.50 committed and ~$2.00 pay-as-you-go; Salesforce Agentforce about $2.00 per conversation or ~$0.10 per action [W, vendor comparison page] — [Intercom](https://www.intercom.com/learning-center/ai-customer-service-agent-pricing-comparison)
- HubSpot moved its AI pricing from per-use to per-resolution [R] — [SaaStr](https://www.saastr.com/hubspot-switching-ai-pricing-from-per-use-to-per-resolution/)
- Buyer preference: outcome-based pricing is the model enterprise buyers most prefer and least often encounter; nearly half mostly encounter usage-based pricing [P survey, n=150, Aug 2026] — [Madrona](https://www.madrona.com/harnessing-enterprise-value-roi-of-ai/)
- Legal: AI-native firms Crosby and Norm Law are reported to bill on outcomes instead of hours [W] — [ibl.ai](https://ibl.ai/blog/ai-native-law-firm-funding-2026-judgment-not-speed)
- Incumbent price pass-through: Accenture said pricing was lower in many areas in its fiscal Q4 2026 as clients push for a share of AI savings; TCS says 10–15% AI productivity can be committed to clients from day one [R] — [The Star/Reuters](https://www.thestar.com.my/tech/tech-news/2026/10/02/accenture039s-forecast-eases-ai-disruption-fears-lifts-battered-it-services-stocks); [UpperEdge](https://upperedge.com/managed-services/q2-2026-msp-earnings-growth-holds-but-bookings-and-margins-show-where-ai-pressure-is-landing/)
- Gartner predicts over 40% of agentic AI projects will be canceled by end of 2027 (prediction, not measurement) [W] — [Drag](https://www.dragapp.com/blog/salesforce-buys-fin/)

**Roll-ups and AI-native service firms**
- Thrive Holdings raised $2B at a $12B valuation (Aug 2026; SoftBank, D1, Altimeter); over 70 businesses. Accounting platform: 50+ firms, 2,000+ professionals, TaxAI agents processed 7,000+ returns at 98% accuracy and cut prep time over 30% (company claims). IT platform Shield: ~20 companies, claims 36x faster help-desk resolution. Third platform planned in regulatory services for the built environment [R] — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/08/12/openai-backed-thrive-holdings-raises-2b-to-bring-ai-to-the-enterprise/)
- Crete Professionals Alliance (Thrive, accounting): described as $300M+ annual revenue with plans to spend $500M on acquisitions (2025-dated secondary) [W] — [PE Insights](https://pe-insights.com/thrive-and-general-catalyst-embrace-pe-style-roll-ups-to-build-ai-native-platforms/)
- Long Lake (General Catalyst, first investment Feb 2024): 40 acquisitions in about 2.5 years; closed the take-private of Amex GBT; claims include HOA managers saving 65+ hours a month, payroll capacity up 30%+, and net job growth across the portfolio. No revenue, EBITDA or margin disclosed [P, promotional] — [General Catalyst](https://www.generalcatalyst.com/stories/manufacturing-outliers-long-lake)
- A third-party newsletter says Long Lake reached $100M EBITDA in under two years [W, unverified] — [Capital and Clarity](https://capitalandclarity.substack.com/p/the-general-catalyst-behind-15-billion)
- Roughly half a dozen top venture firms backed roll-ups by mid-2025, targeting healthcare, accounting, insurance, customer service and manufacturing (June 2025) [R] — [Newcomer](https://www.newcomer.co/p/ai-roll-up-deals-accelerate-as-top)
- Smaller entrants: Numica, an AI-first accounting roll-up, raised about $21.5M to buy firms with under $5M of billings [R] — [CPA Practice Advisor](https://www.cpapracticeadvisor.com/?p=193246)

### Inferences
- At ~$1 per resolution against a US customer-service wage pool of about $121B (section 4), fully automated support converts a large labor pool into a much smaller revenue pool. The vendor revenue opportunity is a fraction of the labor displaced.
- Roll-ups report productivity anecdotes (30% faster tax prep, 30% more payroll capacity), not margin or organic-growth figures. The published evidence is consistent with a conventional private-equity roll-up plus modest productivity, and does not yet show software-like margins.
- Labs are aligned with the best-capitalized roll-ups (OpenAI–Thrive), so an independent roll-up competes for targets against buyers with cheaper capital and privileged model access.

### Gaps
- No source found on liability terms (indemnities, error and omissions cover, service-level penalties) in outcome-priced AI contracts.
- No outcome-priced examples with numbers found for claims, audits, filings or collections.
- Titan MSP, insurance-brokerage roll-ups and other GC "creation" companies: no data reached.
- No audited revenue or margin for Long Lake, Crete, Current or Shield.

## 7. Regulated domains where a license is the moat

### Takeaway
Non-lawyer ownership of law firms is legal in Arizona and several AI-native firms operate there, but investor interest has cooled, other states are passing fee-sharing bans, and capital is moving to management-services structures. In accounting, outside capital owns the non-attest side while audit stays in a licensed CPA entity. No AI-native licensed firm with disclosed revenue at scale was found in any regulated domain.

### Cited Findings

**Law**
