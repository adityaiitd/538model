## A1. Which data center or AI sites run, or plan to run, on trucked CNG or LNG, or keep LNG on site as a second fuel supply? Named deals 2025-2026

### Takeaway
Trucked gas for data center power is real and contracted, but small and concentrated: I found seven-plus Certarus contracts (largest 135 MW, more than $300M over 2.5 years), one Stabilis LNG contract (about $200M over two years), and Oracle sites in Utah and Texas served by Certarus and VoltaGrid. Every named deal is a bridge until a pipeline arrives; I found no named site that keeps LNG on site as a permanent second fuel.

## A2. How long do gas laterals and interconnects take for 25-500 MW on-site plants, how many projects are waiting on gas, and where was a pipeline blocked or delayed?

### Takeaway
A lateral takes roughly 6-12 months of approvals plus construction, about 19 months filing-to-service in the one dated 2026 case found, and it can slip by six months or more on a single land-rights refusal. The best-documented delay is Energy Transfer's Green Chile line to Oracle/OpenAI's Project Jupiter (August 2026 slipped to February 2027 at the earliest). I found no count of projects waiting on gas.

### Cited Findings
- [R] Regulatory approvals for a new lateral "can take six to 12 months after preparation of the application", including an extensive environmental report. A lateral wholly within one state and owned by the plant owner escapes FERC; a lateral that receives gas in a different state is FERC-regulated — [Norton Rose Fulbright, 2 Mar 2026](https://www.projectfinance.law/publications/new-gas-lines-for-data-centers)
- [R] Franklin Farms lateral, Louisiana (ETC Tiger, an Energy Transfer unit): 13.2 miles of 36-inch pipe, about 1 Bcf/d, feeding Entergy plants that serve Meta's Hyperion campus. Federal filing July 2026, in-service target February 2028, about 19 months — [SemiAnalysis](https://newsletter.semianalysis.com/p/what-is-so-hard-about-behind-the)
- [R] Green Chile Project (Energy Transfer / Transwestern) to Project Jupiter, Doña Ana County, New Mexico: FERC application January 2026; contractual in-service date 15 Aug 2026; no approved route by then; new in-service date of 1 Feb 2027 filed 14 Aug 2026. Cause: the New Mexico State Land Office twice refused rights-of-way across state trust land. Cost roughly $60M. SemiAnalysis estimates first gas April or May 2027 and full ramp to 2.45 GW by mid-2028. Oracle told FERC in May 2026 that "time is of the essence" and sent its developer a force majeure notice on 24 Sep 2026 — [Pipeline & Gas Journal, 24 Aug 2026](https://pgjonline.com/news/2026/august/filings-show-project-jupiter-natural-gas-pipeline-delayed-until-2027); [SemiAnalysis](https://newsletter.semianalysis.com/p/what-is-so-hard-about-behind-the); [Crypto Briefing, 8 Oct 2026](https://cryptobriefing.com/oracle-trucked-natural-gas-project-jupiter/)
- [R] Trailblazer (Sweetwater, Texas): the developer budgets $120M for a 31-mile gas line extension — [SemiAnalysis](https://newsletter.semianalysis.com/p/what-is-so-hard-about-behind-the)
- [R] Gas contracted is often far below generation permitted: Nexus Hubbard has 150 MMcf/d contracted with Energy Transfer, enough for about 750 MW, against a pending air permit for a 5,230 MW engine fleet — [SemiAnalysis](https://newsletter.semianalysis.com/p/what-is-so-hard-about-behind-the)
- [R] In Texas an intrastate line needs only Railroad Commission approval, though federal water-crossing permits and easements still apply — [SemiAnalysis](https://newsletter.semianalysis.com/p/what-is-so-hard-about-behind-the)
- [C] Hexagon Composites says power and pipeline infrastructure delays "often exceed four years in many US regions"; its engineering head says sites "may need to upgrade a pipeline, if not establish new pipelines of gas supply" — [Nebraska Public Media, 21 Jul 2026](https://nebraskapublicmedia.org/es/news/news-articles/lincoln-company-helping-to-power-data-centers/)
- [R] Kimmeridge estimates as many as half of proposed US data centers are at risk of delay or cancellation (search-snippet level) — [Bloomberg Government](https://news.bgov.com/artificial-intelligence/half-of-planned-us-data-centers-may-face-delays-kimmeridge-says)

### Inferences
- [E] Trailblazer implies about $3.9M per mile ($120M ÷ 31 miles). Green Chile's roughly $60M is small beside the fuel premium for trucking: at the premium derived in A4, a 135 MW site pays more than that per year for trucked gas. Trucking is therefore a schedule purchase, not a cost choice, and ends when the lateral is live.
- The bridge window for a trucked-gas supplier is the lateral's permitting plus construction time: about 12-24 months per site, longer where land rights are contested. Contract terms found (2 to 2.5 years) match that window.
- [E] The Nexus Hubbard gap (750 MW of gas for 5,230 MW of permitted engines) suggests firm gas supply, not just the lateral, limits many announced sites; this is one data point, not a count.

### Gaps
- No source gives the number of announced on-site power projects waiting on a gas connection.
- No FERC prior-notice or blanket-certificate duration data was retrieved.
- No lateral cost or schedule found specifically for 25-100 MW plants; all dated cases are large.

## A4. Arithmetic check: gas burn per MW, trailer capacity, trucks per day for 50 MW, delivered cost per MWh, upper limit of site size

### Takeaway
A 50 MW engine plant at full load burns about 10,600 MMBtu a day, which is about 24 of the largest standard trailers a day, one an hour. Trucked gas at Certarus's average price costs about $130 per MWh for engines and about $160 for simple-cycle turbines, roughly $100 per MWh more than pipeline gas. The largest named trucked site is 135 MW; above roughly 150 MW the truck cadence falls under 20 minutes.

### Cited Findings
- [V] Average tested heat rates for natural gas, 2025: internal combustion 8,853 Btu/kWh; gas turbine 10,975; combined cycle 7,535 — [EIA Electric Power Annual, Table 8.2](https://www.eia.gov/electricity/annual/html/epa_08_02.html)
- [V] Trailer capacity: TITAN 450 448,200 scf; TITAN 510 507,500 scf — [Hexagon Agility one-pager](https://s3.eu-central-1.amazonaws.com/hexagonassets/Hexagon-Agility-Mobile-Pipeline-TITAN-one-pager_2026-04-21-211435_zuvt.pdf)
- [R] SemiAnalysis estimate relayed by TNW: at 100 MW, each large trailer supplies about 40 minutes of electricity — [The Next Web](https://thenextweb.com/news/oracle-trucked-gas-data-centres-europe)
- [R] Largest named trucked-gas site: about 135 MW with about 200 trailers — [Baxtel](https://baxtel.com/news/certarus-lands-135mw-onsite-gas-supply-deal-for-us-data-center)
- [V] Certarus average revenue and cost per MMBtu inputs: see A3 — [Superior Plus Q3 2026 package](https://www.superiorplus.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/SPB-Q3-2026-Earnings-Release-Full-Package-vF.pdf)

### Inferences
All [E]. Assumption without a source: pipeline gas at about 1,000 Btu per scf, so a TITAN 450 carries about 450 MMBtu and a TITAN 510 about 510 MMBtu nominal. Usable volume is lower because trailers are not drained to zero pressure; no source found for the usable share.
- Gas burn per MW at full load: engines 8.85 MMBtu per MWh = 212 MMBtu per MW-day = about 77,600 MMBtu per MW-year. Simple-cycle turbines 10.98 MMBtu per MWh = 263 MMBtu per MW-day = about 96,100 MMBtu per MW-year.
- 50 MW of engines at full load: 443 MMBtu an hour, 10,620 MMBtu a day = about **24 TITAN 450 loads a day** (one an hour) or about 21 TITAN 510 loads. 50 MW of turbines: 13,170 MMBtu a day = about 29 TITAN 450 loads.
- Cross-check: SemiAnalysis's 40 minutes per trailer at 100 MW equals 36 loads a day; my figure for 100 MW of engines is 47 TITAN 450 loads (one every 30 minutes). The published estimate assumes a larger trailer or a better heat rate; treat 36-58 loads a day per 100 MW as the range.
- Delivered fuel cost per MWh at Certarus's blended $14.78 per MMBtu: engines about **$131 per MWh**, turbines about **$162 per MWh**. At its $2.47 commodity cost the same fuel would be $22 and $27 per MWh. Premium for trucking: about $109 per MWh (engines).

## A5. Rules: hazardous-materials transport, on-site storage permits, air permits

### Takeaway
Fuel transport runs under federal hazardous materials rules and, for composite tube trailers, cylinder approvals or DOT special permits; on-site storage falls under NFPA codes enforced by the state or local authority; the binding constraint for the site is usually the air permit for the generators, not the fuel.

### Cited Findings
- [V] Hexagon TITAN cylinder systems carry DOT (US), ABS and TC (Canada) approvals; allowable modes marine and truck — [Hexagon Agility one-pager](https://s3.eu-central-1.amazonaws.com/hexagonassets/Hexagon-Agility-Mobile-Pipeline-TITAN-one-pager_2026-04-21-211435_zuvt.pdf)
- [R] PHMSA issues special permits for CNG transport in tube trailers (for example DOT-SP 8009 for CNG in DOT 3AAX cylinders by motor vehicle, rail and cargo vessel); permits give "no relief" from the Hazardous Materials Regulations other than as stated; holders must meet the security-plan requirement of 49 CFR Part 172 Subpart I where applicable (search-snippet level) — [PHMSA DOT-SP 8009](https://www.phmsa.dot.gov/hazmat/documents/offer/SP8009.pdf/offerserver/SP8009); [PHMSA DOT-SP 21656](https://www.phmsa.dot.gov/hazmat/documents/offer/SP21656.pdf/offerserver/SP21656)
- [R] Texas regulates CNG and LNG handling through the Railroad Commission (16 TAC chapters 13 and 14), referencing NFPA 52 and NFPA 55 for CNG containers and NFPA 59A for LNG (search-snippet level) — [Texas Administrative Code §13.68](https://regulations.justia.com/states/texas/title-16/part-1/chapter-13/subchapter-c/section-13-68); [Railroad Commission CNG/LNG regulations](https://rrc.texas.gov/media/brzfosix/cng-lng-regulations.pdf)
- [R] On-site LNG at data centers: NFPA 59A governs siting and fire protection for LNG storage and is typically enforced at state level; NFPA 850 (fire protection for generating plants) became a standard with the 2026 edition; performance-based fire modeling is used in place of fixed distances — [Data Center Knowledge, 24 Jun 2026](https://www.datacenterknowledge.com/energy-power-supply/powering-behind-the-meter-power-where-lng-and-process-safety-meet-digital-resilience)
- [R] Air permits: major-source review (PSD) triggers at 250 tons a year of a pollutant (100 for listed industries); Title V at 100 tons. Nonattainment thresholds are lower: Dallas and Houston 25 tons a year; Chicago, Salt Lake City and Las Vegas 50; Northern Virginia and Phoenix 100. At an illustrative 0.10 g/kWh of NOx, a plant reaches the 250-ton line at about 259 MW running all year. Engines that stay on site under 12 months can qualify for the nonroad exemption; Mississippi used it for xAI's trailer-mounted Southaven turbines and the decision is being challenged in federal court. A January 2026 federal rule sets a 5 ppm NOx floor for new large base-load turbines — [SemiAnalysis](https://newsletter.semianalysis.com/p/what-is-so-hard-about-behind-the)
- [R] Permit timing examples: Texas authorized a proposed 560 MW Bloom fuel-cell plant under a standard permit in under a month (19 Dec 2025 to 14 Jan 2026) for a $900 fee. Project Jupiter's New Mexico air applications drew about 7,155 comments, were withdrawn and refiled, and were stayed by the state Supreme Court on 23 Aug 2026 — [SemiAnalysis](https://newsletter.semianalysis.com/p/what-is-so-hard-about-behind-the)

### Inferences
- The fuel hauler's own regulatory load is ordinary for the trade: hazmat-endorsed drivers, DOT-approved cylinders, a security plan, state licensing in states such as Texas. I found nothing in the rules that caps truck movements per site.
- [E] A TITAN 510 module alone weighs 58,200 lb gross; with chassis and tractor the combination is likely close to or over the 80,000 lb federal gross limit, so routing and overweight permits may matter. I have no source for the combined weight.
- The customer's air permit determines whether there is a plant to fuel at all, and nonattainment areas (Salt Lake City at 50 tons) are where bridge power is most demanded and hardest to permit.

### Gaps

## B1. Late-2026 lead times and prices for transformers, switchgear, breakers, generators and static transfer switches, and the premium for immediate delivery

### Takeaway
The shortage has split. Standard distribution and padmount units are back to roughly 20-65 weeks (from over 100 in 2023), while large power transformers (128 weeks or more), generator step-up units (144-160 weeks or more), data-center medium-voltage switchgear (52-104 weeks) and high-voltage breakers (about 125 weeks) remain years out. ### Inferences
- The published lead times conflict for the same class (padmounts at 20-30 weeks, 40-65 weeks and 60-80 weeks) and every source is an interested party. The direction is clear: distribution-class waits have fallen by more than half since 2023.

## B3. Do makers sell forward production slots to a merchant, on what deposit, and can slots be resold?

### Takeaway
Makers do reserve production slots years ahead, usually by letter of intent with price left open, and developers do buy slots without a named project. I found no evidence of a maker selling slots to a merchant for resale, and no evidence of a resale market for transformer slots.

### Cited Findings
- [R] A slot reservation is the allocation of a specific production period to a customer, typically by letter of intent before a binding contract. Price is not fixed at reservation; amount and terms are set in the main contract two to three years before delivery, to avoid raw-material, currency and wage risk. HD Hyundai Electric has most slots filled through 2030 and LS Electric through 2029; GE Vernova said in March 2026 that transformer slots are "already secured through 2031" — [Asia Economy, 15 Apr 2026](https://view.asiae.co.kr/en/article/2026041409530886171)
- [R] Developers "buy production slots well in advance, sometimes without a specific project" — [Reuters via Kitco, 11 May 2026](https://www.kitco.com/news/off-the-wire/2026-05-11/us-power-transformer-buyers-scramble-imports-factory-slots)
- [C] A slot reservation deposit is 5-15% of equipment value to hold a production position while design is finalized (vendor blog, search-snippet level) — [Build Inc](https://build.inc/insights/data-center-electrical-procurement-sequence-workflow.md)
- [C] Maddox lets customers "reserve manufacturing capacity before their specification is fully built" and store finished units in its yards — [Capacity partner content](https://capacityglobal.com/news/how-smart-procurement-and-supply-can-beat-the-transformer-bottleneck/)
- [R] Some utilities "pay to reserve factory slots before projects are approved"; some backlog is inflated by multiple booking — [The Next Web, 9 Jul 2026](https://thenextweb.com/news/us-power-companies-scramble-data-centre-equipment)
- [R] Comparable from earlier notes (gas turbines): two Kentucky utilities paid GE Vernova a $25M reservation fee for one turbine for 2030; GE Vernova prices slot reservations 10 to 20 points above backlog — [Latitude Media](https://www.latitudemedia.com/news/gas-turbine-prices-are-up-and-arent-going-down-anytime-soon/); [POWER](https://www.powermag.com/full-throttle-five-trends-reshaping-the-gas-power-boom/)
- [R] Supply agreements now include capacity-reservation obligations, allocation rights during shortages, and price-adjustment mechanisms; assignment terms are not discussed — [Foley & Lardner](https://www.foley.com/insights/publications/2026/09/the-grids-bottleneck-isnt-just-money-its-materials/)

### Inferences
- Because price is open at reservation and the unit is engineered to the buyer's specification, a transformer slot is not a standardized, tradable right. A merchant holding a slot would still need an end customer whose specification the maker accepts, and the maker sets the final price.
- Multiple booking means some reserved slots will be released if projects slip. That favors buyers with patience more than a merchant paying 5-15% deposits to hold positions.
- The closest working model is Maddox's: reserve capacity at contract manufacturers for standard designs, hold finished stock, and sell from the yard.

### Gaps
- No contract text found on whether slot reservations are assignable.
- No named case of a slot being resold, for transformers or switchgear.
- Deposit terms come from one vendor blog seen only as a snippet.

- [R] Lambda raised about $1B of short-dated private debt arranged by JPMorgan to buy GPUs that Microsoft will lease, two weeks after borrowing $917M against an Nvidia contract; Nebius borrowed $775M against its GPUs — [The Next Web, 28 Aug 2026](https://thenextweb.com/news/lambda-1bn-private-debt-microsoft-lease-nebius-european-neocloud)

- [R] Power equipment leasing and lending (earlier notes): Caterpillar Financial lent ProPetro $172M against its generation fleet; VoltaGrid has a $3.0B asset-based revolver; Solaris's contracts imply about $455,000 of revenue per MW-year excluding fuel (derived) — [ProPetro 8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001680247/000168024726000122/exhibit991-earningsrelea.htm); [Simpson Thacher](https://www.stblaw.com/about-us/news/view/2025/11/10/simpson-thacher-advises-on-$5-billion-debt-refinancing-by-voltagrid)

### Inferences
- [E] Lease revenue arithmetic for a new lessor: a 36-month lease on $100M of equipment at a 14% yield with a 15% booked residual pays about $3.1M a month, about $37M a year. So **$40M of annual lease revenue needs roughly $105-110M of equipment on lease**. At a 70% advance from the lessor's own lender that is about $32M of equity; at 50% (the rate for new operators) about $54M.
- [E] A lessor's revenue is mostly return of capital. If it borrows at 9-11% and leases at 12-15%, the net spread is about 3-6 points on assets, about $3-6M a year on $108M, before credit losses and residual shortfalls.
- [E] A 15% residual at 36 months is at the top of the vendor's 11-19% band for an H100 in 2027. A lessor booking more than that is exposed; booking less means the lessee pays close to full payout, which is a loan in all but name.
- The lessee a new lessor can win is the one banks reject: no investment-grade offtake, short contracts, 12-15% money. That is adverse selection by construction.
- For power and cooling equipment, the leasable asset with redeployment value is mobile generation; no lessor terms for cooling equipment were found.

### Gaps
- No lease rate factor, no named independent GPU lessor with disclosed terms, and no lender advance rate to a lessor were found. All rates above are from brokers and vendors with an interest in deal flow.
- No data on leasing of cooling or electrical equipment to neoclouds.
- The Clifford Chance July 2026 briefing on GPU financing downloaded as an unreadable file beyond its cover — [Clifford Chance](https://www.cliffordchance.com/content/dam/cliffordchance/briefings/2026/07/tl-gpu-infrastructure-v3.pdf)

## C3. Defaults, repossessions or restructurings in 2026

### Takeaway
As of 17 October 2026 no GPU-backed loan had been publicly foreclosed and its chips auctioned. Stress so far shows up as covenant amendments, widened spreads, walked leases and one AI-customer bankruptcy (Character.AI), not as repossessions.

### Cited Findings
- [C] "No GPU-backed loan has been publicly foreclosed" as of 17 Oct 2026, per a tracker run by a firm that sells lender services — [American Compute defaults tracker](https://www.amcompute.com/gpu-risk/defaults)
- [R] Character.AI filed Chapter 11 in Delaware on 12 Oct 2026 with assets and liabilities each estimated at $50-100M and a $20M stalking-horse bid from a Disney affiliate. It is a compute customer, not a lessor or neocloud — [American Compute defaults tracker](https://www.amcompute.com/gpu-risk/defaults)
- [R] CoreWeave: DDTL 3.0 amended to postpone coverage testing and allow unlimited equity cures through 28 Oct 2026; its July 2026 $2.6B loan widened to as much as SOFR+500 at 97; CDS about 855 bp in late July 2026; company rated B+/Ba3 — [Dave Friedman](https://davefriedman.substack.com/p/the-market-is-pricing-payment-certainty); [American Compute risk page](https://www.amcompute.com/gpu-risk)
- [R] Walked or lapsed commitments: CoreWeave exited its anchor agreement for Poolside's 2 GW Horizon campus after Poolside missed its deadline to bring the first cluster online; a 180 MW CoreWeave expansion in Hammond, Indiana expired on 30 Jun 2026 — [Measured AI](https://measuredai.substack.com/p/neoclouds-ai-middle-layer)
- [R] Recovery precedent from bitcoin-mining equipment loans (2022-2024): lenders who took back machines or took a quick cash payoff recovered 37% to 65%; lenders who stayed with a borrower that kept its site and contracts recovered 80% to 100%; Core Scientific's equipment lenders waited 13 months in Chapter 11. In 13 of 24 tracked stress cases the collateral resold for less than the loan — [American Compute risk page](https://www.amcompute.com/gpu-risk)
- [R] Bankruptcy mechanics: the automatic stay halts repossession on filing, and GPU lessors have no equivalent of the aircraft rule that sets a deadline for returning collateral (search-snippet level) — [American Compute](https://www.amcompute.com/blog/what-happens-to-gpus-when-a-neocloud-goes-bankrupt)
- [R] Concentration: Anthropic, Microsoft and Meta hold 78% of disclosed GPU contract value; 43% rests on two AI labs without public ratings — [American Compute risk page](https://www.amcompute.com/gpu-risk)
- [R] Nvidia has sought insurers to cover lender losses if a neocloud defaults and chips resell too low (earlier notes) — [Bisnow relaying the FT, 30 Sep 2026](https://www.bisnow.com/news/national/data-center-capital-markets/nvidia-asks-insurers-to-shoulder-financing-risk-for-some-of-its-largest-customers)

### Inferences
- There is no recovery data for GPU collateral because no one has had to sell any. A new lessor to sub-investment-grade operators would be the first to test it, with a 13-month stay precedent and a 37-65% recovery precedent from the nearest comparable asset.
- The stress pattern (amend, cure with equity, forgive) favors large lenders with relationships and patience; a small lessor with its own leverage cannot wait 13 months.

### Gaps
- The tracker notes seven newer cases checked in October 2026 that are "not yet counted"; I could not see them.
- No neocloud Chapter 11 in 2026 was found. I ran one search; I cannot rule out small private restructurings.

## Cross-cutting: can each reach $40M of gross revenue in 18 months and $100M in 36? (inference only)

### Takeaway
On the evidence, trucked gas is the only one of the three where a single contract delivers $40M of gross revenue with customer-funded equipment; stock-and-resell can reach the revenue but into a falling premium; leasing reaches it only by raising and placing over $100M of equipment into the riskiest credits.

### Cited Findings
- [V] One 135 MW bridge contract is worth about $120M a year; a small supplier (Stabilis) won $100M a year with a $25M customer advance — [Baxtel](https://baxtel.com/news/certarus-lands-135mw-onsite-gas-supply-deal-for-us-data-center); [Stabilis 8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001043186/000143774926004242/ex_917347.htm)
- [C] Giga Energy went from $0.8M (2021) to over $150M (2025) selling fast-delivery electrical gear it largely manufactures — [Pulse 2.0](https://pulse2.com/giga-energy-profile-matt-lohstroh-interview/amp/)
- [C] New GPU operators borrow at 12-15% with 50% equity when they lack a strong offtake — [American Compute](https://www.amcompute.com/gpu-loans)

### Inferences
All [E].
- **Trucked gas.** $40M a year is about 35 MW of engine load, about 16 large trailer loads a day and about 67-70 trailers at Certarus's productivity. $100M a year is one contract the size of Stabilis's or 110-115 MW at Certarus's pricing. Margin: 83% after commodity, about 28% after delivery costs (Certarus); 23-29% gross and "high teens" expected (Stabilis). Capital: perhaps $35-60M of trailers, tractors and compression for $40M of CNG revenue if modules cost about $0.5M (unverified), or about $10-12M for LNG by Stabilis's ratio if liquefied gas can be bought from third parties. Main failure modes: no contract without a fleet and a track record; trailer supply committed to Certarus through 2027-2028; revenue ends when the pipeline arrives (2-2.5 year terms); integrated power providers haul their own gas.
- **Stock and resell.** $40M is reachable by revenue (Maddox-scale), but needs perhaps $13-20M of inventory and sells into lead times that have halved for stockable classes. Margin evidence: 12-15% broker markup; no stockist gross margin is public. Main failure mode: buying inventory at shortage prices just as 2027-2028 capacity and released double-bookings arrive, with tariff rules that changed three times in 2025.
- **Leasing.** $40M of lease revenue needs about $105-110M on lease and $32-54M of equity. Net spread about 3-6% of assets. Main failure modes: adverse selection (the lessees banks decline), residuals of 5-19% within two years on the vendor's own band, no repossession precedent, and a stay that can run 13 months.

### Gaps
- None of the three has a public example of a company founded in 2025-2026 reaching $40M within 18 months. Stabilis (public since 2019) and Giga (founded 2019) are the nearest analogues.
- Trailer price and availability for a new buyer is the single most important unknown for the trucked-gas case and should be settled with a quote from Hexagon Agility or a competitor.
