## Summary verdict

This is the first candidate in this study where the founder's own capital can close the first purchase. A forwarder with $48M of gross revenue costs about $13M, and $11M of cash equity closes it. The lender, the co-investor and an operator all say yes on terms. It still misses the month-18 bar in the central case, for three reasons that the brief does not price.

1. **Timing.** A first-time buyer of a firm that holds a customs broker licence, a Federal Maritime Commission licence and airline accreditation closes in month 9-13, not month 6-8. The trailing-twelve-month figure at month 18 then holds only 5-9 months of revenue.
2. **The revenue is being bought at a freight-rate peak.** Container spot rates are 2.2 times last November's level. About 70% of a forwarder's gross revenue is carrier cost passed through. A 30% fall in rates takes 21% off gross revenue with the same shipments. A $48M business becomes a $38M business.
3. **Nothing is created after the purchase by month 36.** The acquired firms earn about $4.7M of EBITDA when bought. Client loss, a platform overhead of $1.5M and a technology team of $1.2M arrive before the savings. Month-36 EBITDA is $1.5-4.5M. Equity is worth about half of what was put in.

Central case: $26M trailing revenue at month 18 (annualized $38M), $85M at month 36 (annualized $99M), on $42M of total capital and $22M of cash equity. Organic revenue, including duty recovery, is 6% of the three-year total.

**A 30% fall in freight rates** [est]: carrier cost is about 85% of forwarding revenue and forwarding is about 82% of gross revenue, so 70% of gross revenue is rate-linked. - **Cost structure of a $48M firm** [DK]:

| Carrier and other direct cost | 41.0 | |
| Gross profit | 7.0 (14.5%) | Brokerage about $2.9M (21,000 entries at about $140 with filing, extra tariff lines and bond fees), nearly all margin; forwarding margin about 9.5% |
| Staff, about 60 people | 3.6 | Gross profit per employee $105-125K; staff are 50-55% of gross profit |

| EBITDA after replacing the owner with a paid manager | 1.9 (4.0%) | 27% of gross profit. Top-quartile firms reach 30-33%. The brief's 36-38% is a 2026 peak with add-backs |

- **Staff by function** [DK]: 6 managers and owners, 8 sales and account managers, 5 licensed brokers, 12 entry writers and import specialists, 16 ocean and air coordinators, 5 accounting, 4 warehouse and dispatch, 4 other.
- **What agents can take over within 18 months of a closing** [DK]: document capture from invoices and packing lists, first-pass tariff classification on repeat parts, security filings, arrival notices, tracking replies, routine quotes, carrier invoice matching, collections reminders. That is 35-45% of the hours of about 33 people, or 12-15 positions and $0.75-0.95M, which is 15-19% of operating cost. It is realized by not replacing leavers, not by layoffs, because the same people hold the client relationships. The 2026 tariff layers raised the work per entry by 30-50%: stacked additional tariff lines, metal content, country of melt. Staff are overloaded, so the first year of automation removes overtime and backlog, not payroll.
- **Month 18 is the wrong date for the saving.** The first firm will be 5-10 months past closing. He will commit to 3-6% of operating cost by month 18 and 12-15% at 24-30 months after a closing.
- **Error and penalty exposure** [DK]: a classification rule applied wrongly by software repeats across every entry of that part. With duty layers of 10-50%, a missed additional tariff on a $50K entry is $5-25K of unpaid duty; across 400 entries it is $2-10M. The importer carries the penalty under 19 USC 1592 and claims against the broker's errors-and-omissions cover, usually $2-5M at this size. Late or wrong security filings carry $5,000 each in liquidated damages. Uncollectable container detention and demurrage charges are the usual forwarding loss, and ports were congested this month [source: fashionunited link above].
- **What breaks after a change of owner** [DK]:
  1. Two or three salespeople hold 40-60% of freight margin. Non-compete terms are void in California, the largest forwarding market.
  2. Overseas agents route 25-40% of import cargo and can move it to another US partner in a week.
  3. Carrier credit and airline settlement accounts are re-underwritten; several rest on the owner's personal guarantee.
  4. The licensed officer who qualifies the licence. If that person leaves, the firm has 120 days [notes].
  5. A change of operating system in the first year. He forbids it.
  6. Reported gross revenue. Small firms often book agent-basis business gross. A proper accounting review can move 5-15% of reported revenue to net.
- **Decision:** accepts the operating job. Refuses to sign the 20-25% target.
- **Terms:** $325K base, 4-6% equity vesting over four years, seller employed for 18 months, stay bonuses of $0.4-0.6M for 12-15 named people paid at 12 and 24 months, no system migration for 12 months.
- **What would change it:** nothing on the saving by month 18. For 20% at month 30: a target already on one modern system with clean part-level data.

## Persona 2: Product lead at a forwarding and customs software vendor

- **Incentives:** raise revenue per transaction across the installed base. No reason to favour one small customer.
- **Already sold to every customer** [DK]: document capture for commercial invoices; entry filing with the tariff database and additional-tariff logic; security filings; automatic arrival notices and milestone messages; customer tracking portal; carrier booking connections; rate management and quoting; carrier invoice matching; workflow rules. Six agent features are inside the standard packs today [source: Loadstar link above, secondary].
- **What an owner can add on top** [DK]: handling of its own mailbox (reading client emails, opening jobs, chasing documents); a part-level classification memory for each importer; exception handling between systems; screening its own entry history for refund candidates. Perhaps 10-15% of operations hours beyond the vendor's features.
- **Position:** access goes through the paid interface, priced per transaction. Robotic use of screens breaches the licence. Future agents will be priced separately [source above, secondary] at a level that takes 25-40% of the labour they remove [DK]. Prices can change on 30 days' notice and rose 20-50% for many customers in December 2025 [source above].
- **Decision:** not a buyer. Sells the standard product at list. Refuses exclusivity, source access and any price lock beyond 12 months.
- **Consequence:** most of the saving in the brief is a feature the founder's competitors receive in the same release. The owner's own layer is worth 1-2 points of gross profit and is copyable.
- **What would change it:** a customer with 50,000 or more entries a year gets a design-partner seat and early access, not exclusivity.

## Persona 3: Customs and Border Protection and Federal Maritime Commission view

Not a buyer. Sets conditions. All citations [DK].

- **Structure matters.** In a stock purchase the broker licence, national permit, filer code and powers of attorney stay with the company. In an asset purchase none of them transfer: the buyer needs its own licence and every importer must sign a new power of attorney. Sellers prefer asset sales for tax, which is a negotiating cost.
- **Customs broker rules (19 CFR 111):** at least one officer must hold an individual licence, and a licensed permit qualifier must exercise responsible supervision and control. Loss of the qualifier for 120 days ends the licence [notes]. A change of ownership that adds a principal with a controlling interest must be reported in writing without delay. No prior approval is needed, but the new principals are vetted.
- **AI-prepared entries.** No rule bars software from preparing an entry. The licensed broker answers for every one. The supervision factors in section 111.28 include the ratio of licensed brokers to volume, the rejection and error rate, training, written procedures and how often the licensed person reviews work. Cutting entry staff while volume holds pushes on the first two factors. Customs business must be done inside US customs territory by the broker's employees, so offshore review of classification is not available. Records stay in the US; a data breach must be reported within 72 hours.
- **Penalties:** up to $30,000 per broker penalty action under 19 USC 1641, and licence suspension or revocation. Importer penalties for negligence are 20% of the lost duty on a first violation, more after [notes, F3]. False Claims Act referrals in customs rose in 2025-26 [notes, F3]. Refunds claimed in error are false claims.
- **Maritime Commission (46 CFR 515):** the ocean licence needs a qualifying individual with three years of experience; changes of officers and owners are reported within 30 days; a replacement qualifier must be named within 30 days; bonds are $75,000 for a carrier without ships and $50,000 for a forwarder. Detention and demurrage billing rules from the 2022 shipping act apply to the firm's own invoices.
- **Other registrations:** indirect air carrier programme with the transport security agency; airline settlement accreditation with a financial review on change of owner; supply-chain security certification.
- **Decision:** no objection to a stock purchase with the qualifier and qualifying individual under contract. No comfort letter on AI. A new owner whose error rate rises gets a broker compliance review.
- **What would change it:** nothing the founder controls. Two years of low error rates lower the review rate.

## Persona 4: Senior engineer who has built logistics and trade software, asked to be chief technology officer

- **Alternative:** $450K total pay at a funded logistics software company.
- **Assessment** [DK]:
  - The core system is the vendor's. The buildable layer is intake, classification memory, quoting and exception handling. Four engineers, $1.0-1.4M a year, first production agent four to six months after interface access.
  - Each acquired firm arrives on a different system. Migration costs $0.3-0.6M and six to nine months per firm and is the classic cause of client loss. Without migration the agents must be built two or three times.
  - Field-level document accuracy of 95-98% is not enough for a customs declaration. Every line needs licensed review until the error rate is proven, so the first-year labour saving is small.
  - The valuable data is part-level classification history per importer. It belongs to the importer and leaves with the power of attorney.
- **Decision:** declines the chief technology officer seat. Offers to lead automation at $280K with 2% equity, licensing tools instead of building a platform, with a budget of $0.8M a year.
- **Reason:** at 6-7 times EBITDA a buyer pays nothing for software, so the equity is worth little; the work is integration, not product.
- **What would change it:** a platform above $100M of gross revenue on one system, or evidence that a trade buyer paid for the automation layer.

# Capital personas

## Persona 5: Asset-based lender, asked for a receivables line and an acquisition term loan

- **Incentives:** self-liquidating collateral, a field exam it trusts, no reliance on projections.
- **Decision:** issues a term sheet. Refuses the brief's $4M draw toward the price and refuses a $5M term loan.
- **Terms on the $48M firm** [DK]:
  - $6.5M revolver. Advance 85% of eligible billed receivables. Ineligible: over 90 days from invoice, accounts with half their balance past due, balances with overseas agents, amounts owed to the same party, any debtor above 20% of the total.
  - Duty outlays billed to clients: 75% advance, capped at 15% of the borrowing base. Duty collected from clients and not yet paid to Customs is reserved in full, because the government ranks first.
  - Reserve for carrier payables past terms, since a carrier can hold cargo and the client then withholds payment.
  - Availability about $3.0-3.5M on $7.0M of receivables. Minimum unused availability $1.25M. So $2.0-2.5M can go to the price.
  - Term loan $2.4M (1.25 times reviewed EBITDA), repaid 20% a year. Total senior debt at most 2.5 times.
  - Pricing: revolver SOFR plus 2.75-3.5 points, 0.5% on unused; term loan SOFR plus 6-7 points; 1.5% closing fee.
  - Fixed-charge cover of 1.1 times tested when availability is below 12.5%. Cash dominion from day one for a borrower with no fund sponsor. Validity guarantee from the founder.
  - Before closing: field exam, an accounting review that restates revenue under standard rules and normalises 2026 for freight rates and refund-filing fees, credit insurance on the top accounts, the licensed qualifier under contract.
- **Why so little term debt:** asset-light, cyclical, earnings at a rate peak, first-time owner, clients free to leave. Projected automation savings get no credit. The technology team must be funded with equity outside the borrower.
- **What would change it:** a fund sponsor behind the equity; three years of client-level retention above 90%; EBITDA above $5M, when a cash-flow lender offers 3 times.

## Persona 6: Co-investor (family office), asked for $5-10M beside the founder

- **Incentives:** capital preservation, 2.5 times in five to seven years, no blind pools.
- **Decision:** soft commitment of $3-5M on a named target under a signed letter of intent. Refuses $10M and refuses to pre-commit to a second purchase.
- **Terms** [DK]: same security as the founder; 8% preferred return to all cash before any promote; promote 15% above 8%, 20% above two times; two of five board seats; consent over debt, purchases, budget and the chief executive; the operator hired and holding equity before closing; price set on the average of 2024-2026 EBITDA, not on 2026; broken-deal costs are the founder's.

It notes the adviser's own sentence that this meets a revenue target and not a wealth target, and asks why it should fund purchases two and three at all.

## Persona 7: Skeptical growth investor, asked to fund the second and third purchases

- **Value created:** see the bridge. $32M buys $4.7M of EBITDA. At month 36 the group earns $1.5-4.5M.
- **Comparables** [DK unless sourced]: the largest listed US forwarder trades near 15 times EBITDA on a 30% conversion of gross profit to operating income and no debt. A listed US roll-up of forwarders trades at 6-8 times EBITDA and about 0.4 times revenue. Large European forwarders trade at 10-15 times. The one sourced private deal was six times EBITDA [source above]. A $90M group earning $3M is none of the first three. No multiple gain exists below about $10M of EBITDA.

# Competition personas

## Persona 8: Head of corporate development at a large forwarder or a sponsor-backed forwarding platform

- **Does it bid for the same firms?** Yes, for any firm with over $5M of gross profit, a clean customs record and a niche (a trade lane, perishables, project cargo, a strong brokerage).
- **Price** [DK]: 6-9 times EBITDA, cash, part as an earn-out; closes in 60-90 days with its own licences. It can pay one to two turns more than a financial buyer because it replaces the overseas agents with its own offices and keeps both halves of the shipment margin, and it buys carrier space more cheaply.
- **Passes on:** gross profit under $3M, a client above 25%, heavy exposure to low-value e-commerce parcels, a business that is one owner's contacts.
- **Consequence:** $0.25-0.30 per dollar is the price of firms this buyer declined or never saw. The founder's field is owner-led firms not in a process: 5-10 a year of $40M or more, out of an estimated 80-150 independents of that size [DK estimate].
- **Response in the market:** at the spring contract season it quotes the founder's largest accounts from its own contract rates.
- **Would it buy the founder's group?** At 6-7 times EBITDA before platform cost, for the book and the people. It values the agents at zero and moves the accounts to its own system.
- **What would change it:** a seller who refuses trade buyers to protect staff. That seller exists and is the founder's best prospect.

## Persona 9: Head of sales at a venture-funded digital forwarder and customs broker

- **What it sells against the founder's firm:** brokerage at $75-125 an entry against $125-175; a client portal; a free tariff and refund review; drawback with 70% advanced in a day [notes]; all four refund programmes in one offer [notes].
- **Play at change of ownership:** calls the top 50 importers within 60 days: "your broker was sold to a financial buyer with no trade record."
- **Expected take** [est]: 3-6% of the acquired gross profit in year one, mostly technology-minded importers under $50M of imports. It does not win industrial accounts served by a named person for ten years.
- **On duty recovery:** it quotes no fee until the refund is paid, at 10-15% [notes, F3]. The founder's 12-15% holds only where the importer has never been approached.
- **Decision:** competes; not a buyer of the founder's firms.
- **What would change it:** nothing. If the founder's firms show lower error rates and faster release times than its own, it loses fewer of those calls.

## Persona 10: Freight-market analyst (new independent voice)

- **Position today.** Spot container rates are 2.2 times last November's and have fallen four weeks running; the rise is tied to attacks near the Strait of Hormuz [source above]. US imports for 2026 are about 26.0M TEU, up 2%, with a weak first quarter of 2027 expected and port labour unsettled [source above]. Gross duties run at $22-26B a month [notes].
- **Ocean rates, 2027-2028** [DK, est]: the order book is large relative to the fleet and deliveries are heavy in 2027-28. If the Hormuz and Red Sea routes normalise, the composite index returns to $1,700-2,200.

| Scenario | Probability | Composite index, 2027 average | 2028 average |
|---|---|---|---|
| Disruption persists | 25% | $3,400-4,200 | $2,800-3,600 |
| Glide down | 45% | $2,400-2,900 | $1,900-2,200 |
| Collapse | 30% | $1,900-2,300 | $1,400-1,800 |

- **Air rates** [DK]: down 5-10% in 2027; the end of duty-free parcel entry cut transpacific e-commerce volume, while technology hardware and Middle East airspace limits support yields.
- **US import volumes** [DK, est]: 2027 between -3% and +1% in TEU; 2028 +1% to +3%. Two years of buying ahead of tariffs unwind first [source above].
- **Tariff policy** [notes, DK]: the Section 232 and Section 301 layers are durable and are now half of all duties. The forced-labor action is new and untested in court. One ruling or proclamation can add or remove a layer inside a quarter, as IEEPA showed. For a broker, more layers mean more lines per entry and more fee per entry; for a forwarder, tariff shocks pull volume forward and then leave a hole.
- **Effect on a forwarder** [est]: gross revenue per shipment 14-20% below the 2026 average by 2028 in the glide case and 28% below in the collapse. Gross profit per shipment roughly flat, with a temporary gain while rates fall. Gross margin as a percentage rises to 16-19% as rates fall. A firm sold on 2026 trailing revenue will report lower gross revenue in 2027-28 in 75% of scenarios without losing a client.
- **View on the plan:** buying gross revenue in the months after a rate spike is buying the peak of the denominator. The revenue target and the buyer's interest point in opposite directions: the founder wants high rates for the target and pays for them in working capital and in a seller's inflated asking price.
- **What would change it:** a sustained closure of the Hormuz or Suez routes through 2027.

**Duty recovery on the acquired book** [notes, est]. A $48M firm's clients pay about $70M of duty a year. About 55% is on drawback-eligible authorities [notes, F3]. Most clients import for US sale. Perhaps 10-15% of them re-export, and the larger ones already file through specialists at 3-12% [notes, F3]. Unclaimed and practically claimable: $0.3-0.6M of refunds a year, plus a one-time lookback of $0.8-1.5M, limited because the higher eligible duty base dates only from 2026 [notes, F3]. At a realised 12% that is $0.10-0.18M of lookback fees and $0.04-0.07M a year. Corrections to the firm's own past entries cannot be billed on results: the client will say the broker filed them wrong, and the correction invites a claim. Corrections for newly won clients add $0.05-0.1M. IEEPA refunds were filed through existing brokers in 2026 and are 77% paid [notes]; the fees are in the seller's 2026 earnings, not the buyer's future. A drawback specialist costs $150-250K a year, and the general broker bought rarely has one. Net contribution in the first 18 months is about zero. It is still better than the stand-alone start in F3, because client acquisition cost is nil, and its real use is retention.

## What accumulates, and what does not

| Asset | Accumulates beyond scale? | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Powers of attorney and entry history | Yes, modestly | Terminable at will, but rarely moved without a failure |
| Part-level classification data per importer | Yes | The one input that makes agents more accurate with use; it belongs to the client and leaves with it |
| Licences, permit, bonds, security certifications | Step, not compounding | Inherited in a stock purchase; any competitor has them |
| Carrier buying power | Yes, with volume | Real above about 10,000 TEU a year; trade buyers have far more |
| Overseas agent network | No | Personal and reciprocal; weakens at a change of owner |
| Agents and workflow software | Barely | Vendor features reach every forwarder; the owner's layer is copyable |
| Visibility of refund candidates in own entry data | Yes, small | Worth $0.1-0.5M a year per book |
| Staff | Leaves | Salespeople and licensed brokers are mobile |

Product company or service company: a service company. A buyer prices it on EBITDA at 6-8 times and pays nothing for the software.
