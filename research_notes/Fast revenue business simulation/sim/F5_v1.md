# F5 v1: Physical-work data for robotics labs (organic)

## What is sold
Recorded hours of real physical work for companies training robot models.
1. Multi-view first-person video with hand pose and annotations, captured from real workers in real workplaces: warehouses, commercial kitchens, light assembly, laundries, hotels.
2. Teleoperation trajectories on the customer's own robots, at leased sites staffed by the company.

## Supply side
Contracts with employers, staffing firms and facility operators. Workers consent and are paid a bonus to wear capture rigs. The company owns the rigs, the processing and the annotation.

## Standard unit
One annotated hour.

## Price and contract
- First-person video: $40-150 per annotated hour. Teleoperation: $150-250 per hour. These are the founder's assumptions. The only figures in the notes are unverified: $3-25 per worker-hour for raw first-person video and $118-200 per loaded hour for teleoperation.
- Programs of 100,000 to 1,000,000 hours, ordered quarterly.

## Founder's assumptions
- A 1,000,000-hour program at $40 is $40M.
- Buyers: humanoid and robot-model companies and the robotics groups of large technology companies. One humanoid company has committed over $1bn to data and compute over 12 months.
- Research notes: no robotics-data supplier has disclosed revenue near $40M. One supplier has raised $70M and has about 20 customers. One vendor estimates densely annotated data is worth about 100 times raw footage and costs about 20 times more to make.
- Gross margin 35-45%.

## Known weak points the founder wants tested
- The largest buyers built collection in-house because vendors "couldn't hit the throughput, diversity, or quality bar".
- Raw video is cheap to crowdsource abroad. What stops price collapse?
- Hardware rigs, annotation quality, privacy and labour law in workplaces.
- Will simulation or internet video make this unnecessary?
- Buyer concentration.
