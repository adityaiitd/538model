## 1. Buyers

Buyers 1 and 6 are one organisation (Lab A). Orders are not counted twice.

### Buyer 1. Director of model evaluation, frontier lab that paused a supplier in 2026 (Lab A)

- Incentives: gold labels and failure analysis on multi-hour finance and accounting runs, on unreleased models, with no second incident on this persona's watch. Wants a stable cohort so agreement with the lab's gold set stops drifting each time gig reviewers churn.
- Constraints: lab policy since the pause caps any one vendor at about half of a domain [DK]. Budget moves quarterly with research themes [notes].
- Alternatives: two remaining marketplaces; 10-30 directly contracted experts on the lab's own tool [r2]; model judges; an employed-team vendor already on the approved list (competitor 11).
- Decision: **signs a paid trial. Does not grant primary status. A standing program follows only if the trial ranks first on the lab's gold set.**
- Trial: 600 accepted reviews at $420, about $250K, five weeks, reviewers on the lab's tool.
- Standing program after a winning trial: 150-250 accepted reviews a week at $400-450, so $3.1-5.7M annualized, starting at the low end. No minimum. 30-day termination. 90% acceptance target, rejected reviews unpaid, 72-hour turnaround.
- Primary status (40-50% of the domain, never sole): after two quarters ranked first, two independent sites, and proof of supply through January to April. Then 350-500 reviews a week, $7-11M annualized. Rubric and gold-set authoring would add $2-4M. Earliest date: month 15-18.
- Price path: the lab will supply model-written pre-annotations from the second half of 2027. Review time falls from about 2.5 hours to 1.2-1.5 hours and the lab resets the price to $280-350 [DK].
- Reasons: the security posture makes the vendor eligible for unreleased-model work, which gig vendors lost at this lab. Eligibility is not allocation. Allocation follows the weekly scorecard.
- Doubt about the supply: a four-year senior at a regional firm can grade close, tax and audit work. Cannot grade treasury, planning, deal models or insurance reserving. Half the domain is outside that staff's competence [DK].
- What changes the decision: nothing shortens the two-quarter rule. A named researcher asking for the vendor moves the trial forward by one to two months.

### Buyer 2. Head of data operations, large technology company's AI group

- Incentives: fewer vendors, each under a master agreement. A mandate to consolidate.
- Constraints: procurement onboarding takes four to six months for a new legal entity; $10M cyber cover; approved-country list [DK].
- Alternatives: five to eight managed vendors and large outsourcers already under contract (facts 2 and 3).
- Decision: **signs a paid trial, but as a subcontractor under an existing prime vendor's agreement. Refuses direct vendor status in year one.**
- Terms: 500-800 reviews at $300-450, $200-300K gross. The prime keeps 15-20%. Year one $0.4-1.0M gross. Year two $2-5M if the scorecard holds, with direct status possible after 12 months.
- Reasons: a new vendor is a cost to this persona. A specialist under a prime is free. Fact 2 shows this buyer type scaling an employed-team vendor from $3M to $20M, but that vendor had a decade of record.
- What changes the decision: SOC 2 Type II plus a year of clean delivery under the prime.

### Buyer 3. Chief executive, agent company in finance and accounting automation, $80M revenue

- Incentives: fewer errors in production and a benchmark to show customers. Review feedback is this company's own advantage.
- Constraints: runs contain customers' ledgers. Any outside reviewer is a sub-processor needing customer consent [DK]. Employs 15-25 accountants for quality already [DK].
- Alternatives: own staff; customers' controllers, who approve output inside the product; a marketplace's packaged evaluation suite [r2].
- Decision: **signs a small purchase order for release testing on synthetic and consented data. Refuses to outsource the production feedback loop.**
- Terms: $60-90K trial, then $15-35K a month around releases. $0.2-0.45M a year. Runs are 15-60 minutes, so $90-200 per accepted review, not $350-600.
- Reasons: quality spend is about 3-5% of revenue, $2.4-4M, and four-fifths is internal headcount [DK].
- What changes the decision: an enterprise customer or its auditor demanding independent review. Then up to $0.8M.

### Buyer 4. Head of AI quality, insurer or bank deploying agents in claims or finance operations

- Incentives: no regulatory finding; a defensible validation file.
- Constraints: runs contain policyholder or customer data. Third-party risk onboarding takes six to nine months. Validation stays with the institution's own second line [notes, first-study ledger].
- Alternatives: its own adjusters and accountants, of which it has thousands; a Big Four assurance engagement for the independent opinion (competitor 12); the agent vendor's own testing.
- Decision: **refuses a standing program and refuses a 2027 trial.**
- Reasons: staff of a regional accounting firm reading claim files is a new data-sharing arrangement with no regulatory benefit. Reviewers who know this institution's policies are already on its payroll.
- What it would buy instead: rubric design and adjudication software used by its own staff, $100-250K. That is a different product.
- What changes the decision: SOC 2 Type II, on-premises or in-tenant review, and a regulator or external auditor accepting the output. Then $0.2-0.5M a year from 2028.

### Buyer 5. Vice president of product, enterprise-software company shipping agents in an accounting or planning product

- Incentives: ship agents without a public failure; evidence for customers' auditors.
- Constraints: has a partner channel of thousands of accounting firms and could contract them directly [DK]. Has no program-management team for it.
- Alternatives: partner firms direct; in-house solution consultants; a marketplace suite.
- Decision: **signs a paid trial, then a standing program.**
- Terms: trial $75-150K. Program $0.3-0.9M a year. Runs of 20-90 minutes at $100-220 per accepted review. Quarterly statement of work, 30-day termination.
- Reasons: the large marketplaces serve labs first and answer slowly [r2]. Employed accountants in a room is an easy story for this buyer's own customers.
- Dispute with the brief: the round-one persona's $0.8-1.5M is this buyer's whole outside evaluation budget including tools. Expert review is about half of it.
- What changes the decision: measured error reduction on its own benchmark raises it to $1.2M. No higher.

### Buyer 6. Chief information security officer, Lab A

- Decision on approval: **approves the trial in four to six weeks instead of six to ten, and approves eligibility for unreleased-model work, on conditions.** [DK]
- Conditions
  - Review happens only inside the lab's hosted environment. The vendor stores no runs, no rubrics and no outputs. This matters more than the rooms (fact 1).
  - Devices are owned and managed by the founder's company, not by the accounting firm. A firm's laptops hold client tax data and its own software.
  - Each supplier firm is a named subprocessor with its own assessment, three to five weeks per site, and an on-site audit of each room.
  - SOC 2 Type I at trial, Type II within 9-12 months. $10M cyber and errors-and-omissions cover.
  - Uncapped confidentiality liability on the founder's company. The accounting firm will cap its own liability at fees, so the founder's company carries the gap.
  - No offshore site for unreleased-model work.
- Decision on primary status: **the security design does not make this vendor primary. It removes a veto.** The persona requires two independent sites before any vendor holds more than a quarter of a domain.
- Reasons: the 2026 incident was a platform compromise. Rooms and managed devices address leaks and account sharing, which are real but were not the cause. An employed-team vendor with certified centres is already approved (competitor 11), so the posture is not unique.
- New risk v3 creates: fourth parties. The lab now depends on the security of three or more small professional firms it has never assessed.
- What changes the decision: nothing makes this persona a sponsor. A leak at one supplier site ends the program at all sites.

## 2. Suppliers

### Supplier 7. Managing partner, 400-person regional accounting and advisory firm

- Incentives: revenue for idle months; realization; keeping staff. Many peer firms now have private-equity owners who want bench hours sold [DK].
- Constraints: busy season. No staff from mid-January to mid-April and little from September to mid-October [DK]. Standard rates: staff $120-160, seniors $170-230, managers $250-325, senior managers $325-420 [DK]. Loaded cost of a senior is about $58-70 an hour [calc on DK salaries].
- Alternatives: internal projects; secondments to clients at 70-80% of standard rate; the same offer from a marketplace.
- Decision: **supplies staff, by the hour, in off-season blocks. Refuses pay per accepted review. Refuses exclusivity.**
- Terms
  - 15-30 seniors and supervisors, 20-25 hours a week, May to August and November to December. About 6,000-10,000 hours a year from this firm.
  - $115-125 an hour for seniors, $150-170 for managers. Minimum of 12 weeks per block, paid whether or not the lab sends work.
  - Net 30. Liability capped at fees paid. The firm's name is not given to the lab's public materials.
  - Exclusivity only against a guaranteed $1.5M a year.
- Reasons: idle salaried hours have no marginal cost, so $115 is acceptable. The firm will not carry acceptance risk on a rubric the lab can change. A block must be guaranteed because pulling staff off internal work for a program that can stop in 30 days costs the firm.
- Goes direct to labs? **No.** No access, no vendor-management function, and too small for a lab to onboard. It will take a marketplace's offer of 80% of billings the week it arrives.
- Effect on the brief: at a lab price of $420 per review and 2.5 hours a review, billing is $168 an hour worked. After 10% rejected reviews it is about $151. Supplier cost of $120 is then 79% of billings, not 65-70% [calc].
- What changes the decision: a 12-month minimum of $1.5M buys exclusivity and year-round partial supply of 6-10 people.

### Supplier 8. Operations head, outsourcing company with 2,000 finance and accounting staff

- Incentives: fill seats; a new service line with "AI" in the name.
- Constraints: 85-90% of staff are offshore and process transactions. About 150-250 are qualified accountants [DK].
- Decision: **supplies staff on its rate card and reserves the right to sell the same service itself.**
- Terms: offshore $28-45 an hour, onshore or nearshore $70-95. Year-round, 40 hours a week, certified delivery centres, its own managed devices. Three-month minimum. Non-solicitation limited to named buyers on named programs for 12 months.
- Goes direct? **Yes, within 6-12 months.** It has a sales force and existing agreements with large technology companies. Fact 3 shows a peer already sells this.
- Reasons: the founder's company is a free pilot of a market the outsourcer wants. The outsourcer learns the rubric, the price and the buyer.
- Limits for the founder: Lab A will not accept offshore sites for unreleased-model work (buyer 6). For work that can go offshore the buyer resets the price to $120-200 a review.
- What changes the decision: nothing stops direct entry. A revenue share on buyers the founder introduces is the most the persona offers.

### Supplier 9. Senior manager at the accounting firm, asked to give 20 hours a week

- Incentives: promotion to partner depends on managed revenue, realization and new clients. Salary and bonus do not change with this work.
- Decision: **refuses 20 hours a week. Accepts three to five hours a week as adjudicator and calibration lead, if the hours count as chargeable at full credit.**
- Reasons
  - 20 hours of transcript review at a third of this persona's standard rate damages every measure the persona is paid on.
  - The persona gains nothing personally. The same work on a marketplace pays an individual $80-120 an hour directly (fact 4), which the firm's outside-work policy forbids.
  - Accuracy falls after about two hours of reading long transcripts in one sitting [r2].
- Who actually does the work: seniors and supervisors with three to seven years' experience. Not 15-year practitioners.
- Retention risk: the best reviewers learn that their hour sells for $120 while their salary is about $55 an hour. Some leave for direct work.
- What changes the decision: a firm bonus of $25-40 per accepted review paid to the reviewer. That takes another 6-9 points of the founder's margin.

## 3. Competitors

### Competitor 10. Chief revenue officer, expert-data marketplace above $1bn run-rate

- Response while the entrant is under about $5M at a shared lab: none.
- Response above that
  - Launches "firm teams": signs accounting and staffing firms at 78-82% of billings on non-exclusive terms. Lead time six to ten weeks. The same managing partner (supplier 7) accepts.
  - Offers the lab price per accepted review 10% under the entrant, bundled with data programs at a 20-25% margin [r2].
  - After the 2026 breach it already runs hosted, locked-down review environments [fact 1, remediation reported, detail unverified].
- Alternative response: buys the entrant. This marketplace bought two small suppliers in 2026 [notes]. Price offered: one to two times run-rate revenue.
- What would stop it: exclusive supply contracts, which the entrant can only get with guaranteed minimums.

### Competitor 11. Founder, managed-team data vendor with secure facilities

- Position: employed staff, certified centres, approved at several labs and hyperscalers, programs of $20-30M (fact 2). Staff are mostly generalists and offshore. Onshore credentialed accountants are a gap.
- Response: **offers the entrant a subcontract and, in parallel, hires its own onshore finance pod.**
  - Subcontract: entrant supplies reviewers under this vendor's agreement at 80-85% of the buyer's price. Buyer 2 prefers exactly this.
  - Own pod: 30-50 accountants in a US centre in 8-12 weeks, or the same accounting firms signed directly.
- Reasons: the entrant's only distinct asset is supplier-firm contracts, which are non-exclusive.
- What the entrant gains: faster first revenue. What it loses: the buyer relationship and 15-20 points of price.

### Competitor 12. Head of a Big Four firm's AI assurance practice

- Decision: **does not compete for lab review work. Takes the enterprise "independent assurance" budget.**
- Reasons
  - Rate card of $250-450 an hour onshore. Lab review at $140-170 is below it. Offshore delivery centres could do it at $40-70 but the risk committee sees little fee and real independence questions where a lab or its investors are audit clients [DK].
  - For banks and insurers (buyer 4) this persona sells an assurance report over controls on agents at $0.3-2M an engagement. A regulated buyer wants the opinion from a firm whose name the regulator knows.
- Would partner with the entrant? No. It uses its own centres for testing.
- Effect: closes most of the bank and insurer segment to the entrant and sets the expectation that "independent review" means an assurance firm.
- What changes the decision: lab alliances already in place at these firms turning into paid evaluation work. Then it supplies experts at alliance rates, and the entrant loses the top of the domain.

## 4. Capital

### Persona 13. Venture investor who has backed two suppliers to AI labs

- Decision: **refuses $5-8M now. Offers $3-4M at $14-18M pre-money, half on a lab purchase order of at least $250K.** Refuses the $20-50M round until $8M recognized in a trailing six months, two buyers each under 55%, and audited gross margin of 24% or more.
- Reasons
  - The two portfolio suppliers grew because individual supply scaled in weeks. Firm supply scales one partner meeting at a time and stops for busy season.
  - Mismatch: suppliers want 12-week guaranteed blocks; buyers give 30 days. At a $40M rate with 70% supplier cost, eight weeks of uncovered minimums after a pause is about $4.3M [calc]. That is most of the equity.
  - The founder buys by the hour and sells by the accepted review. Productivity and rejection risk sit with the company.
  - A comparable raised at three times gross run-rate at $100M [r2]. At 24-28% gross margin and 30-day orders, value at $40M is $80-160M.
  - Still no route to the researchers who hold the budget [r2, objection 13]. v3 does not address it.
- What v3 improves, in this persona's view: fraud and identity risk are near zero; payables are net 30 instead of weekly, which cuts working capital to about $1.5M per $10M of billing from $1.9M [calc on r2 inputs]; no contractor-classification exposure.
- What changes the decision: a lab research lead as adviser; one top-25 accounting firm under a year-round exclusive; a second domain.

Revenue type: repeat purchase orders terminable on 30 days. Not contracted. Not recurring in the subscription sense. The cost side carries 12-week commitments.

Is v3 plainly unworkable? No. It is a workable services company with a likely first sale and a base case of about $11M trailing at month 18 and $34M in year three. Against v2 it gains trial speed, eligibility for sensitive work, lower fraud and lower working capital. It loses supply elasticity, which is what produced the fast ramps in the notes. It does not reach the bar in this panel's base case, and the high case reaches the annualized rate before the trailing figure.
