## Persona 1. Founder-owner, aged 64, forwarder and licensed broker: $45M gross revenue, $6.5M gross profit, 60 staff, 300 accounts

**Who.** Started the firm 31 years ago. No child in the business. Holds an individual broker licence but is not the day-to-day qualifier. Personally guarantees the bank line, two carrier credit lines and the surety indemnity [DK: common at private forwarders].

**His numbers [sim].**

| Year | Gross revenue | Gross profit | Adjusted EBITDA | What drove it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2023 | $33M | $5.4M | $1.1M | Rates at the floor |
| 2024 | $41M | $6.2M | $1.9M | Red Sea rate spike |
| 2025 | $36M | $5.8M | $1.4M | Front-loading, then rates fell to the $1,859 index level by November |
| 2026 (trailing) | $45M | $6.5M | $2.0M | Rates at 2.2 times a year ago; record October volumes; about $0.3-0.5M of one-off IEEPA refund filing fees |

- Staff cost is about $4.0M (61% of gross profit) and other costs $1.1M, so reported EBITDA is $1.4M and adjusted EBITDA $2.0M after adding back his own pay above a manager's salary. The brief's $1.8-3M on $5-8M of gross profit assumes 36-37% of gross profit converts to EBITDA; a 60-person firm converts 25-31%. [DK ratios, sim figures]
- Three-year average adjusted EBITDA is $1.75M. A lender and a quality-of-earnings firm will use something near that, less the one-off filing fees: $1.5-1.6M.
- Top ten accounts are 38% of gross revenue [sim; DK that 35-45% is usual].

**Alternatives.** He gets two to four unsolicited approaches a year [DK]. Live options: a private-equity-backed forwarding platform; an Asian or European forwarder that wants a US licence and office; his overseas agent partner; a sale to managers through an employee trust.

**Decision.** He sells in 2027 on 2026 numbers. He would rather take $13.5M from a strategic buyer with 90% cash, a 10% escrow for 12 months and a 100-day close than $15M from a first-time buyer with a financing condition.

**Terms on which he signs a letter of intent with the founder.**
- $15M enterprise value (7.5 times trailing adjusted EBITDA, 2.3 times gross profit, $0.33 per dollar of gross revenue), on a stock sale so the licences stay in place.
- $12.75M cash at closing. Seller note of $2.25M (15%), 9%, three years, second lien and a pledge of the shares.
- Proof of the full equity before exclusivity. A lender's term sheet at signing. $300K deposit that becomes non-refundable when the quality-of-earnings report is delivered. No financing condition after day 45. 60 days' exclusivity.
- No earn-out tied to gross revenue. He will accept up to 10% of price tied to gross profit, with a floor.
- He is released from every personal guarantee at closing.
- He stays 12-24 months as a consultant at $250K a year and makes the introductions to the top accounts and the overseas agents.

**What he says about the plan.** "If you tell my people that agents will remove a quarter of the cost, my four best account managers will be working for a competitor by spring, and their accounts go with them." He would require a 12-month no-layoff commitment or funded severance.

**Probability [sim].** He signs a letter of intent with the founder rather than a strategic buyer: 35%. Given a letter of intent, it closes: 55-60%. The usual failure is the gap between his peak-year price and the lender's normalised earnings.

**What would change it.** Committed equity of $13M in a bank account, a lender commitment letter, and a buyer who has already closed one deal.

## Persona 2. Owner of a $20M-gross-revenue customs brokerage with a small forwarding arm

**Who.** Aged 58. About 50 staff, six of them licensed. Roughly 60,000 entries a year [sim].

**His numbers [sim].** Brokerage fees $7.5M, booked net. Forwarding and drayage $12.5M gross with $1.6M of gross profit. Total gross profit $9.1M (45% of gross revenue). EBITDA $2.4M in 2026, of which about $0.5M is one-off IEEPA refund filing at roughly $10 an entry with a $500 minimum. Normalised EBITDA $1.8-1.9M.

**Why this matters to the brief.** The duty-recovery idea needs a book with many importer powers of attorney. The revenue target needs cheap pass-through freight. These are different firms. His revenue is mostly fees, so it costs $0.9-1.0 per dollar of gross revenue, two to three times the brief's $0.25-0.50. The $45M forwarder in persona 1 files entries for perhaps half of its 300 accounts [sim].

**Decision.** Refuses 5-8 times normalised EBITDA ($9-15M). He would sell at 8 times trailing EBITDA, $19M, 90% cash, no earn-out, to a forwarder that lacks customs capability. Buyers are acquiring customs capability as a differentiator [source: Lincoln; search summary of https://theloadstar.com/forwarders-face-margin-squeeze-as-growth-cools-and-disruption-persists/]. A seller note above 10% is refused.

**On duty recovery.** He already sells it, in the only forms his clients accept:
- Post-summary corrections at $85 an entry plus $175 an hour; protests at $350 plus hours.
- Drawback referred to a specialist that pays him 20% of its fee. Those clients are under contract to the specialist.
- "I will never charge a share of the refund for correcting an entry my own office filed. The client's lawyer would ask why it was wrong and send the bill to my errors-and-omissions insurer."

**What would change it.** Nothing on price. He is not a route to $40M of gross revenue at all: buying him adds $20M for $19M.

## Persona 3. Logistics M&A banker

**Incentives.** Paid on closed deals. Protects sellers from buyers who cannot fund.

**Who buys forwarders and brokers in 2026 [DK unless labelled].**
1. Private-equity-backed forwarding platforms doing tuck-ins [source: Lincoln].
2. Top-50 forwarders adding customs or a trade lane [source: Lincoln].
3. Asian and European forwarders buying a US licence, office and agent replacement.
4. Truck brokers and warehousing firms adding international.
5. Employee trusts and managers.
6. Independent sponsors and search funds. These are the founder's peer group and close the fewest.

**Prices [DK, with the sourced points shown].**

| Target | EBITDA multiple | Gross-profit multiple | Per dollar of gross revenue |
|---|---|---|---|
| Forwarder, EBITDA under $1M | 3.5-5 | 1.0-1.5 | $0.12-0.22 |
| Forwarder with brokerage, EBITDA $1-3M | 5.5-7.5 | 1.6-2.4 | $0.22-0.40 |
| Same, EBITDA $3-7M | 6.5-9 | 2.0-3.0 | $0.30-0.50 |
| Brokerage-heavy, EBITDA $1.5-4M | 7-9.5 | 1.8-2.6 | $0.70-1.10 |
| Platforms, EBITDA above $10M | 9-12 | n/a | n/a |

Sourced anchors: Navegate at six times [source: FreightWaves]; a marketing page calls forwarding the top multiple in logistics with third-party logistics at 5-10 times [source: https://ctacquisitions.com/?p=35413, search snippet; notes]. The brief's 5-8 times is right as a range. The dispute is which earnings it is applied to.

**Peak-year problem.** Sellers will bring 2026 accounts to market in February-April 2027. Buyers and lenders will use a three-year average and strip out refund-filing fees. On persona 1 that is $2.0M against $1.5-1.6M, a $3M gap at 7.5 times. The usual bridge is 20-30% of price deferred against gross profit for two years, which the seller resists. This gap, not regulation, is what delays closings in 2027.

**Supply [DK estimate].** Independent, founder-owned US forwarders holding a broker licence with $20-60M of gross revenue: 250-400 firms. About 3-5% change hands a year: 8-15 deals, half of them quiet sales to a known partner. In the $40-60M band: 80-120 firms and 3-6 sales a year. A marketed firm of this size draws 3-8 serious bids.

**Timing.** Seller engages banker to closing: 6-9 months. Letter of intent to closing: 75-120 days for a strategic buyer, 120-170 days for a buyer that must arrange a lender, a co-investor and a quality-of-earnings report. A stock sale needs notice to CBP, not prior approval [source: eCFR 111.28], and by my understanding notice to the Federal Maritime Commission within 30 days and a financial review by the air cargo settlement body [DK]. An asset sale would need new licences and permits and is not used.

**The founder's calendar.** Start 24 November 2026. Adviser and lender term sheets by month 1. Outreach months 1-4; processes on 2026 accounts open in months 3-5. Letter of intent month 3-5. Closing month 7-10. Median first closing: month 9.

**How a first-time buyer with $8M is treated.**
- Gets the information memorandum only after proof of funds.
- $8M of equity supports about $10-11M of enterprise value after fees and a working-capital cushion: a $25-32M-revenue forwarder. For $40M and above he needs a named co-investor before the letter of intent; a co-investor's own diligence adds 4-6 weeks.
- Ranked last on certainty. Wins exclusivity in about one marketed process in four, usually by paying 0.5-1 turn more or posting a deposit. Does better on direct approaches to owners who will not sell to a competitor.
- Buy-side mandate: $25K a month plus 2.5% of value, minimum $400K.

**Lender view [DK; notes for the 2.5 times figure].** A receivables line advances 80-85% of eligible invoices, but it already funds the gap between 45-day receivables and 20-day carrier payables. Senior lenders stop at 2-2.5 times normalised EBITDA for a business with no hard assets. On $1.5-1.6M that is $3-4M of senior debt, and it has to cover both the purchase draw and the seasonal working-capital draw. The seller note sits behind it.

## Persona 4. Licensed customs broker, the firm's qualifying officer

**Who.** Vice president of brokerage, aged 52, 19 years at the firm, $150K. One of four licence holders among 60 staff [sim]. She is the national permit qualifier and the person the clients' compliance staff call.

**Market for her.** Exam pass rates are 12-22% [notes]. Tariff changes in 2025-2026 raised demand for licence holders. She has offers at $180-220K from a larger forwarder and from an importer [sim].

**What the law puts on her [DK unless labelled].** The broker must exercise responsible supervision and control over customs business; CBP weighs factors such as training, reject rates, the ratio of licence holders to volume and how often the qualifier reviews work. Customs business must be done inside US customs territory. Penalties fall on the firm and can reach her individual licence. If the firm has no licensed officer for 120 days the licence lapses [notes].

**Decision.** Stays, on these terms:
- $195K salary, a $150K stay bonus paid over 24 months, 1.5% equity or the cash equivalent on a sale, 12 months' severance.
- A written policy, which she owns, on what agents may file without human review. Her opening position: agents extract documents, propose classifications and chase missing data; a licence holder or trained entry writer approves every new part and every exception; only repeat parts already in an approved parts database file without review.
- The number of licence holders is not cut. Errors-and-omissions cover names her.

**On the automation saving.** The filing software already auto-classifies repeat parts from a parts database, which is 70-85% of lines for established importers [DK]. The agent's gain is on the rest, plus quoting, tracking and document chasing. She sees 6-8 of 60 posts going by attrition over 18 months: 8-12% of operating cost, not 20-25%.

**If she leaves.** The licence survives because three other holders remain. But she takes 10-25 importer accounts worth 15-25% of brokerage fees within six months [sim]. A power of attorney is revocable at will and a new one is signed in a day [notes]. Non-compete clauses are weak or void in several states [DK].

**What would change it.** Being asked to sign off entries she has not reviewed. She leaves.

## Persona 5. Logistics manager, $400M-revenue consumer-goods importer (top-ten account)

**Who.** Buys about $9M of international freight a year across three forwarders and two direct carrier contracts [sim]. Gives this forwarder $3.0M of gross revenue (about 7% of the firm's total) and 2,500 entries a year. The company holds its own continuous bond and pays duty from its own account. Duties are about $30-35M a year at 2026 rules [sim from notes' rates].

**Incentives.** Cost per container and no stranded cargo. A forwarder that cannot pay a carrier leaves his containers held under the carrier's lien [DK].

**Decision after the change of owner.**
- No termination. Requalifies the vendor: financial statements, insurance certificates, security questionnaire, trusted-trader status.
- Sees acquisition debt on a thin-margin forwarder and moves 25% of his volume to his second forwarder at once.
- Rebids everything at the annual tender for contracts starting 1 May 2027. He does this every year anyway. With spot rates falling for four weeks from a peak [source], he expects 20-30% lower ocean rates.
- If told entries are prepared by agents: asks for the entry fee to fall from $95 to $65 and for the conditions in persona 8.

**Expected outcome [sim].** 70% chance the account stays at 75-90% of its volume. 30% chance it is lost at the tender. In dollars the account falls from $3.0M to $1.8-2.3M in the following twelve months, most of it from rates.

**Duty recovery from the broker.** Refuses a share-of-refund fee.
- Corrections on entries the broker filed: expected free, or at the per-entry charge.
- Drawback: Canadian sales are 6% of volume and a specialist has claimed them since 2020 at 9% [sim, consistent with notes' 8-12%]. The new layers (forced-labor duty, the 2026 surcharge) sit on the same entries and the specialist files them.
- IEEPA: refunded mid-2026 through this broker for a flat fee [notes].

**What would change it.** A drawback desk with a licensed specialist, three references and a quote below 8%. Then the broker is one of three bidders at the next renewal.

**Who.** Imports about $11M of customs value, 70 containers a year. Pays the forwarder about $420K a year at 2026 rates and about $2.0M of duty [sim]. The forwarder lays out some duty and freight on 30-day terms. One account manager knows his business.

## Persona 7. Export manager at a machinery maker

**Who.** Ships machines by ocean and air under letters of credit. Gives this forwarder about $1.2M a year. Chooses on lane knowledge, the overseas agent and the one operator who gets his documents right.

**Decision.** Stays, shipment by shipment. He already takes three quotes for each large move. Change of owner is irrelevant unless his operator leaves or the overseas agent changes. No contract, no commitment.

**Duty recovery.** This is the one account type where drawback is real: imported components, exported machines.
- Components $40M a year; duties $8M, most of it Section 232, where only manufacturing drawback on qualifying goods is allowed [notes]. Half the exports go to Canada and Mexico under the lower-of rule [notes: F3].
- Claimable: $0.5-0.8M a year plus about $1.5M of lookback, after a manufacturing ruling and privileges that take 6-10 months [notes].
- The imports are cleared by a different broker chosen by purchasing. The controller decides. He will make an introduction and nothing more.
- Controller takes three quotes at 10-12%. A broker with no drawback record wins perhaps one in four. Expected fee to the firm in 18 months: about $35K; $150K if won [sim].

**What would change it.** The firm also holding the import entries, and a named drawback specialist with rulings experience.

## Persona 8. Trade compliance lead, mid-size importer ($800M revenue, team of two)

**Incentives.** Paid for zero penalties. The importer of record owes reasonable care; a broker's error is her company's error. Negligence penalties run from 20% to 100% of the duty lost [notes: F3, 19 U.S.C. 1592]. With duties at three to four times 2024 levels [notes], each error costs three to four times as much, and CBP information requests and whistleblower exposure are up [notes: F3, Foley].

**On agent-prepared entries filed under her power of attorney.** Does not refuse. Every broker already uses a parts database and document capture, and three brokers have pitched her "AI entries" this year [sim]. To her it is a reason for a lower fee and a tighter contract, not a reason to choose a broker.

**Conditions.**
1. Agents classify only from her approved parts database. New parts come to her.
2. A licence holder audits 5% of entries each month and sends her the results. Error rate above 0.5% is a default.
3. A named licence holder on the account. Departure of the qualifying officer lets her terminate.
4. Processing in the United States, an independent security audit, no model training on her data, deletion on exit.
5. Errors-and-omissions cover of at least $5M naming her company, and the standard liability cap (by trade custom about $50 an entry [DK]) raised to at least 12 months of fees.
6. Entry fee down 15-25%.

**Decision.** Stays if conditions 1-5 are in the contract within 90 days of the change of owner. Otherwise rebids. A change of control is itself a trigger for her vendor audit.

**Duty recovery from the broker.** Refuses contingency. She orders corrections herself at the per-entry fee and uses a drawback specialist at 6-8%.

**What would change it.** Twelve months of audited error rates from the agent workflow below the firm's human baseline.

## Persona 9. Sales manager at an ocean carrier or consolidator that gives the forwarder credit

**Who.** Holds the forwarder's service contract (about 1,500 forty-foot containers a year) and a $600K credit limit on 21-day terms, supported by 15 years of payment history and the seller's personal guarantee [sim; DK that guarantees are common].

**Incentives.** His quota is volume, so he wants the account kept. Credit decisions belong to a credit department that treats a change of control at a private customer as a new customer [DK].

**What credit sees.** An opening balance sheet with $4M of new debt, goodwill in place of equity, negative tangible net worth, and no personal guarantor.

**Decision.** The contract and rates run to 30 April 2027. Credit either:
- cuts the limit to $300K and terms to 15 days for six months; or
- keeps the limit against a $400-500K standby letter of credit or the founder's personal guarantee, with quarterly financial statements.

**Across all suppliers [sim].** Two carriers and two co-loaders act the same way. The air cargo settlement body reviews finances after a change of owner and can ask for a guarantee [DK]. Truckers do not change. The one-off working-capital need is $0.8-1.5M in the first 90 days, at the moment the receivables line has been drawn to pay the seller.

**His view of 2027.** Spot rates have fallen four weeks running from a level 2.2 times a year ago [source]. He is guiding customers to trans-Pacific contract rates 20-35% lower from May 2027, on new ship deliveries and a possible return to Suez [source: Lincoln for the direction; the percentage is DK]. A forwarder's gross revenue follows with a lag.

**What would change it.** The seller staying on as guarantor for six months (persona 1 refuses), or equity left in the company at closing.

## Persona 10. Surety and customs-bond underwriter

**What is bonded [DK for amounts].**
- The firm's own bonds: ocean intermediary bonds with the maritime regulator (about $75K and $50K), a CBP international carrier bond ($50-100K), a custodial bond if it runs a container station ($100K). Total penal sum $225-325K. Premium is a few thousand dollars.
- The clients' bonds: about 250 importer continuous bonds placed through the firm as the surety's agent, earning it $60-120K a year in commission [sim]. Bond sizes rose with duties in 2025-2026 [source: Foley, Trucordia].
- Drawback bonds, needed for accelerated payment, are in each claimant's name and sized to the claims [notes].

**On a change of owner.** The bonds belong to the legal entity and continue after a stock sale. The indemnity agreement behind them was signed by the seller and his spouse. The underwriter continues cover for 60-90 days, then requires:
- a new indemnity from the company and from the founder personally; or
- cash or a letter of credit for 100% of the firm's own bonds, $225-325K.
Three to five weeks of underwriting. It is a closing condition, not an obstacle.

**Where bonds do bite.**
- Duty outlays. If the firm pays duty for a quarter of its book, it has $0.5-1.0M outstanding at any time at 2026 duty levels, unsecured against small importers [sim]. Before 2025 the same practice tied up a third of that.
- Duty recovery. Each drawback claimant needs its own bond for accelerated payment. For small importers with thin balance sheets he asks for 25-100% collateral or declines. That removes accelerated payment for 30-40% of small accounts, whose refunds then wait one to three years [notes for the wait; share is sim].

**What would change it.** Audited statements showing positive tangible net worth after the purchase.

## What the panel implies

### Gross revenue and the rate cycle

- Spot ocean rates are 2.2 times a year ago and have fallen for four weeks [source]. A $45M trailing figure is a peak-year figure. On persona 1's history the same firm did $33M, $41M and $36M in 2023-2025 [sim].
- Ocean freight is 50-60% of a forwarder's gross revenue; about a third of a spot-rate move reaches gross revenue within a year because of contract rates, air, trucking and fees [DK].

| 2027 case | Probability | Ocean rates against 2026 average | Gross revenue of the "$45M" firm before client losses |
|---|---|---|---|
| Rates normalise | 55% | down 25-40% | $38-41M |
| Rates hold | 30% | within 15% | $43-46M |
| New disruption | 15% | up | $47-52M |

- Client losses after a sale take a further 4-8% in year one.

## Does anything accumulate?

Little. The licences and client powers of attorney transfer with the shares and are worth what was paid for them. Powers of attorney are revocable. Freight is rebid yearly or per shipment. Recovery adds a few hundred thousand dollars of margin. Automation savings are shared with clients and matched by rivals. What grows is gross revenue, at the price of equity.
