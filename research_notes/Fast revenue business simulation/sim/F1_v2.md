# F1 v2: Expert quality control for long-horizon agent work, with data resale second (organic, redesign after round one)

## What changed and why

| Round-one objection | Change in v2 |
|---|---|
| Labs do not sign quarterly licences. Data is bought one corpus at a time. | Lead product is now a service the labs order every week: expert review of long agent runs, billed by the hour. Data is sold opportunistically, per corpus. |
| Data owners want fixed cash, which makes the company a principal buyer with inventory risk against rivals holding up to $1bn | The company buys a corpus only against a signed first order that covers its cost. No speculative purchases. |
| In the chosen domains the seller lacks title to its clients' information | Corpus work is limited to owners with clean title: a firm's own internal work, and archives sold in insolvency under court order. Regulated client screens are dropped. |
| Gross margin is 5% with one buyer per corpus | Corpus margin is no longer the plan. Quality control at a 30-35% gross margin is. |
| Five buyers at $2M a quarter is not how this market buys | Target is two to three labs with standing review programs, plus smaller buyers |

## What is sold
1. A standing bench of senior practitioners who review multi-hour agent runs against a rubric and the real outcome, find the step where the run went wrong, and write the correction. Domains: corporate finance and accounting, insurance, software operations. Research notes and both round-one panels say quality control for long-horizon tasks is short, and that finding experts is easy while managing them and controlling quality is hard.
2. Per-corpus sales of real work data with clean title, bought only against a first order.

## Standard unit
One reviewed agent-hour, billed gross.

## Price and contract
- $140-220 per expert hour (round-one panels' range). Experts are paid 60-65% of that.
- Standing weekly programs on purchase orders, terminable at 30 days.
- Corpus sales $0.5-1.5M each, one-off.

## Founder's assumptions
- $40M of gross revenue is about 220,000 billed hours a year at $180: roughly 250-350 part-time experts and two or three labs running programs of $10-20M a year each.
- The round-one delivery panel's own base case was $15M of trailing revenue at month 18 and $50M in year three for v1. The founder believes weekly billed hours ramp faster than one-off corpus sales.
- Working capital: experts are paid in two to four weeks, labs pay in 45-60 days. $5-10M covers about $40M of annual billing.
- Differentiation offered: reviewers work inside a measured calibration system with inter-rater agreement reported per program, and the founder's finance background supplies senior reviewers in a domain the notes say is in demand.

## Known weak points the founder wants tested
- The largest marketplaces already supply expert hours at over $1bn of run-rate each. Why would a lab add a small vendor?
- Is "quality control of long-horizon runs" a separate budget, or the same expert-hours purchase under another name?
- One buyer pausing removes half of revenue in a week.
- Gross margin of 30-35% on resold labor: is this a product company?
- Do labs bring review in-house or automate it with models?
