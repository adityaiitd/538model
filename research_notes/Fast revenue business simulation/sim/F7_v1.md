# F7 v1: Buy a freight forwarder with a customs brokerage, run it with AI agents, and sell duty recovery to its importers (acquisition-led, new after round two)

## Why this candidate enters now
Round two showed that bought revenue reaches the bar only where it costs well under one times revenue. A panel adviser ranked nine service industries for this play and put freight forwarding with customs brokerage first: about $0.25-0.50 per dollar of gross revenue, client retention of 85-92% because of powers of attorney and compliance history, and entry and document work that agents can take over now. The same adviser said plainly that the revenue is cheap because most of it passes through to carriers, and that this "satisfies a revenue target, not a wealth target".

It also uses what the customs-recovery candidate (F3) lacked. F3's panels said the importer's broker holds the entry data and the relationship, that a licensed drawback broker is the scarce input, and that a broker keeps the recovery work on its best accounts. Here the company is that broker.

## What is sold
1. International freight forwarding under the company's own bills of lading (ocean and air), booked as gross revenue.
2. Customs brokerage: entries filed for importers under power of attorney.
3. New after closing: duty recovery for existing importer clients (drawback and corrections), paid as 12-15% of refunds.

## Standard unit
One shipment moved and one customs entry filed.

## Entry and plan
1. Buy one independent forwarder and licensed broker with $40-55M of gross revenue, $5-8M of gross profit and $1.8-3M of EBITDA, for $10-18M (5-8 times EBITDA, adviser's range). Funding: receivables line at 80-85% advance (about $4M), seller note 15-20%, cash equity $7-10M from the founder's $8M and, if needed, a co-investor.
2. Keep the licensed officers and account managers. Move entry preparation, tariff classification, document handling, tracking and quoting to agents with licensed sign-off. Target: operating cost down 20-25%.
3. Start duty recovery on the existing book in the first 90 days.
4. Buy $50-60M more gross revenue by month 36 for $15-30M.

## Founder's assumptions
- Gross margin 13-17% of gross revenue. EBITDA 4.5-6% today, 7-9% after automation and recovery fees.
- Trailing revenue of $40M at month 18 needs a $50M business closed by month 8, or a $40M business closed by month 6.
- Year-three enterprise value on $100M of gross revenue: $35-45M at today's margins (adviser), more if the margin gain holds.

## Known weak points the founder wants tested
- Are forwarders of this size for sale at these prices in late 2026, and how long does a first-time buyer take to close?
- Do the licensed officers and the top accounts stay?
- Gross revenue moves with ocean and air rates and with tariff-driven import volumes. What does a rate fall do to the revenue target?
- Duty outlays, cargo liability, bonded status and working capital.
- Is the automation saving real, and do software vendors hand the same tools to every forwarder?
- Is duty recovery on the existing book worth anything, given the F3 panels' numbers?
- Is this a product company, and is any equity value created after the purchases?
