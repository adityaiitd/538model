No, on any version of the numbers [calc].

## Delivery personas

### 1. Sales director, maker of composite CNG modules (largest North American maker archetype), with a steel-tube maker's view

- **Incentives and constraints.** One customer has just placed a record $100M order with a $25M option to 2028 [source: euronext link above]. Contracted demand runs into 2027 [source: quartr link above]. Wants a second and third customer to reduce concentration, but will not risk the anchor's delivery schedule or extend credit to a company with no revenue.
- **Alternatives.** Sell remaining 2027 slots to existing fleet operators with credit history, who are all asking.
- **Decision: accepts a purchase order on its terms. Does not hold slots without cash.**
- **Price.** About $0.60-0.66M per 40 ft, 450 Mscf module on a chassis for a 50-unit first order [DK]. The larger 45 ft module is allocated to the anchor customer first. Its 58,200 lb module weight [notes] probably puts the combination over 80,000 lb, so it needs overweight routing [DK].
- **Terms.** 30% non-refundable at order, 60% before shipment, 10% on acceptance. No rental pool worth mentioning. No assignment of slots.
- **Lead time for a new buyer ordering now.** 10 units in Q3 2027, 15 in Q4 2027, 25 in the first half of 2028 [DK]. So 20-25 of 50 inside 12 months, all 50 in 14-18 months.
- **Second composite maker.** 5-8 units a month from about month 7, similar price, shorter service record [DK].
- **Steel tube trailers.** $0.25-0.30M, 16-24 weeks, but 120-180 Mscf a load [DK]. That triples loads and drivers. Workable for commissioning gas, not for a 35 MW block.
- **Disputes.** $0.5M per trailer is low by 20-30%. The founder's deposit alone on 50 units is $9.3-9.9M, which is the whole of the founder's starting capital.
- **What changes the answer.** A larger deposit (50%) or a framework order of 150 or more units moves the buyer up the queue by one quarter at most.

### 2. Supplier of compression and decanting equipment; and of small-scale liquefaction and cryogenic storage

**Compression and decant**
- **Decision: sells on deposit. Offers no financing and no performance guarantee beyond equipment warranty.**
- **Scope for 7.3 MMscf/d.** About 850-1,000 hp of average duty from a 700 psi inlet, 1,400 hp from 250 psi, 2,000 hp from a 60 psi distribution main [calc, DK]. Installed with a spare: three 1,000 hp electric packages.
- **Price.** $1.6-2.3M per package, $0.8M dryer, $1.6M for six chilled fill posts, $1.5M for the 3 MW electrical service, about $14M installed with civil works and the tap contribution. Decant equipment at the site $2.5-3.5M [DK].
- **Lead time.** 36-52 weeks for packaged compressors. The hub's own 3 MW electrical service waits on a padmount transformer at 40-65 weeks [notes B1]. Gas-engine drives avoid that but engines are short for the same reason the customer exists, and they need an air permit.
- **Rental.** A few mobile compression units at $60-90K a month each, enough for 5-10 MW [DK].
- **Disputes.** $8-12M for compression and decant is low. Panel: $17M.

**Liquefaction and cryogenic storage**
- **Decision: sells storage tanks and vaporisers on deposit. Will not quote a liquefier a start-up can fund.**
- A 50,000-100,000 gallon-a-day liquefier costs $60-120M and takes 24-30 months [DK]. Stabilis's larger Galveston plant needs $350-400M [notes].
- Merchant liquefied gas for resale is scarce. Stabilis is serving much of its own contract with third-party supply [notes]. Plant-gate price when available about $7-10 per MMBtu [DK].
- Backup storage for 35 MW for 72 hours: about 22,300 MMBtu, about 270,000 gallons, fifteen 18,000-gallon tanks plus vaporisers, $7-10M, tanks at 30-50 weeks [DK, calc]. Stored liquid boils off at 0.1-0.3% a day and must be burned or vented [DK].
- A capacity fee that returns 15% on that equipment is about $1.5-2.5M a year per 35 MW site [calc]. No named customer for standing backup exists [notes]. This second product is too small to carry the $100M target.

### 3. Pipeline or local distribution company manager asked for a tap and firm supply

- **Incentives and constraints.** Tariff rules, creditworthiness rules, human-needs priority in winter, and a preference for long firm contracts. The affiliate that builds laterals is the same company.
- **Decision.**
  - Interstate pipeline: **yes to an interconnect, no to firm capacity where the line is fully subscribed.** Facilities study 2-4 months, tap and meter station 6-12 months, shipper prepays $1-3M. Interruptible or secondary-firm service only [DK].
  - Distribution company: **yes to interruptible transport service in 6-10 months with prepaid facilities; firm only with a 5-10 year minimum bill or a system upgrade payment** [DK].
  - Texas intrastate line: fastest, 3-6 months, but wants a 3-5 year minimum volume [DK].
- **Credit.** An unrated shipper posts three months of charges in cash or a letter of credit [DK].
- **Reasons.** 7,500 Dth a day is small and short. A 24-month bridge does not justify firm upgrades. The hub would be curtailed in exactly the cold snap when roads are also worst. Nexus Hubbard shows firm gas, not pipe, is the limit at many sites [notes].
- **What changes the answer.** A 5-year minimum bill backed by a creditworthy guarantor. The founder cannot give it against a 24-month customer contract.
- **Note.** The request tells the midstream company where the demand is.

### 4. Hazardous-materials carrier and driver-staffing manager

- **Decision: signs a dedicated-fleet contract on its terms.** Twelve-month minimum, 60 days to start, fuel surcharge, detention at $95 an hour after the first hour. The shipper trains drivers on connection and decant.
- **Hours of service.** 11 hours driving in a 14-hour duty window, 70 hours in 8 days. Loads per driver shift: three at 50 miles, two at 100 miles, one to one and a half at 150 miles [DK].
- **Staffing for one block at 80% load [calc].** 9 drivers at 50 miles, 14 at 100 miles, 22 at 150 miles. Seventeen at 100 miles and full load. Seven or eight tractors with spares.
- **Cost per load [DK].**

| One-way miles | Own fleet | Third-party carrier | Per MMBtu at 400 a load |
|---|---|---|---|
| 50 | $380-430 | $520-620 | $0.95-1.55 |
| 100 | $600-650 | $900-1,050 | $1.50-2.60 |
| 150 | $900-1,000 | $1,350-1,550 | $2.25-3.90 |

- **Reasons.** Drivers with hazardous-materials and tank endorsements are short in the data center corridors. Certarus's own operating cost rose to $8.13 "primarily due to the increased use of third-party drivers" [notes]. Turnover in this work runs 60-90% a year [DK]. Carrier needs $5M of auto liability for bulk flammable gas under 49 CFR 387.9 [DK].
- **What changes the answer.** A 24-month commitment with a volume floor cuts the rate about 8%.

### 5. State and federal regulators

- **Decision: no refusal in principle. The calendar is 3-12 months and cannot be bought down.**
- **Federal [DK unless stated].**
  - USDOT number and hazardous-materials registration: days to weeks.
  - Composite modules run under manufacturer approvals and special permits [notes]. The operator must comply with every condition and carry a security plan.
  - A new carrier gets a new-entrant safety audit inside its first 12 months. Whether the federal safety permit for bulk methane applies depends on cylinder size and needs counsel's ruling. It does apply to liquefied gas tankers.
  - Gross weight limit 80,000 lb. The largest module likely needs state overweight permits [notes, DK].
- **State and local [DK unless stated].**
  - Texas: Railroad Commission licence, certified staff, registered transports and site plan review, 2-4 months [notes for the rule set].
  - Other states: fire marshal review under NFPA 52 and 55 [notes], zoning or conditional-use approval for a hub with 30-40 heavy truck movements a day, 4-9 months, longer if contested.
  - Air permit for the hub: none or permit-by-rule with electric drives. With gas-engine drives, 1-2 months in Texas and 3-6 months elsewhere, longer in non-attainment areas.
  - On-site storage of loaded trailers at the customer's site: local authority's call. No published rule found [notes gap].
- **In a state that refused a lateral.** The parties who stopped the pipe will contest the hub's zoning and the customer's air permit. Project Jupiter's air applications drew about 7,155 comments and a court stay [notes]. A 100 MW site means about 100 truck movements a day on county roads, which invites a road-use fight.
- **Realistic total.** Texas 3-5 months. Elsewhere 6-12 months.

### 6. Veteran operations manager from an established virtual-pipeline company

- **Real cost.** $5.50-6.50 per MMBtu of field cost on a dedicated 60-80 mile data center run with employed drivers. $7-9.50 all-in at 100-150 miles or with contract drivers [DK]. Agrees with the panel's $7.90 for a single block.
- **Utilisation.** Fleet average is about a quarter of a load per trailer per day [notes]. A dedicated data center run reaches 0.3-0.4 [calc]. Site buffer of 12-24 hours ties up 10-18 full trailers.
- **What kills new entrants [DK].**
  1. One hub, no backup. A compressor trip, wet gas or a pipeline pressure drop stops deliveries, and the customer's site goes dark within hours. Incumbents cover from a second hub.
  2. Pipeline-arrival clauses. Take-or-pay usually falls away or drops to a small standby fee 60-90 days after the lateral is in service. The headline term is not the firm term.
  3. Winter. Curtailment at the tap and closed roads arrive together.
  4. Drivers. Turnover, then a rollover.
  5. One serious incident ends a single-contract company. Relief-device releases and regulator freeze-ups from heater failure are routine events that need trained crews.
  6. Price falls when the oilfield softens and idle fleets chase the same work. Wellsite pricing fell in early 2026 [notes].
- **Redeployment.** Trailers move in a day. Mobile compression moves in a week. A fixed hub is stranded unless other customers sit within 150 miles. Gaps between jobs run 2-5 months. Winter utility standby and summer pipeline-outage work fill gaps at $350-600 per trailer-day [DK]. In the last downturn used composite trailers changed hands at 50-60% of new [DK].
- **View.** "A block" is not a business. A hub network is.

- **Reasons.** No loss history. Heavy-truck verdicts. A flammable gas in bulk.
- **What changes the answer.** Hauling through an established carrier with its own $5M and three clean years, or an experienced management team, opens $25M. Two clean years cut the premium by a quarter.
- **Gap this leaves.** Customers ask for $25-50M. Year-one capacity is $10-15M. The contract cannot be signed until that gap is closed.

### 8. Senior operations or engineering leader considering joining as co-founder

- **Position.** Vice president of operations at an established operator. $300-400K plus long-term incentives. Twelve-month non-compete and non-solicit [DK].
- **Decision: declines to join before a contract or committed capital exists.**
- **Terms to say yes.** A signed anchor contract or $30M of committed equity. 8-12% of the equity, $350K salary, indemnity against a non-compete claim, sole authority over safety and the right to refuse a job.
- **Reasons.** The employer is growing and has the trailer allocation. The start-up has no fleet, no hub and no loss history. The founder's block economics do not survive the corrections in section 0.
- **Counter-proposal on their terms.** Buy a regional operator with 60-100 trailers, working hubs, permits, drivers and a loss record, then grow it. That is not the organic plan in this brief.
- **Consequence.** Without this person, personas 7, 9, 10 and 12 all say no.

## Capital and competition personas

### 9. Equipment lender asked for 60% against trailers and compression gear on a 12-30 month contract

- **Decision: no at 60% of block cost. Conditional yes at about 40% of hard equipment cost.**
- **Terms [DK].**
  - Trailers: 75-80% of appraised orderly liquidation value. Value at delivery is about 60-65% of cost because two or three buyers make the whole resale market. Result: about 48% of cost.
  - Compression: 50% of skid cost. Nothing against installation, civil works or the tap.
  - Blended: about $19-22M on $48M of hard cost.
  - 9.5-12% all-in, 1.5% fee, funds at delivery only, six-month reserve, coverage of 1.3 times.
  - Balance at contract end must not exceed liquidation value, or a termination payment must cover it.
  - Assignment of the contract, step-in rights, sponsor equity in first, parent guarantee.
- **Reasons.** The contract is 12-30 months and the asset lives 20 years. Full amortisation inside the contract costs more than the EBITDA. The pipeline-arrival clause makes the term shorter than stated. The counterparty may be a development vehicle or a neocloud, not the hyperscaler. Asset-based lending in this field has gone to operators with fleets and history: VoltaGrid's $3.0B revolver and Caterpillar Financial's $172M to ProPetro [notes].
- **What changes the answer.** An investment-grade counterparty with a non-cancellable term and a termination payment equal to the outstanding debt brings the advance to 60-65% and the rate to 8-9%.

### 10. Private equity or infrastructure investor asked for $10-15M of equity per block

- **Infrastructure fund: refuses.** A 12-30 month contract on a 20-year asset leaves over 70% of value in uncontracted years. That is outside the mandate.
- **Energy private equity: refuses the per-block deal. Would sign a platform term sheet on its own terms.**
  - $50-75M commitment drawn against signed contracts.
  - 60-80% ownership and board control. 8% preferred return. Founder promote of 10-15%, vesting on returns.
  - Conditions: a veteran chief operating officer in place, an anchor contract with a creditworthy counterparty and a termination payment, and insurance bound.
  - Stated preference: buy an existing operator as the platform.
- **Reasons [calc].** Real equity per block is $25-30M, not $10-15M, once the lender is at 40% and the customer advance is uncertain. A block yields 8-13% EBITDA on capital. The comparable trade was 8.5 times EBITDA for the market leader [notes]. Building at 8-12 times with ramp risk offers no discount. A 20-25% target return needs redeployment at full margin for seven years or a sale at a higher multiple.
- **What changes the answer.** EBITDA above 22% of capital on a signed contract, or a contract whose fixed payments repay the debt and half the equity inside its term.

### 11. Incumbents responding rationally

**(a) Largest virtual-pipeline operator, 919 trailers going to about 1,000, 25 hubs [notes]**
- Bids every data center request above 20 MW at $9-11 above commodity, with a second hub as backup and a parent balance sheet behind damages clauses.
- Is capacity-constrained through 2027, so it will not cut price to block an entrant. It does not need to. It holds the trailer allocation and the option for $25M more [notes].
- Response to an entrant that wins a contract: recruit its drivers and crew, offer the entrant's customer a backup supply agreement, and bid for the trailers when the contract ends.
- May sub-hire the entrant's trailers for overflow at $350-500 per trailer-day [DK]. That is revenue for the entrant on the incumbent's terms.

**(b) Liquefied natural gas supplier with its own liquefaction**
- One tanker carries about 830 MMBtu against 400 for a compressed module [DK]. Beyond about 150 miles, or where days of on-site storage matter, liquefied gas wins. Wins the long-term backup product for the same reason.
- Has shown a small company can win $100M a year with a $25M advance [notes], but as a listed company with 55,000 deliveries behind it [notes].
- Will not sell liquid to a competing entrant except at spot when it has spare volume.

**(c) Midstream company**
- Wants the 10-20 year firm contract on the lateral. Bundles bridge fuel through a subcontractor or its own portable liquefied gas product [notes] to win that contract.
- Controls the tap the entrant needs and sets its timing and price.
- For a 35 MW site, a 10-mile lateral at about $3.9M a mile [notes] costs about $39M against a trucking premium of about $22M a year [calc]. The customer builds the pipe as fast as land rights allow. The bridge is 12-24 months [notes].

**(d) Behind-the-meter power providers that do fuel logistics themselves**
- Own the customer and sell power per kilowatt-hour with fuel inside. One hauled its own gas in Texas. Another is the buyer on the Stabilis contract [notes].
- **Decision on the entrant: purchase orders for overflow hauling only.** 30-90 days, $7.00-8.50 above commodity, no advance, no take-or-pay.
- This is the entrant's most likely first revenue, and it is one-off.

**What the entrant would control that incumbents do not.** Nothing structural today. The only things it could own are a permitted hub with a tap next to a cluster where no incumbent has one, and delivery slots for trailers if it orders before it has a customer. Both require spending most of the founder's $5-10M before any contract exists.

### 12. New independent buyer: developer of a 100 MW site in a state where a lateral has been refused

- **Need.** 21,200 MMBtu a day at full load, about 53 loads a day, about 150 trailers, $80-90M a year [calc].
- **Constraints.** The tenant and the project lenders must accept the fuel supplier. The developer is a project vehicle with thin credit.
- **Decision: refuses to give primary supply to a first-time operator. Awards it to the largest operator or a liquefied gas supplier.**
- **What it would sign with the entrant.** A secondary tranche of about 10 MW-equivalent, 2,100 MMBtu a day, about $9M a year gross:
  - 12 months, $9.00 above commodity, take-or-pay on 70% of volume.
  - No advance. Start conditional on a working hub and 15 trailers by a fixed date.
  - 30-day termination for missed deliveries. 60-day termination when any pipeline arrives.
  - $25M of liability cover.
- **On the 12% advance.** Refused without a bank guarantee or title to the trailers. Counter-offer: the developer owns the equipment and pays the entrant an operating fee. That is asset-light, but gross revenue falls to a few million dollars a year.
- **Reasons.** A month of idle servers costs far more than any fuel discount, so the buyer pays for certainty, and certainty means a record. The buyer's own weak credit then blocks the entrant's lender (persona 9).
- **What changes the answer.** Twelve months of incident-free delivery elsewhere, a second hub, a parent guarantee, or the incumbent being unable to deliver before the tenant's deadline.
