# F6 v1: Buy medical billing companies at about one times revenue and run them with AI agents (acquisition-led, new after round one)

## Why this candidate enters now
Round one tested five businesses against the bar of $40M of gross revenue by month 18. Organic versions reached about $12-15M in the panels' base cases. The acquisition-led version (hospital payment recovery) failed on price: those firms cost 2.5-3.3 times revenue, so $40M of revenue needed $80-100M of purchases. The arithmetic only works where revenue can be bought for about one times revenue or less and where AI changes the cost base.

In the first study, panels on an AI billing start-up said three things that point here. A veteran operator would sell a billing firm at 6-9 times EBITDA and advised the founder to buy one and automate it. A roll-up could buy billing companies at about one times revenue and get live accounts with no sales cycle. And clients fear the cash dip of switching vendors, which makes existing accounts sticky.

## What is sold
Outsourced medical billing for physician groups, surgery centers and post-acute providers: eligibility, coding, claim submission, denial follow-up, payment posting, patient statements. Priced as a percentage of collections.

## Standard unit
One claim taken from encounter to cash.

## Price and contract
The acquired companies' existing contracts: 3-7% of collections depending on client size (first-study panels: 2.5-4% for large groups, 5-8% for small practices). One-to-three-year terms.

## Entry and plan
1. Buy one or two billing companies totalling $35-45M of revenue within 9-12 months, at about one times revenue (6-7 times EBITDA at a 15% margin). Funding for $40M of purchases: lender 2.5-3 times EBITDA (about $16M), seller notes 15% ($6M), equity $18M: the founder's $8M plus a co-investor.
2. Keep client-facing staff. Move claim status checks, payment posting, eligibility, denial triage and appeal drafting to AI agents with human sign-off. Target: cost to serve from about 85% of revenue to 60-65% within 18 months.
3. Use cash flow, debt and $15-20M more equity to buy $50-60M more revenue by month 36.

## Founder's assumptions
- $40M of trailing revenue by month 18 requires owning about $40M of revenue from month 6. That needs the first closing by month 5-6.
- Client loss after a change of owner under 10% a year.
- Fee pressure of about half a point of collections over three years.
- No new-logo sales are needed to reach the targets.

## Known weak points the founder wants tested
- Are billing companies of $15-40M revenue for sale at one times revenue in late 2026, and can a first-time buyer close one in six months?
- Will lenders and a co-investor fund it?
- Do clients stay, and do they demand a lower fee once they know agents do the work?
- Is the cost reduction real, given records-vendor access limits and compliance sign-off?
- Records vendors bundle billing. Does the acquired book shrink under the buyer?
- Funded AI roll-ups and the AI labs' deployment ventures are buying the same firms.
- Is there a better industry for this exact play: cheaper revenue, stickier clients, prices that do not fall as costs fall?
- After paying for the acquisitions, is any equity value created?
