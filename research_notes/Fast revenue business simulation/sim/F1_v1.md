# F1 v1: Real-work data for AI labs, licensed from the enterprises that own it (organic)

## What is sold
Two products for frontier AI labs and large model developers.
1. Licensed datasets of real professional work, bought from the enterprises that own it: de-identified workflow traces (screen and tool recordings with outcomes), finished work files with revision history, and decision records. First domains: finance operations, insurance claims, accounting close, customer operations.
2. Expert quality control of long-horizon agent work: senior practitioners grade multi-hour agent runs against the real outcome.

Research notes say buyers report shortage in exactly these two things: observed expert behaviour on real work, and quality control for long-horizon tasks. Supply of expert headcount is not short.

## Supply side
Enterprises and outsourcing operators are paid a revenue share (35% of sales of their data) or a fixed fee, under a licence that lets the company resell non-exclusively. Employees consent and are paid a bonus for capture. The company does privacy processing, de-identification, formatting and quality control.

## Standard unit
One verified hour of real work trace with its outcome, or one graded agent run.

## Price and contract
- Dataset licences: $1-5M per quarter per buyer per domain, non-exclusive (founder's assumption; notes say environment contracts run six to seven figures per quarter).
- Quality control: $250-400 per expert hour, billed gross.
- Quarterly orders. No long-dated obligations.

## Founder's assumptions
- Five buyers averaging $2M a quarter reach $40M a year. The founder's enterprise sales skill is used on both sides: signing data owners and selling to labs.
- Gross margin 40-45% after the data owner's share and processing.
- The founder's $5-10M pays for advances to data owners, privacy tooling and a quality team.
- Precedents in the notes: AfterQuery reached a $100M run-rate in 14-15 months with about 30 staff. Fleet went from $1M to $60M annualized in about six months. Total supplier revenue to labs is about $7-9bn gross in 2026.

## Known weak points the founder wants tested
- Will enterprises sell traces of their work at all? Client confidentiality, privacy law, employee consent, works councils.
- Will labs buy from a new supplier, or get this from existing suppliers, their own enterprise customers, or in-house capture?
- One buyer can pause overnight (Meta paused a large supplier after a breach in 2026).
- Labs' payment terms and the cash needed to advance data owners.
- Is anything owned here that lasts past two years?
