# F1 v3: Expert review of long agent runs, delivered by firm-employed teams and priced per accepted review (organic, second redesign)

## What changed and why

| Round-two objection | Change in v3 |
|---|---|
| Undifferentiated entry at the incumbents' price. Reviewers work for several vendors. | Supply comes from firms, not gig individuals: contracts with mid-size accounting, advisory and claims firms and outsourcers whose employed, cleared staff review in a secured environment on managed devices. The firm is the paid supplier. |
| Employed senior practitioners need their employer's approval | Their employer is the contracting party |
| Reviewer count is three times too low at five hours a week | Firm teams work 20-30 hours a week in blocks |
| Buyers want price per accepted review, not hours | Priced per accepted review with an agreed rubric and acceptance rate |
| Two or three labs is too concentrated, and one pause removes half of revenue | Second buyer group added: companies that build agents for finance, accounting and insurance, and large enterprises testing agents before deployment. The round-one enterprise-software persona placed a trial order and offered $0.8-1.5M a year. |
| After a supplier breach in 2026 a lab paused a vendor overnight | Security is the lead claim: employed staff, managed devices, audited rooms, no personal machines |
| Gross margin 22-30% | Unchanged. This is resold expert time with a quality system. The founder accepts that. |

## What is sold
Review of multi-hour agent runs in corporate finance, accounting, insurance and software operations. A reviewer finds where the run went wrong, grades it against a rubric and the real outcome, and writes the correction. Sold to AI labs and to companies building or deploying agents in those fields.

## Standard unit
One accepted review of one agent run.

## Price and contract
- $350-600 per accepted review of a multi-hour run (a round-two buyer's range), or $135-190 per hour where the buyer insists.
- Supplier firms are paid 65-70% of billings.
- Weekly programs on purchase orders, 30-day termination.

## Founder's plan and assumptions
- Month 1-3: sign three supplier firms and pass one lab's security review. Month 4-9: trials and first standing programs.
- The round-two delivery panel's base case for v2 was $18M of trailing revenue at month 18 with quarter-six revenue of $7.5M, a $30M annualized rate, and $55M in year three. Its high case, at 15%, was $45M trailing and $140M in year three.
- Target for v3: one lab makes the company a primary vendor in one domain at $10-20M a year, two others run $3-6M programs, and eight to ten agent companies and enterprises buy $0.5-1.5M each.
- Cash to a $40M annual rate: $11-14M, with a receivables line after two quarters.

## Known weak points the founder wants tested
- Will accounting and advisory firms supply staff for this, at what price, and will they then sell to labs directly?
- Does firm-employed, secured review win primary-vendor status, or do labs not care?
- How large is the agent-company and enterprise budget for outside review in 2027?
- Do model judges take the volume before month 18?
- Does anything accumulate?
