# F3 v1, round 1: delivery, capital and competition panel

Date of simulation: 24 November 2026. Everything below is a simulated hypothesis by archetype personas, not customer validation. No real person is quoted.

Evidence labels: **[notes]** = `contingency_recovery.md` Parts A and C; **[source: URL]** = checked in this session; **[DK]** = domain knowledge, unverified; **[est]** = my own arithmetic, with inputs stated.

Web checks used (6 of 6). Three returned something useful:
- Section 232 metals: from 6 April 2026 the duty is charged on **full customs value**, 50% (Annex I-A) or 25% (Annex I-B), "regardless of the actual value of the embedded metal content". Goods outside Chapters 72, 73, 74 and 76 with under 15% metal by weight are excluded. The article expects more CF-28 requests, audits, penalties and False Claims Act risk. [source: https://www.foley.com/insights/publications/2026/05/what-every-multinational-should-know-about-the-new-rules-for-section-232-tariffs-on-steel-aluminum-and-copper-derivatives/]
- Bonds: on 16 June 2026 CBP turned drawback bond validation errors from informational to fatal, and an accelerated payment is removed from a claim that fails (CSMS 68974644, as summarised by the search tool, not read in full) [source: https://content.govdelivery.com/accounts/USDHSCBP/bulletins/41c7834]. Sureties ask weaker importers for letters of credit of up to 110% of the bond limit; customs bond insufficiencies were about $3.6B in FY2025 [source: https://www.foley.com/insights/publications/2026/06/what-every-multinational-should-know-about-the-new-customs-enforcement-realities-part-i-managing-rising-bond-and-collateral-requirements/].
- CBP organisation: drawback specialists were consolidated under the Petroleum, Natural Gas and Minerals Center from 6 October 2025, with a further routing change on 22 April 2026 [source: https://info.expeditors.com/newsflash/cbp-transitions-drawback-to-petroleum-natural-gas-and-minerals-cee].

Not found: any official drawback total for FY2022-FY2026; any 2026 Series A for Pax, Caspian or Zollback (search returned only the 2025 seeds, plus one more Y Combinator entrant, Forge); any current CBP processing time for privilege applications; drawback bond premium rates.

## 2. Delivery personas

### Persona 1. Veteran licensed customs broker who ran a drawback practice

**Who.** Twenty-five years in drawback at a specialist firm, now independent. Incentive: sell her time or her small book at a good price. She would be the scarce hire.

**How a claim is really built.** [DK throughout]
- Import side: ACE entry-summary data is at tariff-line level and the importer can pull five years of it from its own ACE portal account. The brief overstates the current broker's control of entry data. The broker does hold the commercial invoices and packing lists that map tariff lines to part numbers, and those are needed.
- Export side is where claims die: proof of export when the client is not the exporter of record (goods sold to a distributor who exported), small parcels with no AES filing, and inventory records that link an import lot to an export.
- Destroyed goods need notice to CBP on Form 7553 before destruction. Past destruction without notice is mostly unclaimable, so the "destroyed" lookback in the brief is close to zero.
- Past exports of unused goods without prior notice need a one-time waiver of prior notice, granted at CBP's discretion.
- Exports to Canada and Mexico are restricted: no same-condition substitution, and a lesser-of rule on manufacturing claims. That removes roughly a third of US exports from the best claim types.
- Manufacturing claims need a ruling and bills of materials.

**Hours.**
| Step | Traditional | With good software |
|---|---|---|
| Onboard a mid-market client to first claim | 150-400 staff hours over 3-6 months | 40-120 hours, same elapsed time |
| Each later quarterly claim | 10-40 hours | 3-10 hours |
| Active clients per specialist | 8-15 | 25-40 |

AI removes 50-70% of preparation hours. It removes almost none of the elapsed time, which is client data chasing, the privilege queue and the bond.

**Time from signing to cash.** Power of attorney and data, 3-6 weeks. Analysis, 2-6 weeks. Privilege applications filed in month 2-3. CBP response 4-8 months in 2026 (statutory 90 days, missed 60% of the time even in 2018-19 [notes]). First claim month 5-10. Accelerated payment 3-6 weeks after acceptance. **Cash 6-11 months after signing, median 8.** The brief's "4-8 months to first claim" is the optimistic half.

**Refund per client, first producing year / later years.** [est]
| Importer | Lookback | Ongoing per year |
|---|---|---|
| E-commerce brand, under $50M revenue | $50-250K | $30-150K |
| Mid-market distributor, $100-500M revenue, 5-15% re-exported | $0.4-1.5M | $0.2-0.7M |
| $1B+ importer newly exposed by the 10% tariffs | $1-5M | $0.5-3M |
| Legacy large exporters (petroleum, chemicals, autos) | $5-50M+ | held by incumbents or filed in-house |

Clients of $3.3M a year exist, but she counts 300-500 of them nationally and almost all have a filer.

**Rejection and audit.** ACE rejects 10-25% of first submissions for a new filer on validation errors, before payment. After payment, an experienced filer loses 2-5% of dollars at liquidation; a new filer with machine-built matches should plan for 5-15% until it is calibrated. Full desk review can take over three years [notes]. Records must be kept three years after liquidation, so each claim is a liability for five to six years.

**Decision.** Will not join as an employee. Would join as an officer for $280-350K base, 3-5% equity and E&O cover of at least $10M, or would sell her book (about $1.5M of fees) at 1.5-2x revenue. Wages for experienced drawback specialists are up 30-50% since 2025 and she estimates only 300-500 such people in the US.

**What would change it.** The company buys an existing practice with privileges, rulings and claim history in place, so she is not starting every client from month zero.

### Persona 2. CBP: drawback specialists at the processing centre, and regulatory audit

**Who.** Not a buyer. Sets the clock and the penalty.

**Position.**
- Privilege applications (accelerated payment, waiver of prior notice, one-time waiver) are reviewed by hand, per claimant. A new filer's volume does not earn priority. Current practice: 4-9 months [DK]. The centre absorbed a reorganisation in October 2025 [source: Expeditors link above] and the trade workforce spent 2026 on 53 million IEEPA entries [notes].
- Accelerated payment is not liquidation. Any excess is repaid with interest within 30 days of liquidation [notes, 19 CFR 190.92].
- Since 16 June 2026 a bond shortfall is a fatal error and strips accelerated payment from the claim [source: CSMS link above].
- A new claimant filed by a new filer is the highest-risk profile for selective review. Claims in review are not paid early.
- Penalties under 19 USC 1593a: up to three times the revenue loss for fraud; for negligence 20% of the loss on a first violation, 50% on a second, up to 100% after that for claimants outside the drawback compliance programme [DK]. The claimant carries the penalty. The broker faces 19 USC 1641 penalties up to $30,000 per violation and licence action [DK]. False Claims Act referrals for customs matters increased in 2025-26 [source: Foley May 2026 link above, as an expectation].
- A post-summary correction that lowers Section 232 duty triggers a request for mill certificates and cost breakdowns.
- Policy: drawback availability is set proclamation by proclamation. It was barred for Section 232 autos and semiconductors and for IEEPA fentanyl duties, and allowed for Section 122 and the forced-labor action [notes]. One sentence can zero the forward pool on any layer.

**Decision.** No commitment on timing. Will not pre-clear a filer or its software beyond standard ABI certification.

**What would change it.** Nothing a start-up controls. A filer with two years of claims liquidated without change gets fewer reviews.

### Persona 3. Surety underwriter asked for drawback bonds for a start-up's clients

**Who.** Customs bond underwriter at a top-five surety. Incentive: premium without loss in a year of record bond insufficiencies.

**Position.**
- The principal on a drawback bond is the claimant, not the start-up. She underwrites each claimant's financials.
- The bond must cover all accelerated payments outstanding until liquidation, which can be one to three years. A client with a $1M lookback and $400K a year therefore needs $1.5-2.5M of bond outstanding, not $400K. [est]
- Pricing: 0.5-1.5% of the bond amount a year for strong credits; 1.5-3% plus 50-110% cash or letter-of-credit collateral for weak ones [DK for rates; collateral level from the Foley June 2026 link above].
- The start-up's indemnity is worth nothing to her. She looks at the filer's claim history, and it has none.

**Decision.** Declines a programme facility for the start-up's book. Writes bonds one claimant at a time. Caps her aggregate exposure to claims prepared by this filer at $10-25M in year one. For sub-investment-grade claimants she requires collateral, which means the mid-market client ties up revolver capacity to get its own refund early.

**What would change it.** Twelve months and 200 or more claims with under 3% of dollars changed at liquidation; an officer with a known drawback record; $10M of E&O; or a filer that came with an acquired practice she already knows.

### Persona 4. Trade lawyer on Section 232 metal-content corrections, protests and post-summary corrections

**Who.** Partner at a customs boutique. Incentive: hourly and hybrid fees; cannot split fees with a non-lawyer firm.

**Size.**
- The metal-content basis ran from June 2025 to 5 April 2026. After that the duty is on full value [source: Foley May 2026 link]. So this is a closed stock, not a flow.
- Section 232 derivative duty assessed in that period: about $31.5B ($12.99B FY2025 + $18.54B FY2026) [notes].
- Deadlines [notes for the rules, est for dates]: a post-summary correction must be filed within 300 days of entry, so the last date for a 5 April 2026 entry is about 30 January 2027. A protest must be filed within 180 days of liquidation, about 494 days after entry. Entries before mid-July 2025 are already out of time. The last protest date for the last entry is about 12 August 2027.
- If the company files its first corrections in March 2027, only entries from about November 2025 are still open: $13-15B of duty, running off at about $2.7B a month. [est]
- Overpayment (importers who paid on full value because suppliers would not give metal cost): 5-15% of duty, so $0.7-2.2B in theory. Supplier cost evidence can be obtained for 10-20% of that: **$70-450M documentable**. [est, low confidence]

**Winnability.** A correction filed before liquidation with supplier cost statements: 60-75% accepted, after a CF-28. A protest with after-the-fact supplier declarations: 40-60%, and CBP has two years to decide; cash in 2028. [DK]

**Risks she raises.**
- A full line-by-line review finds underpayments as well as overpayments. The client then needs a prior disclosure, and a results-only vendor has produced a bill instead of a refund.
- A wrong correction is a false statement under 19 USC 1592 against the importer. The importer will look to the vendor's indemnity.
- Classification argument and court work is legal practice. A broker may file a protest; only counsel can take a deemed denial to the Court of International Trade.
- The ongoing pool after April 2026 is annex and exclusion errors (50% versus 25% versus the 15% Annex III cap, the under-15%-by-weight exclusion, US-melted metal at 10%). On $65-75B a year of duty, 2-4% over-assessed and one tenth found by third parties gives $130-300M of refunds a year for the whole market. [est]

**Decision.** Will take referrals on protests at hourly or capped hybrid fees. Refuses a contingency split. Views the metal-content line as real, small, law-firm-shaped and mostly gone before the company has a filing engine.

**What would change it.** A new proclamation reopening or extending metal-content treatment, or CBP guidance granting blanket relief for full-value payers.

### Persona 5. Senior trade-data engineer considering the technical co-founder seat

**Who.** Built entry and classification systems at a digital forwarder. Alternative: a staff role at $450K total compensation, or joining a funded entrant.

**What breaks first, in order.** [DK]
1. Export proof and inventory linkage. This is a records problem at the client, not a model problem. Each client's ERP is a custom integration.
2. Document extraction accuracy. 95-98% per field is not enough when each line is a statement to CBP. Every designated line needs human review until error rates are proven, so the labour saving in year one is nearer 40% than 70%.
3. The eligibility rule base. Drawback status depends on tariff authority, date, claim type and destination, and it changed at least six times in 2026 [notes]. A stale rule produces false claims at volume.
4. ABI certification for drawback (type 47) and CATAIR changes: two to four months before the first direct filing.
5. Evidence retention for five to six years per claim.

**What is not a moat.** Substitution matching under the lesser-of rule is a solved optimisation; incumbents' software has done it for decades. Pax, Caspian and Zollback have a two-year lead, and Caspian is already a CBP-approved software vendor [notes]. Four engineers rebuild the core in six to nine months.

**Decision.** Declines the co-founder seat for an organic start. Would accept with 8-12% equity if the company first acquires a filing practice with five years of claim data, because that data is the only training and validation set that matters.

## 3. Capital and competition personas

### Persona 6. Skeptical venture investor, asked for the seed and the Series A

**Pool check.** Uses section 1: drawback $4-7B a year on generous assumptions, $1B verified. Total fee pool for every vendor $0.6-1.2B. $40M of fees is 4-7% of national fees in 18 months; the leader's estimated revenue is $25-75M [notes].

**Comparables.** Zollback: $10M refunded and $1M+ of bookings in about 18 months, turning clients away [notes]. Pax and Caspian: about $5M seeds, no disclosed revenue [notes]. No 2026 Series A found for any of them [search, negative result]. No AI-native drawback firm has shown eight-figure revenue [notes].

**Revenue quality.** The first producing year of a client is 55-70% lookback. Year-two revenue per client is 35-50% of year one. That is negative net retention: the company must replace half its revenue each year before it grows. Fees are contingent, uncontracted in amount, and depend on proclamations.

**Policy precedent that worries him most.** In 2025 the IEEPA reciprocal duties were drawback-eligible. The court struck them down, CBP refunded $128B free of charge, and $18.9B tied to drawback claims and protests is stuck outside the free portal [notes]. Clients who paid a vendor to file drawback on those duties ended up behind those who waited. The forced-labor Section 301 action is 55-60% of the eligible base and is four months old.

**Value at $40M of fees.** Contingency recovery comparables: PRGX about 1x revenue at low margins; Ryan about 2.7x in 2022 ($2.5B on $916M); Performant 5.4x for payer contracts; Cotiviti 7x at 39% margins [notes; PRGX multiple DK]. For a policy-dependent pool with front-loaded revenue he pays 1.5-3x: **$60-120M**, up to $160-200M if growth is above 100% and liquidation losses are under 3%.

**Decision.** Passes on a Series A of $20-50M now. Offers a seed of $3-4M at $15-18M post-money only alongside a named drawback principal with a book. Series A terms: $3M trailing fees, 30 producing clients, under 5% clawed back at liquidation, and year-two revenue per client at least 40% of year one.

### Persona 7. Lender asked for a line to advance 70% of expected refunds

**Who.** Specialty finance credit officer. Incentive: yield with recourse and a short tenor.

**Analysis.**
- The obligor is the US government, but the risk is claim validity, which means the lender underwrites a start-up's filing accuracy with no history.
- The Assignment of Claims Act bars assignment. CBP pays only the claimant or a Form 4811 designee, and Treasury can offset [notes]. The collateral cannot be perfected in the usual way.
- Accelerated payment is repayable at liquidation up to three years later. The exposure does not end when the cash arrives.
- Pricing in the brief does not work in either case. With accelerated payment the advance is out for about five weeks: five points on 70 is 7.1% for five weeks, about 75% a year. A client with a revolver at SOFR plus 1.5-2% will not take it. Without accelerated payment the advance is out one to three years, and 7.1% in total is a 2.4-7% annual yield on unsecured claim risk, below the cost of funds.
- The period when the client most wants cash is the 4-8 months before privileges are granted, when no claim has been filed and there is nothing to lend against.
- Loss case: on a $50M advance book with 8% of dollars denied or clawed back and half of those clients slow or unable to repay, the loss is $2M, against $3.6M of gross advance fees and before funding cost. [est]

**Decision.** Refuses at launch. After 12 months of history offers $5-10M: 50% advance, only on claims accepted in ACE with accelerated payment approved and a sufficient bond, full recourse to the claimant and to the company, 20-30% first-loss equity, SOFR plus 6-9%, 1% unused fee, concentration limit of 10% per claimant.

**What would change it.** Two years of liquidation data with under 3% loss; a surety or insurer wrap on claim validity; payment directed to a controlled account by Form 4811.

### Persona 9. Treasurer of a $900M-revenue importer, pitched by three recovery vendors this quarter

**Profile.** Imports of about $350M customs value, duties of about $45-60M a year, 6% of units re-exported to Latin America and Europe. Estimated drawback: $1.2M lookback and $0.7M a year. Broker is a large forwarder. Revolver at SOFR plus 1.75%. [est, archetype]

**The three offers.** Established drawback specialist at 12% sliding to 8% above $2M, one-year term. Digital forwarder at 15% with a 70% advance. The founder at 20%, 25% with advance, three-year engagement.

**Her constraints.** The company is the claimant. An officer signs, the bond is in the company's name, and 1593a penalties and the six-year record burden sit with her, not the vendor. The work costs her 100-300 internal hours, a bond premium, and audit exposure. "Nothing to pay unless money comes back" is not how she reads it.

**Decision.** **Refuses** the founder's offer. **Signs** with the established specialist at 12%/8%, one year, terminable for convenience. Declines the advance from anyone: 7.1% for five weeks against a revolver at about 6% a year. Offers the founder one thing: a non-exclusive second-pass review of entries the primary vendor did not claim, 25% of incremental refunds, 12 months, no minimum. Expected yield to the founder: $150-300K of refunds, $40-75K of fees.

**What would change it.** Fee at or below 12% with a cap, a 12-month term, $10M of E&O, an indemnity for penalties caused by vendor error, three reference clients with liquidated claims, SOC 2.
