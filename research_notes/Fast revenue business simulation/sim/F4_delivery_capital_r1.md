# F4 delivery, capital and competition panel, round 1: hospital payment recovery entered by acquisition

Date of simulation: 24 November 2026 (month 0). Brief tested: `F4_v1.md`.

Everything below is a simulated hypothesis. Personas are archetypes. No real person was interviewed, no real firm was approached and no real offer exists.

Evidence labels: **[notes]** = founder research notes (`contingency_recovery.md` Part B, and `C3_delivery_r1.md` where stated); **[source: URL]** = web search run for this panel (6 searches, all read through search summaries, not the original pages); **[DK]** = domain knowledge, unverified.

## Summary verdict

The business works as a small, levered services roll-up. It does not work against the stated targets. Revenue in this plan is bought, and the price is set by sponsor-backed roll-ups that pay cash, close faster and have a lower effective multiple after synergies. $40M of recognized revenue in a trailing twelve months by month 18 needs about $40M of revenue owned from month 6, which is $80-100M of deal value closed in six months on $5-10M of equity. No persona on the capital side would fund that.

A first acquisition closes at the end of month 7: search, letter of intent, quality-of-earnings review, financing and hospital consents take a first-time buyer 6-10 months.

# Delivery personas

## Persona 1: Veteran operator, ran a 300+ staff contingency recovery business

- **Incentives:** none in the founder's deal; would take a paid operating role.
- **Revenue per analyst [DK]:**

| Line | Revenue per full-time employee | Loaded cost per employee |
|---|---|---|
| Underpayment and zero-balance review | $250-400K | $85-110K |
| Clinical denial appeals (nurses) | $180-260K | $110-130K |
| Complex claims: workers' compensation, veterans, motor vehicle | $110-160K | $65-80K |
| Aged and small-balance follow-up | $70-110K | $55-70K onshore, $16-20K offshore |
| Blended, whole firm | $150-190K | |

A $12M firm has 65-85 staff, payroll at 58-65% of revenue, and adjusted EBITDA of 18-25% after owner add-backs.

- **Recovery rates by placement tier [DK]:**

| Tier | Yield | Fee |
|---|---|---|
| First-pass underpayment review | Finds 0.5-1.5% of commercial net revenue reviewed; collects 60-75% of what it finds | 12-22% |
| Second pass | 0.1-0.4% | 22-30% |
| Third pass | 0.03-0.1% | 30-35% |
| Denials placed at day 30-60 | 35-50% of dollars overturned | 10-15% |
| Denials aged over 120 days | 20-35% | 15-22% |
| Denials over a year old or written off | 5-12% | 25-35% |
| Small balances | 8-20% of dollars | 20-30% |

A 20-40 client firm is mostly a second-pass or niche vendor. The first-pass seat belongs to the enterprise vendor.

- **Share of work that can be automated today [DK]:** identification is already 70-85% automated by contract-modeling software, so AI adds little there. Appeal drafting 50-70%. Reading contracts and building payment models 30-50%, which cuts a 6-10 week build to 2-3 weeks. Payer follow-up by phone and portal 20-35%. Posting, reconciliation and invoicing about 60%. Blended: 35-45% of task hours can be automated; 15-25% is realized in the first year inside an acquired firm running on spreadsheets and the client's own work queues.
- **On "three times the claims per analyst":** claims are the wrong unit. The added claims are small balances that yield a fifth to a tenth of the dollars per claim. Payers answer bulk small-balance appeals by bundling them into settlement projects at 30-60 cents on the dollar, shortening retrospective review windows from 24-36 months to 12 in contract renewals, and inserting clauses against third-party contingency auditors [DK]. Net effect: recovered dollars per client up 10-20%; labor cost per recovered dollar down 20-30% over 18-24 months.
- **Churn after a sale [DK]:** normal dollar churn is 10-15% a year, driven by a new chief financial officer, a switch of primary vendor, the hospital being acquired, or a move to in-house work. After a sale to an unknown first-time owner, 15-25% of dollars are at risk in year one, concentrated in the top five clients, which are typically 50-60% of revenue and belong personally to the seller. In-flight inventory keeps paying for 6-12 months after a client stops placing, so the loss shows up late, after the earnout is measured. Renewals also cost 1-3 points of fee.
- **Cash lag [DK]:** placement to identification 2-6 weeks; payer reprocessing 45-120 days; monthly invoice; hospitals pay in 30-60 days by contract and 60-90 in practice [contract terms 30-60 days: notes]. Placement to cash is 4-8 months. A new client is 6-10 months from signature to first material cash. Disputes over credit ("it would have been paid anyway") remove 3-8% of invoices. Payer take-backs trigger fee refunds.
- **Decision:** does not endorse the plan as written. Would serve as operating partner at $400K a year and 3-5% of equity, provided the first target has at least $20M of revenue and its own contract-modeling software.
- **What would change it:** a seller who rolls 20-30% and stays 24 months; five years of client-level revenue showing dollar retention above 90%.

## Persona 2: Integration lead, has merged small healthcare-services firms

- **What breaks in the first 12 months, in order [DK]:**
  1. People. The owner, two or three client leads and the one person who understands the contract models. Staff attrition of 15-25% in the year "AI agents" are announced; the best analysts go to sponsor-backed rivals.
  2. Consents. A stock purchase avoids assignment, but 30-50% of hospital master agreements carry change-of-control clauses. Twenty to forty consent requests are twenty to forty openings to cut the fee or rebid.
  3. AI approval. Each hospital's AI governance and security committee must approve patient data going to a model supplier as a new subcontractor. Three to nine months per client. Expect 40-60% of clients approved within 12 months. Until then the AI runs on a minority of volume.
  4. Systems. Work is done inside the client's records system under named logins, plus a home-built database. There is often no clean claims history to train or measure on.
  5. Quality of earnings. Contingent fees accrued but not billed, credit disputes, undisclosed offshore subcontractors, and add-backs that do not survive review. Typical haircut to seller EBITDA: 10-20%.
  6. A second deal during the first integration. Two workflows, two fee schedules, sometimes the same hospital as a client of both.
  7. Earnout disputes, because the buyer changed the workflow the earnout depends on.
- **Budget:** 7-10% of purchase price per deal, $1.5-2.5M for the first.
- **Decision:** would take the job only with no second closing in the first nine months and a ring-fenced integration budget. The brief's cadence of a second deal by month 12-15 is accepted only if the first has passed month nine with dollar retention above 90%.
- **Timeline:** 9-12 months from close before AI is in production on more than half of volume.

## Persona 3: Partner manager, clearinghouse or records system

- **Incentives:** transaction volume, protecting payer relationships, selling the house AI products.
- **Remittance data:** electronic remittances belong to the hospital. The recovery firm receives them by file transfer from the hospital's patient accounting system or through the hospital's clearinghouse account. A stock purchase does not break the feeds. New feeds take 4-10 weeks per client [DK].
- **Contracts:** payer contracts are supplied by the hospital under confidentiality clauses that allow disclosure to agents but not pooling across clients. Negotiated rates are now largely public through price-transparency files, so rate knowledge is not scarce; the interpretive terms (carve-outs, stop-loss, implant and drug clauses) are what matter and stay confidential [DK].
- **Clearinghouse decision:** signs the firm as an ordinary vendor or channel partner. Refuses cross-client data rights and any bulk interface to payer portals. Claim-status transactions $0.08-0.15 each, minimum $100-250K a year [DK; same range as C3 notes].
- **The clearinghouse competes:** it announced automated clinical appeals in January 2026, a tool that matches payer take-backs to claims in April 2026, and says its AI cuts 90% of the time spent on denial appeals (company claim) [source: https://hitconsultant.net/2026/01/14/fintech-waystar-agentic-intelligence-autonomous-revenue-cycle-2026/; https://www.techtarget.com/revcyclemanagement/news/366641428/Waystar-tackles-40B-denials-problem-with-new-AI-capability].
- **Records vendor decision:** allows third-party access only as named users inside the hospital's instance, which the hospital must sponsor, or through the paid partner program (3-9 months to certify [notes, C3]). Refuses automated agents logging in as users without the hospital's written approval, and refuses bulk write-back.
- **What would change it:** nothing earns data rights. A reseller agreement for the clearinghouse's own product earns better pricing.

## Persona 4: Regulator and enforcement view

- **No license is needed** for commercial underpayment recovery, and a percentage fee is lawful where funds land in the hospital's account [DK].
- **False Claims Act exposure sits in government lines:** Medicare Advantage, Medicaid managed care, veterans' community care and TRICARE. Appeals that change a diagnosis group or add diagnoses on these claims are claims on federal money. Work that rebills to a higher-paying code on a contingency fee is the highest-risk line. The enforcement working group and February 2026 inspector-general guidance target AI output accepted without meaningful review [notes, C3 panel sources]. Inspector-general guidance has long treated percentage fees as an incentive to overstate [DK].
- **The 60-day rule [DK]:** a vendor that reviews every remittance also sees overpayments. If it reports only underpayments because that is what it is paid for, the hospital has identified overpayments it has not returned. Hospital compliance officers will demand two-sided reporting, which the fee does not pay for.
- **Bulk filing:** payers have sued vendors that filed large volumes of ineligible payment disputes in the federal arbitration process [DK, unverified]. "Work every claim" with agents invites the same response in appeals.
- **Successor liability:** a stock purchase inherits every appeal the firm filed for 6-10 years. Representation-and-warranty insurance excludes or sublimits billing and False Claims Act matters [DK].
- **Veterans' claims:** filed through third-party administrators with a 180-day filing limit and 90 days for reconsideration; pre-certification denials began September 2026; the program is under payment-integrity review [source: https://triwest.com/en/provider-handbook/billing-and-claims; https://www.va.gov/COMMUNITYCARE/revenue-ops/Disputes-and-Appeals.asp; https://paymentaccuracy.gov/assets/scorecards/Q3%202026/VA%20Community%20Care.pdf]. These are direct federal claims.
- **Workers' compensation:** state fee schedules, state fraud statutes, state-by-state dispute forums [DK].
- **Privacy:** business associate of every client; the model supplier is a subcontractor; one breach means 30 notifications. Some client and Medicaid contracts bar offshore access [notes, C3].
- **State AI laws** passed in 2026 restrict payers' use of AI in denials, which helps appeals at the margin [notes, C3 panel source].
- **Decision:** no approval to grant or refuse. Conditions for operating safely: a named compliance officer, clinician sign-off on clinical appeals, a claim-level record of model version and reviewer, two-sided reporting, and no contingency fee on code-upgrade work in government lines. Cost $0.6-1.0M a year at $12-20M of revenue, which is 3-5 points of margin.

## Persona 5: Senior engineer at a health-AI company, asked to be chief technology officer of the acquired firm

- **Alternatives:** stay, or join a funded specialist. Recent rounds: $40M Series C, $20M Series A, $17M, $17M and $16M for companies doing AI denial and underpayment work [source: https://techtarget.com/revcyclemanagement/news/366640740/Adonis-raises-40M-to-accelerate-AI-in-revenue-cycle; https://www.fiercehealthcare.com/ai-and-machine-learning/midstream-health-nabs-20m-ai-financial-platform-improve-hospital-margins; https://www.citybiz.co/article/835644/amperos-health-raises-16-million-series-a-to-expand-ai-denial-management-platform/]. Two sponsor-owned recovery vendors already have the capability: one launched AI underpayment recovery and appeals in April 2026, another bought an AI underpayment-detection start-up in August 2026 [source: https://www.accessnewswire.com/newsroom/en/healthcare-and-pharmaceutical/revecore-launches-ai-powered-underpayment-recovery-and-denial-appeals-1161486; https://local12.com/news/local/tennessee-firm-buys-2-year-old-cincinnati-startup-enablecomp-revenue-recovery-healthcare-helix-advisory].
- **Decision:** no at the terms implied. Options in a levered subsidiary rank behind $7M of senior debt and a seller note.
- **Terms to say yes:** 6-10% of the holding company; a technology budget of $2.5-4M a year held outside the lender's covenant group; 8-10 engineers; a target with at least three years of claims and remittance history in its own data store and contract language permitting de-identified use.
- **What is real [DK]:** contract reading, appeal drafting, routing and evidence assembly. A second detection pass on claims that rules miss adds 5-15% to finds. Browser agents on payer portals are fragile. Payer phone lines increasingly refuse automated callers.
- **What breaks:** per-client AI approvals; three acquired firms means three stacks; outcome labels arrive 45-120 days late; client contracts block cross-client training by default.
- **The number that matters:** a credible team costs about $3M a year, which is more than the first acquisition's entire EBITDA of $2.6M. Six months to the first production agent; 12-18 months to 1.5x throughput.
- **What would accumulate:** appeal outcomes by payer, denial code and argument, if contracts allow it. Contract terms do not accumulate across clients.

# Capital and competition personas

## Persona 6: Acquisition lender, asked to fund the purchase of a $12M-revenue contingency firm

- **Who:** lower-middle-market cash-flow lender. A $2.6M-EBITDA borrower with no fund sponsor is below most direct lenders' $3-5M minimum [DK].
- **Market reference:** unitranche for companies under $20M of EBITDA priced at SOFR plus 500-550 basis points at 4.25-5.25x in late 2025; spreads firmed 25-50 points in 2026; floating-rate borrowers paid 9.5-11% all-in in April 2026 [source: https://privateequitylist.com/intelligence/md/private-equity-lenders; https://cms.tpg.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/07-09_Analysis_ExpQA-TPG-Twin-Brook_PDI-MM-Lending_Jun26.pdf; https://www.abfjournal.com/middle-market-debt-weekly-equities-ride-an-intel-led-rally-to-fresh-records/]. Those terms are for sponsored credits several times this size.
- **Decision:** issues a term sheet, not a commitment. Refuses 4-5x.
- **Terms [DK]:**
  - $6.5-7.5M senior term loan, which is 2.5-2.75x lender-adjusted EBITDA, plus a $1.0-1.5M revolver against billed receivables only.
  - SOFR plus 700-800 basis points, about 11.0-12.5% all-in; 2% upfront fee; five years; 5% annual amortization; 50% excess-cash-flow sweep.
  - Total leverage at most 3.5x, stepping to 3.0x at month 18. Fixed-charge cover at least 1.20x. Minimum liquidity $1.5M.
  - No client above 20% of revenue and top five below 55%, or leverage is cut by half a turn.
  - Cash equity at least 45-50% of capitalization. Seller note fully subordinated, with payments blocked above 3.0x.
  - Seller employment and non-compete for 24 months. Cash-basis quality-of-earnings review.
- **Treatment of at-will contracts:** no credit for contract term. Underwrites five years of client-level revenue and tenure. Stress case: a 25% revenue loss ($3M) at a 50% decremental margin cuts EBITDA from $2.6M to $1.1M and takes a $7M loan to 6.4x. That is the reason for 2.5x.
- **Unbilled contingent fees** are not collateral.
- **AI spending:** $1.5M of engineers in the borrower's cost base takes fixed-charge cover to about 0.9x. It must be funded with equity outside the credit group. Projected AI savings get no credit.
- **First-close stack on a $22M price plus $1.3M of fees:** senior $7M; seller note $3M; earnout $2.5M; rollover $1.5M; cash equity $9.3M.
- **Later:** at $8-10M of combined EBITDA with clean retention, 3.5-4.0x at SOFR plus 575-650 with a delayed-draw line for acquisitions. Total debt capacity by month 36: $28-38M.
- **What would change it:** contracts with minimum placement commitments; a fund sponsor guaranteeing equity cures; two years of post-close retention data.

## Persona 7: Private-equity partner running a competing roll-up in this niche

- **Who:** owns a platform with 1,000+ hospital relationships of the kind in the notes, with a drawn acquisition facility. One such platform closed two add-ons in 2026 [notes; source: https://hitconsultant.net/2026/01/12/enablecomp-acquires-h-roi-creating-the-industrys-most-comprehensive-complex-revenue-platform/].
- **Decision:** outbids on any clean asset. Passes on impaired ones and lets the founder have them.
- **Price:** 9-12x adjusted EBITDA for a $12M firm with margins above 20%, no client above 25% and its own software: $24-32M, 2.0-2.6x revenue. All cash, no financing condition, 45-60 days to close, 20-30% seller rollover into a platform marketed for exit at 14-18x [DK].
- **Why it can pay more:** cross-sale into its existing hospitals and removal of 30% of overhead bring its effective multiple to 5-6x. The founder paying 8.5x gets 8.5x.
- **Deal flow:** every banker-run sale reaches it, and it calls owners directly. Panel estimate: 40-70 independent US firms of $8-15M in this niche, 5-8 for sale in any year, 1-3 not already in a process with sponsors [DK estimate].
- **On the AI claim:** already has it, bought or built (see Persona 5 sources).
- **Would it buy the founder's company?** Yes, at 10-12x once EBITDA is above $8M with a clean compliance record. It is the exit as well as the competitor.
- **What would change the outbid:** nothing on price. A seller who distrusts consolidators, or an asset with a defect it will not underwrite.

## Persona 8: Skeptical growth investor, asked to fund the second and third acquisitions

- **Incentives:** fund return; will not pay a technology multiple for services revenue.
- **Value created versus price paid:** see the capital table. $57M of cash equity buys a company worth $40-85M to equity at month 36. Roughly four-fifths of month-36 revenue was purchased at 8.5x and would be sold at 10-12x. The value is multiple arbitrage and modest cost savings, less platform overhead and a technology team. The sellers and the lender collect most of it.
- **Plausible exit:** sale to a sponsor-backed platform or to a revenue-cycle outsourcer at 10-13x EBITDA, about 2-3x revenue. No public offering. The $4.1bn reference was 400+ health systems including 47 of the top 50 [notes], sold at 2022 prices after five years and many add-ons [source: https://www.newmountaincapital.com/portfolio/cloudmed/].
- **Decision:** refuses common equity at a growth valuation. Refuses anything before the first deal has two quarters of post-close results.
- **Terms offered:** $20-25M of preferred with a 12% accruing dividend, 1.5x minimum return, conversion into 35-45% of the company, board approval of every acquisition and of the technology budget. Conditions: dollar retention at or above 90% at month six after close; cost per recovered dollar down at least 15% on AI-approved clients; seller still employed.
- **Effect on the founder:** in the base case the preferred accretes to about $58M by month 36 on $49M invested. Equity of $40-85M leaves -$18M to +$27M for the common, which is the founder's $8M and the sellers' $6M. Founder outcome at month 36: loss of capital to about 2x.
- **What would change it:** signed minimum-placement contracts; a first-pass seat at a health system above $3bn of revenue; organic growth above 25% for two quarters.

## Persona 9: Incumbents responding rationally

**(a) Largest recovery vendor inside a revenue-cycle outsourcer.** 400+ health systems [notes]. Does not respond to a $12M firm by name. At renewal it bundles underpayment review, code validation and denials into an enterprise agreement at a blended 12-18%, and cuts 2-4 points where contested [DK]. It holds the first-pass seat. The founder's firm keeps second and third pass, where yield per client is a fifth or less. Decision: no partnership, no referral; might buy the company later for clients, not technology.

**(b) Clinical revenue-integrity AI company paid on found revenue.** Works before the bill drops, so it removes underpayments and denials before they exist. Now extending into appeals; its parent paid $1.25bn for one such business [notes]. Customers are moving to performance-based contracts [notes]. Effect: denial inventory placed with outside contingency vendors at its clients falls 10-20% over three years [DK]. Decision: ignores the founder; competes for the same found-revenue budget line.

**(c) Clearinghouse offering autonomous resubmission.** Prices per transaction or by subscription. For technical denials and small balances, which is the tier the brief targets with agents, it resubmits before the claim ages, at perhaps a tenth of a 20% contingency fee [DK; product launches: sources under Persona 3]. Its pitch to the hospital: why pay 20% for what resubmits itself. It leaves contract-interpretation underpayments and clinical disputes to vendors.

**(d) Hospital's records vendor.** Ships contract-management and appeal-drafting features inside the billing work queues as part of maintenance, and controls third-party access [DK; AI features announced by one ambulatory vendor in August 2026: notes, C3]. Within two to three years hospitals do first-pass denial follow-up in-house. Decision: standard partner program, no priority.

**Net effect on the brief:** 2-4 points of fee compression over three years; placed inventory arrives later and harder; 18-25% holds only on second pass and complex claims.

## Persona 10: Independent seller, owner of a $6M-revenue workers' compensation and veterans' claims specialist

- **Who:** founder-owner near 60; 40-45 staff; EBITDA $1.5M plus $0.3-0.4M of owner add-backs; 25-30 hospital clients; top three are 45% of revenue; contracts terminable on 60-90 days [DK archetype].
- **Alternatives:** sale to a complex-claims platform, whose core business is exactly these claim types and which is actively buying [notes; source: https://hitconsultant.net/2026/01/12/enablecomp-acquires-h-roi-creating-the-industrys-most-comprehensive-complex-revenue-platform/], at 8-10x cash with a rollover; a second sponsor-backed vendor that markets veterans' claims work [source: https://www.aspirion.com/the-72-hour-rule-how-missing-one-deadline-is-costing-your-hospital-millions-in-va-claims/]; or keeping $1.5M a year of distributions.
- **Decision:** refuses the founder's structure. A first-time buyer with a seller note, bank debt and an earnout measured on clients the buyer will put through a new AI workflow is three risks the seller is asked to carry.
- **Terms that would be accepted:** $15M headline (about 8.3x adjusted); at least 80% cash at close; seller note at most 10%, secured, at 9%; no retention earnout; proof of committed funds; a $500K payment if financing fails; 12-month transition. Otherwise the platform's 9x in cash.
- **On revenue survival:** "Three of my top five would rebid if I left." (simulated)
- **What would change it:** an all-cash offer one to one-and-a-half turns above the platforms, closing in 60 days. That takes the price to 10-11x for a $6M book.

## What accumulates, and what does not

| Asset | Accumulates? | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Hospital relationships | Weakly | At will, non-exclusive, personal to sellers |
| Appeal-outcome data by payer and argument | Yes, if contracts permit | The one compounding asset; rivals with 400-1,000 hospitals have 10-30 times more |
| Contract-term library | No | Confidential per client; rates already public |
| Trained analysts | Leaves | 15-25% attrition after a sale |
| Software | Partly | Three or four acquired stacks to merge |
| Scale for lenders and buyers | Yes | Leverage rises from 2.5x to 3.5-4x and exit multiple from 8x to 10-12x above $8M of EBITDA |

Product company or outsourcer: an outsourcer. Buyers at exit will price it on EBITDA.

## Probabilities (this version)

- First paid commercial delivery within 9 months: **40%**. This is the chance a first acquisition closes by month 9 and collects fees (about 35-40%), plus a small chance of a paid recovery under the founder's own contract (under 10%; a new vendor is 6-10 months from signature to cash).
- $40M of gross recognized revenue in a trailing twelve months by month 18: **2%**. On a pro-forma run-rate basis, 6%.
- $100M by month 36: **3%**.
- Year five if it works: revenue about $105M, of which roughly $75M bought; gross margin 52-57%; EBITDA 25-28%; enterprise value $290-380M at 11-13x; equity $200-290M after about $90M of debt, on about $100M of cumulative cash equity. Revenue is repeat business that must be re-earned each month: contingent, at will, dependent on placements. It is not contracted.
