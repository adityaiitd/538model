# Power-equipment clearinghouse / slot market-maker: US fact base (as of 23 Nov 2026)

Reader notes on source quality (applies throughout):
- I could not open the paywalled originals (WSJ, FT, Bloomberg, Reuters.com, Wood Mackenzie reports). Figures attributed to them come from syndicated copies or secondary write-ups, and are marked "via".
- Several secondary sources (Panabee, MLQ.ai, 24/7 Wall St, Open Factory, Substacks) are aggregators or newsletters. Where a number rests only on one of these it is flagged.
- "Wood Mackenzie Q2 2025 survey" numbers are widely re-published in 2026 as if current. They are 2025 data.
- New-turbine "$/kW" figures are not comparable across sources: Wood Mackenzie's ~$600/kW is turbine equipment only; Reuters/Aurora's $2.0-2.5M/MW appears to be a delivered/packaged cost. Treat them as different bases.

## 1. Current lead times and price changes since 2020, by equipment class

### Takeaway
Large transformers, GSUs, heavy-duty turbines, aeroderivatives and large recips are all still on multi-year waits in late 2026 (GSU >160 weeks, new US heavy-duty turbines ~3 years by GE Vernova's own count and ~6 years by Wood Mackenzie's, Caterpillar recips booked into 2029). Distribution/pad-mount transformers, BESS and fuel cells are not scarce in the same way, which narrows where a slot clearinghouse has anything to clear.

### Inferences
- The scarce, high-value classes are GSUs/large power transformers, data-center-spec MV gear, heavy-duty and aeroderivative turbines, and large recips. Pad-mount/distribution transformers already have a functioning stock-and-reman channel (Maddox, Sunbelt Solomon) with weeks-level delivery.
- BESS scarcity is a compliance problem (non-FEOC cells), not a slot problem. Prices fell through early 2026. It is a poor fit for a "scarce slot" pitch.
- Fuel cells are sold by Bloom as the fast alternative, so they compete with the clearinghouse's premise (buy time) rather than supply it.
- GE Vernova's own "about three years, and the turbine is often not the binding constraint" undercuts the six-year headline. A buyer who gets a turbine early may still wait on permits, gas supply, GSUs and interconnection.

### Gaps
- No primary 2026 Wood Mackenzie transformer survey found; the newest hard survey is Q2 2025 plus the Q1 2026 GSU figure via Reuters.
- No sourced $ price per large power transformer or per GSU, and no sourced $/kW for Caterpillar or Jenbacher gensets (the only implied number is about $2,000/kW installed from the Anthropic deal, section 2).
- No published lead time in weeks for Jenbacher or Wärtsilä; no TM2500-specific lead time or price.
- No US BESS lead-time data for 2026.

## 2. Evidence of slots or equipment being resold or flipped, and at what premium

### Takeaway
There are real, large secondary transactions in 2026 (Anthropic taking over OnePWR's Caterpillar order for about $3.4B; Hallador buying 460 MW of unfired Siemens turbines for $350M; Chevron/Engine No. 1 redirecting reserved 7HAs to a Microsoft project), but I found no disclosed case of a turbine slot reservation being sold to a third party at a stated premium. The biggest test case, SoftBank, forfeited about $2.85B of deposits instead of reselling.

### Cited Findings
- FT (8 Nov 2026): Anthropic agreed in late September to pay about $3.4B for 1.7 GW of Caterpillar gas generators originally ordered about two years earlier by OnePWR Solutions of San Antonio, which needed financing to complete the orders. Anthropic took over the orders and did not buy OnePWR — [MLQ.ai summary of FT](https://mlq.ai/news/anthropic-pays-34-billion-for-17-gw-of-caterpillar-gas-generators-that-onepwr-had-on-order-ft-reports/); [DCD](https://www.datacenterdynamics.com/en/news/anthropic-signs-34bn-gas-generator-deal-to-support-17gw-of-data-center-power/)
- Caveats on that deal: Reuters could not verify it; the $3.4B includes engineering and construction as well as equipment; implied about $2,000/kW; Piper Sandler (OnePWR's exclusive adviser) lists a completed $3.4B sale of OnePWR's "power generation platform" to "a frontier AI lab"; any premium over the original order price is not disclosed — [MLQ.ai](https://mlq.ai/news/anthropic-pays-34-billion-for-17-gw-of-caterpillar-gas-generators-that-onepwr-had-on-order-ft-reports/); [dev.to summary](https://dev.to/frankchu/34b-bought-anthropic-17-gw-of-gas-turbines-2ce2)
- Hallador Energy agreed (30 May/1 June 2026) to buy about 460 MW of never-fired Siemens gas turbines, generators and a steam turbine from Energy World Corporation for $350M, about $760/kW; delivered cost projected near $450M after shipping and restoration; revenue expected late 2028 to mid-2029 — [Turbomachinery](https://www.turbomachinerymag.com/view/hallador-energy-bypasses-oem-backlog-secures-unfired-siemens-turbine-in-350-million-deal); [Power Technology](https://www.power-technology.com/news/hallador-to-buy-siemens-turbines-ewc/)
- Chevron and Engine No. 1 reserved seven GE Vernova 7HA turbines in January 2025 with no named site; by June 2026 they had assigned 2.67 GW of it to a 20-year Microsoft contract in West Texas (same holder, new project) — [Open Factory](https://open-factory.com/articles/slot-economy/)
- SoftBank reserved more than 13 GW of gas turbines, largely from GE Vernova, then booked a loss of about $3.7B on cancelling the surplus: about $2.85B forfeited non-refundable advance payments plus about $0.88B to modify or settle contracts (results of 10 Nov 2026, WSJ reporting) — [TIKR via Yahoo](https://finance.yahoo.com/energy/articles/softbank-takes-3-7-billion-163245843.html)
- SoftBank can claim rebates of up to $2.1B on future purchases over ten years — [The Next Web](https://thenextweb.com/news/softbank-loss-7bn-ai-data-centre-power-equipment-writedown)
- No SoftBank resale attempt has been reported; GE Vernova said there is "no negative or positive impact" and expects slots through 2030 to be mostly sold by end-2026 (AI-generated secondary source) — [Panabee](https://www.panabee.com/insights/softbank-turbine-loss-why-not-resell-the-slots-11112026)
- Used-turbine prices (Reuters, 9 Nov 2026): well-maintained used units about $1.5-1.7M/MW, refurbished about $1.3M/MW, new $2.0-2.5M/MW (Origin Partners, Aurora Energy Research) — [Reuters via BOE Report](https://boereport.com/2026/11/09/ai-power-rush-sends-data-centres-hunting-for-used-turbines-and-jet-engines/)
- ReflowX brokered two used 14.5 MW Solar Turbines units for $12.5M combined; comparable new units would cost about $35M and take five years — [Reuters via BOE Report](https://boereport.com/2026/11/09/ai-power-rush-sends-data-centres-hunting-for-used-turbines-and-jet-engines/)
- EthosEnergy CEO: the secondary market is "very, very hot right now" and asking prices have escalated; Sulzer gets a used-turbine request for a data center every few weeks — [Reuters via BOE Report](https://boereport.com/2026/11/09/ai-power-rush-sends-data-centres-hunting-for-used-turbines-and-jet-engines/)
- Reuters: there are no reliable estimates of how much the secondary market adds to a turbine supply worth about $25B a year; Enverus says it is "unlikely to offset a meaningful share of the delays" — [Reuters via BOE Report](https://boereport.com/2026/11/09/ai-power-rush-sends-data-centres-hunting-for-used-turbines-and-jet-engines/)
- Transformers: developers "often pay significant premiums or buy production slots well in advance, sometimes before a specific project exists" (Burns & McDonnell) — [Kitco/Reuters](https://www.kitco.com/news/off-the-wire/2026-05-11/us-power-transformer-buyers-scramble-imports-factory-slots)
- GE Vernova said in December 2025 that new reservations were pricing above current orders — [Open Factory](https://open-factory.com/articles/slot-economy/)
- Unverified newsletter claim: secondary turbine prices repriced 2-3x since 2023 and "slot positions themselves now trade at premiums" (no named transaction; from a search summary) — [Public Markets Substack](https://publicmarkets.substack.com/p/the-gas-turbine-bottleneck)
- Scarcity premium shows up in whole-plant sales: NRG is selling about 4.9 GW to LS Power for $4B with an implied about $1,400/kW at Jack County, above the just-over $1,000/kW it expects to pay for a new 1.5 GW Texas build — [24/7 Wall St](https://247wallst.com/investing/2026/11/09/shortage-of-turbines-drives-market-for-second-hand-market-for-ai-data-centers/)

### Inferences
- Used and refurbished equipment trades at a discount to new per MW (roughly 25-45% below on the Reuters figures; the ReflowX pair went for about 36% of new). The "premium" is versus pre-2023 used prices and versus waiting, not versus list. A broker earns on the spread and fee, not on a markup above OEM price.
- The secondary deals that did happen were whole-position transfers by distressed or over-committed holders (OnePWR lacked financing, Energy World had stranded units). These are corporate-finance transactions run by investment banks (Piper Sandler on OnePWR), at $350M to $3.4B a piece. That is a banker's market, not obviously a startup marketplace's.
- SoftBank is the strongest evidence against freely tradable slots: a holder with 13 GW of the scarcest asset in the market ate about $2.85B rather than sell it on. Either transfer needed OEM consent that was not given, or the OEM's rebate offer beat any resale. Either way the OEM captured the reassignment value.
- Chevron/Engine No. 1 shows slots are portable across the holder's own projects, which is the option value buyers pay 20% for. Portability across owners is not shown.

### Gaps
- No published index or disclosed price for a resold turbine slot. The Open Factory article says it covers the resale value of a 2028 slot, but that section is paywalled.
- No sourced example of a transformer or switchgear slot resold at a premium, only general statements.
- The OnePWR original order price is undisclosed, so the flip premium cannot be computed.
- Whether Caterpillar or Siemens consented to, or charged for, the OnePWR and Energy World transfers is not reported.

## 3. How much equipment gets stranded

### Takeaway
Project attrition is very high (75% of 2000-2020 interconnection requests withdrew; more than 750 GW withdrew in 2025 alone; more than 40% withdraw even after signing an interconnection agreement), and 2026 produced the first large named pools of orphaned power orders (SoftBank, OnePWR, Energy World). But most withdrawals happen before major equipment is ordered, and I found no data on the volume of ordered-then-orphaned equipment.

### Cited Findings
- LBNL Queued Up 2026: of requests made 2000-2020, 13% were online by end-2025 and 75% had withdrawn; the withdrawal rate after signing an interconnection agreement is above 40% — [APPA summary of LBNL](https://www.publicpower.org/periodical/article/backlog-power-plants-seeking-transmission-grid-connection-eased-somewhat-2025-lbnl); [LBNL](https://emp.lbl.gov/publications/queued-2026-edition-characteristics)
- More than 750 GW withdrew in 2025; active queue 2,061 GW, down 10%; gas in queue 253 GW, up 86%; solar 773 GW (-19%), storage 749 GW (-16%) — [APPA summary of LBNL](https://www.publicpower.org/periodical/article/backlog-power-plants-seeking-transmission-grid-connection-eased-somewhat-2025-lbnl)
- Only 18% of queued gas capacity has a draft or executed interconnection agreement — [APPA summary of LBNL](https://www.publicpower.org/periodical/article/backlog-power-plants-seeking-transmission-grid-connection-eased-somewhat-2025-lbnl)
- Sightline Climate (via Bloomberg): 30-50% of planned 2026 US data center capacity could slip or be cancelled, largely from equipment shortages — [KEYT/Stacker](https://keyt.com/stacker-technology/2026/10/19/tech-companies-need-more-data-centers-so-why-are-nearly-half-of-all-planned-projects-delayed-or-canceled/)
- Disputed: Sightline had counted only nine actually cancelled projects and SemiAnalysis disputes even the delay estimate (secondary) — [Vucense](https://vucense.com/ai-intelligence/industry-business/us-ai-data-center-transformer-shortage-crisis-2026/)
- Heatmap Pro: at least $260B of data center investment cancelled after local opposition in 2026 through early October, about $130B of it in Q3 (cited in a vendor press release) — [SecondBloom press release](https://www.einpresswire.com/article/955580439/secondbloom-auctions-moves-into-data-centers-as-canceled-projects-top-260-billion-in-2026)
- Newly elected governors in Texas, Ohio and other states campaigned on pausing data center construction; those states hold about 40% of planned US capacity per Cleanview — [Yahoo/Bernstein piece](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/stocks/articles/72-upside-ge-vernova-bernstein-094056522.html)
- GE Vernova's 10-K warns reservation counterparties "may not place orders equal to the value of their reservation amount or at all"; 58 GW of its 122 GW is reservations — [Open Factory](https://open-factory.com/articles/slot-economy/); [GE Vernova](https://www.gevernova.com/news/ge-vernova-reports-strong-third-quarter-2026-financial-performance)
- GE Vernova management said there were no slot exits in Q3 2026 (before the SoftBank disclosure) — [Utility Dive](https://www.utilitydive.com/news/ge-vernova-in-9b-deal-to-buy-high-voltage-manufacturer-as-gas-turbine-orde/873223/)
- PwC warned of the reverse stranding too: completed transmission lines idle while awaiting transformers — [Kitco/Reuters](https://www.kitco.com/news/off-the-wire/2026-05-11/us-power-transformer-buyers-scramble-imports-factory-slots)

### Inferences
- LBNL withdrawal counts overstate strandable equipment. Most projects quit before a transformer or turbine is ordered. The post-IA withdrawal rate (above 40%) is the better proxy, since GSUs are usually ordered around that stage.
- 2026's cancellations are mostly driven by local opposition and permitting, which hits sites, not always equipment. An owner with a cancelled site and a live slot will usually redeploy to another of its own sites (as Chevron/Engine No. 1 did). Stranding needs an owner with no other project, i.e. small or distressed developers.
- Supply of orphaned positions is lumpy and counter-cyclical: it grows exactly when demand for those positions weakens.

### Gaps
- No dataset on dollars or MW of ordered equipment orphaned by cancelled projects.
- LBNL primary PDF returned HTTP 403; figures are via APPA's summary.
- No named case of a cancelled renewable or data center project selling an ordered GSU or switchgear lineup.

## 4. Who already does this, and is it crowded

### Takeaway
The field is occupied at every layer: OEMs reassign their own slots, investment banks run the big position transfers, PE-backed remanufacturers own stock transformers, well-funded power-as-a-service firms hoard generation, and a long tail of brokers plus at least two purpose-built sites (SecondWatt, SecondBloom) already court data center buyers. I found no VC-backed "slot exchange" with disclosed funding, which reads as either white space or a sign the model does not clear.

### Cited Findings
- Maddox Industrial Transformer: founded 2015, HQ Greer SC; stocks and remanufactures pad-mount and substation units; employee estimates range from about 200 (company release, Feb 2026) to "over 400" (Wikipedia). Third-party revenue estimates conflict wildly ($1.9M to $250M) and are unusable — [Business Wire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20260209717354/en/Maddox-Industrial-Transformer-Supports-Reindustrialization-of-American-Manufacturing-Startups-with-a-Free-American-Made-Transformer-Donation); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maddox_Industrial_Transformer)

## 5. The catch: fungibility, warranty and assignment, tariffs and FEOC

### Takeaway
The contracts are the binding constraint. The one current OEM contract I could read (Virginia Transformer, Feb 2026) bars assignment without the seller's written agreement, charges a $10,000 assignment fee, lets the seller refuse, and starts a short warranty clock at shipment. Large transformers are only partly standard (impedance is the hard part), and turbine reservations are non-refundable options that the OEM, not the holder, re-monetises.

### Cited Findings

Assignment, cancellation and warranty (primary document)
- Virginia Transformer standard terms (23 Feb 2026), assignment: no assignment of the order or buyer's rights without the seller's prior written agreement; all amounts due plus a $10,000 assignment fee must be paid first; buyer must provide financials on the assignee; "Seller reserves the right to deny any request for assignment" — [VTC Terms](https://www.vatransformer.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/FINAL-2.23.2026-VTC-Standard-Terms-and-Conditions.pdf)
- Same terms, cancellation: 30% of order amount after order entry, a further 15% after drawing submission, then materials commitments and percent completion on top once manufacturing starts; the same charges apply to cancellation for convenience — [VTC Terms](https://www.vatransformer.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/FINAL-2.23.2026-VTC-Standard-Terms-and-Conditions.pdf)
- Same terms, rescheduling: buyer delays beyond 20 days trigger a fee of 30% of order value; no schedule changes after winding starts; delays cannot exceed six months or the order is deemed cancelled; post-test storage costs 2.5% of transformer value per 30 days plus $25,000 — [VTC Terms](https://www.vatransformer.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/FINAL-2.23.2026-VTC-Standard-Terms-and-Conditions.pdf)
- Same terms, warranty: 12 months from first energization or 18 months from shipment, whichever comes first; voided by storage or installation not per seller instructions, by tank entry without written authorisation, or by non-payment — [VTC Terms](https://www.vatransformer.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/FINAL-2.23.2026-VTC-Standard-Terms-and-Conditions.pdf)
- Same terms, tariffs: new or increased tariffs between proposal and shipment pass through to the buyer above a 2% aggregate threshold; tariff reductions do not reduce price — [VTC Terms](https://www.vatransformer.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/FINAL-2.23.2026-VTC-Standard-Terms-and-Conditions.pdf)

Turbine reservations
- A slot reservation fee is non-refundable, creditable against the final supply contract, and kept by the OEM if the contract is not signed by the agreed date unless the OEM defaulted; the OEM grants no exclusivity — [Synergy Consulting](https://www.synergyconsultingifa.com/industry-knowledge/rethinking-procurement-in-conventional-power-projects-amid-gas-turbine-supply-constraints/)
- GE Vernova generally requires on the order of 20% of turbine contract price before it calls something a reservation (secondary); a 384 MW 7HA.02 implies a roughly $60M payment on a roughly $300M machine (author's estimate) — [Business Model Analyst](https://businessmodelanalyst.com/gas-turbine-oligopoly-pricing-power/); [Open Factory](https://open-factory.com/articles/slot-economy/)
- If a project fails, "the manufacturer keeps the deposit and resells the slot into a market that's short anyway" (commentary) — [Open Factory](https://open-factory.com/articles/slot-economy/)
- Transfers of reservations "typically need the maker's consent" (unsourced assertion in an AI-generated piece; consistent with the VTC clause above but not verified for turbine contracts) — [Panabee](https://www.panabee.com/insights/softbank-turbine-loss-why-not-resell-the-slots-11112026)

Fungibility
- DOE (2024): many large-transformer parameters such as voltages and MVA ratings "have been at least somewhat standardized, but others, especially the transformer impedance, are more difficult to standardize" — [DOE LPT Resilience Report](https://www.energy.gov/sites/default/files/2024-10/EXEC-2022-001242%20-%20Large%20Power%20Transformer%20Resilience%20Report%20signed%20by%20Secretary%20Granholm%20on%207-10-24.pdf)
- GAO: utility officials said standardizing transformers "would be very expensive and could require rebuilding parts of the grid" — [GAO](https://www.gao.gov/assets/830/828093.pdf)
- Bolt Electrical's owner blames utility and EPC qualification rules and approved-vendor lists, not physical shortage, for limiting access to alternative suppliers — [POWER](https://powermag.com/transformers-in-2026-shortage-scramble-or-self-inflicted-crisis/)
- Turbines are more fungible in practice: developers deploy more than 100 small turbines at one site "mixing models and brands" (SVI Bremco) — [Reuters via BOE Report](https://boereport.com/2026/11/09/ai-power-rush-sends-data-centres-hunting-for-used-turbines-and-jet-engines/)

Refurbished equipment norms
- Maddox warrants new and reconditioned padmount and substation units for 3 years (5-year option), LV dry-type 5 years, MV dry-type 1 year — [Maddox FAQ](https://www.maddox.com/dev/faqs-v2)
- Used turbines can have unclear maintenance records, limited remaining life and thin long-term service support (Wood Mackenzie analyst) — [Reuters via BOE Report](https://boereport.com/2026/11/09/ai-power-rush-sends-data-centres-hunting-for-used-turbines-and-jet-engines/)

Tariffs and FEOC
- From 18 Aug 2025, transformers carried a 50% Section 232 tariff on the value of non-US steel and aluminum content — [tED Magazine](https://tedmag.com/trump-administration-increases-tariffs-on-certain-transformers/)
- A 2 April 2026 proclamation moved steel/aluminum/copper derivative tariffs to full customs value; products with 15% or less metal content by weight fall outside; certain metal-intensive industrial and electrical grid equipment get a 15% rate through 2027 — [White & Case](https://www.whitecase.com/insight-alert/united-states-modifies-steel-aluminum-and-copper-section-232-tariffs)
- CRS (2 Sep 2026 update) tracks Section 232 actions including transformers and components; exact rate by HTS line not confirmed here — [CRS IF13006](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-09-02_IF13006_07b058112b883780702188ffec7cac6d198dab48.html)
- Copper faces tariffs of up to 50%; the "One Big Beautiful Bill" tightened FEOC rules — [POWER](https://powermag.com/transformers-in-2026-shortage-scramble-or-self-inflicted-crisis/)
- Teneo projected FEOC limits and tariffs could raise 2027 US front-of-meter storage system prices about 80% versus pre-OB3 expectations — [Teneo](https://www.teneo.com/insights/articles/ob3s-impact-on-us-energy-storage-a-bump-or-a-fork-in-the-road/)
- US demand still leans on imports: Astor Enerji (Turkey) booked $769M of US power transformer contracts for 2026-2028; LS Electric won a $312M 525 kV order for delivery 2027-2029 — [Kitco/Reuters](https://www.kitco.com/news/off-the-wire/2026-05-11/us-power-transformer-buyers-scramble-imports-factory-slots)

### Inferences
- A reassigned transformer order is a three-party deal by contract. The broker cannot deliver a slot without the OEM's signature, and the OEM can decline and reprice. The practical model is "OEM-approved channel partner", not independent market-maker.
- Warranty timing punishes surplus. With 18 months from shipment, a built unit that sat a year in a cancelled project's yard reaches the second buyer with months of cover left, and storage outside the OEM manual voids it. The reseller must buy an extended warranty, re-test, or self-insure.
- VTC's 45%-plus cancellation charge sets the seller's reservation price: an orphaned-order holder will rationally sell for anything above about 55% recovery. That is the spread a broker works inside, if the OEM consents.
- Impedance, voltage taps and utility vendor lists make GSUs and substation units project-specific. Data-center MV/LV transformers at common ratings and gensets/turbine packages are the fungible end.
- Tariff pass-through clauses mean an inherited import order can carry an unknown liability at shipment. That needs pricing into any assignment.

### Gaps
- I did not obtain an actual GE Vernova, Siemens Energy, Mitsubishi or Caterpillar reservation or supply agreement, so the assignment-consent point for turbines and gensets rests on one transformer OEM's terms plus commentary.
- No source on whether OEM warranties formally survive an approved assignment, or what OEMs charge for consent beyond VTC's $10,000.
- Exact current Section 232 rate for power transformer HTS codes not confirmed.
- No FEOC guidance specific to transformers or switchgear found (the rules bite mainly on storage and tax credits).

## 6. Working-capital norms: can a broker run back-to-back with no inventory

### Takeaway
OEMs take 20-30% up front and most of the rest before or at shipment, so a holder's position is cash-heavy and a taker must refund it. Pure brokerage on a success fee is feasible with almost no capital; back-to-back principal is only feasible if the buyer's deposit lands before the broker pays the seller, and the OEM consents.

### Cited Findings
- Virginia Transformer standard schedule: 30% with order, 30% on drawing submission, 40% at shipment, net 30; 1.5% per month late charge; "Payments shall not be contingent upon the end-user providing payment to the Buyer" — [VTC Terms](https://www.vatransformer.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/FINAL-2.23.2026-VTC-Standard-Terms-and-Conditions.pdf)
- VTC may demand full or partial advance payment if the buyer's financial condition does not justify the agreed terms, and takes a security interest in the goods — [VTC Terms](https://www.vatransformer.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/FINAL-2.23.2026-VTC-Standard-Terms-and-Conditions.pdf)
- Transformers, switchgear and gensets hold slots at 10-30% of equipment cost (Terrapin, June 2026; Global Data Center Hub, Aug 2026, via secondary) — [Open Factory](https://open-factory.com/articles/slot-economy/)
- GE Vernova customers had paid $25.8B in advance at Power and $40.0B company-wide in contract liabilities at June 2026; the author estimates about 21% of committed equipment value is paid — [Open Factory](https://open-factory.com/articles/slot-economy/)
- Modine took a $165.0M up-front deposit against more than $4B of expected 2027-2029 sales, about 4% — [Open Factory](https://open-factory.com/articles/slot-economy/)
- Hallador needed $600M of construction financing around a $350M equipment purchase and took title months before its final investment decision — [Turbomachinery](https://www.turbomachinerymag.com/view/hallador-energy-bypasses-oem-backlog-secures-unfired-siemens-turbine-in-350-million-deal)
- SecondWatt withholds pricing until a buyer signs an NDA and funding is confirmed, and charges its fee only on a closed purchase — [SecondWatt](https://secondwatt.com/)
- OnePWR, a capital-light holder of a large order book, could not finance completion and had to sell the position — [MLQ.ai](https://mlq.ai/news/anthropic-pays-34-billion-for-17-gw-of-caterpillar-gas-generators-that-onepwr-had-on-order-ft-reports/)

### Inferences
- OnePWR is the cautionary tale for the principal model: holding OEM positions without the balance sheet to take delivery ends in a forced sale. A startup taking principal risk on slots is short a financing option.
- Back-to-back works only as a simultaneous close: buyer wires the seller's sunk deposits plus premium, OEM novates the contract to the buyer. The broker never holds title. Economically that is brokerage, and the fee norms in section 7 apply.
- VTC's clause that payment is not contingent on the end user paying means an intermediary who signs as buyer carries full credit risk.

### Gaps
- No sourced payment schedule for turbine supply contracts after reservation, or for Caterpillar gensets.
- No source on what deposits surplus dealers collect from buyers (the USPE Global "inspection, NDA and deposit process" guide exists but I did not open it: https://uspeglobal.com/articles/how-to-buy-a-used-gas-turbine/).
- No evidence either way on OEMs accepting an intermediary as contracting party.

## 7. Margins and deal sizes

### Takeaway
Published broker fees in power equipment run 2-7% of price; cost-plus importers take 12-15%; I found no sourced gross margin for surplus dealers or remanufacturers. Ticket sizes span four orders of magnitude, from about $12M for a pair of small used turbines to $3.4B for an order book.

- A broker paid by both buyer and seller has a conflict of interest — [Turbomachinery](https://turbomachinerymag.com/view/three-questions-to-ask-your-equipment-consultant-or-broker)
- Bolt Electrical adds 12-15% to factory cost on imported substation transformers — [POWER](https://powermag.com/transformers-in-2026-shortage-scramble-or-self-inflicted-crisis/)

- Revenue will be lumpy and concentrated. The largest deals attract banks charging advisory fees on the same pool.
- The fee band is thin relative to venture expectations unless the company takes principal spread (needs capital, section 6) or layers on finance.

## 8. Standardization efforts that support the "repeatable spec" step

### Takeaway
Standardization is happening where one vendor controls the whole block (Schneider, Vertiv, Eaton power skids; VoltaGrid, ProEnergy, Bloom generation blocks), not across buyers. Federal bodies have urged standard large transformers for years and utilities have resisted, so a third party pooling demand around a neutral spec is pushing against both OEM product strategy and utility practice.

### Cited Findings
- NIAC (June 2024 draft) urged federal support for utilities, trade associations and manufacturers to standardize transformer designs; DOE found standardization "could increase production significantly" and was exploring a mass-producible standard transformer — [CISA/NIAC](https://www.cisa.gov/sites/default/files/2024-06/DRAFT_NIAC_Addressing%20the%20Critical%20Shortage%20of%20Power%20Transformers%20to%20Ensure%20Reliability%20of%20the%20U.S.%20Grid_Report_06052024_508c.pdf)
- DOE workshop: standardized transformers are needed "despite the utility sector's reluctance to deviate from their own standards" — [NETL/DOE](https://www.netl.doe.gov/sites/default/files/2023-10/Advanced%20Transformers%20Workshop%20Report.pdf)
- Schneider Electric launched standardized prefabricated power modules and skids at 2, 2.25 and 2.5 MW on 16 Sep 2026, Europe first, North America later in 2026 — [DCD](https://www.datacenterdynamics.com/en/news/schneider-electric-debuts-25mw-power-modules-for-ai-data-centers/)
- Vertiv sells power modules and skids up to 2,500 kW per unit built for repeatable manufacturing — [Vertiv brochure](https://www.vertiv.com/4ad7ce/globalassets/products/facilities-enclosures-and-racks/integrated-solutions/vertiv-powernexus/vertiv-prefabricated-power-solutions-brochure.pdf)
- A power block is typically 500 kW to several MW with defined interfaces, but a block designed to one hyperscaler's spec "cannot necessarily integrate with infrastructure built to another's" — [ARC Advisory](https://www.arcweb.com/industry-best-practices/power-block-how-modular-architectures-rewiring-data-centers-ai)
- Pooled forward demand already goes direct: Vantage reserved more than 1 GW of Heron Power solid-state transformer capacity in October 2026 (secondary); INNIO reserved 1.25 GW of engine capacity for Rehlko — [Data Center Richness](https://datacenterrichness.substack.com/p/solid-state-transformers-could-reshape); [Open Factory](https://open-factory.com/articles/slot-economy/)
- Long-term agreements and sales of future production slots are what give manufacturers the certainty to expand (Burns & McDonnell) — [Kitco/Reuters](https://www.kitco.com/news/off-the-wire/2026-05-11/us-power-transformer-buyers-scramble-imports-factory-slots)
- DOE's distribution transformer efficiency standard takes effect for units made or imported from 23 April 2029; DOE reopened it with a June 2026 request for information — [DOE](https://www.energy.gov/cmei/buildings/distribution-transformers); [Federal Register](https://regulations.justia.com/regulations/fedreg/2026/06/15/2026-11971.html)

### Inferences
- The "standard spec ordered against pooled demand" step competes with OEMs' own catalogue products and with large buyers' direct framework agreements. The addressable buyers are those too small for a framework deal: second-tier colo developers, neoclouds, co-ops and munis.
- Co-ops and munis already pool purchases through joint action agencies, so even that segment is partly served.
- Generation blocks (50 MW aeroderivative packages, 2.5 MW recip gensets) and data-center MV/LV skids are standard enough to pool. Utility-side GSUs and substation transformers are not.

### Gaps
- No hyperscaler-published open reference design for power blocks found.
- No evidence of an existing third-party demand-pooling consortium for data center power equipment.
- No 2026 DOE initiative on a standard large power transformer found; the federal material is 2023-2024.

## 9. Licensing and regulatory barriers to starting within weeks

### Takeaway
I found no licence required to broker or trade this equipment in the US. The gating items are commercial and compliance-related: OEM consent, tariff liability on imports, efficiency-rule cut-offs on distribution transformers, and site air permits for anything with an engine.

### Cited Findings
- DOE distribution transformer standards attach to manufacture or import date (23 April 2029), so earlier-built units can still be sold after that date — [DOE](https://www.energy.gov/cmei/buildings/distribution-transformers)
- Large power transformers above 34.5 kV fall outside that efficiency rule (industry guide) — [TransNine](https://www.transnineelectric.com/resources/us-transformer-efficiency-standards-2026-doe-2016-nema-tp-1-and-what-buyers-must-know/)
- Air permits and grid connections can still delay projects that have secured used turbines — [MLQ.ai summary of Reuters](https://mlq.ai/news/data-centers-hunt-for-used-gas-turbines-and-converted-jet-engines-as-new-units-slip-to-2030/)
- Importers bear Section 232 exposure on full customs value under the April 2026 framework — [White & Case](https://www.whitecase.com/insight-alert/united-states-modifies-steel-aluminum-and-copper-section-232-tariffs)
- Large plant sales need HSR, FERC and state approvals (NRG to LS Power), but those attach to operating generation assets, not loose equipment — [24/7 Wall St](https://247wallst.com/investing/2026/11/09/shortage-of-turbines-drives-market-for-second-hand-market-for-ai-data-centers/)
- Secured-party and bankruptcy sales of data center equipment run through UCC Article 9 and Chapter 7/11 processes handled by auction firms — [SecondBloom press release](https://www.einpresswire.com/article/955580439/secondbloom-auctions-moves-into-data-centers-as-canceled-projects-top-260-billion-in-2026)

### Inferences
- A broker can start in weeks. Legal friction is ordinary: state sales-tax resale certificates, export controls if selling abroad, and contract drafting for novation. None of this is specific to the sector, and I am stating it from general knowledge, not from a source found here.
- Reselling financial exposure to slots without physical delivery could raise commodity or securities-law questions. This is a hypothesis to put to counsel; I found no source addressing it.

### Gaps
- No source found on EPA rules for resale of used stationary engines (NSPS/NESHAP certification) or TSCA PCB rules for older oil-filled transformers; both are known compliance areas that I could not document in this pass.
- No source on state dealer licensing for industrial equipment; I believe there is none of general application but could not cite it.

## 10. Signs the shortage is easing or could reverse

### Takeaway
As of November 2026 lead times and prices for the scarce classes are still rising, but the first cracks appeared this quarter: GE Vernova's contracted-GW growth slowed to 6 GW, SoftBank walked away from a large block of reservations, AI power stocks sold off, and a wave of transformer and turbine capacity comes online in 2027-2028. A clearinghouse built on scarcity is long a shortage that OEMs are spending billions to end.

### Inferences
- The business has a regime problem. In a tight market holders do not sell and OEMs reassign slots themselves. In a loosening market supply of orphaned positions appears, but the premium for them collapses. The profitable window is the turn, when some holders are distressed while others are still short. November 2026 may be the start of that window.
- 48% of GE Vernova's contracted gas GW is reservations, i.e. options. If conversion slows, OEMs will have freed slots to sell directly, which competes with any secondary seller.
- The distribution-transformer and BESS experience shows these shortages do clear within two to three years once capacity lands. For GSUs and heavy turbines the capacity dates cluster in 2027-2028.

### Gaps
- No hard evidence of a hyperscaler capex cut as of 23 Nov 2026; the signals are SoftBank, analyst target cuts and politics.
- No Q3 2026 Wood Mackenzie transformer survey located to confirm whether large-unit lead times have peaked.
- The "6 GW" slowdown has an innocent reading (GE Vernova is nearly sold out through 2030, so there is less to sell); I cannot separate the two from public sources.
