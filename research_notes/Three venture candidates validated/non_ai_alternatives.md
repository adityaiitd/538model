## 1. IEEPA tariff refund claims (and duty drawback as the adjacent contingency business)

### Takeaway
The refund trade is real but late: the Supreme Court struck the IEEPA tariffs on 20 Feb 2026, CBP's CAPE system had refunded over 75% of the ~$166B pool (about $126B) by 2 Oct 2026, and the remaining ~$40B is mostly finally-liquidated entries of non-plaintiff importers that can already get paid in days by filing their own CIT case. Claim discounts compressed from 20-30 cents on the dollar (mid-2025) to 70-85 cents (spring 2026), so the principal spread is largely gone; duty drawback is a durable but small (~$1B/yr official figure) broker-licensed contingency niche with several AI startups already in it.

### Cited Findings

**The ruling**
- Learning Resources v. Trump decided 20 Feb 2026, 6-3, Roberts writing: IEEPA's power to "regulate" does not extend to imposing tariffs. The Court did not address refunds. [S] — [Thomson Reuters](https://tax.thomsonreuters.com/blog/supreme-court-tariff-ruling-in-learning-resources-inc-v-trump-what-corporate-tax-and-trade-teams-need-to-know/); [Reed Smith](https://www.reedsmith.com/our-insights/blogs/trade-compliance-resource-hub/5000/scotus-strikes-down-ieepa-tariffs-leaves-refund-question-open/)
- Pool size estimates at decision time: ~$170B to ~$175B of duties potentially refundable; later government filings use ~$166B collected. [S]/[F] — [Ropes & Gray](https://ropesgray.com/en/insights/alerts/2026/02/supreme-court-strikes-down-ieepa-tariffs-key-takeaways-and-implications-for-importers); [DWT](https://www.dwt.com/insights/2026/10/cit-certifies-ieepa-duty-refund-importer-class)
- About 330,000 importers paid IEEPA duties (330,566 per a March 2026 CBP count). [F]/[S] — [DWT](https://www.dwt.com/insights/2026/10/cit-certifies-ieepa-duty-refund-importer-class); [Orrick](https://www.orrick.com/zh-TW/Insights/2026/03/CBP-Delivers-Progress-Report-on-IEEPA-Tariff-Refund-System)

**The refund process (CIT + CBP)**
- CIT refund order issued 4 Mar 2026; CBP built CAPE (Consolidated Administration and Processing of Entries) inside ACE; CAPE opened 20 Apr 2026. Phases 1-2 cover most unliquidated entries and entries liquidated within the last 80 days. [F]/[S] — [DWT](https://www.dwt.com/insights/2026/10/cit-certifies-ieepa-duty-refund-importer-class); [PPAI](https://www.ppai.org/media-hub/cbp-issues-over-20b-in-ieepa-refunds-in-first-month/)
- Only the importer of record or the customs broker that filed the original entry can submit a CAPE declaration; a lender or claims purchaser generally cannot file. Refunds go by ACH to the importer; a "4811 party" designation may not be honored. [F] — [Sidley](https://www.sidley.com/en/insights/newsupdates/2026/04/ieepa-tariff-refund-claims-key-considerations-for-lenders-borrowers-and-claims-purchasers)
- CAPE Phase 3 (finally liquidated entries, i.e. liquidated more than 80 days ago) opened 6 Oct 2026, but only on a rolling basis for nearly 7,000 importers that filed nearly 4,000 CIT lawsuits. Non-plaintiff importers with finally liquidated entries remain locked out while the government's partial appeal (filed 3 Jun 2026) of Judge Eaton's universal injunction is pending; the injunction is stayed for non-plaintiffs. [F] — [DWT](https://www.dwt.com/insights/2026/10/cit-certifies-ieepa-duty-refund-importer-class)
- On 8 Oct 2026 the CIT (Judge Eaton) certified a class in Freestyle World, Inc. v. CBP: all importers of record that paid the challenged IEEPA duties between 1 Feb 2025 and 19 Feb 2026, excluding those with their own suits. No classwide relief order yet; government "all but sure to appeal." Brazil and India IEEPA duties are outside the class definition. A class motion in V.O.S. Selections is pending. [F] — [DWT](https://www.dwt.com/insights/2026/10/cit-certifies-ieepa-duty-refund-importer-class)
- Individual CIT refund orders have "typically issued within four business days of filing," and CAPE access within one business day after that; refund suits under 28 U.S.C. § 1581(i) can be filed through 4 Feb 2027 at the earliest. [F] — [DWT](https://www.dwt.com/insights/2026/10/cit-certifies-ieepa-duty-refund-importer-class)

**How much has been paid**
- May 2026: ~$20.6B certified to Treasury in the first month. [S] — [PPAI](https://www.ppai.org/media-hub/cbp-issues-over-20b-in-ieepa-refunds-in-first-month/)
- 29 Jun 2026: $104.29B authorized, $71.06B paid including interest; Cato projected refunds could reach ~$130B of $166B. [S] — [Cato](https://www.cato.org/blog/ieepa-refunds-update-good-progress-still-ways-go) (page returned 403 on fetch; figure from search summary)
- 2 Oct 2026: over 75% of ~$166B refunded with interest [F]; trade trackers put it at ~$136.6B accepted and ~$126B sent to Treasury, up from $122B in the September report [S]. — [DWT](https://www.dwt.com/insights/2026/10/cit-certifies-ieepa-duty-refund-importer-class); [GHY](https://www.ghy.com/trade-compliance/cbp-cape-ieepa-refund-progress/); [Baker Tilly](https://www.bakertilly.com/insights/ieepa-refunds)
- 23 Oct 2026 data: $137.9B potential and certified, 222,754 validated declarations. [S] — [corp-intl](https://corp-intl.com/news/what-is-the-status-of-ieepa-refunds) (secondary tracker citing a 2 Nov National Law Review piece)
- September 2026: ~20,184 refunds worth $1.3B on hold because the importer had not supplied ACH banking details. Small firms have had trouble with CAPE. [S] — [GHY](https://www.ghy.com/trade-compliance/cbp-cape-ieepa-refund-progress/); [Cato](https://www.cato.org/blog/us-tariff-refunds-mostly-good-developments-still-long-way-go)

**Claims-purchase market and pricing**
- Reported pricing path: ~20-30 cents (mid-2025), 30-40 cents just before the ruling, 40-50 cents right after (Reuters via CryptoSlate), up to 60 cents (Sidley, April 2026, calling these "journalistic approximations"), 55-75 cents by early April once CAPE went live, and hedge funds paying over 70 cents. [S]/[F] — [Global Trade Magazine](https://www.globaltrademag.com/tariff-refund-monetization-retailers-sell-claims-for-quick-cash/); [CryptoSlate](https://cryptoslate.com/did-stablecoin-backed-treasurys-custodian-cantor-fitzgerald-buy-tariff-refunds-for-20-cents-on-the-dollar/); [Sidley](https://www.sidley.com/en/insights/newsupdates/2026/04/ieepa-tariff-refund-claims-key-considerations-for-lenders-borrowers-and-claims-purchasers); [Riffon](https://riffon.com/insight/ins_fg8zzqpcc4is)
- One reported deal: Oaktree said BJ's agreed to sell a $29M claim for about $20M (~70 cents); BJ's allegedly withdrew after CBP announced the refund portal in April. [S, exact source page among the trade-press results not opened] — [CBIZ](https://www.cbiz.com/insights/article/hedge-funds-capitalize-on-tariff-refund-opportunities); [Global Trade Magazine](https://www.globaltrademag.com/tariff-refund-monetization-retailers-sell-claims-for-quick-cash/)
- Seller-side marketing page (late 2026) quotes discounts of 10-15% for clean unliquidated claims, 15-20% for entries liquidated within 180 days, 20-30% for complex or finally liquidated entries; another commercial buyer advertises 75-85 cents for claims of $500K+. Both are commercial sources. [S] — [tariffresolution.com](https://tariffresolution.com/insights/sell-ieepa-tariff-refund-immediate-capital/); [tariffclaimprocessing.com](https://tariffclaimprocessing.com/feeds/blog/lutnick-tariff-refund)
- Buyers/lenders: hedge funds and private credit funds buying outright; commercial banks, hedge funds and private credit offering term loans against claims at ~50% LTV, PIK interest, $10M minimum loan against $20M+ claims. Sidley cites press talk of a secondary market "up to $100 billion" (journalistic approximation). No named buyers. [F] — [Sidley](https://www.sidley.com/en/insights/newsupdates/2026/04/ieepa-tariff-refund-claims-key-considerations-for-lenders-borrowers-and-claims-purchasers)
- Legal friction for buyers: under the Assignment of Claims Act (31 U.S.C. § 3727) an assignment is valid only after the claim is allowed, the amount decided and a warrant issued, so an outright assignment of most IEEPA claims "could be void or voidable"; UCC 9-408 does not override it; workable structure is seller collects and remits proceeds. Other risks: bankruptcy (11 U.S.C. § 552(a)), early CAPE rejection rate ~15%, consumer class actions and customer recoupment claims against the importer's refund. [F] — [Sidley](https://www.sidley.com/en/insights/newsupdates/2026/04/ieepa-tariff-refund-claims-key-considerations-for-lenders-borrowers-and-claims-purchasers); [Covington](https://www.cov.com/en/news-and-insights/insights/2026/03/consumer-class-actions-arising-from-ieepa-tariff-refund-efforts)

**Duty drawback (adjacent)**
- Official size: GAO (Dec 2019, GAO-20-182) says CBP disburses about $1B per year in drawback; CBP denied ~$163M of improper claims over four years. This figure is seven years old. [S] — [GAO-20-182](https://www.gao.gov/products/gao-20-182)
- Unverified newer claims: "$1.5B processed in FY2024" (blog attributing to CBP) and "$11-15B/yr unclaimed" (traced to startup Zollback's own research, March 2026). [S] — [globalviewsworld](https://globalviewsworld.com/cbp-tariff-refunds-1-5-billion-in-2024-claims/); [Zollback](https://www.zollback.com/blog/tariff-refunds-for-smbs)
- Eligibility limits: Section 232 steel/aluminum duties were barred from drawback by the 2018 proclamations ("No drawback shall be available..."). Vendor/law-firm summaries say Proclamation 11021 (April 2026) and CBP CSMS #68253075 now allow only manufacturing drawback (19 U.S.C. 1313(a),(b)) for steel, aluminum and copper articles meeting four conditions including melt-and-pour documentation; unused-merchandise and substitution drawback stay barred; auto/MHDV parts have manufacturing drawback only since 1 Nov 2025. [S] — [Alliance CHB](https://alliancechb.com/duty-drawback/section-232-drawback/); [Pax AI matrix](https://www.paxai.com/resources/section-232-drawback-matrix); [White & Case](https://www.whitecase.com/insight-alert/united-states-modifies-steel-aluminum-and-copper-section-232-tariffs); [2018 analysis](https://www.npllptradelaw.com/2018-6-6-duty-drawback-and-the-steel-and-aluminum-tariffs-a-fight-to-be-fought/)
- Players visible in 2026 search results: Pax AI, Zollback ("first AI-powered drawback platform for SMBs"), LightSource, OnRamp, Passport, GingerControl, Alliance CHB, Zarach. [S] — [Wisp list](https://www.wisp.blog/blog/best-duty-drawback-software-maximize-claims); [Zollback press release](https://www.abnewswire.com/pressreleases/zollback-launches-aipowered-duty-drawback-platform-to-help-us-businesses-recover-billions-in-unclaimed-tariff-refunds_796222.html)

### Inferences
- Remaining unpaid pool is roughly $166B minus ~$126B = ~$40B (plus interest), and not all of it is addressable: some belongs to large litigants already in Phase 3, some is Brazil/India duties outside the class, some will never be claimed.
- The discount that made claim-buying a hedge-fund trade (legal risk on the merits, then process risk) has been resolved by events. With a CIT order obtainable in about four business days and a certified class behind it, the residual "risk premium" on a clean claim is a time-value and hassle discount of 10-30%, competed for by funds with far cheaper capital. This is a shrinking, late-stage market, not an entry point.
- The only residual wedge is service, not principal: the long tail of the ~330,000 importers that have not filed (small firms, missing ACH data, finally liquidated entries). That is a contingency-fee filing business with a hard clock (limitations period begins expiring around Feb 2027 for earliest entries) and it needs a licensed customs broker and/or CIT-admitted counsel as partner because only the importer or its broker can file in CAPE.
- 30-day legal start: yes as a funder/participation buyer (contract only, no license), with Assignment of Claims Act exposure; no as a direct filer without a broker/attorney partner. Revenue ceiling (my arithmetic, not sourced): capturing even 2% of the ~$40B residual at a 10-15% fee/discount is $80-120M gross, but that requires winning ~$800M of claims from tens of thousands of small importers in under six months against law firms, brokers and funds already marketing to them. Realistic first-18-month revenue for a November 2026 entrant: low single-digit to low tens of millions, then zero as the pool empties.
- Drawback is structurally a ~$1B/yr refund stream with typical contingency economics; even a 10% share of the official figure at a 15-25% fee would be ~$15-25M revenue. The IEEPA refunds also remove a large block of duties that would otherwise have been drawback-eligible. It cannot reach $100M in 18 months on verified numbers.

## 2. Provenance-cleared critical minerals and rare-earth magnets ahead of the DFARS ban (1 Jan 2027)

### Takeaway
The price dislocation is extreme and current (dysprosium ~19x, terbium ~8x, yttrium ~125x China domestic in Oct-Nov 2026), and the DFARS mine-to-magnet rule starts in five weeks, but the spread exists because compliant material does not exist in volume, not because nobody is intermediating. The scarce asset is physical non-Chinese supply already locked in government-backed offtakes, and Project Vault procurement is run through Hartree, Traxys and Mercuria; a new entrant without supply relationships and trade finance has nothing to sell.

### Cited Findings

**The rule**
- DFARS 252.225-7052: effective 1 Jan 2027, for NdFeB magnets the restriction covers the entire supply chain from mining through finished magnets; tungsten and tantalum reach back to ore/feedstock. The date was already slipped one year (from 1 Jan 2026) by the FY2024 NDAA. [S] — [acquisition.gov DFARS text](https://acquisition.gov/dfars/252.225-7052-restriction-acquisition-certain-magnets-tantalum-and-tungsten.); [Federal Register 2024 final rule](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2024-05-30/pdf/2024-11513.pdf)
- Covered materials under 10 U.S.C. § 4872: SmCo and NdFeB magnets, tungsten metal powder and heavy alloys, tantalum metals and alloys, molybdenum, and (from 18 Dec 2027) gallium and germanium; covered countries China, Russia, Iran, North Korea. [F] — [The Oregon Group](https://theoregongroup.com/latest-news/pentagon-china-minerals-ban-supply-10-years/)
- Executive Order 14415 (20 Jul 2026) ends routine waivers after 1 Jan 2027 unless the contractor has an accepted mitigation plan documenting "exhaustive" efforts. Contracts with the older 7961 clause awarded before 1 Jan 2027 can still deliver some covered material. [F]/[S] — [The Oregon Group](https://theoregongroup.com/latest-news/pentagon-china-minerals-ban-supply-10-years/); [Steptoe](https://www.steptoe.com/en/news-publications/international-compliance-blog/executive-order-tightens-covered-materials-waivers-and-expands-defense-supply-chain-due-diligence.html); [Pillsbury](https://www.pillsburylaw.com/en/news-and-insights/time-running-short-update-dfars-covered-materials-clauses.html)

### Inferences
- The ex-China premium is a scarcity rent that accrues to holders of compliant physical supply (Lynas, MP, Almonty, Serra Verde, recyclers), and those volumes are already tied to DoD/Ex-Im-backed offtakes and three named trading houses. A trader cannot arbitrage China-to-West because the binding constraint is a Chinese export licence plus, for defense buyers, a legal ban on Chinese origin at every tier.
- The addressable heavy-rare-earth pie is small in dollars: Lynas's 23 t/quarter of Dy+Tb at $4,100-8,100/kg is roughly $95-185M per quarter gross if all sold at spot (my arithmetic; most is contracted). A market-maker's margin on a slice of that cannot be $100M.
- Tungsten, antimony, gallium and germanium are larger and messier and do reward relationship traders, but margins there come from pre-financed offtakes and inventory, which are balance-sheet-heavy and relationship-gated, the opposite of capital-light and fast.
- A defensible adjacent role for the founder's skill set is provenance/compliance attestation (chain-of-custody evidence for DFARS mitigation plans), but that is a compliance-software sale to primes and tier-2s, i.e. the long enterprise cycle the founder refuses.
- 30-day legal start: yes in the narrow sense (no federal licence is needed to buy and sell physical metal domestically). Practically no: KYC/bank trade-finance lines, supplier qualification (aerospace qualification can take years per AIA) and counterparty onboarding take months. Realistic first-18-month revenue for an entrant without supply: gross trading turnover in the low tens of millions at best, with low-single-digit-million gross margin; I found no example of a 2025-2026 startup trader reaching scale.
- Policy risk cuts both ways: an AIA-style waiver glide path or H.R. 8800 would relieve urgency; a Chinese re-tightening on 27 Nov 2026 / 10 Jan 2027 would widen spreads but not create supply.

### Gaps
- No verified data on trader margins, trade-finance terms, or any startup critical-minerals trader's funding or revenue in 2026 (searches returned nothing).
- No consolidated dollar size for the non-Chinese market per metal; no verified tantalum data.
- Price series conflict widely across Platts, Fastmarkets, Benchmark, SMM and Argus (e.g. Dy multiple 4.9x vs 16-19x); I did not access the primary price sheets.
- Trafigura's and other majors' specific positions in rare earths/tungsten were not found.
- Whether Project Vault has actually bought from any non-named trader is unverified; the "open to all" statement is from April 2026.
- Outcome of the 27 Nov 2026 gallium/germanium/antimony suspension expiry is not yet known.

## 3. Receivables/invoice collateral verification and clearing after First Brands and Tricolor

### Takeaway
The failure mode is documented and large (First Brands: ~$2.3B factoring plus ~$2.3B SPV financing with invoices never checked against the debtor; Tricolor: $2.2B pledged against $1.4B of real collateral, ~29,000 of 70,000 loans sharing VINs), and lenders are tightening, but the visible response is counsel-driven diligence checklists plus one incumbent product (Setpoint PledgeCheck, bank-backed). I found no evidence of a registry mandate, no evidence of a fast principal-spread business built on verification, and one cautionary data point (Raistone, a platform with ~80% revenue from First Brands, filed Chapter 7).

### Cited Findings

**What happened**
- First Brands first-day figures: ~$5B annual sales; ~$6.1B on-balance-sheet funded debt; ~$2.3B SPV financing; ~$2.3B factoring exposure; ~$800M supply-chain obligations (categories "not additive"). DIP of $4.4B ($1.1B new money, $3.3B roll-up). [F] — [Nelson Mullins, 3 Sep 2026](https://www.nelsonmullins.com/insights/blogs/red-zone/bankruptcy-litigation/first-brands-after-conversion-what-the-collapse-actually-teaches-lenders-and-trade-creditors)
- Timeline: examiner approved 9 Jan 2026; SDNY indicted Patrick and Edward James 29 Jan 2026 (fake/inflated invoices, double- and triple-pledged collateral; both pleaded not guilty); interim examiner report 27 Apr 2026; plan confirmation denied 24 Aug 2026; cases converted to Chapter 7 on 1 Sep 2026. The estates needed $1.9-2B of litigation proceeds just to pay administrative and priority claims; litigation trust began with ~$75M. [F]/[S] — [Nelson Mullins](https://www.nelsonmullins.com/insights/blogs/red-zone/bankruptcy-litigation/first-brands-after-conversion-what-the-collapse-actually-teaches-lenders-and-trade-creditors); [GTR](https://www.gtreview.com/news/americas/weaknesses-in-us-factoring-programmes-critical-to-first-brands-alleged-fraud/)
- Examiner (27 Apr 2026): "widespread fraud" against third-party receivables financiers; lenders "typically did not see the actual underlying invoices or verify the data against the invoices stored in [First Brands'] database"; routing repayments through First Brands was "critical to the fraudulent scheme." Assets were moved to SPV affiliates and re-pledged to other lenders. Patrick James disputes the report as one-sided. Findings are allegations, not adjudicated. [S] — [GTR](https://www.gtreview.com/news/americas/weaknesses-in-us-factoring-programmes-critical-to-first-brands-alleged-fraud/); [GTR on double financing](https://www.gtreview.com/news/americas/first-brands-faces-investigation-into-double-financing-of-receivables-inventory/)
- Alleged scheme involved ~$2.3B in factoring facilities and $682M of supply chain finance; Raistone drew up to 80% of revenue from First Brands and filed Chapter 7 on 23 Feb 2026; Morningstar DBRS estimates trade credit insurance losses of $300-600M base case, over $1B adverse. [S, secondary sources] — [tradefinance.news](https://www.tradefinance.news/articles/2026-03-07-first-brands-receivables-fraud); [ABF Journal](https://www.abfjournal.com/after-first-brands-how-the-largest-private-credit-fraud-in-history-is-rewriting-middle-market-underwriting/); [BCR](https://bcrpub.com/news/receivables-finance-the-verification-gap-after-first-brands/)
- Tricolor: prosecutors allege $2.2B of collateral pledged against $1.4B real; ~29,000 of 70,000 active loans shared identical VINs; JPMorgan charge ~$170M, Fifth Third $178-200M, Barclays also exposed; executives charged Dec 2025; investors sued JPMorgan, Barclays and Fifth Third (Feb 2026) for missing "giant red flags." [S] — [CNN](https://www.cnn.com/2025/12/17/business/auto-lender-tricolor-executives-criminal-charges); [Claims Journal](https://www.claimsjournal.com/news/national/2026/03/02/335984.htm); [Cobalt Intelligence](https://newsletter.cobaltintelligence.com/p/tricolor-29-000-loans-pledged-twice)

**What lenders have done since**
- Recommended practice (counsel): tie ERP records to POs, shipping evidence, invoices, credit memos and lockbox activity; account-debtor confirmations; unique asset identifiers; anti-duplication reps; direct read-only access to ERP and bank data; lien releases and control agreements as closing deliverables. [F] — [Nelson Mullins](https://www.nelsonmullins.com/insights/blogs/red-zone/bankruptcy-litigation/first-brands-after-conversion-what-the-collapse-actually-teaches-lenders-and-trade-creditors); see also [Willkie/ABA](https://www.americanbar.org/groups/business_law/resources/business-law-today/2026-february/first-brands-avoid-being-two-timed-by-collateral)
- Funds with receivables exposure are reportedly running independent verification sweeps on existing portfolios and renegotiating documents. [S, trade press] — [ABF Journal](https://www.abfjournal.com/after-first-brands-how-the-largest-private-credit-fraud-in-history-is-rewriting-middle-market-underwriting/)
- Wolters Kluwer commentary urges third-party or shared-registry certification that each asset is in only one active facility before each advance, and higher spreads / lower advance rates for borrowers that refuse. This is advocacy, not a rule. [S] — [Wolters Kluwer](https://www.wolterskluwer.com/en/expert-insights/double-pledging-after-tricolor-stop-treating-collateral-like-a-suggestion)

**Existing players**
- Setpoint launched PledgeCheck (reported 30 Oct 2025): borrowers upload master tapes and facility-level reports for each lender; automated cross-lender checks for uniqueness, data integrity, completeness and single-record. No pricing, named adopters or covered volume disclosed. Setpoint itself says centralized universal asset registries "remain impractical." [F]/[S] — [ABF Journal](https://www.abfjournal.com/setpoint-launches-industry-standard-to-stop-double-pledging/); [Setpoint](https://setpoint.io/solutions/capital-providers)
- Setpoint funding: $31M Series B (Aug 2024) led by 645 Ventures with Citi, Wells Fargo and Andreessen Horowitz; $76M total raised at that time. No 2026 round found. It served as third-party reviewer on Figure's $316.3M HELOC securitization (FIGRE 2026-EXP2). [S] — [Business Wire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20240813720509/en/Setpoint-Secures-$31M-Series-B-From-Wells-Fargo-Citi-645-Ventures-Andreessen-Horowitz-and-Others-to-Revolutionize-Credit-Infrastructure); [FinanceInsyte](https://financeinsyte.com/setpoint-facilitates-figure-316-3m-heloc-securitization/)
- UCC filings are state-based, blanket and not asset-specific, so they do not catch invoice-level double pledging; one ABL lender describes pre- and post-funding UCC searches with continuous monitoring as its control. Other vendors marketing into the gap: Botminds (UCC lien search), Alterest, Vaas (LatAm registry analogy). [S, vendor sources] — [Vaas](https://www.getvaas.com/en/discover/discover-blog/26); [SFNet](https://www.sfnet.com/home/industry-data-publications/the-secured-lender/magazine/tsl-article-detail/built-in-discipline-how-asset-based-lending-stays-secure-and-efficient-amid-emerging-risk); [Botminds](https://www.botminds.ai/solutions/ucc-lien-search/double-pledging-collateral)

### Inferences
- Demand is real but it is being met by (a) lawyers and field examiners tightening documents and (b) an incumbent whose cap table includes Citi and Wells Fargo. A registry only works with multi-lender adoption, which is a consortium sale, the slowest kind.
- A "principal-spread wedge" would mean taking credit risk on receivables one has verified (i.e. becoming a factor, credit insurer or first-loss guarantor). That is balance-sheet business, not capital-light, and the post-First Brands environment (insurer losses of $300M-$1B+, Raistone's failure) makes capital providers more, not less, demanding of new platforms. No comparable firm was found earning a clearing spread on verified receivables at scale.
- 30-day legal start: yes for verification software/services (no licence). For principal factoring/lending, commercial finance is lightly licensed in most states but not all, and funding lines take months; I could not source the state-by-state position this session.
- Largest realistic first-18-month revenue from comparables: low single-digit millions (verification SaaS / per-facility fees). Setpoint, the best-funded comp, has raised ~$76M over several years and discloses no revenue. Nothing here supports $100M.

### Gaps
- No data on PledgeCheck adoption, pricing or volume; no revenue figures for Setpoint, Finley, Cascade or MonetaGo (MonetaGo's US activity was not found at all; Finley and Cascade did not surface in 2026 results).
- The First Brands examiner report itself and creditor-level losses (Jefferies/Point Bonita, UBS O'Connor, Onset, Evolution) were not opened or quantified here.
- No survey or rating-agency data quantifying how many lenders now require third-party invoice verification; KBRA and S&P pieces were cited by secondary sources but not accessible.
- No evidence found either way on banks' in-house tools.
- State licensing requirements for commercial factoring not verified.

## 4. No Surprises Act IDR filing on contingency for out-of-network providers

### Takeaway
This is the one candidate where the comparables clearly support nine-figure revenue quickly: 2.6M disputes in 2025 (+77%), 1.75M in H1 2026 (+50%), providers winning ~85% at a median 445% of QPA, about $15.6B of awards above QPA over 2023-2025, and HaloMD reportedly taking ~20% of net recovery while filing ~27% of dispute lines. It is also concentrated (three filers hold over three-quarters), politically radioactive ($22.4B of added cost, premium increases), and faces a live bill to end IDR for services from 1 Jan 2028, though insurer lawsuits against filers have so far been dismissed and the June 2026 rule cut filing fees.

### Cited Findings

**Volumes**
- 2025: 2.6M new disputes, up 77% from 2024 (original federal estimate was ~17,000-22,000/yr). H1 2025 ~1.2M vs ~590,000 in H1 2024; H2 2025 ~1.4M. H1 2026: 1.75M, 50% above H1 2025. IDR entities resolved ~2.7M in 2025. [F] — [Georgetown CHIR, $22.4B analysis](https://chir.georgetown.edu/spending-on-idr-process-pushes-no-surprises-act-costs-to-more-than-22-4-billion-over-just-four-years/); [Georgetown CHIR, early look at 2025](https://chir.georgetown.edu/the-no-surprises-act-idr-process-an-early-look-at-2025-data/)
- As of May 2026, more than 6.3M disputes initiated cumulatively and nearly 5.9M resolved. [S] — [Health Affairs Forefront](https://www.healthaffairs.org/content/forefront/no-surprises-act-idr-process-early-look-2025-data)

**Win rates and awards**
- Provider win rate: 81% (2023), 85% (2024), 88% (H1 2025), ~85% for full-year 2025. By filer: Radiology Partners 96%, TeamHealth 95%, SCP Health 92%, AGS Health 91%. [F] — both CHIR pieces above
- Median prevailing provider award 445% of QPA in 2025 (447% in 2024); 90th percentile 1,771% (1,226% in 2024); median prevailing plan award 105% of QPA. Specialty medians 2025: neurology/neuromuscular 2,450%, surgery 1,355%, radiology 468%, emergency 315%. [F] — [CHIR](https://chir.georgetown.edu/spending-on-idr-process-pushes-no-surprises-act-costs-to-more-than-22-4-billion-over-just-four-years/)
- By filer, H1 2025 medians: HaloMD 920% (Q1) and 835% (Q2) of QPA; Radiology Partners 582%/594%; SCP Health ~370%; TeamHealth 277%. [F] — [CHIR early look](https://chir.georgetown.edu/the-no-surprises-act-idr-process-an-early-look-at-2025-data/)
- Arbitrator variance: iPRO ruled for providers in 99.4% of Q4 2025 arbitrated disputes (median 601% of QPA); Medical Evaluators of Texas 54.5% (median 159%). [F] — [CHIR](https://chir.georgetown.edu/spending-on-idr-process-pushes-no-surprises-act-costs-to-more-than-22-4-billion-over-just-four-years/)

- 2025 share of resolved dispute lines: Radiology Partners 30%, HaloMD 27%, TeamHealth 20% (over three-quarters combined). ### Inferences
- Implied revenue scale for the leading third-party filer (my arithmetic, not sourced): if 2025 above-QPA awards were on the order of $10-12B and HaloMD's share of dollars is anywhere near its 27% share of dispute lines, 16-20% of net recovery implies several hundred million dollars of annual revenue. Even at one-tenth of HaloMD's share, a filer would clear $30-60M. This is the only one of the five markets where a contingency intermediary demonstrably operates at $100M+ scale, and the 2022 start of IDR means it got there in roughly three years.
- The market is live and still growing in late 2026 (H1 2026 +50%), and the June 2026 rule lowered filing cost, which favors high-volume automated filers. The founder's AI/eligibility-underwriting skills map directly: the measurable edge is fewer ineligible filings (18% found ineligible), arbitrator selection (99.4% vs 54.5% provider win rates by IDR entity) and batching.
- Sales cycle is closer to SMB than enterprise: clients are out-of-network physician groups, ASCs, neuromonitoring and surgical practices signing a contingency agreement; but the dominant volume belongs to large PE-backed groups that self-file (Radiology Partners, TeamHealth, SCP), which are not addressable.
- Kill risks: (1) statutory sunset risk, with a named bill ending IDR for 2028 dates of service and a plausible narrower fix in a December 2026 package (award caps or QPA-primary would cut the 445% median toward ~105%); (2) cash conversion, since awards are often paid late and the filer or client fronts $425-1,125 per dispute; (3) continued insurer litigation (Ninth Circuit appeal pending) and reputational exposure from being the named cause of premium increases; (4) dependence on provider-friendly arbitrators.
- 30-day legal start: plausibly yes. IDR initiation is done through the federal portal on behalf of a provider and the leading intermediary is not a law firm or carrier; I found no licensing requirement for third-party representatives, but did not verify this against CMS rules. Realistic first-18-month revenue for a new entrant: $5-30M if it signs mid-size specialty groups quickly; $100M would require matching a meaningful fraction of HaloMD's book within the window before any 2028 sunset.

### Gaps
- HaloMD's actual revenue, client count and the SEC-filed services agreement were not verified from primary filings; the 20%/16% contingency figures come from weak secondary sources. Contingency norms for other vendors not found.
- Whether third-party IDR representatives face any licensing, registration or unauthorized-practice-of-law constraint is unverified.
- Full-year 2026 volumes and any effect of the August 2026 rule on eligibility rejections are not yet published.
- Status of the Ninth Circuit appeal in Anthem v. HaloMD and the other pending insurer suits (BCBS Texas) as of Nov 2026 unknown.
- Whether any IDR provision is actually in the December 2026 funding package is unknown.
- Time from award to cash and the share of awards unpaid were not quantified.

## 5. Stablecoin cross-border B2B payments and FX for emerging-market importers/exporters

Volumes are large and growing (stablecoin payments ~$482B in Jan-Sep 2026 per Allium, B2B the largest segment) and emerging-market spreads are still fat (~300 bps median in Nigeria; 312-621 bps USDT/ARS), and a new firm can go live quickly on a licensed orchestration partner. **Licensing reality after GENIUS**
- GENIUS Act takes effect 18 Jan 2027; OCC final implementing rule issued 21 Oct 2026 (Federal Register 23 Oct), effective the same date; Treasury interim final rule 30 Sep 2026; most other agency rules still proposed. After the effective date only permitted payment stablecoin issuers may issue payment stablecoins in the US; OCC applications are deemed approved if not denied within 120 days, first decisions expected Q1/early Q2 2027. This regime governs issuers. [S] — [OCC Bulletin 2026-50](https://www.occ.gov/news-issuances/bulletins/2026/bulletin-2026-50.html); [Federal Register](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/10/23/2026-21706/implementing-the-guiding-and-establishing-national-innovation-for-us-stablecoins-act-for-the-issuance-of-stablecoins-by-entities-subject-to-the-jurisdiction-of-the-office-of-the-comptroller-of-the-currency); [Skadden](https://www.skadden.com/insights/publications/2026/10/occ-finalizes-genius-act-rule); [Davis Polk](https://www.davispolk.com/insights/client-update/occ-finalizes-regulatory-framework-implement-genius-act)

### Inferences
- The 300-600 bps spreads in Nigeria and Argentina are where the margin is, but they reflect local-currency liquidity, banking access and FX-control risk, i.e. the edge is local licences, bank accounts and OTC liquidity in each country, not US-side technology. In liquid corridors pricing is already under 5 bps and the rails are owned by Stripe and Mastercard.
- A $100M revenue line needs roughly $10-25B of annual flow at a 40-100 bps blended take (my arithmetic). Venture-backed comps took 2-4 years and tens of millions of equity to reach $10-45B annualized volume, and their revenue at that point was in the tens of millions. The fastest verified revenue comp (BVNK, ~$40M annualized after ~3 years) is well short of $100M in 18 months.
- 30-day legal start: yes, technically, by contracting with a licensed orchestration provider and registering as needed, though partner KYB/onboarding and corridor bank accounts are the real gating items; I found no sourced time-to-launch figure. Being the regulated principal in the flow (own MTLs, local EM licences) is a multi-quarter to multi-year project.
- Realistic first-18-month revenue for a new entrant on partner rails: $1-10M, with gross margin shared with the partner. Verdict: live and growing but crowded, consolidating into incumbents, and take rates compressing.

### Gaps
- No audited or primary revenue figures for Bridge, Conduit, Yellow Card, Bitso Business, Zerohash or OpenFX; no evidence of any stablecoin payments firm reaching $100M revenue within 18 months of launch.
- No sourced time-to-launch on Bridge/Zerohash/BVNK partner programs, and no verification of which states/corridors each partner covers.
- Whether GENIUS implementing rules (or state regimes) impose new obligations on non-issuer payment intermediaries from Jan 2027 was not established.
- LatAm/Africa B2B stablecoin volume figures conflict by orders of magnitude across vendor sources.
- Take-rate trend data (compression over 2025-2026) not found from a neutral source.

## 6. Cross-candidate screen: can a new company legally transact in ~30 days, and what is the largest realistic first-18-month revenue?

### Takeaway
On the evidence gathered, none of the five cleanly supports a capital-light market-maker reaching $100M revenue in 12-18 months from a November 2026 start; NSA IDR contingency filing is the only one where a comparable intermediary operates at that scale, and it carries an explicit legislative sunset risk. Tariff refund claims are a closing window, critical minerals are supply- and relationship-gated, receivables verification is a slow software sale, and stablecoin B2B is crowded with compressing take rates.

### Cited Findings
- Tariff refunds: over 75% of ~$166B already refunded by 2 Oct 2026; individual CIT orders issue in ~4 business days; class certified 8 Oct 2026. — [DWT](https://www.dwt.com/insights/2026/10/cit-certifies-ieepa-duty-refund-importer-class)
- Critical minerals: compliant aerospace-grade material "not commercially available today"; Project Vault procurement led by Hartree, Traxys, Mercuria. — [The Oregon Group](https://theoregongroup.com/latest-news/pentagon-china-minerals-ban-supply-10-years/); [Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-04-17/us-mineral-stockpile-to-be-open-to-all-traders-ex-im-bank-says)
- Receivables verification: incumbent Setpoint (Citi/Wells Fargo-backed) launched PledgeCheck Oct 2025 and itself calls universal registries impractical. — [ABF Journal](https://www.abfjournal.com/setpoint-launches-industry-standard-to-stop-double-pledging/); [Setpoint](https://setpoint.io/solutions/capital-providers)
- NSA IDR: 1.75M disputes in H1 2026 (+50%), median provider award 445% of QPA, three filers over 75% of lines; Pallone bill would end IDR for services from 1 Jan 2028. — [CHIR](https://chir.georgetown.edu/spending-on-idr-process-pushes-no-surprises-act-costs-to-more-than-22-4-billion-over-just-four-years/); [Healthcare Dive](https://www.healthcaredive.com/news/new-bill-would-overhaul-no-surprises-acts-arbitration-process-frank-pallone/832713/)
- Stablecoin B2B: BVNK ~$40M annualized revenue at ~3 years; OpenPayd $107M run rate after 8 years, stablecoin a small part. — [Fortune via Yahoo](https://finance.yahoo.com/news/exclusive-crypto-startup-bvnk-raises-130000509.html); [Analytics Insight](https://www.analyticsinsight.net/cryptocurrency-analytics-insight/openpayd-hits-107m-annualized-revenue-run-rate-as-stablecoin-business-gains-momentum)

### Inferences
Summary table (all revenue figures are my estimates from the comparables above, not sourced numbers):

| Candidate | Status late 2026 | Legal start in ~30 days? | Capital-light? | Largest realistic 18-month revenue | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| IEEPA refund claims | ~$126B of ~$166B paid; discounts down to 10-30% | Yes as funder (contract only; Assignment of Claims Act risk); filing needs broker/attorney partner | No for claim purchase; yes for contingency filing | Low single-digit to low tens of $M, then zero | Shrinking / nearly over |
| Duty drawback | ~$1B/yr official (2019); Sec. 232 mostly ineligible | Only via licensed customs broker | Yes | ~$5-25M | Live but small, several AI startups |
| Critical minerals / magnets | Spreads extreme; compliant supply tiny and pre-contracted | Legally yes; practically months (supply, qualification, trade finance) | No | Low tens of $M turnover, low-single-digit $M margin | Live but closed to newcomers |
| Receivables verification | Need proven; response is diligence + Setpoint | Yes for software; principal role needs balance sheet | Software yes; principal no | Low single-digit $M | Live need, slow sale, no spread wedge found |
| NSA IDR contingency | Growing 50% y/y; 85% win rate; 445% of QPA | Plausibly yes (no licence identified; unverified) | Yes (fees fronted per dispute; cash lag) | $5-30M realistic; $100M+ only by matching a large share of HaloMD's book | Live and lucrative, concentrated, sunset risk from 2028 |
| Stablecoin B2B EM | Growing ~45% y/y; consolidating | Yes via licensed partner (unverified timing) | Mostly (prefunding/liquidity needed in EM) | $1-10M | Live but crowded, take rates compressing |

- Ranking by fit to the stated constraints (fast legal start, contingency/spread economics, AI underwriting edge): IDR filing first by a wide margin; residual tariff-refund filing for small importers as a short-lived cash project; the other three fail on either access (minerals), speed of sale (receivables) or crowding (stablecoin).
- The IDR candidate's main non-financial cost is that its economics are the direct cause of the $22.4B cost figure regulators and Congress are reacting to; a business plan there should assume the 2026-2027 award levels do not persist beyond 2028.
