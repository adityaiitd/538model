# Industrial, electrical and retail surplus: do goods sell at liquidation prices because they cannot be verified? (US, as of November 2026)

Scope: (1) industrial surplus downgraded by missing paperwork (pipe, plate, valves, flanges without mill test reports; idle MRO spares), (2) used and surplus electrical distribution gear, (3) retail returns and excess consumer inventory.

Reading notes for the report writer:
- Research budget was about 28 searches and fetches. Source quality is uneven. Company filings, CPSC, NEMA, UL, NRF and the White House are primary. Most price-gap figures for pipe, used electrical gear and return pallets come from dealer or vendor marketing pages and are labelled "vendor" below.
- Where a search summary gave a number but did not say which page it came from, the finding is marked "attribution uncertain" and the candidate pages are linked. Check the page before quoting.
- "MTR" is a mill test report. "PMI" is positive material identification (handheld alloy analysis). "OCTG" is oil-country tubular goods. "MCCB" is a molded-case circuit breaker. "GMV" is gross merchandise volume.

## 1. Size: annual value traded and the stock of idle or excess goods

### Takeaway
Retail returns are by far the largest pool ($850B a year by NRF's count, $706B by Appriss), but the organised liquidation platforms that publish numbers handle well under 1% of that. For industrial paperwork-downgraded steel and for used electrical gear I found no credible market-size figure at all, only dated or vendor estimates of idle MRO stock ($12B unused per year, undated Grainger study) and a new-equipment market for data-center power gear heading to $65B by 2030.

### Inferences
- Liquidity Services' retail GMV ($447M) is about 0.05% of NRF's $849.9B returns figure. Even if B-Stock is several billion dollars, the published platforms touch a low single-digit percentage of returns at most. Most returned goods are restocked, sent back to vendors, handled by contract (ReturnPro-type) processors, or dumped, not auctioned.
- The MRO "idle stock" numbers are too old and too vendor-driven to size a market. The honest statement is "tens of billions of book value, real resale value unknown".
- No one publishes a size for "steel downgraded only because the MTR is missing". It is a subset of secondary and surplus pipe, which also includes genuine mill rejects.

### Gaps
- No figure found for the annual US dollar value of secondary, surplus or used pipe, plate, valves or flanges, or for surplus OCTG stock in tons. Vallourec's Q3 2026 deck tracks months of OCTG inventory but the number was not retrieved.
- No figure found for the size of the US used or reconditioned electrical equipment market, or revenue of Bay Power, Southland Electrical, National Switchgear (NAS), Circuit Breaker Sales or similar. All are private.
- No authoritative 2025 or 2026 percentage for the share of returns sent to liquidation.
- NRF's 2026 returns report was not found. The latest NRF figure is the October 2025 projection.

## 2. The discount: price gap between verified and unverified goods, and what causes it

### Takeaway
Documented gaps are large in all three markets (pipe about 30% or more below prime; used electrical gear at 20 to 60% of new; return pallets at 10 to 40% of retail), but every source that explains the gap attributes much of it to real defects, wear, mixed condition and handling cost, not to missing paperwork alone. I found no source that isolates the paperwork-only share.

### Cited Findings
Steel and pipe
- A pipe seller states the price difference between prime and surplus or secondary pipe "can exceed 30%". Prime carries MTRs and a guaranteed specification (for example ASTM A500 Grade B); secondary is sold as-is with no MTRs and no warranty (vendor, no independent benchmark found) — [All Steel Pipe](https://www.allsteelpipe.com/new-vs-surplus-pipe.html)
- The same source says secondary pipe is "often rejected by the mill for minor flaws such as weld height, wall variance, or chemistry misses" and may have rust, dents or missing coating. So part of the discount reflects real non-conformance — [All Steel Pipe](https://www.allsteelpipe.com/new-vs-surplus-pipe.html)
- Dated single price point: a Texas supplier's September 2018 newsletter listed 10-inch secondary welded pipe at $45.00 per hundredweight, defining secondary as steel with minor imperfections that keep it from meeting prime standards — [Steel Supply LP](https://www.steelsupplylp.com/newsletter-archives/9-26-2018)
- Freight matters: buyers outside the source regions pay freight that "can erase the savings"; the seller recommends sourcing fencing pipe within 200 miles — [All Steel Pipe](https://www.allsteelpipe.com/new-vs-surplus-pipe.html)

Electrical gear
- Dealer statements: tested used gear typically sells at 30 to 60% of new pricing; used switchgear at 20 to 60% of new replacement cost (vendor) — [PowerGen Enterprises guide](https://powergenenterprises.com/surplus-electrical-equipment-guide); [PowerGen, who buys used switchgear](https://powergenenterprises.com/who-buys-used-switchgear-surplus-electrical)
- A 2026 pricing guide reports used 480 V switchgear observed at $2.76 to $48.75 per amp over 2024 to 2026, with refurbished units clustering at $16.40 to $25.97 per amp. The top of the range is an asking price, not a clearing price (vendor) — [SecondWatt](https://secondwatt.com/resources/switchgear-pricing-2026-costs-by-voltage-class)
- Buy-side quotes from a breaker buyer: surplus ABB 1200A XT7 about $1,200 to $2,500; 2000A Emax E2.2 about $3,500 to $6,000; 15 kV 1200A ADVAC vacuum breaker about $5,000 to $9,000 (vendor) — [Circuit Breaker Buyer USA](https://circuitbreakerbuyerusa.com/blog/how-to-sell-abb-breakers-data-center-decommissions-northern-virginia)

Retail returns
- Liquidation recovery estimates: 15 to 30 cents on the dollar (attributed to Optoro) and 10 to 15 cents (another source). Attribution uncertain between pages — [ReverseLogix](https://www.reverselogix.com/industry-updates/how-retailers-recoup-costs-from-returns/); [Old National](https://www.oldnational.com/resources/insights/fraud-is-only-the-tip-of-retail-s-850-billion-returns-challenge/)
- Pallet outcomes run about 15 to 40% of declared retail "depending on whether the goods are manifested"; a mixed general-merchandise pallet with manifested retail of $3,000 to $8,000 sells at auction for $600 to $2,400. The source warns declared retail is a list-price reference and that secondhand resale clears far below it (vendor blog) — [Closo](https://closo.co/blogs/inventory-liquidation/american-liquidation)
- On a modelled pallet, moving the unsellable share from 12% to 30% costs about $180, "the difference between a working margin and none" (vendor blog) — [Closo](https://closo.co/blogs/inventory-liquidation/american-liquidation)
- ReturnPro (formerly goTRG) marketing: 60 cents on the dollar through graded resale against 12 cents through bulk liquidation. An HP representative is quoted saying refurbished products can recover 80 to 100% of value depending on timing and demand (vendor) — [ReturnPro](https://www.returnpro.com/resources/blog/retail-refurbishment-for-returns-2026)
- A Michigan State supply-chain professor estimates a return costs the retailer about 60% of the item's cost. Attribution uncertain — [Old National](https://www.oldnational.com/resources/insights/fraud-is-only-the-tip-of-retail-s-850-billion-returns-challenge/)
- Most pallet sales are as-is, with sellers allowing inventory discrepancies up to a set variance — [B-Stock](https://bstock.com/blog/pallet-liquidations-near-me-what-a-buyer-should-know)

### Inferences
- The ReturnPro claim (12 cents bulk versus 60 cents graded) is the closest thing to a "verification multiplies value" data point, about 5x. But the 60 cents includes labour for testing, repair, repackaging and item-level selling, so it is a gross figure, and it comes from the company selling the service.
- For pipe, a 30% discount on a commodity with freight cost that "can erase the savings" leaves a thin gross spread for an intermediary who must also pay for testing.
- For electrical gear, the 40 to 80% discount to new is the widest absolute gap per item, on items worth thousands of dollars each. But see section 3: the largest-volume item, the molded-case breaker, cannot legally be reconditioned for installation under the NEC.

### Gaps
- No independent price index found for prime versus secondary or no-MTR pipe, plate, flanges or valves. No current (2026) price pair found.
- No source separates the paperwork-only discount from the defect discount in any of the three markets.
- No direct manifested-versus-unmanifested pallet price comparison from a platform's own sold data.
- No documented price for "new surplus with traceability" versus "used" breakers of the same model.

## 3. Can verification restore full value?

### Takeaway
Mostly no for steel and electrical, and only partly for returns. ASME allows recertification of unidentified material but only by the code-stamped vessel or part manufacturer, not by a dealer or broker. The 2026 National Electrical Code bans installing reconditioned molded-case breakers, panelboards and many other items outright, and permits reconditioning only for power circuit breakers, switchgear, switchboards and protective relays, with the original listing mark removed. For returns, grading does lift recovery but the only numbers are vendor claims.

### Cited Findings
Steel (ASME, API)
- ASME Section VIII Division 1, paragraph UG-10 lets material that is not fully identified be recertified to a permitted specification. In the 2025 edition this is UG-10.3, covering material "not fully identified as required by a specification permitted by this Division" — [ASME BPVC VIII-1 2025 text (unofficial copy)](http://www.chinajorson.cn/indexfile/upload_37ccfa9f20f0d461721b7b175515ed8a.pdf)
- "Only a Certificate Holder (vessel or part Manufacturer) shall recertify material." Third-party recertification is not permitted. Recertified paperwork carries the notation "Certified per UG-10" — [ASME BPVC VIII-1 2023 text (unofficial copy)](https://havacaran.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/ASME-Sec-VIII-Div1-2023.pdf); [Inspection for Industry](https://www.inspection-for-industry.com/pressure-vessel-plate.html)
- Recertification needs evidence that melting practice, deoxidation, chemistry, mechanical properties and heat treatment meet the target specification (secondary summary of UG-10(a)) — [Scribd pressure vessel plate summary](https://www.scribd.com/document/519318999/Pressure-vessel-plate)
- PMI is an addition to MTRs, not a replacement: a Process Industry Practices specification says PMI is required in addition to mill test reports and shall follow API RP 578 — [PIP VESPMI01 (Scribd copy)](https://www.scribd.com/document/555220601/PIP-VESPMI01-2019)
- Traceability under UG-93 is lost when the heat number or material identification cannot be positively linked to the material test report (secondary blog) — [Industrial Monitor Direct](https://industrialmonitordirect.com/blogs/knowledgebase/asmeen-raw-material-retest-requirements-for-pressure-vessels)
- Market practice per a pipe seller: pressure applications (oil, gas, steam, compressed air) "require Prime pipe with MTRs"; surplus is acceptable for fencing, bollards and small signs, and conditional on engineer approval for piling and billboards — [All Steel Pipe](https://www.allsteelpipe.com/new-vs-surplus-pipe.html)

Electrical (NEC, NEMA, UL, PEARL)
- The 2026 NEC (per NEMA CS 70011-2026, Appendix A.1) does not permit reconditioned versions of the following to be installed: molded-case circuit breakers (240.3), panelboards (408.3), low-voltage power circuit breaker electronic trip units (240.3), transfer switch equipment, busway (Mylar-wrapped and powder-coated, 368.3), arc-fault and ground-fault interrupters, fuseholders and nonrenewable fuses, surge protective devices, fire pump controllers, energy storage systems, and others — [NEMA CS 70011-2026](https://ieci.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/NEMA-CS-70011-2026-Technical-Position-on-Reconditioned-Equipment-CS-100.pdf)
- The 2026 NEC does permit reconditioned (Appendix A.2): low- and medium-voltage power circuit breakers (240.3), high-voltage circuit breakers (245.3), switchgear or sections of switchgear (408.3 and 495.3), switchboards or sections (408.3), and electromechanical protective relays — [NEMA CS 70011-2026](https://ieci.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/NEMA-CS-70011-2026-Technical-Position-on-Reconditioned-Equipment-CS-100.pdf)
- NEMA (the manufacturers' trade body) additionally says transformers (cast resin, control, dry-type and liquid-filled), sealed-for-life switchgear, relays (overload, protective, electronic, digital), metering equipment and capacitors are "not suitable for reconditioning" (Appendix B.1). This is NEMA's position, not NEC text. NEMA lists motors, motor starters, drives, generators, UPS equipment and storage batteries as suitable (Appendix B.2) — [NEMA CS 70011-2026](https://ieci.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/NEMA-CS-70011-2026-Technical-Position-on-Reconditioned-Equipment-CS-100.pdf)
- NEC 110.21(A)(2) (2026): reconditioned equipment must be marked with the reconditioner's name, the date and the word "reconditioned"; "The original listing mark shall be removed or made permanently illegible"; "Approval of the reconditioned equipment shall not be based solely on the equipment's original listing." There is an exception for industrial occupancies — [NEMA CS 70011-2026](https://ieci.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/NEMA-CS-70011-2026-Technical-Position-on-Reconditioned-Equipment-CS-100.pdf)
- NEMA tenets: where the NEC requires a listed product, the reconditioned product should be listed as reconditioned by a nationally recognised testing laboratory or field-labelled by a Field Evaluation Body; the local authority having jurisdiction may also approve on documentation. Reconditioning must use "design qualified" parts and OEM guidance, and "where no guidance is available from the OEM or industry standards, reconditioning is not recommended" — [NEMA CS 70011-2026](https://ieci.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/NEMA-CS-70011-2026-Technical-Position-on-Reconditioned-Equipment-CS-100.pdf)
- NEMA on history: understanding prior electrical stresses and maintenance is important, and "No access to such information could have an adverse impact on the safety, reliability, and performance of reconditioned equipment." Equipment exposed to fire or water may not be suitable — [NEMA CS 70011-2026](https://ieci.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/NEMA-CS-70011-2026-Technical-Position-on-Reconditioned-Equipment-CS-100.pdf)
- UL: listed equipment can be rebuilt under a UL program by "either the original manufacturer or another UL Solutions authorized party", after an initial investigation and under factory surveillance, only where UL has a rebuilt program for that category. The original listing mark is removed. A field evaluation is another route — [UL Solutions FAQ](https://ul.com/resources/faqs-about-safety-certification-reconditioned-equipment)
- The reconditioning ban on molded-case breakers dates from the 2020 NEC — [UL Solutions 2020 NEC guide](https://www.ul.com/news/reconditioned-electrical-equipment-2020-nec-guide)
- PEARL (Professional Electrical Apparatus Reconditioning League) is an ANSI-accredited standards developer with an Inspect and Test standard and an ANSI-approved reconditioning standard (EERS); it accredits companies after a site inspection and certifies technicians at three levels; members apply a PEARL seal — [PEARL](https://pearl1.org/); [PEARL accreditation](https://pearl1.org/about-accreditation/); [IPS newsletter](https://store.ips.us/newsletter-what-does-it-mean-to-be-pearl-accredited.aspx)
- PEARL pushed the 2017 NEC changes that first required reconditioned equipment to be marked with the reconditioner's identity — [IAEI Magazine, July 2017](https://iaeimagazine.org/2017/julyaugust-2017/reconditioned-electrical-equipment-now-included-in-the-nec-revisions-underscore-the-safety-and-reliability-of-reconditioned-electrical-equipment/)

Counterfeit breakers
- CPSC recalls of counterfeit "Square D" breakers, 2006 to 2010: Scott Electric about 30,000 units (2006); North American Breaker Co. about 50,000 (sold 2003 to 2006 at $3 to $85); Connecticut Electric about 64,000 (2007); Specialty Lamp International about 371,000 (2008); Miami Breaker about 43,600 (2010, under a consent order in a Square D lawsuit). Recalled breakers may fail to trip on overload — [CPSC Scott Electric](https://www.cpsc.gov/Recalls/2006/scott-electric-co-inc-recalls-counterfeit-circuit-breakers-due-to-fire-hazard); [CPSC NABCO](https://www.cpsc.gov/Recalls/2007/north-american-breaker-co-recalls-counterfeit-circuit-breakers-due-to-fire-hazard); [CPSC Connecticut Electric](https://www.cpsc.gov/Recalls/2007/connecticut-electric-recalls-counterfeit-square-d-circuit-breakers-due-to-fire-hazard); [CPSC Specialty Lamp](https://www.cpsc.gov/Recalls/2008/counterfeit-circuit-breakers-recalled-by-specialty-lamp-international-due-to-fire); [CPSC Miami Breaker](https://www.cpsc.gov/Recalls/2010/Miami-Breaker-Recalls-Counterfeit-Square-D-Circuit-Breakers-Due-to-Fire-Hazard)
- Current case: Eaton Corporation v. Breakers Unlimited Inc., Southern District of Indiana, 1:25-cv-00103, filed January 2025. Eaton alleges the defendants sell altered and defaced Eaton breakers presented as different breakers, and sought an ex parte seizure order and restraining order. No ruling found; docket still active in November 2026 — [CourtListener](https://www.courtlistener.com/docket/69580945/eaton-corporation-v-breakers-unlimited-inc/)

Retail returns (AI grading)
- Two Boxes (Denver) describes AI for image classification, anomaly detection and fraud identification in returns processing and says it processes nearly $1 billion of returned inventory a year with partners including Radial, Stord and Cart.com (company claim) — [Business Wire, April 2026](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20260407584127/en/Two-Boxes-Accelerates-AI-Powered-Returns-Processing-Platform-with-New-Funding-Led-by-Assembly-Ventures)
- Liquidity Services put "AssetPics", an in-house AI cataloguing service, into production beta in fiscal 2026 — [Liquidity Services FY2026 8-K exhibit](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001235468/000119312526470517/lqdt-ex99_1.htm)
- Amplio (industrial surplus) says its AI agents appraise inventory at SKU level and that customers realise "value recovery up to 5x industry standards" (company claim) — [Business Wire, September 2025](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20250924769724/en/Amplio-Secures-11.1M-Series-A-to-Scale-AI-Powered-Surplus-Solutions-for-Global-Manufacturers/)

### Inferences
- Steel: a handheld analyser plus a dealer's letter does not make material code-legal. The legal act of recertification belongs to the ASME Certificate Holder who builds the vessel, under its own quality system and its Authorized Inspector. An intermediary can at most supply test data that makes a fabricator willing to recertify. That caps the value an intermediary can capture and explains why sellers still say "never" for pressure service.
- Electrical: the code splits the market in two. High-ticket, serviceable gear (power circuit breakers, switchgear, switchboards) has a legal reconditioning path and an existing accreditation body (PEARL). High-volume commodity items (molded-case breakers, panelboards, bus plugs if treated as busway fittings, trip units) have no legal reconditioning path for installation, so their value rests on proving they are genuine, unused and unaltered. That is a provenance problem, and the Eaton case shows the manufacturers treat resellers as adversaries.
- Transformers: NEMA says "not suitable for reconditioning" but that is a manufacturer trade position and the NEC lists in the NEMA appendix do not ban them. Treat the legal status as open and check the NEC text.
- Returns: verification here is condition grading, not provenance. The tools exist and are already deployed by incumbents and funded startups.

### Gaps
- UG-10 text was read only from unofficial copies and secondary summaries. The exact test requirements (per-piece chemical and mechanical tests) should be checked in the official code.
- Nothing found on API 5L or API 5CT rules for pipe that has lost traceability, or on ASME B31.3 treatment of unlisted or unknown materials.
- Could not confirm from a primary source that handheld XRF cannot measure carbon (which would mean it cannot grade carbon steel on its own). This is commonly stated in the trade but was not sourced here.
- OSHA's position (29 CFR 1910.303 and the requirement for listed or approved equipment) was not retrieved. The UL FAQ does not mention OSHA.
- No independent measurement of AI grading accuracy or recovery uplift for returns. All uplift numbers are vendor claims.
- Whether bus plugs fall under the busway ban (368.3) was not confirmed.
- Several 2025 articles on TheWattDigest give counterfeit failure statistics (for example 68% AFCI failures) that could not be verified and were excluded.

## 4. Effect of 2025 to 2026 conditions

### Takeaway
Conditions in late 2026 raise the value of domestic surplus in steel and electrical gear: Section 232 duties are 50% on steel, aluminium and copper articles, US OCTG prices jumped to about $3,031 per ton in September 2026 with inventories below five-year averages, and switchgear and transformer lead times run from 44 weeks to more than three years. For returns, volume and fraud are both rising, which helps processors but does not change the verification picture.

### Cited Findings
Tariffs and steel
- Presidential proclamation of 2 April 2026, effective 6 April: the 50% Section 232 rate applies to articles made entirely or almost entirely of steel, aluminium or copper, on full customs value regardless of metal content; derivatives substantially made of those metals pay a flat 25% on full value — [White House fact sheet](https://www.whitehouse.gov/fact-sheets/2026/04/fact-sheet-president-donald-j-trump-strengthens-tariffs-on-steel-aluminum-and-copper-imports/); [S&P Global](https://www.spglobal.com/market-intelligence/en/news-insights/articles/2026/4/trump-cuts-some-duties-clarifies-language-for-steel-aluminum-copper-tariffs-100354359); [EY](https://taxnews.ey.com/news/2026-0788)
- AISI and Nucor said the change ensures steel pipe and tube products get the full 50% protection — [US Department of Commerce](https://www.trade.gov/press-release/what-they-are-saying-president-trump-strengthens-us-steel-aluminum-and-copper)
- Further adjustments were announced on 2 June 2026; antidumping activity on OCTG continues, including a preliminary affirmative determination on the UAE, Taiwan and Austria — [SteelOrbis](https://www.steelorbis.com/steel-news/latest-news/us-adjusts-section-232-tariffs-on-steel-imports-1456314.htm)

## 6. Business model norms

### Takeaway
Consignment with no inventory capital is already the norm among the large marketplaces: 86% of Liquidity Services' GMV in its latest quarter, guided to the low 90s. Take rates run about 10% (government surplus) to about 22% (RB Global). Retail liquidation is the exception, where purchase-and-resell models remain common and gross margins on revenue are under 30%.

### Cited Findings
- Consignment was 86% of Liquidity Services' consolidated GMV in the fiscal fourth quarter of 2026; revenue fell 10% in the quarter "with pricing model shift to consignment" while profit rose — [Liquidity Services press release](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001235468/000119312526467778/lqdt-ex99_1.htm)
- For Q1 fiscal 2027 the company expects consignment in the "low 90s" percent of GMV including Invaluable, consolidated revenue as a percentage of GMV in the high teens, and segment direct profit at low-to-mid 60s percent of revenue — [Liquidity Services FY2026 8-K exhibit](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001235468/000119312526470517/lqdt-ex99_1.htm)
- Retail segment (which still buys inventory): direct profit 28.5% of revenue for fiscal 2026 and 35% in Q4, up about 910 basis points as mix moved to consignment — [Liquidity Services FY2026 8-K exhibit](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001235468/000119312526470517/lqdt-ex99_1.htm)
- RB Global's 2025 take rate of 21.6% rose on a higher average buyer fee rate, partly offset in Q3 2025 by a lower average seller commission rate. Management expects modest take-rate pressure in 2026 — [RB Global FY2025 results](https://investor.rbglobal.com/news/news-details/2026/RB-Global-Reports-Fourth-Quarter-and-Full-Year-2025-Results/default.aspx)
- Ticket sizes: a return pallet sells for $600 to $2,400 (vendor); a used power breaker for $1,200 to $9,000 (vendor) — [Closo](https://closo.co/blogs/inventory-liquidation/american-liquidation); [Circuit Breaker Buyer USA](https://circuitbreakerbuyerusa.com/blog/how-to-sell-abb-breakers-data-center-decommissions-northern-virginia)
- Retailer-side targets for liquidation recovery: guides cite a 35% minimum, or a 40 to 60% floor, of landed cost (vendor, conflicting) — [Racklify](https://racklify.com/encyclopedia/improving-liquidation-recovery-rate-operational-tactics-for-retailers/)

### Inferences
- Liquidity Services' average transaction is about $1,100 (Q4 GMV of $404M over about 365,000 transactions, my arithmetic). At a 10 to 20% take that is $110 to $220 of revenue per transaction, which leaves little room for a physical inspection visit on small lots.
- Electrical dealers appear to buy outright (their marketing is "we buy"), so that segment runs on purchase and inventory, not consignment. This is an inference from dealer websites, not a sourced norm.

## 7. Legal start: licences and liability

### Takeaway
There is no federal licence to resell surplus goods, but auction-format selling triggers state auctioneer rules that vary and are tightening for online sellers. The heavier constraint is product liability and code compliance: reconditioned electrical gear must lose its original listing mark and carry the reconditioner's name, and ASME material can only be recertified by the code-stamp holder.

### Cited Findings
- State auction rules vary: California requires no auctioneer licence but requires a bond of at least $20,000; Arizona and Arkansas do not regulate online auctions; Georgia law has no exemption for online auctions, though from 1 July 2026 (House Bill 1254) Georgia no longer licenses auction companies; Mississippi says there is no exemption for platforms that bring buyers and sellers together; Louisiana requires an auctioneer licence for live or online auctions; Ohio's 2022 law exempts sellers of no more than $10,000 a year through an auction mediation company such as eBay — [EstateSales.org state summary](https://estatesales.org/university/auction-license-laws-by-state); [Georgia Secretary of State](https://sos.ga.gov/page/georgia-department-auctioneers-faq); [Mississippi Auctioneer Commission](https://www.auctioneers.ms.gov/sites/default/files/2019-12/Online.Protocol.12.3.2019.pdf); [Louisiana Auctioneers Licensing Board](https://lalb.org/licensing.php)
- Electrical: the reconditioner must mark the equipment with its name, the date and "reconditioned", which provides "traceability to the reconditioning organization"; the original listing mark must be removed — [NEMA CS 70011-2026](https://ieci.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/NEMA-CS-70011-2026-Technical-Position-on-Reconditioned-Equipment-CS-100.pdf)
- Electrical: a third party can become a UL-authorised rebuilder only after an initial UL investigation and under ongoing factory surveillance, and only in categories where UL runs a rebuilt program — [UL Solutions FAQ](https://ul.com/resources/faqs-about-safety-certification-reconditioned-equipment)
- Electrical: manufacturers litigate against resellers. Eaton sought seizure orders against Breakers Unlimited and related sellers (2025); Square D obtained a consent order against Miami Breaker (2010) — [CourtListener](https://www.courtlistener.com/docket/69580945/eaton-corporation-v-breakers-unlimited-inc/); [CPSC Miami Breaker](https://www.cpsc.gov/Recalls/2010/Miami-Breaker-Recalls-Counterfeit-Square-D-Circuit-Breakers-Due-to-Fire-Hazard)
- Electrical: an engineering firm's guidance on the 2023 NEC is titled "using reconditioned equipment, buyer beware" (not read in full) — [IMEG](https://imegcorp.com/insights/blog/2023-nec-using-reconditioned-equipment-buyer-beware/)
- Steel: only an ASME Certificate Holder may recertify material under UG-10 — [ASME BPVC VIII-1 2023 text (unofficial copy)](https://havacaran.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/ASME-Sec-VIII-Div1-2023.pdf)
- Reselling a recalled consumer product is illegal (CPSC recall notices) — [CPSC Connecticut Electric](https://www.cpsc.gov/Recalls/2007/connecticut-electric-recalls-counterfeit-square-d-circuit-breakers-due-to-fire-hazard)

### Inferences
- Whoever puts its name on reconditioned electrical equipment becomes the traceable party for any later failure. That is a product-liability position, not a marketplace position, and it needs insurance and qualified technicians.
- A fixed-price or negotiated-sale model avoids most state auctioneer licensing; an auction model does not.

### Gaps
- No source found on secondhand-dealer licensing for business-to-business industrial goods, on product-liability case law for resold reconditioned breakers, or on insurance costs.
- OSHA requirements for employers installing used or reconditioned equipment were not retrieved.
- No source found on liability of a broker who supplies PMI data that a fabricator relies on.

## 8. Why the discount persists even when verification is cheap

### Takeaway
The sources point to five named reasons: the rules reserve certification to parties other than the reseller; part of the discount is real defect or unknown service history that a scan cannot see; freight and small lots eat the spread; manufacturers actively fight secondary resale; and declared values overstate what goods are worth.

### Cited Findings
- Certification is reserved: only the ASME Certificate Holder can recertify material — [ASME BPVC VIII-1 2023 text (unofficial copy)](https://havacaran.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/ASME-Sec-VIII-Div1-2023.pdf)
- Some items cannot be requalified at any price: the NEC bars reconditioned molded-case breakers and panelboards from installation — [NEMA CS 70011-2026](https://ieci.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/NEMA-CS-70011-2026-Technical-Position-on-Reconditioned-Equipment-CS-100.pdf)
- NEMA's stated reasons equipment may be unsuitable: factory calibration that cannot be reproduced; "Testing of reconditioned equipment to confirm that it maintains safety requirements is not possible"; certification testing "is normally destructive"; lack of serviceable parts — [NEMA CS 70011-2026](https://ieci.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/NEMA-CS-70011-2026-Technical-Position-on-Reconditioned-Equipment-CS-100.pdf)
- Hidden history: lack of information on prior overloads and maintenance "could have an adverse impact on the safety, reliability, and performance" — [NEMA CS 70011-2026](https://ieci.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/NEMA-CS-70011-2026-Technical-Position-on-Reconditioned-Equipment-CS-100.pdf)
- Real defects: secondary pipe is often a mill reject for weld height, wall variance or chemistry — [All Steel Pipe](https://www.allsteelpipe.com/new-vs-surplus-pipe.html)
- Logistics: freight can erase the savings on surplus pipe beyond about 200 miles — [All Steel Pipe](https://www.allsteelpipe.com/new-vs-surplus-pipe.html)
- Buyer rules: large billboards and piling need engineer-approved material and "the buyer bears the risk if it fails a load test" — [All Steel Pipe](https://www.allsteelpipe.com/new-vs-surplus-pipe.html)
- Brand resistance: Eaton monitors resale sites, makes blind purchases and sues resellers — [Electrical Contractor Magazine](https://ecmag.com/section/your-business/counterfeits-can-kill-how-online-purchases-can-lead-real-world-dangers); [CourtListener](https://www.courtlistener.com/docket/69580945/eaton-corporation-v-breakers-unlimited-inc/)
- Overstated baseline: declared retail on pallets is a list-price reference and resale clears far below it; the unsellable share decides whether a pallet makes money — [Closo](https://closo.co/blogs/inventory-liquidation/american-liquidation)
- Excess MRO stock is created by OEM-recommended spares levels, overhauls and discontinued parts, so much of it is obsolete, not merely undocumented. Attribution uncertain — [LNS Research](https://blog.lnsresearch.com/blog/bid/204252/why-you-need-to-get-rid-of-dead-mro-inventory)

### Inferences
- Across the three markets, the binding constraint is who is allowed to vouch, not the cost of measuring. In steel it is the code-stamp fabricator, in electrical gear it is the OEM, a testing laboratory or the local inspector, and in returns it is the brand or retailer. An intermediary with a scanner does not hold that authority by default.
- Scorecard against the founder's thesis, on this evidence:
  - Retail returns: very large, but the gap is condition and handling more than provenance, the space is crowded, and the named venture outcome (Optoro) was poor.
  - Paperwork-downgraded steel: the gap is real (about 30% or more) but the legal recertification step sits with the fabricator, part of the discount is real defect, and no market size could be found.
  - Used electrical gear: strongest tailwind (lead times of 44 to 160 weeks, breaker prices up 47% since 2019), high tickets, a legal reconditioning path for switchgear and power breakers, and an accreditation body already in place. Against it: molded-case breakers and panelboards are excluded by code, the reseller carries liability by name, and OEMs are hostile.
- MRO spares sit between these: Amplio is already funded to do AI appraisal and resale for manufacturers, so the idea is validated enough to attract a Series A but is not open ground.

### Gaps
- No direct evidence on buyer procurement rules (approved-vendor lists, insurer or owner specifications that ban used or reconditioned equipment). This is widely asserted in the trade but was not sourced here.
- No evidence found on brand restrictions in retailer liquidation contracts (delabelling, export-only, no-Amazon clauses), though these are known to exist.
- No case found of an intermediary that verified unidentified steel and resold it at prime pricing.
