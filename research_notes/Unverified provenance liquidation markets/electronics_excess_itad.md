# Electronics: excess/obsolete components (open market) and retired data-center IT hardware (ITAD), as of November 2026

Research note conventions: dates are given for every figure. "Vendor claim" means the number comes from a company with a commercial interest and was not independently verified. "Snippet only" means I saw the figure in a search-result summary and did not open the underlying page. About 30 searches and fetches were used.

## 1. Size: open-market component distribution, excess inventory, ITAD and secondary server/memory/drive market, and the 2025-2026 memory shortage

### Takeaway
Both markets are large and real: the ten biggest independent distributors booked about $12.8B in 2024, ITAD is estimated at roughly $18-23B a year, and Iron Mountain's ITAD arm alone is heading for about $1B in 2026. The 2025-2026 memory and drive shortage pushed used server memory up roughly 250-335% year on year, which means the secondary market is currently clearing at high prices rather than at liquidation prices.

## 2. The discount: what excess components and used drives/memory sell for, and how much hardware is destroyed

### Takeaway
An outright lot sale of excess components to a broker returns about 5-10% of cost, against a claimed 50-60% of market value on consignment, so the liquidation discount is real and large. No source separates how much of it comes from missing traceability versus oversupply, obsolescence and the buyer's holding risk. In ITAD, tens of millions of drives a year are shredded, but in 2026 used memory and drives are selling at or above what owners paid.

### Cited Findings

Components
- Component Sense: outright purchase "typically ranges from 5-10% of component cost"; consignment gives "recovery rates of 50-60% of market value"; consignment generates on average five times the income of an outright sale. Vendor claim. — [Component Sense](https://www.componentsense.com/blog/consignment-vs-outrightpurchase)
- Mobius Materials: brokers "will bid around 5% of the value of the parts so they can resell them". Vendor claim, Sept 2024. — [Mobius Materials](https://www.mobiusmaterials.com/post/how-to-sell-excess-electronic-components)
- Other vendor ranges from one search: 5-15% of cost on lot buys; 10-40 cents on the dollar through excess brokers; one master distributor claims 87% average recovery on stock it accepts. Snippets only. — [Neotel](https://global.neotel.tech/excess-electronic-component-inventory/); [iBuyXS](https://ibuyxs.com/how-oems-get-maximum-roi-on-excess-electronic-components/); [Closo](https://closo.co/blogs/optimization-growth-strategies/the-million-dollar-reel-how-to-sell-excess-electronic-components-without-getting-scammed)
- Corning case: Component Sense recovered £1.55M from 2.5 million components; Kenny McGee says Corning later sold the balance to Component Sense for cash "at the original valuation", and that it can "typically get the big manufacturers all their money back". Vendor claim. — [Component Sense](https://www.componentsense.com/blog/consignment-vs-outrightpurchase); [Altium](https://resources.altium.com/p/why-factories-crush-perfectly-good-components)
- One Scottish factory held £3.2M (about $5M) of excess; a large buyer may pay about $1 for a chip that a small buyer pays about $5 for, which is the spread excess sellers trade into. — [Altium](https://resources.altium.com/p/why-factories-crush-perfectly-good-components)
- McGee on trace: he buys stock directly from the original buyer and says "If I have traceability that's better than any inspection"; untraceable excess ends up "fueling the counterfeit economy". — [Altium](https://resources.altium.com/p/why-factories-crush-perfectly-good-components)
- TechBullion: one-off brokers and auction houses reach a limited buyer pool and recover "a fraction" of remaining value. No percentage. — [TechBullion](https://techbullion.com/the-two-sided-inventory-problem-quietly-disrupting-electronics-manufacturing/)

### Inferences
- A lot buyer pricing at 5-10% of cost is pricing a portfolio where most line items never sell. The 5x gap between lot sale and consignment is mostly a gap between "sell it all today" and "sell the good lines over months". It is a time, effort and demand-matching discount at least as much as a provenance discount.
- Excess that comes straight from the OEM or EMS that bought it from a franchised distributor already has traceable paperwork. The trace is broken by the sale into the broker chain, not by anything lost at the source. That means the "verification" needed for first-hand excess is largely document handling, which incumbents such as Component Sense already do.
- In drives and memory the 2026 shortage has closed the price gap: used product sells near or above original purchase price. The remaining value destruction is what gets shredded by policy, not what gets discounted for doubt.
- Rough scale of value destroyed: tens of millions of drives a year at even $20-70 each of resale value implies several hundred million to low billions of dollars a year. This is my arithmetic on the FT "tens of millions" figure and the SK tes per-drive average in section 7, not a sourced estimate.

### Gaps
- No source quantifies how much of the component discount is due to missing traceability or counterfeit risk versus oversupply and obsolescence. This is the central question for the thesis and it is unanswered in public data.
- No sourced price comparison for the same part number with full trace versus without trace.
- No measured count of drives shredded per hyperscaler, and no sourced dollar estimate of value destroyed.
- I could not open the original FT or BBC articles.
- No sourced ITAD buyback price list for 2026.

## 3. Verification today: standards, lab cost and time, certified erasure, who accepts and who refuses

### Takeaway
Counterfeit detection is a lab activity governed by SAE AS6081 and AS6171, with quoted turnarounds of 3 days to 2 weeks per lot, and ERAI data show that electrical testing alone misses many fakes. Drive erasure, by contrast, is already done on site at scale with software, and the blocker there is customer policy and trust, not the technology.

### Cited Findings

Component counterfeit detection
- AS6081A (distributor standard) now defers to AS6171 for detection test methods; AS6171 sets statistical sampling requirements per lot and applies to independent and in-house labs. Snippet summary. — [SAE AS6171](https://saemobilus.sae.org/standards/as6171-test-methods-standard-general-requirements-suspect-counterfeit-electrical-electronic-electromechanical-parts); [ERAI white paper](https://www.erai.com/customuploads/ca/wp/AS6171_Benefits_White_Paper_062017_7.pdf)
- Micross (independent test house) advertises complete AS6081 testing "fast as 3 days" and full-lot AS6171 testing "as fast as 2 weeks"; an older flyer says standard lead times under 10 days. Vendor claims. — [Micross](https://micross.com/products-and-services/counterfeit-mitigation)
- CALCE/Lockheed Martin presentation hosted by DLA (2017): running every AS6171 test is not practical for all applications and "could be cost prohibitive"; a risk-based selection of tests is recommended. — [DLA PSMC](https://www.dla.mil/Portals/104/Documents/LandAndMaritime/V/VA/PSMC/Spring%2017/LM_Counterfeit%20Mitigation_CALCE_Univ%20of%20Md_Spring%202017%20PSMC170526.pdf)
- AERI: test cost was a "contentious issue" when AS6171 was released. — [AERI](https://www.aeri.com/as6171-counterfeit-detection-methods-standard-finally-released/)
- Astute Electronics claims to be the only distributor with AS6171-certified labs in the UK, Europe and the US. Vendor claim. — [Electronic Specifier](https://www.electronicspecifier.com/?p=36887)
- ERAI 2025 Annual Report (posted May 2026): total parts reported fell versus 2024; mix was about 55% suspect counterfeit, 31% nonconforming, 13.5% both; programmable logic, analog, microprocessor and memory ICs were the largest categories; 24% of suspect counterfeit parts passed electrical testing and only 12% failed it; about 39% of reports came from US organizations. — [ERAI](https://www.erai.com/erai_blog/3192/2025_annual_report)
- ERAI 2024: 1,055 suspect counterfeit and nonconforming parts reported, up 25% on 2023. — [Supply Chain Connect](https://www.supplychainconnect.com/counterfeit/article/55311316/2024-counterfeit-electronic-parts-report-from-erai)
- Fast inspection research: the EU-funded CHIPCHECK project built a low-cost X-ray system inspecting components on tape reels "in less than one second" each; it was a prototype and the project is closed. — [TWI](https://www.twi-global.com/what-we-do/research-and-technology/research-programmes/public-funded-projects/chipcheck)

Who accepts and who refuses
- DFARS 252.246-7008 (Sources of Electronic Parts; clause dated January 2023, DFARS change effective Nov 10, 2025): defense contractors must buy from the original manufacturer, authorized suppliers or contractor-approved suppliers; when parts are not available from those and come from another source, the contractor must notify the contracting officer and is responsible for inspection, testing and authentication to industry standards; those costs may be charged. — [acquisition.gov](https://www.acquisition.gov/dfars/252.246-7008-sources-electronic-parts.); [Lockheed Martin supplier guidance](https://www.lockheedMartin.com/content/dam/lockheed-martin/eo/documents/suppliers/mfc/mfc-quality-counterfeit-dfars-252.246-7008.pdf)
- Franchise agreements between chipmakers (Analog Devices and Xilinx named) and their distributors can bar redistribution of excess. — [Altium](https://resources.altium.com/p/why-factories-crush-perfectly-good-components)

Data erasure
- SK tes hyperscale case: 72,000 storage drives, 96% yield on on-site drive wiping, followed by a second wipe at its own facility. Vendor case study. — [SK tes](https://www.sktes.com/case-studies/maximizing-value-in-hyperscale-server-decommissioning)
- Blancco reading of IEEE 2883.1-2025: Clear is the minimum before internal reuse; for external resale, Purge (including properly conditioned cryptographic erase) leaves media reusable. Vendor interpretation. — [Blancco](https://blancco.com/resources/blog-should-your-business-destroy-hard-drives/)
- Shredding has been "deprecated in recent specifications" because high media density can leave recoverable data on fragments. Snippet only; exact standard not confirmed. — [Data Center Knowledge](https://www.datacenterknowledge.com/ai-data-centers/the-future-of-data-destruction)
- Blancco 2026 survey: certifiable erasure over the network is used by only 23% of organizations for data center assets, 18% for laptops/desktops and 32% for mobile; 53% say they lack adequate technology to sanitize without destroying; 73% are very or extremely confident data is removed before disposal. — [Resource Recycling](https://resource-recycling.com/e-scrap/2026/05/19/ai-data-anxiety-push-enterprises-to-destroy-working-devices-report/)
- Open Compute Project published a paper, "Data Sanitization for the Circular Economy". Not opened. — [OCP](https://opencompute.org/documents/data-sanitization-for-the-circular-economy-1-pdf)

### Inferences
- Component authentication does not look mobile today. The accepted evidence (decapsulation, X-ray, XRF, electrical test under AS6171) needs lab equipment and accredited staff, and 24% of suspect parts pass electrical test. A phone-and-AI inspection would not satisfy AS6081/AS6171 buyers. Visual and document checks can be fast and partly automated, but they are the weakest tier of evidence.
- The high-reliability buyers who pay most for hard-to-find parts are also the ones bound to authorized sources first. Tested open-market parts are a fallback they use when parts are unavailable, with the test burden on them. The willing buyers of unverifiable parts are consumer and industrial manufacturers, who pay the least.
- For drives and servers, verification is already "mobile": on-site software erasure with a certificate, at 96% yield in the SK tes case. The opportunity there is persuading owners to allow it, which is a trust and liability problem.

### Gaps
- No published per-lot price for AS6081 or AS6171 testing. Labs quote case by case.
- IDEA-STD-1010 and AS5553 specifics were not retrieved this session.
- NIST SP 800-88 Rev 2 status and exact wording were not verified this session (I believe a revision was finalized in 2025, unverified).
- Automotive (IATF 16949) and medical rules on open-market parts were not found. No named OEM policy that flatly refuses tested broker parts was retrieved.
- No evidence found either way on startups doing AI-based inline authentication of components (for example Cybord); the search returned nothing on them.
- Blancco pricing per erasure licence not found.

## 4. Who already does this: incumbents, revenue, margins, funding

### Takeaway
Both sides are crowded with established firms. Components have multi-billion-dollar independents with their own test labs plus a 25-year-old consignment specialist; ITAD has a public consolidator growing about 90% a year and buying smaller operators at mid to high single-digit EBITDA multiples. Venture funding into the niche is small.

## 5. Business model norms: consignment, commissions, margins, ticket sizes, payment terms

### Takeaway
Consignment with no inventory capital is a standard, widely offered option in excess components, alongside outright lot purchase and on-site managed programs. Exact splits are negotiated and not published; the visible numbers are 5-10% of cost for cash lot buys and 50-60% of market value returned to the seller on consignment.

### Cited Findings
- Three standard models at Component Sense: outright cash purchase, consignment, and InPlant (stock stays at the client site). — [Altium](https://resources.altium.com/p/why-factories-crush-perfectly-good-components)
- Outright purchase: bid within 24 hours, payment within 48 hours of acceptance, 5-10% of component cost. Consignment: seller receives up to 50-60% of market value; stock is often collected within 24 hours; cash arrives slowly as lines sell. — [Component Sense](https://www.componentsense.com/blog/consignment-vs-outrightpurchase)
- Other providers: return percentages are "negotiated upfront based on component type, market conditions, and inventory volume" (A2 Global); profits are "shared at an agreed-upon percentage" (snippet). — [A2 Global](https://a2globalelectronics.com/excess-inventory/slow-moving-component-inventory-find-your-optimal-return-strategy/)
- Ticket sizes seen: £1.55M recovered on one Corning program (2.5M components); about $5M of excess at one Scottish factory. — [Component Sense](https://www.componentsense.com/blog/consignment-vs-outrightpurchase); [Altium](https://resources.altium.com/p/why-factories-crush-perfectly-good-components)
- ITAD ticket sizes: $4.9M resale proceeds on one 72,000-drive hyperscale project within 90 days; Iron Mountain single deals of 60,000 drives and 75,000 IT items. — [SK tes](https://www.sktes.com/case-studies/maximizing-value-in-hyperscale-server-decommissioning); [Resource Recycling](https://resource-recycling.com/e-scrap/2026/05/05/iron-mountain-puts-itad-at-the-center-of-its-growth/)
- Cyclicality: Smith's revenue rose 34.7% in 2024 while Quiksol fell 23.3% and 4 Star fell 48.3%; ITAD revenue moves with memory prices (10% of Iron Mountain ALM revenue swing attributed to memory pricing). — [Electronics Sourcing](https://newpowerww.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/09/ES-2025-Global-Independent-Distributors-Report.pdf); [Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/stocks/articles/iron-mountain-incorporated-q1-2026-205459083.html)
- Authorized distributors run about 10-11% gross margin (Avnet, Arrow), the floor against which open-market margins are judged. — [MDM](https://www.mdm.com/news/operations/earnings/asia-carries-avnets-4q-sales-uptick/)

### Inferences
- If the seller gets 50-60% of market value on consignment, the intermediary and downstream brokers keep 40-50% for selling, testing, storage and risk. That is the gross margin pool a new entrant would be sharing with existing players, not a new one it would create.
- Consignment removes inventory capital but not operating cost: storage, counting, inspection, listing and customer acquisition are paid before any sale, and most line items in an excess lot sell slowly or never.

### Gaps
- No published consignment split or commission rate from any named firm.
- No sourced broker gross margin in shortage versus glut. Industry talk of 20-30%+ in shortages is unverified background and I found no citation.
- ITAD revenue-share terms with enterprise clients (typical 50/50 to 70/30 claims) not sourced this session.
- Payment terms to buyers (net 30, prepayment for brokers) not sourced.

## 6. Legal start: licences, export controls, tariffs, recycling certifications

### Takeaway
I found no trading licence requirement for components or used IT hardware, but the practical gate is certification and compliance: R2 or e-Stewards for ITAD, AS6081/AS9120-type quality certifications for components, and export controls that tightened again in November 2026 for advanced GPUs.

### Cited Findings
- R2 certification costs (SERI guidance, 2021): $2,000 annual SERI/R2 licence, plus other required management-system certifications, plus an annual audit; cost "varies greatly" with size and scope; process-based structure is meant to make it achievable for small and startup facilities. — [SERI steps to getting certified](https://sustainableelectronics.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/03/Steps-to-Getting-Certified-2021.03.pdf)
- R2v3 was published July 1, 2020; all R2:2013 certificates expired June 30, 2023, which squeezed some facilities. — [Resource Recycling](https://resource-recycling.com/e-scrap/2023/06/22/r2-certification-deadline-puts-squeeze-on-some-facilities); [NSF](https://www.nsf.org/ca/en/knowledge-library/r2v3-certification-transition)
- BIS interim final rule issued November 16, 2026 (effective on Federal Register publication November 19, 2026; comments due January 18, 2027): rescinds the January 2025 AI Diffusion rule, controls remote access to advanced compute, and adds a licence requirement for shipments of advanced GPUs to or within Indonesia, Malaysia, Singapore, Thailand and Brazil. — [Skadden](https://www.skadden.com/insights/publications/2026/11/ai-diffusion-2-bis-introduces-new-export-controls); [Greenberg Traurig](https://www.gtlaw.com/ar/insights/2026/11/bis-interim-final-rule-targets-remote-access-to-advanced-semiconductors-rescinds-ai-diffusion-rule)
- Under the EAR it is a violation to knowingly purchase GPUs that were exported without the required licence, even if the GPUs are already outside the US. — [Greenberg Traurig](https://www.gtlaw.com/ja/insights/2026/9/navigating-gpu-export-controls-and-ai-use-restrictions-in-data-center-operations)
- January 2026: BIS revised licence review policy for certain advanced computing exports to China and Macau, alongside a Section 232 semiconductor action with broad exceptions for domestic use. — [Covington](https://www.cov.com/news-and-insights/insights/2026/01/us-commerce-department-revises-license-review-policy-for-exports-of-certain-advanced-computing-commodities-to-china-and-macau); [Pillsbury](https://pillsburylaw.com/en/news-and-insights/trump-advanced-ai-semiconductors-actions.html)
- E-waste: exports of e-waste to developing countries have been restricted for years yet continued; about 20% of used TVs imported to Ghana worked (McGee's estimate). — [Altium](https://resources.altium.com/p/why-factories-crush-perfectly-good-components)
- Regulatory downside of bad disposal: the SEC fined Morgan Stanley $35M (2022) after thousands of drives were auctioned without being erased. — [Slashdot summary of FT](https://hardware.slashdot.org/story/22/10/06/1030211/big-tech-banks-government-departments-shred-millions-of-storage-devices-they-could-reuse)

### Inferences
- A reseller of used servers with GPUs now carries knowledge-based export liability and needs screening of every buyer and destination. This raises the compliance cost of cross-border resale, which is where much of the demand for older hardware sits (Asia was 42% of independent distributor sales in 2024).
- Because R2 requires underlying management-system certifications and a facility audit, a new ITAD operator cannot be certified before it has an operating site. Enterprise sellers commonly require the certificate, so brokering through a certified partner is the realistic first step.

### Gaps
- No source confirming that no licence is needed to trade components or used IT equipment in the US, UK or EU. Local waste-carrier, WEEE and Basel Convention rules for non-working equipment were not researched.
- No months-to-certification figure for R2v3 or e-Stewards for a new applicant, and no e-Stewards cost.
- Section 232 semiconductor tariff rates and whether they apply to used or refurbished equipment were not found.
- AS6081, AS9120 and ISO 9001 certification time and cost for a new component broker not found.

## 7. Evidence that better verification raises the realised price, and by how much

### Takeaway
The strongest quantified evidence is on the ITAD side and comes from vendors: verified on-site erasure plus resale returned $2.3M more than shredding on one 72,000-drive project. On the component side the uplift evidence is the 5x gap between lot sale and consignment, which mixes traceability with patient selling.

### Cited Findings
- SK tes hyperscale case study: 72,000 drives; client received $4.9M from resale within 90 days, "netting $2.3 million more than a traditional drive pull and shred approach"; a second page calls it a 34% higher financial return; 96% on-site wipe yield. Vendor marketing, no independent audit. — [SK tes](https://www.sktes.com/case-studies/maximizing-value-in-hyperscale-server-decommissioning); [SK tes PDF](https://www.sktes.com/hubfs/SK%20tes%20Campaign%20Images%20and%20Videos/Data%20Center%20Decommissioning/SK%20tes_Case%20Study_Major%20Hyperscale%20Decommissioning%20Project%20(1).pdf)
- Techbuyer offered a six-figure sum for thousands of used drives at one London data center, conditional on wiping; the owner shredded them. — [TechRadar Pro](https://www.techradar.com/pro/shred-less-save-more-why-enterprises-are-ditching-destruction-for-smart-sanitization)
- Component Sense: consignment with trace intact returns 50-60% of market value versus 5-10% of cost for an outright broker lot sale, about 5x. — [Component Sense](https://www.componentsense.com/blog/consignment-vs-outrightpurchase)
- McGee: with first-hand stock from the original buyer, traceability is "better than any inspection", and Component Sense can "typically get the big manufacturers all their money back". — [Altium](https://resources.altium.com/p/why-factories-crush-perfectly-good-components)
- Iron Mountain is winning contracts where drives are "decommissioned, sanitized, and remarketed" (60,000 drives), showing large owners do switch to resale when a trusted party does the sanitization. — [Resource Recycling](https://resource-recycling.com/e-scrap/2026/05/05/iron-mountain-puts-itad-at-the-center-of-its-growth/)
- Blancco 2026: 77% of organizations would prefer reuse over destruction if devices can be completely sanitized. — [Blancco](https://blancco.com/resources/blog-should-your-business-destroy-hard-drives/); [Resource Recycling](https://resource-recycling.com/e-scrap/2026/05/19/ai-data-anxiety-push-enterprises-to-destroy-working-devices-report/)
- Counter-evidence: 38% of organizations in the Blancco survey had a data leak in the past 12 months, and 32% of those attributed a leak to redeployed drives or devices that still held data. — [Resource Recycling](https://resource-recycling.com/e-scrap/2026/05/19/ai-data-anxiety-push-enterprises-to-destroy-working-devices-report/)
- Counter-evidence: 24% of suspect counterfeit parts reported to ERAI in 2025 passed electrical testing. — [ERAI](https://www.erai.com/erai_blog/3192/2025_annual_report)

### Inferences
- $4.9M across 72,000 drives is about $68 per drive, and the $2.3M uplift is about $32 per drive (my arithmetic; the resale total may include other equipment). The uplift from verification is real but modest per unit, so it is a volume and logistics business.
- In the SK tes case the party capturing the uplift was the incumbent ITAD firm with the hyperscaler contract. The uplift exists, and incumbents with trust and scale are already taking it.
- For components, the verified price uplift accrues mainly where trace is preserved from the original purchaser. Testing can partly substitute for lost trace but does not restore full price for regulated buyers.

### Gaps
- No independent (non-vendor) study quantifying price uplift from verification in either market.
- No data on price realised for AS6171-tested parts versus untested parts of the same lot.
- No 2026 update of the shred-versus-resell economics under shortage pricing, where the uplift per drive should be much larger than in the SK tes case.

## 8. Reasons sellers still choose destruction or scrap even when resale value is high

### Takeaway
Destruction is mostly a policy choice driven by breach liability, regulation and contract terms, and the sellers who destroy the most (hyperscalers, banks, government) are the least price-sensitive. Component scrapping is driven by accounting loss-aversion, franchise restrictions and lack of management attention more than by doubt about authenticity.

### Cited Findings
- FT (2022): the motive for shredding is fear that data could leak if drives are not wiped correctly, "triggering anger from customers and huge fines from regulators". — [Slashdot summary of FT](https://hardware.slashdot.org/story/22/10/06/1030211/big-tech-banks-government-departments-shred-millions-of-storage-devices-they-could-reuse)
- Morgan Stanley was fined $35M by the SEC after an ITAD chain resold thousands of unwiped drives. — [Slashdot summary of FT](https://hardware.slashdot.org/story/22/10/06/1030211/big-tech-banks-government-departments-shred-millions-of-storage-devices-they-could-reuse)
- Hyperscale operators typically want drives broken to dust so there can be no leak. — [Slashdot summary of BBC story](https://it.slashdot.org/story/23/06/06/1845205/why-millions-of-usable-hard-drives-are-being-destroyed?sdsrc=next)
- Blancco 2026: 41% cite new or evolving privacy rules as the main driver of changes in end-of-life data handling; 56% call data security a major barrier to sustainability goals; nearly 60% raised privacy-compliance spending, by an average of 40% over 2024; only about one-third call sustainability a major influence. — [Resource Recycling](https://resource-recycling.com/e-scrap/2026/05/19/ai-data-anxiety-push-enterprises-to-destroy-working-devices-report/)
- Blancco 2020 survey of 1,850 decision makers: many large enterprises physically destroyed end-of-life equipment believing it was "better for the environment". — [Blancco white paper](https://edge.sitecorecloud.io/nurnbergmes0e18-nmmultisite6e34-prod50da-6065/media/Project/NuernbergMesse/it-sa/Knowledge/2020/Whitepaper-2020/itsa365-20200924-WP-en-poor-sustainability-pactices-e-waste-problem-blancco.pdf)
- Components, per McGee: franchise agreements bar distributors from taking back or redistributing stock (Analog Devices, Xilinx named); companies resist selling below book value "when it's still on the books at full value"; excess projects are handed to junior staff, stall and get shelved; engineers and supply-chain teams define "obsolete" differently. — [Altium](https://resources.altium.com/p/why-factories-crush-perfectly-good-components)
- Electronics Sourcing (July 2024) frames the three outcomes for excess as destroyed, recycled or sold. Not opened. — [Electronics Sourcing](https://electronics-sourcing.com/2024/07/08/destroyed-recycled-or-sold-the-three-options-for-excess-electronic-components-inventory/)
- Even with resale possible, 44% of destroyed data center assets were still functional (Blancco 2026). — [Resource Recycling](https://resource-recycling.com/e-scrap/2026/05/19/ai-data-anxiety-push-enterprises-to-destroy-working-devices-report/)

### Inferences
- For a bank or hyperscaler, the downside of one leak (a $35M fine, customer loss) dwarfs the resale value of a drive (tens of dollars). Better verification does not change that asymmetry unless liability is also transferred, which a startup balance sheet cannot credibly do.
- The addressable sellers are the middle tier: enterprises and mid-size manufacturers without a strict destroy-everything policy. They are already served by ITAD firms and excess brokers, and their lots are smaller.
- The component-side reasons for scrapping are organisational (write-down avoidance, low priority). A service that removes effort, such as on-site managed consignment, addresses them better than a verification technology does. That is the InPlant model that already exists.

### Gaps
- No direct evidence found this session on brand protection, grey-market channel conflict or OEM contract clauses requiring destruction of excess or returned product, though these are commonly cited in the industry.
- No hyperscaler's written media-destruction policy was retrieved.
- No data on what share of excess components is actually scrapped versus sold.
