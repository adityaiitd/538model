## 1. Size: annual dollar volume traded and stock outstanding

### Takeaway
The largest verifiable flows are charged-off consumer debt (Encore and PRA alone deploy roughly $2.5-2.7 billion a year of purchase price, which is tens of billions of face value) and tax-lien and tax-deed sales (roughly $4-5 billion a year each, against $22 billion of annual unpaid property tax). Foreclosure auctions are growing fast in 2026 but still sit about a third below early-2020 levels, heirs' property is a $32-42 billion stock that barely trades, and the solar and Tricolor pools are large stocks ($8 billion-plus Mosaic loans, 500,000-plus SunStrong customers, about 100,000 Tricolor loans) that changed hands once, to incumbent lenders, not in an open market.

## 2. The discount: clean versus unverified records, and how much is documentation

### Takeaway
Documented discounts are large (foreclosure buyers pay about 65% of estimated retail value; charged-off debt trades at 4-10 cents on the dollar; tax-foreclosed homes have sold for under 10% of assessed value), but in almost every case the sources attribute the gap to something other than, or in addition to, missing records: seller reserve prices, condition and no-inspection risk, credit quality and debt age, statutory redemption and surplus rules, or outright fraud. I found no source that isolates a pure "documentation discount" with a clean-versus-unclean price pair in any of these markets.

### Inferences
- Foreclosure auctions: the buyer price (65.3%) sits one point above the seller's credit bid (64.2%). The price level is set by the lender's reserve and buyer cost assumptions, not by a bid-side title haircut. A third party clearing title would not obviously move the lender's reserve.
- Charged-off debt: price tracks age and expected collections. Documentation matters as a legal precondition to sue (see section 8), so missing "media" lowers value, but I found no price series split by document availability.
- Solar: the prices that cleared ($25 million cash plus credit bid; $7 million stalking horse; $11 million ATLAS settlement; no bidder for Mosaic) are for servicing platforms and residual interests. The financed assets stayed with existing ABS trusts and lenders. The discount reflects servicing cost, consumer claims and collateral performance. The KBRA aging-convention episode shows that data definitions, not only credit, moved reported losses.
- Tricolor is the cleanest case where records (duplicate VINs, double pledges) are the core problem. It is fraud, and resolving it is a priority dispute between secured lenders in court.
- The 15.5 cents FTX "pristine" versus 2-3% lower figure is a real documented clean-versus-unclean spread, but it is small (2-3 points), which argues against a big documentation premium in claims.

## 3. Can the gap be closed fast? Time and cost to cure

### Takeaway
Mostly no. The defects that carry the largest discounts are cured by a court order or the passage of time (quiet title after a tax deed takes 3 to 18 months or more; probate for heirs' property can take years; Tricolor's double pledges are being sorted in Chapter 7), while defects curable by records review alone are narrow. The one fast path with evidence behind it is the heirship affidavit, which some states accept as proof of ownership without probate.

### Cited Findings

Tax deeds
- Most underwriters will not insure a recent tax-deed acquisition without a quiet title action or a substantial seasoning period. Some specialty underwriters will insure for a higher premium. **[search summary; vendor and forum sources]** — [PropertyOnion, Florida tax deed title insurance](https://propertyonion.com/education/?p=23671); [BiggerPockets](https://www.biggerpockets.com/member-blogs/9383/54139-how-to-solve-title-problems-following-a-tax-deed-buy)
- Quiet title timelines: at least 3 months per a Florida law firm; forum estimates of 6-9 months uncontested and 9-18 months or more contested; 6 months to 2 years in Texas. **[search summary; anecdotal]** — [DeWitt Law](https://dewittlaw.com/foreclosure/8104/quiet-title-actions-tax-deed-sale); [BiggerPockets forum](https://biggerpockets.com/forums/70/topics/683845-which-tax-deed-states-require-a-quiet-title-action)
- Quiet title cost anecdotes range from about $2,000 and 4-6 months uncontested to one case of 9 years and $45,000. Most actions are won on default where notice was proper. **[search summary; anecdotal]** — [BiggerPockets blog](https://www.biggerpockets.com/member-blogs/6994/80128-why-quiet-title-is-better-than-title-insurance)
- A Florida forum thread reports an attorney saying no title insurance for 5 years after a tax deed. A Pennsylvania investor disputes that insurers accept title after 1 year. **[search summary; anecdotal]** — [BiggerPockets](https://biggerpockets.com/forums/41/topics/494806-florida-tax-deed-attorney-says-no-title-insurance-for-5-years)

Heirs' property
- Cost to clear title ranges from a few thousand dollars to tens of thousands. **[search summary]** — [Alabama Cooperative Extension](https://www.aces.edu/blog/topics/home/costs-to-clear/)
- A 2021 study found legal fees upwards of $10,000 even for five acres, and attorneys in Georgia and Alabama said owners typically cannot afford them. **[search summary]** — [Center for Public Integrity](https://publicintegrity.org/inequality-poverty-opportunity/law-helps-vulnerable-heirs-property-owners-but-only-if-they-can-afford-it/)
- "As the years and generations pass, the number of owners increases, and the cost and difficulty of clearing titles increases exponentially." **[search summary]** — [National Agricultural Law Center, 2024](https://nationalaglawcenter.org/wp-content/uploads//assets/HeirsProperty/Heirs-Property-Research-Report_2024.pdf)
- NCLC 2026 survey (61 legal services providers and 27 title professionals in 31 states): barriers to completing probate were cost (92% of legal respondents), lack of representation (84%), unknown or missing heirs (75%), family conflict (74%), complexity (74%), court delays (36%). One case study had been in probate for six years. **[fetched]** — [NCLC 2026](https://www.nclc.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/2026.09_Report_Clearing-the-Path-to-Title.pdf)
- Heirship affidavits: 11 states have express statutory authorisation, 2 more support use through title standards, 11 accept case by case, 6 plus Puerto Rico report little or no use, 19 plus DC had no survey response. 55.6% of title-professional respondents said affidavits are used in their state; 41% said rarely, not used or unsure. In some states title standards allow marketable title immediately on recording an affidavit if the death was more than two or three years earlier. **[fetched]** — [NCLC 2026](https://www.nclc.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/2026.09_Report_Clearing-the-Path-to-Title.pdf)

### Inferences
- Curable by records review plus inspection: confirming that a solar system exists and is producing, matching VINs and titles to loan tapes, identifying heirs from public records, confirming notice was given before a tax sale.
- Needs a court or elapsed time: quiet title after a tax deed, probate or partition for heirs' property, lien priority between lenders on double-pledged collateral, statutory redemption periods, and consumer claims and defences that travel with solar paper.
- Servicing transfers in solar happened in 2-3 months, so speed is possible in that step. The parties who did it were the secured lenders and an incumbent servicer.

### Gaps
- No authoritative (non-forum) data on quiet title cost and duration by state.
- No source on time or cost to rebuild a charged-off account file, or per-document "media" fees charged by sellers.
- No data on how many financed solar systems were found uninstalled, non-operational or without permission to operate after the 2024-2026 bankruptcies.
- Whether the states' Mosaic objection was resolved, and on what terms, was not found.

## 4. Are the records digital and accessible?

### Takeaway
Partly. Mortgage eNotes are still a minority (about 13-15% of new MERS registrations in 2026), county eRecording covers most of the population but far from all jurisdictions, and loan-level solar and auto data sit inside servicers and trustees, where the Tricolor case shows they can be wrong without anyone noticing.

### Cited Findings
- The MERS eRegistry passed 3 million eNotes in March 2026. eNotes were 15.19% of all loans registered on the MERS System in January 2026. **[search summary]** — [ICE Mortgage Technology](https://mortgagetech.ice.com/blog/3-million-enotes-historic-milestone-for-digital-mortgage-ecosystem)
- eNotes were 12.97% of MERS registrations in April 2026, up from 9.85% in April 2025. **[search summary]** — [ICE Mortgage Technology](https://mortgagetech.ice.com/blog/a-digital-milestone-mers-system-hits-record-enote-share)
- MISMO cites 3.2 million registered eNotes. **[search summary]** — [National Mortgage Professional](https://nationalmortgageprofessional.com/news/mismo-updates-enote-standard-digital-mortgage-adoption-tops-32m-registered-loans)
- A vendor release puts the eRecording market at more than 1,500 jurisdictions representing 76% of the US population. Undated vendor claim. **[search summary]** — [National Mortgage Professional, Indecomm](https://nationalmortgageprofessional.com/news/60027/indecomm-global-services-document-management-group-hits-county-mark)
- PRIA maintains a list of eRecording counties dated August 12, 2026; the count was not retrieved. — [PRIA](https://pria.us/?p=697)

- eRecording coverage measures the ability to submit documents electronically. It does not mean historical chains of title are indexed or machine-readable, which is the gap Dono, Titl and Pippin are funded to close.

## 5. Who already does this, and is it crowded?

### Takeaway
Every segment has entrenched incumbents with scale or a structural position: Auction.com in foreclosure auctions, Encore and PRA in charged-off debt, SunStrong, Omnidian and Forbright's Solar Servicing in orphaned solar, and DIP lenders taking assets by credit bid. The AI-title startup layer is real but small, with 2026 rounds at seed size ($2.5 million to $6.5 million).

### Cited Findings
- Auction platforms named in trade guides: Auction.com, Hubzu (Altisource), Xome, RealAuction and Bid4Assets for county tax sales. **[search summary]** — [Real Estate Bees guide](https://realestatebees.com/guides/software/listing-sites/foreclosure-auctions/)
- AI title and records startups: Titl (Miami) raised a $2.5 million seed in January 2026 and aims to operate in nearly 20 states by end 2026; Dono raised a $6.5 million seed in February 2026 citing demand from title insurers; Pippin Title raised a $3 million seed extension for $8 million total (date not confirmed); Paraito (Quebec) raised C$2.65 million pre-seed. No Series A or later round was found. **[search summary]** — [HousingWire on Titl](https://www.housingwire.com/articles/titl-raises-2-5m-to-expand-title-automation-technology/); [SiliconANGLE on Dono](https://siliconangle.com/2026/02/10/dono-raises-6-5m-seed-round-modernize-property-records-ai/); [National Mortgage News](https://www.nationalmortgagenews.com/news/property-records-tech-draws-fresh-vc-interest)
- Debt buyers: Encore (MCM) and PRA are described by the CFPB as the two largest. The FTC's 2013 sample of nine buyers held over 75% of the industry. **[search summary]** — [CFPB 2015](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/about-us/newsroom/cfpb-takes-action-against-the-two-largest-debt-buyers-for-using-deceptive-tactics-to-collect-bad-debts/); [Consumer Law & Policy Blog](https://clpblog.citizen.org/ftc-study-on-the-debt-buying-industry/)
- Solar: SunStrong was formed in Q3 2024 specifically to manage solar assets otherwise abandoned in bankruptcy and closed a $900 million financing in September 2025 backed by 64,000-plus assets in 18 states. **[search summary]** — [SunStrong](https://sunstrongmanagement.com/company/); [PV Tech](https://www.pv-tech.org/sunstrong-raises-900-million-refinance-sunpower-residential-portfolio/)
- GreenLancer expanded nationwide repair and "orphaned" system services in August 2025. Trina Solar markets orphaned rooftop systems as a 2026 opportunity for installers. SolarInsure lists close to two dozen large bankrupt solar companies as of January 2026. **[search summary]** — [pv magazine USA on GreenLancer](https://pv-magazine-usa.com/2025/08/21/greenlancer-expands-residential-solar-repair-and-orphaned-system-services-nationwide-2/); [Trina Solar](https://www.trinasolar.com/us/resources/blog/Opportunity-in-Orphaned-Rooftop-PV-Systems/)
- Sunnova's buyers were its DIP lenders (Oaktree, Farallon, Owl Creek, Keyframe) plus GoodFinch. Mosaic's unsecured creditors argued the financing and short sale process was skewed towards the company's lenders. **[search summary]** — [Bloomberg Government](https://news.bgov.com/esg/solaris-acquires-assets-of-bankrupt-solar-company-sunnova); [Bloomberg Law](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/bankruptcy-law/solar-mosaic-creditors-challenge-lender-control-of-bankruptcy)
- Tricolor: Vervent was put forward as primary servicer; Holman works with the trustee on vehicle sales. **[search summary]** — [Bloomberg Law](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/financial-accounting/vervent-set-to-take-over-servicing-tricolors-auto-loans)
- Loan-sale advisers active: Newmark, JLL, First Financial Network (quarterly loan sale events). **[search summary]** — [Newmark](https://www.nmrk.com/services/capital-markets/loan-sale-advisory); [FFN](https://ffncorp.com/services/quarterly-loan-sale-event)
- Claims trading: Xclaim operates a marketplace. **[search summary]** — [The Block](https://www.theblock.co/amp/post/202029/ftxs-5-billion-haul-fails-to-boost-price-of-bankruptcy-claims-xclaim-says)

### Inferences
- Solar is the newest segment and already has three scaled incumbents. A new entrant would be a subcontractor (field verification, data) to them or to ABS trustees, not the controlling party.
- Tiny seed rounds in AI title suggest investors see a tools business, not a principal-risk arbitrage.
- In court-run liquidations, pre-petition lenders and DIP lenders control the outcome through credit bids. Outside intermediaries have not captured the uplift in the cases found.

### Gaps
- No revenue, margin or funding figures found for Auction.com, Xome, Hubzu, Bid4Assets, RealAuction, Omnidian, SolarInsure, Mission Capital, Garnet or DebtX.
- No Encore or PRA margin or collection-multiple data for US 2026 purchases retrieved.
- Distressed funds active in tax liens and heirs' property partition buying: not researched.

## 6. Business model norms: fees, take rates, sharing uplift without buying

### Takeaway
Verified fee points are thin: foreclosure auction platforms charge buyers about 5%, county tax-sale platforms and counties add fixed or roughly 10% premiums, and M&A-style success fees run 1-6%. I found no sourced example of an intermediary earning a share of valuation uplift for curing title or documents without owning the asset.

### Cited Findings
- Auction.com buyer's premium is reported by third-party guides as 5% or $2,500, whichever is greater, with no listing fee for sellers. **[search summary; not an official fee schedule]** — [Real Estate Bees](https://realestatebees.com/guides/software/listing-sites/foreclosure-auctions/); [BiggerPockets](https://biggerpockets.com/forums/48/topics/565019-what-costs-do-auctioncom-make-you-pay-on-top-of-the-winning-bid)
- Hubzu lists a 4.5% buyer's premium plus a $299 technology fee on some properties. **[search summary]** — [Real Estate Bees](https://realestatebees.com/guides/software/listing-sites/foreclosure-auctions/)
- One county second-round online tax sale adds a 10% buyer's premium and a $30 deed recording fee. **[search summary]** — [tax-sale.info](https://tax-sale.info/round2)
- Ten-X (LoopNet) cites an industry average commercial auction fee of about 5% of the winning bid. **[search summary]** — [LoopNet](https://www.loopnet.com/cre-explained/auctions/streamlined-transaction-fee)
- General sell-side success fees: 4-6% below $10 million, 2-4% from $10-50 million, 1-2% above $100 million, with boutique minimums of $400,000-750,000. M&A benchmark, not loan-sale specific. **[search summary]** — [Windsor Drake](https://windsordrake.com/advisory/how-success-fees-work/)
- FFN advertises a "success based fee" for loan sales without a published rate. **[search summary]** — [FFN](https://ffncorp.com/services/quarterly-loan-sale-event)
- After Mosaic's bankruptcy the ABS administrator (a Forbright subsidiary) is owed a higher monthly administration fee than before plus newly added uncapped fees, while servicing fees were unchanged. **[search summary]** — [KBRA press release](https://www.kbra.com/publications/gvyNyGRR)
- PRA defines purchase price multiple as gross dollars expected to be collected per dollar invested. **[search summary]** — [PRA Q3 2026](https://www.morningstar.com/news/pr-newswire/20261105ph90304/pra-group-reports-third-quarter-2026-results)
- NAUPA warns that communications involving a fee for recovering unclaimed property come from third-party finders, and that state claims are free. **[search summary]** — [NAUPA](https://unclaimed.org/how-states-return-missing-money/)

### Inferences
- The norm is a percentage of sale price (auction, adviser) or a per-account or basis-point servicing fee, not a share of uplift. The Mosaic administrator fee increase shows successor servicers can reprice upwards when the alternative is no servicer.
- Debt buyers capture uplift only by owning the asset and bearing collection and compliance risk.

### Gaps
- Loan-sale adviser fee schedules (Mission Capital, DebtX, Garnet, FDIC advisers) were not found.
- Special servicer fee norms were not sourced in this session. Unverified recall: CMBS special servicers typically earn about 25 basis points a year plus workout and liquidation fees near 1%.
- Title curative pricing and heir-finder contingency fees (and state caps on finder fees) were not found.
- Backup or successor servicing fees per solar system or per loan were not found.

## 7. Legal start: licences and how long they take

### Takeaway
A new firm cannot lawfully start in days in the consumer-facing markets. Collection and debt-buyer licences are state by state and a compliance vendor puts typical approval at 120-180 days, although individual states can be much quicker (Alaska 10-14 business days, Oregon about a month).

### Cited Findings
- Collection agency licensing varies by state and by service. A compliance vendor estimates most licences take 120-180 days. **[search summary; vendor estimate]** — [Harbor Compliance](https://www.harborcompliance.com/debt-collection-agency-license)
- Oregon requires a debt-buyer licence separate from the collection agency licence, filed through NMLS, with errors and omissions insurance. Oregon's directory lists about one month processing for a collection agency licence. **[search summary]** — [Oregon DFR](https://dfr.oregon.gov/business/licensing/financial/Pages/debt-buyer.aspx); [Oregon licence directory](https://apps.oregon.gov/SOS/LicenseDirectory/LicenseDetail/644)
- Alaska says a complete collection agency application can take 10-14 business days. **[search summary]** — [Alaska DCCED](https://www.commerce.alaska.gov/web/cbpl/ProfessionalLicensing/CollectionAgencies/FrequentlyAskedQuestions)
- Maine issues debt collector licences (collection agency, debt buyer, repossession company) through NMLS. **[search summary]** — [Maine BCCP](https://www.maine.gov/pfr/consumercredit/industry/licensing/debt_collector.htm)
- California licenses debt collectors and defines a debt buyer as an entity regularly purchasing charged-off consumer debt for collection. **[search summary]** — [California DFPI](https://dfpi.ca.gov/regulated-industries/debt-collection-licensee/)
- Some states require an exam and a surety bond. **[search summary]** — [Wolters Kluwer CT Corporation](https://www.wolterskluwer.com/en/solutions/ct-corporation/collection-agency-licensing)

### Inferences
- Data, verification and inspection services sold to licensed owners, servicers, trustees or title agents can likely start without a state financial licence, since the customer holds it. Acting as servicer, collector, broker, auctioneer or title agent cannot. This is my reading; no source addressed it directly.
- Subservicing under a licensed master servicer (as Concord does for Mosaic paper) is the documented fast route into solar loan servicing.

### Gaps
- Licence requirements and timelines for real-estate broker, auctioneer, title agent, mortgage servicer and consumer loan servicer roles were not researched.
- Whether passive debt buyers are exempt in particular states was not established.
- Requirements to bid at county tax sales (registration, deposits, state limits on institutional bidders) were not researched.

## 8. Regulatory and reputational hazards

### Takeaway
The hazards are concentrated exactly where the discounts are biggest. Tax-sale equity capture is being legislated away after Tyler v. Hennepin (Illinois complied in July 2026), debt buyers have a decade of CFPB orders specifically about collecting without documentation, and solar paper carries consumer claims that 25 states insisted must follow the loans to any buyer.

### Cited Findings

Tax sales
- Illinois HB 4537, signed July 10, 2026, brings the state into line with Tyler v. Hennepin County (2023) and creates a temporary fund to compensate owners who lost surplus equity. One outlet calls Illinois the last state to comply. **[search summary]** — [National Mortgage Professional](https://nationalmortgageprofessional.com/news/illinois-changes-property-tax-foreclosure-process-return-surplus-equity)
- Idaho, Nebraska, Maine and South Dakota banned equity capture after Tyler; Oregon adopted most reforms in 2025. Advocacy source. **[search summary]** — [Pacific Legal Foundation](https://pacificlegal.org/states-bring-back-home-equity-theft/)
- Pung v. Isabella County, decided June 23, 2026: per NCLC, the Supreme Court limited what a former owner receives after a tax taking and declined to impose a fair-market-value regime as constitutional law, leaving it to states. **[search summary]** — [NCLC Digital Library](https://library.nclc.org/article/supreme-court-limits-what-former-owner-gets-after-property-tax-taking)
- Bloomberg Law reports open questions remain on "home equity theft" after the ruling. **[search summary]** — [Bloomberg Law](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/business-and-practice/supreme-court-ruling-leaves-open-questions-on-home-equity-theft)
- NCLC has published reports critical of tax-lien sales, and NTLA has issued a formal response. **[search summary]** — [NCLC tax lien sales report](https://www.nclc.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/09/tax-lien-sales-report.pdf); [NTLA response](https://members.ntla.org/news-releases/Details/response-to-national-consumer-law-center-199964)
- New York City's Independent Budget Office published an April 2026 report on the city's tax lien sale. Not read. — [NYC IBO](https://www.ibo.nyc.gov/assets/ibo/downloads/pdf/taxes-and-other-revenues/2026/2026-april-nycs-tax-lien-sale.pdf)

Debt documentation
- 2015: the CFPB found Encore and PRA bought debts that were often inaccurate, undocumented or unenforceable, barred them from collecting unsubstantiated debt, and ordered refunds and penalties (reported as $61 million in refunds and $79 million in total by trade press). **[search summary]** — [CFPB](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/about-us/newsroom/cfpb-takes-action-against-the-two-largest-debt-buyers-for-using-deceptive-tactics-to-collect-bad-debts/); [American Banker](https://www.americanbanker.com/news/consumer-finance/cfpb-takes-enforcement-action-against-two-debt-buying-giants-1083665-1.html)
- 2020: the CFPB sued Encore alleging hundreds of lawsuits filed without original account-level documentation, at least 100 suits filed after the statute of limitations, and more than 250 unanswered requests for original account documents. **[search summary]** — [Consumer Finance Monitor](https://www.consumerfinancemonitor.com/2020/09/15/cfpb-sues-debt-collectors-and-debt-buyers-for-alleged-violations-of-cfpa-fdcpa-and-2015-consent-order/)
- April 10, 2026: the CFPB under the Trump administration opposed Portfolio Recovery Associates' bid to undo its 2023 order. **[search summary; truncated]** — [Bloomberg Law](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/banking-law/trumps-cfpb-opposes-debt-collector-bid-to-undo-2023-settlement)
- The CFPB's Encore enforcement page lists an order terminating the consent order; date not seen. **[search summary]** — [CFPB](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/enforcement/actions/encore/)
- A CFPB order against a New Jersey law firm and debt buyer found that relying only on summary information from the client was an unfair means of collection. **[search summary]** — [Carlton Fields](https://www.carltonfields.com/insights/publications/cfpb-sanctions-law-firm-and-debt-buyer-for-failing)
- FTC 2013: consumers disputed 3.2% of sold debts, implying about 1 million disputed debts a year industry-wide, and buyers verified only about half of disputed debts. **[search summary]** — [Consumer Law & Policy Blog](https://clpblog.citizen.org/ftc-study-on-the-debt-buying-industry/)

Solar
- 25 states and DC demanded that buyers of Mosaic consumer credit contracts stay liable for TILA-related claims and defences; several states had open investigations into alleged deceptive lending and false marketing. **[fetched, truncated]** — [Bloomberg Law](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/bankruptcy-law/solar-mosaics-proposed-loan-sale-prompts-pushback-from-states)
- Press coverage in 2026 describes homeowners still paying for panels that never produced power and customers left with broken systems. **[search summary]** — [Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/sectors/energy/articles/solar-firms-went-bust-panels-154000686.html); [CBS News Texas](https://www.cbsnews.com/texas/news/bankruptcies-leave-some-solar-customers-with-broken-panels/)

Heirs' property
- NCLC ties heirs' property loss to tax foreclosure, lack of access to repair and disaster aid, and notes heirs are disproportionately older adults and Black households in the South (one survey: 77% of Black households have no will). **[fetched]** — [NCLC 2026](https://www.nclc.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/2026.09_Report_Clearing-the-Path-to-Title.pdf)
- Heirs' property owners are "particularly susceptible to below-market-value partition or tax sales". **[search summary]** — [National Agricultural Law Center](https://nationalaglawcenter.org/wp-content/uploads//assets/HeirsProperty/Heirs-Property-Research-Report_2024.pdf)

### Inferences
- In tax sales, the post-Tyler regime sends surplus above the tax debt back to the former owner. That removes much of the buyer's windfall in tax-deed states that used strict forfeiture, and it shrinks the spread an intermediary could share.
- In consumer debt, better documentation is a compliance cost of entry that incumbents already bear under consent orders. It does not obviously create resale uplift.
- In heirs' property and solar, a for-profit entrant buying cheaply and reselling higher sits on the wrong side of state attorneys general and consumer advocates. A service-to-owner or service-to-lender model has less exposure.

### Gaps
- Current status of CFPB Regulation F and any 2025-2026 changes was not found.
- State-level documentation statutes for debt buyers (for example California's and New York's) were not retrieved.
- State limits on tax-lien investing and a full list of post-Tyler statutes were not compiled.
- Uniform Partition of Heirs Property Act adoption count was not retrieved.
