# No venture candidate passes every filter

No candidate in the research passes every filter, and the revenue targets are the reason. The only companies found that reached this revenue scale on about $10M or less of outside equity are Hyperliquid, Midjourney and Surge AI, and all three earn net fees or high-margin service revenue. No physical-goods principal trader has done it. The closest fit is an agent desk that clears titled surplus generation blocks and data-center electrical gear with a third-party warranty wrap. It can start in weeks with no capital at risk, but my estimate of its 18-month fee revenue is $5M to $30M, not $100M. A standby liquidation mandate for GPU loan collateral is the hedge, because it pays when the AI capital cycle turns and the surplus desk's buyers pull back. No Surprises Act arbitration filing is the fastest cash of the three, but a pending bill would end it for services from 1 January 2028. The recommended Day-0 wedge is the surplus desk run as agent, proven or killed in seven days against the thresholds in the last table.

## Three companies reached this scale on $10M or less, and none trades goods

Hyperliquid has a core team of 11, no external funding, and about $713M of revenue in the twelve months to early November 2026 ([Crypto Briefing](https://cryptobriefing.com/hyperliquid-713m-revenue-past-year/)). Midjourney is bootstrapped and was reported at about $200M of revenue, then $300M in 2024 ([WebProNews](https://www.webpronews.com/midjourneys-quiet-cash-machine-how-a-40-person-startup-blew-past-200-million-without-venture-capital/)). Surge AI is bootstrapped and reportedly passed $1B of revenue in 2024 ([AIM Media House](https://aimmediahouse.com/retail/the-ai-startups-making-millions-without-a-dime-of-venture-capital)). These are aggregator figures and press reports, not audited accounts, and Hyperliquid's funding history was not confirmed from a primary source. Midjourney has no sourced $1B year, and Surge took about four years to reach $1B on the researchers' reading. The other fast cases raised far more first. Wiz had over $600M of funding when it reached $100M of ARR in 18 months ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2022/08/10/cloud-security-startup-wiz-reaches-100m-arr-in-just-18-months/)). The researchers found no sourced case of a capital-light principal trader of physical goods reaching $1B of revenue in two to three years on $10M.

The $100B target has the same gap. No pure trader, distributor or broker in the comps is above $100B. Glencore's market cap is about GBP 53B and WESCO's is $13.7B ([StockAnalysis GLEN](https://stockanalysis.com/quote/lon/GLEN/statistics/), [WCC](https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/wcc/statistics/)). The intermediaries near $100B are exchanges: CME at $110B of market cap and ICE at $118B of enterprise value ([CME](https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/cme/statistics/), [ICE](https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/ice/statistics/)). ICE was formed in 2000 around a power exchange its founder bought in 1997, so the path took about 25 years ([Institutional Investor](https://www.institutionalinvestor.com/Articles/2632366/Jeffrey-Sprecher.html)).

ASC 606 forces a choice. An intermediary books gross revenue only if it controls the good before transfer, and momentary title in a back-to-back trade may not qualify. The indicators are fulfillment responsibility, inventory risk and price-setting ([RevenueHub](https://www.revenuehub.org/principalagent-considerations-gross-vs-net)). Gross revenue and no balance-sheet risk cannot both be had on the same trade. Investors restate the number anyway: Flexport's $3.2-3.3B of 2021 gross revenue was about $660M net ([Sacra](https://sacra.com/research/flexport-at-3-3b-revenue/)). Balance-sheet capacity is small. Trafigura carries equity of about 20% of assets today and ran at 11-14% in 2016-2018, which puts $10M of equity at roughly $50-90M of open positions on the researchers' arithmetic ([Trafigura FY2025](https://www.trafigura.com/news-and-insights/insights/2025-trafigura-annual-results/financial-review/), [2018 annual report](https://lib.nus.edu.sg/nus_hlc/annrep/trafint2018.pdf)).

## Titled surplus power gear with a warranty wrap is the closest fit

The business acts as agent for holders of delivered, titled aeroderivative and reciprocating generation packages and MV/LV switchgear from cancelled or delayed projects. It sells to buyers that have interconnection and no equipment, and attaches third-party recertification and warranty.

**The catch.** Undelivered slots are not freely tradable. Virginia Transformer's February 2026 terms bar assignment without the seller's written agreement, charge a $10,000 assignment fee and let the seller refuse ([VTC terms](https://www.vatransformer.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/FINAL-2.23.2026-VTC-Standard-Terms-and-Conditions.pdf)). Turbine reservation fees are non-refundable and the OEM keeps them if no supply contract is signed ([Synergy Consulting](https://www.synergyconsultingifa.com/industry-knowledge/rethinking-procurement-in-conventional-power-projects-amid-gas-turbine-supply-constraints/)). SoftBank reserved more than 13 GW of turbines and forfeited about $2.85B of advance payments, and no resale attempt was reported (WSJ reporting via [TIKR/Yahoo](https://finance.yahoo.com/energy/articles/softbank-takes-3-7-billion-163245843.html)). The turbine consent point rests on one transformer maker's terms plus commentary, because no turbine contract was obtained. Equipment the seller already owns needs no OEM consent. Hallador bought 460 MW of never-fired Siemens turbines from Energy World for $350M ([Turbomachinery](https://www.turbomachinerymag.com/view/hallador-energy-bypasses-oem-backlog-secures-unfired-siemens-turbine-in-350-million-deal)).

The Day-0 problem with titled gear is warranty. VTC's cover runs 12 months from first energization or 18 months from shipment, whichever comes first, and storage outside the maker's instructions voids it. Used turbines often have unclear maintenance records and thin service support ([Reuters via BOE Report](https://boereport.com/2026/11/09/ai-power-rush-sends-data-centres-hunting-for-used-turbines-and-jet-engines/)). Reseller warranties exist: Maddox warrants reconditioned transformers for three years ([Maddox](https://www.maddox.com/dev/faqs-v2)). The notes contain no insurer or OEM-service quote for a wrap on generation blocks, so the fix is unproven until the 7-day test returns one. Pricing that wrap is where the founder's underwriting skill applies. GSUs and substation transformers are excluded because impedance and utility vendor lists make them project-specific ([DOE](https://www.energy.gov/sites/default/files/2024-10/EXEC-2022-001242%20-%20Large%20Power%20Transformer%20Resilience%20Report%20signed%20by%20Secretary%20Granholm%20on%207-10-24.pdf)). Small turbines are fungible. Developers run more than 100 at one site, mixing models and brands.

**Market and legal start.** Aeroderivative lead times are about 40 months ([Pipeline & Gas Journal](https://pgjonline.com/news/2026/september/dash-for-small-gas-turbines-set-to-impact-data-center-costs)). Caterpillar is booking large recips into 2029 ([Baxtel](https://baxtel.com/news/caterpillar-ramps-engine-and-turbine-output-as-data-center-orders-reach-2029)). 15 kV switchgear takes 52-80 weeks against 24 before the pandemic ([Industrial Sage](https://www.industrialsage.com/switchgear-lead-times-2026/)). Used turbines sell at $1.5-1.7M per MW and refurbished at about $1.3M, against $2.0-2.5M new ([Reuters via BOE Report](https://boereport.com/2026/11/09/ai-power-rush-sends-data-centres-hunting-for-used-turbines-and-jet-engines/)). On the supply side, more than 40% of projects withdraw after signing an interconnection agreement ([APPA on LBNL](https://www.publicpower.org/periodical/article/backlog-power-plants-seeking-transmission-grid-connection-eased-somewhat-2025-lbnl)), and at least 47 large US data-center projects were delayed, withdrawn or blocked in 2026 ([Dealroom citing FT](https://dealroom.co/news/163868-americas-10tn-ai-build-out-runs-into-towns-grids-and-lenders-at-least-47/)). No dataset measures how much ordered equipment is orphaned, and Reuters found no reliable estimate of the secondary market's size. The researchers found no licence needed to broker this equipment, and could not document EPA rules on resale of used engines.

Banks take the large tickets. Piper Sandler was exclusive adviser on the reported $3.4B OnePWR sale ([MLQ.ai summary of FT](https://mlq.ai/news/anthropic-pays-34-billion-for-17-gw-of-caterpillar-gas-generators-that-onepwr-had-on-order-ft-reports/)). ReflowX, Origin Partners, SecondWatt and SecondBloom already work the smaller tickets, and none discloses funding or revenue ([SecondWatt](https://secondwatt.com/), [SecondBloom release](https://www.einpresswire.com/article/955580439/secondbloom-auctions-moves-into-data-centers-as-canceled-projects-top-260-billion-in-2026)). The shortage is also cracking. GE Vernova's contracted gas position grew 6 GW in Q3 2026 after double-digit quarters ([Utility Dive](https://www.utilitydive.com/news/ge-vernova-in-9b-deal-to-buy-high-voltage-manufacturer-as-gas-turbine-orde/873223/)). Wood Mackenzie expected turbine orders to peak in 2026 and Mitsubishi plans to double capacity through 2028 ([Wood Mackenzie](https://www.woodmac.com/press-releases/gas-turbine-prices-soar-195-as-market-faces-supply-demand-crisis/)). New transformer plants from Siemens Energy, Eaton and Hitachi come online between late 2026 and 2028 ([POWER](https://powermag.com/transformers-in-2026-shortage-scramble-or-self-inflicted-crisis/)). Jefferies still sees 87 GW of turbine capacity against 129 GW of demand by 2028 ([MLQ.ai summary of Reuters](https://mlq.ai/news/data-centers-hunt-for-used-gas-turbines-and-converted-jet-engines-as-new-units-slip-to-2030/)).

**Unit economics.** Published broker fees run 2-5% of price, and one turbine broker's schedule runs from 7% on single small units to 2% on parks ([Power Engineering](https://www.power-eng.com/operations-maintenance/surplus-used-equipment-surplus-power-equipment-satisfies-urgent-needs/), [MWPS](https://mobile.mwps.world/faq/what-are-mwps-commission-fees)). The table uses 4%. All results are my arithmetic.

| Mode | $100M revenue needs | $1B revenue needs | What that means |
|---|---|---|---|
| Agent at 4% fee | $2.5B transacted | $25B transacted | Seven Hallador-size deals in 18 months. $25B equals Reuters' figure for a year of total turbine supply. |
| Principal at $1.3-1.7M per MW | 59-77 MW | 590-770 MW | One 50 MW package is $65-85M, about all that $10M of equity carries. At an assumed 3-10% margin, gross profit is $3-10M per $100M. |

| Reference deal | Size | Fee at 4% | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| ReflowX pair of 14.5 MW units | $12.5M | $0.5M | Typical small-broker ticket |
| Hallador and Energy World | $350M | $14M | Buyer took title before its final investment decision |

Sources: [Reuters via BOE Report](https://boereport.com/2026/11/09/ai-power-rush-sends-data-centres-hunting-for-used-turbines-and-jet-engines/), [Turbomachinery](https://www.turbomachinerymag.com/view/hallador-energy-bypasses-oem-backlog-secures-unfired-siemens-turbine-in-350-million-deal), [MLQ.ai summary of FT](https://mlq.ai/news/anthropic-pays-34-billion-for-17-gw-of-caterpillar-gas-generators-that-onepwr-had-on-order-ft-reports/).

The agent model needs no working capital. The buyer funds third-party escrow and the fee is paid at close, which is how SecondWatt already works. The principal model carries full credit risk, because OEM terms state that payment does not depend on the end user paying. OnePWR held a large order book without the balance sheet to take delivery and had to sell it. Principal revenue would also be valued on trader multiples.

Lock-in is unproven. A randomized trial on a freelance marketplace found that higher trust increases bypass ([Gu and Zhu](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:inm:ormnsc:v:67:y:2021:i:2:p:794-807)). In high-value, infrequent B2B deals a take rate is seen as unfair once the parties know each other ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2020/11/04/b2b-marketplaces-will-be-the-next-billion-dollar-e-commerce-startups/amp)). The wrap, the escrow and the inspection record are the reasons to keep the desk in the trade, and no source measures how much bypass they prevent.

The later stages are plausible and unproven within three years. Vendors already standardize power blocks, as Schneider did with 2-2.5 MW modules in September 2026, but a block built to one hyperscaler's spec does not always fit another's ([DCD](https://www.datacenterdynamics.com/en/news/schneider-electric-debuts-25mw-power-modules-for-ai-data-centers/), [ARC Advisory](https://www.arcweb.com/industry-best-practices/power-block-how-modular-architectures-rewiring-data-centers-ai)). Equipment finance could follow the forward-flow model, in which Carvana sold $4.7B of receivables to Ally without recourse in 2025 ([Carvana 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1690820/000169082026000009/cvna-20251231.htm)). Copart took about 20 years to become an all-online fee platform ([Copart](https://copart.com/Content/US/EN/About-Copart/Our-History)).

**Filters it fails:** $100M in 12-18 months as agent, $1B in 2-3 years, proven lock-in, and it is long a shortage that OEMs are spending billions to end.

## GPU loan collateral has $65B of facilities and no tested exit

Plain GPU brokering fails today. No GPU-backed loan has been publicly foreclosed ([American Compute](https://www.amcompute.com/gpu-risk/defaults), an interested vendor). H100 rents are up nearly 60% in a year to about $3.10 an hour on Silicon Data's index, and Ornn's settled at $2.78 on 19 November, so owners re-rent and do not sell ([Silicon Data](https://www.silicondata.com/products/silicon-index/h100), [Ornn](https://data.ornn.com/markets/h100-sxm)). Nvidia rents back idle GPUs at a fixed rate on an estimated 6.5 GW ([DCD](https://www.datacenterdynamics.com/en/news/nvidia-acts-as-backstop-for-customer-gpus-in-return-for-cut-of-cloud-revenue/), [SemiAnalysis](https://newsletter.semianalysis.com/p/nvidia-gpu-debt-backstop-unleashes)). Nvidia's warranty does not pass to a second owner on vendor accounts, and its software terms bar transfer ([Compute Exchange](https://compute.exchange/blogs/h100-gpu-price-2026), [NVIDIA licensing guide](https://docs.nvidia.com/ai-enterprise/planning-resource/licensing-guide/latest/licensing.html)). GB200 racks draw about 132 kW with liquid cooling and are close to site-bound ([SecondWatt](https://secondwatt.com/equipment/gpus/nvidia-gb200-nvl72)). Rental indices and residual-value insurers are funded already: Silicon Data raised $30.5M, and Barkr writes cover backed by Munich Re ([HPCwire](https://www.hpcwire.com/bigdatawire/this-just-in/silicon-data-raises-30-5m-for-ai-compute-pricing-and-benchmarks/), [Capital Relief](https://hodgey1.substack.com/p/the-missing-insurance-market-behind)). A 16 November 2026 BIS rule presumes knowledge of a violation when a foreign data center will not document its customers, and tells resellers to record data centers' customer checks before exporting ([WilmerHale](https://www.wilmerhale.com/en/insights/client-alerts/20261117-us-government-restricts-remote-access-to-advanced-ai-chips-and-formally-rescinds-the-ai-diffusion-rule)).

**The catch and the gap.** Residual-value insurance pays only after an eligible sale inside a 90-day window, and requires OEM warranty for the policy term. No claim has been paid, and the largest disclosed limit is $25M ([Capital Relief](https://hodgey1.substack.com/p/the-missing-insurance-market-behind)). Published hardware values are listing aggregates. The researchers found no transaction-based hardware index. The 25 largest GPU-backed financings total $65.3B at headline size including undrawn commitments ([American Compute tracker](https://www.amcompute.com/gpu-debt/largest-gpu-loans), an interested vendor and not an audited total). CoreWeave's five-year CDS reached 1,115 bp in October 2026, and it has $10.6B due by end-2027 ([Top1Markets](https://www.top1markets.com/news/neocloud-stocks-selloff-coreweave-nebius-iren-october-2026)). The product is a paid mandate as the named liquidation agent in policies and loan documents, plus an index built from the sales it runs. The warranty condition cannot be fixed on Day 0 because Nvidia controls it.

**Market and legal start.** US-to-US sales appear to need no licence, which is an inference from secondary sources and not a cited legal authority. The customer list is under 15 borrowers and their lenders. The largest loans rest on investment-grade offtake, where lenders "underwrite the offtake counterparty far more than the hardware" ([Peony](https://www.peony.ink/blog/gpu-cluster-financing-data-room)). American Compute already sells lender-matching, valuation, monitoring and insurance, and is the natural incumbent for this mandate.

**Unit economics.** The retainer rate is my assumption and has no source. The rest is arithmetic on vendor prices.

| Line | Basis | Result |
|---|---|---|
| 1,000 used H100s | 125 nodes at $150-195K each | $19-24M |
| Fee on 1,000 GPUs | 5-8% buyer premium | $1-2M |
| Fee on 10,000 GPUs | 5-8% | $9.5-19M |
| $100M of fees | 5-8% | $1.25-2.0B liquidated, or 52,000-105,000 H100s |
| Standby retainer | Assumed 5-10 bp a year of covered facility size | $2.5-5M a year on $5B covered |

Sources: [Hashrate Index](https://hashrateindex.com/blog/used-gpu-market-pricing-deprecation-secondary-ai/), [American Compute](https://www.amcompute.com/gpu-server-values/h100), [ClusterBid](https://clusterbid.com/blog/secondary-gpu-market-buy-used-h100-h200-b200-guide).

Revenue stays near the retainer until defaults arrive. One reading puts forced-sale value at 65% of listing value, which shrinks the fee base in the scenario that pays ([American Compute](https://www.amcompute.com/gpu-server-values/h100)). The business earns when credit breaks, which is when the surplus desk's buyers lose financing.

The mining precedent argues against it. Bitcoin-mining lenders that took machines back or accepted a cash payoff recovered 37-65% of their claim. Lenders that stayed with a borrower that kept its site and contracts recovered 80-100% ([American Compute](https://www.amcompute.com/gpu-risk/defaults)). The lender plan on xAI's $5B facility is to take over the cluster in place and rent it out ([CipherTalk](https://ciphertalk.substack.com/p/nobody-knows-what-a-used-gpu-cluster)). Hardware sales will be a small share of the debt total.

**Filters it fails:** every revenue target, demand that exists today, and a broad customer base. Chip vendors and one incumbent already cover parts of the role.

## Arbitration filing pays fast and a bill would end it in 2028

Contingency filing under the No Surprises Act is the only candidate with a comparable intermediary at scale. Providers started 2.6M disputes in 2025, up 77%, and 1.75M in the first half of 2026. They won about 85% at a median 445% of the insurer's benchmark rate, and awards above that rate totalled $15.6B over 2023-2025 ([Georgetown CHIR](https://chir.georgetown.edu/spending-on-idr-process-pushes-no-surprises-act-costs-to-more-than-22-4-billion-over-just-four-years/)).

**The catch.** The process added $22.4B of cost over four years, and UnitedHealthcare cites 2-6% of added premium expense ([Georgetown CHIR](https://chir.georgetown.edu/spending-on-idr-process-pushes-no-surprises-act-costs-to-more-than-22-4-billion-over-just-four-years/)). Three filers hold 77% of resolved dispute lines: Radiology Partners 30%, HaloMD 27% and TeamHealth 20%. Only HaloMD is a third party, so the self-filing groups are not customers ([Health Affairs](https://www.healthaffairs.org/content/forefront/no-surprises-act-idr-process-early-look-2025-data)). Late and unpaid awards are a recognized problem. A bill introduced in October 2026 would end the process for services from 1 January 2028, and a narrower measure could ride the 11 December 2026 funding package ([Healthcare Dive](https://www.healthcaredive.com/news/new-bill-would-overhaul-no-surprises-acts-arbitration-process-frank-pallone/832713/), [Holland & Knight](https://www.hklaw.com/en/insights/publications/2026/10/rep-pallone-proposes-replacement-of-no-surprises-act-arbitration)).

**Market and legal start.** The researchers found no licence requirement for third-party filers and did not verify that against CMS rules. A California federal court dismissed Anthem's suit against HaloMD in April 2026, and Anthem appealed ([Healthcare Finance News](https://www.healthcarefinancenews.com/news/elevance-subsidiary-no-surprises-act-lawsuit-dismissed)).

**Unit economics.** HaloMD reportedly charges 20% of net recovery, a weakly sourced figure, with an unverified cut to 16% from July 2026 ([YesPress](https://yespress.io/halomd)). At those rates $100M of revenue needs $500-625M of net recovery (my arithmetic). The researchers estimate a new entrant at $5-30M in 18 months. Under HaloMD's reported model the client pays arbitration fees of $425 to $1,125 per dispute up front. The AI edge is measurable: arbitrators found 18% of 2025 disputes ineligible, and provider win rates in Q4 2025 ranged from 54.5% to 99.4% by arbitration entity ([Georgetown CHIR](https://chir.georgetown.edu/spending-on-idr-process-pushes-no-surprises-act-costs-to-more-than-22-4-billion-over-just-four-years/)).

**Filters it fails:** generational scale, $1B, market-maker structure, and survival past 2028. It is a fee on a regulatory arbitrage that raises health costs, and I do not recommend building on it.

## Run the surplus desk as agent and sign the GPU mandate as the hedge

The sharpest Day-0 wedge is the surplus desk as agent on titled, delivered equipment with a warranty wrap. It needs no licence that the researchers found, no inventory and no OEM consent. The founder's underwriting skill prices the one thing small brokers do not offer. My estimate of fee revenue in the first 18 months is $5M to $30M. That range is $125M to $750M closed at 4%, between ten ReflowX-size trades and two Hallador-size trades.

$100M in 18 months needs one of three things. The desk closes $2.5B, which means winning mandates that now go to banks. Or the take rises to about 10% through wrap margin and finance placement, an assumed rate with no source, which still needs $1B closed. Or the desk goes principal on 59-77 MW and books gross revenue with single-digit gross profit, inventory risk and a trader multiple. Each path also needs cancellations to release titled equipment while buyers still pay scarcity prices.

The GPU standby mandate is the hedge because the two are opposite bets on one cycle. Stress has started in credit. AI-related debt issuance fell from $113B in June 2026 to $23B in September on Morgan Stanley data seen only through a LinkedIn post ([post](https://ru.linkedin.com/in/david-osipov-92b9966b)). Bonds backed by hyperscaler-leased data centers traded about 146 bp wider than tenant debt on 10 November ([The Edge](https://theedgemalaysia.com/node/826075)). Spending has not turned. Alphabet and Amazon both guide 2027 capex higher ([Data Center Knowledge](https://www.datacenterknowledge.com/data-center-construction/hyperscalers-say-ai-race-has-entered-a-new-phase)). The mandate costs a few lender meetings to test and nothing to hold.

| Candidate | Seven-day, zero-capital test | Pass | Kill |
|---|---|---|---|
| Surplus desk | List about 30 holders of titled surplus from queue withdrawals and cancelled or delayed projects, and 30 buyers with interconnection and no equipment. Ask sellers for signed non-exclusive listings with spec sheets and ask prices. Ask buyers for LOIs to fund third-party escrow on a named unit.
