# Abundant production pays where buyers queue

As of November 24, 2026, the research supports one conclusion a founder may not expect. Abundant production cuts delivered prices by an estimated 1-30% by 2032 and cuts an entrant's lead times by 65-85%, so the money is in selling speed into existing queues, not in making things cheap. No product from aircraft down to cabinets passes all seven conditions that produced the Model T expansion. The largest shortages at today's prices (airliners about $95B a year, gas turbines about $20B, naval ships about $14B, all modelled estimates) need $0.5-20B and 4-8+ years before first revenue. The reachable shortages are long-lead metal parts for turbines, rockets and missiles, and standard power equipment for data centers. The consumer products with the most latent demand (home solar and batteries, backyard homes, cheap cars) are held back by installation, permits, finance and trade law, which a factory does not fix. The ranked recommendation is to build a fast, qualified metal-parts company that starts with buyers who qualify suppliers themselves, or, with less capital and a shorter window, an asset-light supplier of standard power blocks to data-center developers. The second-round stress tests demoted five first-round favourites: merchant rocket motors, satellite buses, delivery-drone airframes, cell-therapy manufacturing platforms and humanoid robots.

Labels used below, carried from the notes: [measured] is a filing, regulator figure or reported actual. [company claim] is a company's own statement. [estimate] is a third-party estimate or forecast, or the researchers' own arithmetic. [search summary] means the figure was seen in a search result and the page was not opened. [unverified recollection] was not sourced. [modelled] is an output of the cross-candidate market model, which is an estimate built partly on unsourced assumptions and is not a forecast.

## Production alone cuts lead times 65-85% but prices only 1-30%

The Model T is the right test, and it is harder to pass than the usual telling suggests. Ford's real price fell about 75% from 1909 to 1921 while Ford output rose about 91 times, but the step that mattered was a roughly sixfold cut against the average 1908 car, aimed at farmers who owned no car, with no status penalty, field repair, public roads and consumer credit. Where tariffs made the same car 20-130% dearer abroad, no mass market followed [estimate] ([NBER WP 31454](https://www.nber.org/papers/w31454)). The notes turn this into seven conditions: demand elasticity above 1, low penetration, price far above the materials floor, production as the binding constraint, complements in place, demand that is not about status, and a 15-25% learning rate. That learning rate shows up only in small, modular, frozen-design factory goods and is near zero for large, site-built or regulated systems [estimate] ([Construction Physics](https://www.construction-physics.com/p/where-are-my-damn-learning-curves)).

Automation has a measured ceiling on cost. In the Second World War, B-17 labor-hours fell about sevenfold while average aircraft cost fell 32% [estimate] ([Construction Physics](https://www.construction-physics.com/p/how-to-build-300000-airplanes-in)). The market model applies the same logic to 19 product lines. Production alone lowers delivered price by 1-30% by 2032 in its plausible case and never approaches a threefold cut. Lead times for an entrant's customers fall 65-85% in metal parts, power equipment, grid components and drone components, because most of today's lead time is queue [modelled]. The notes found no disclosed operating data from any company supporting threefold or greater cost gains from AI-run factories.

China has already run the experiment on cost. BYD sells the Seagull for $7,800-10,300 at home [measured] ([Electrek](https://electrek.co/2025/04/08/byds-low-cost-seagull-ev-now-starts-under-8000-china/)). Unitree sells humanoid robots at about $24,000 on a 63% gross margin, mostly to labs [measured] ([The Robot Report](https://www.therobotreport.com/unitree-ipo-shows-a-real-hardware-business-the-humanoid-case-is-still-early/)). Turnkey grid storage cost $73/kWh in China against $219/kWh in the US in 2025 [estimate] ([Energy-Storage.news](https://www.energy-storage.news/battery-storage-system-prices-continue-to-fall-sharply-bnef-and-ember-reports-find/)). The Western gap is tariffs, security bans, certification, installation and finance. Cheap production also does not make a good business by itself: China's four largest solar module makers lost about $1.54B in the first half of 2025, while TransDigm, a sole-source certified parts maker, held an EBITDA margin near 54% [measured, estimate] ([PV Tech](https://www.pv-tech.org/chinas-top-four-solar-manufacturers-suffer-1-54-billion-losses-h1-2025/), [Motley Fool](https://www.fool.com/data-news/2025/08/06/transdigm-tdg-q3-margin-hits-54)).

"Latent demand" is three different things, and the model keeps them apart. Shortage at today's prices is about $154B a year across the 19 lines, but 84% sits in airliners, gas turbines and naval ships [modelled]. Extra volume from lower prices is small: at the plausible 2032 price, unit volumes rise 1.0-1.7 times, the dollar gain is under $0.5B a year for every line, and ten lines lose revenue when price falls because elasticity is below 1 [modelled]. Every multiple above two comes from new uses that do not exist at scale today. The closest true Model T pattern is low-cost munitions, where Tomahawk buys of 20-55 a year are giving way to plans for roughly 7,000-9,000 cruise missiles a year at $350,000-450,000 [estimate] ([TWZ](https://www.twz.com/air/usaf-plans-to-buy-28000-low-cost-cruise-missiles-in-five-years-advance-with-new-deals), [CRS R49023](https://www.everycrsreport.com/reports/R49023.html)). That demand has one buyer, and in November 2026 the Senate funded the autonomous-warfare office at $3B against a request near $55B [measured] ([Breaking Defense](https://breakingdefense.com/2026/11/senate-appropriators-unveil-1-1t-pentagon-spending-bill-folding-in-reconciliation-priorities/)).

The notes separate two kinds of regulatory drag. One is time before market, such as certification, interconnection and clinical trials. The other is rules that add cost inside the build, such as building codes and nuclear quality assurance. Faster reviews fix only the first. Three points follow. First, the products with the longest regulatory clocks (new aircraft types, drugs, grid-connected generation) are the ones where a faster factory changes least. Second, reform since 2024 is real in nuclear licensing and light aircraft, stalled for drones, and absent for interconnection and airliner certification. The notes advise discounting announced regulatory dates by about a year, since both the drone and supersonic rules slipped by that much. Third, a company helps with regulation mostly by choosing the regime, not by changing it: sell to buyers who self-qualify, sell behind the meter, use declaration routes, pick jurisdictions, and treat legal compliance on origin (China-free magnets, motors, batteries) as the product. Software shortens drafting and completeness review. It does not shorten comment periods, flight-test hours, trial durations, queue studies or a utility's wish for ten years of history.

Timing follows from these responses. Demand for data-center equipment is solid through about 2028: 66 GW is under construction in North America and 95% is pre-committed [estimate] ([JLL](https://www.jll.com/en-us/newsroom/data-center-demand-exceeds-expectations-in-h1-2026)). The model finds that a 2027-funded entrant reaches volume after most shortage dollars have gone: 13% of 2027-2032 shortage dollars remain for metal parts and none for power blocks. Reaching volume one year earlier raises 2032 revenue by about two-thirds for metal parts and one-third for power blocks [modelled]. The stress tests name the same failure in every sector, which is a plant built before a buyer has qualified the output. A $469M shell-body plant on an unproven process had produced nothing two years on [estimate] ([Breaking Defense](https://breakingdefense.com/2026/07/army-well-short-of-155mm-ammo-production-goal-texas-facility-in-the-spotlight/)).

The model was built without sight of the stress tests. They agree on the bottom of the list and on the main finding that no line reaches a tenfold market on price. They disagree in six places, and the stress test is the later and better-checked view in each.

### What could not be sourced

No public figure exists for how long a turbine or engine maker takes to qualify a new casting source. The ranges used here are the researchers' estimates from dated cases, and the times for quality accreditations are unverified recollection. No published price compares compliant and Chinese drone motors, controllers, packs or actuators. No source gives the motor share of missile cost, a delivery-drone unit cost, or a market total for bushings, breakers or tap changers. The 2-5 year figure for utility vendor approval is unverified recollection. No measured demand elasticity was found for metal parts, grid equipment, satellites, delivery drones, cell therapy, kitchens, backyard homes or light aircraft. Unit economics are undisclosed for Hadrian, Machina, Cellares, Neko, Base Power, Slate and every prefab backyard-home firm. The model's cost splits for nine lines and its market totals for seven are its own assumptions, and its author re-checked only three first-round figures. Several first-round inputs rest on search summaries, including the 61-month interconnection figure and the $120B airliner delivery value. Whether the drone rule was published between November 18 and 24 was not checked.

## The founder should build qualified metal parts first

The ranked recommendation has four entries, and the order reflects durability and fit with the question asked, not ease.

First, build the fast metal-parts company in its combined form: castings and inert missile hardware for rocket, expendable-engine, missile and Navy buyers, then land-turbine parts. It is the only candidate the stress tests scored 8 of 10 on both demand certainty and production as the true bottleneck. It faces no Chinese price, and the exit benchmark is an engine maker paying roughly 18-26 times estimated EBITDA for a foundry group [estimate] ([Turbomachinery Magazine](https://www.turbomachinerymag.com/view/ge-aerospace-cpp-acquisition-spacex-turbine-blade-foundry)). It is also slow. The base case is $120-250M of 2032 revenue on $300-500M, the first two years bring $1-25M, and it needs scarce metallurgists. It works as a qualification ladder paid for by customers and fails as a cheap alternative to Howmet.

Second, build asset-light power blocks if the team is stronger in electrical supply chains than metallurgy and wants revenue in 12-18 months. Capital is roughly a quarter of the metal-parts plan. The premium for speed is 10-20% in 2027-2028 and gone by 2029, so the company must turn early orders into qualified designs, a service base or a frame agreement, and should expect an acquisition at about one to two times revenue.

Third, build hard China-free components as a smaller, cheaper company with a likely sale to a prime. Fourth, for a founder set on consumers, build the regional solar and battery installer for the 2029 window when subsidised leases end, knowing it earns installer margins of 17-18%.

Each should start with the 90-day test from the stress tests, none of which builds a factory.

## Conclusion

The pattern across all 19 lines is that where demand is proven and growing, the organisation closest to the customer has already taken production in-house, and where a shortage is deep, the buyers are purchasing the suppliers. What remains for a new producer is a qualified, independent position in parts that integrated players need and do not want to make. That is a narrower prize than the Model T, and it is paid in margin and a strategic sale, not in a volume explosion.

Three numbers would change the ranking, and a founder can get all of them directly in weeks: a turbine maker's real qualification calendar, the real price gap between compliant and Chinese components, and whether professionals or developers will put down deposits for guaranteed delivery. Several dated events will also move it within months: the Energy Department's rule on Chinese grid equipment due December 24, 2026, the expiry of drone-component carve-outs and the start of the defense magnet ban on January 1, 2027, the fiscal 2028 budget request, and Slate's first year of deliveries. The ranking should be re-run when those land.
